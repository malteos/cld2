# Gun / Gungbe vs. confusable languages

Gun sits inside the Gbe dialect continuum with Fon, Aja, and Ewe, and the main LID confusions are with these close relatives. A secondary confusion is with Yoruba, the neighbouring Volta-Niger language.

## Gun (guw) vs. Fon (fon)

- guw and fon are mutually intelligible and share nearly all function words (**ɖò, tò, ná, lɛ́, ɔ́, má, kò, nyí**). Diagnostic differences are mostly lexical and phonological:
  - Gun retains **s / ts** where Fon has **h** in some positions: Gun *sɔ́* vs. Fon *hɔ̀*.
  - First-person pronoun is **un / nyɛ̀** in Gun, **un / nyɛ̀** in Fon (near-identical), but some aspect particles differ (Gun *tò*, Fon *ɖò / wɛ̀*).
  - Place names (*Pɔ̀tó-Nòvó*, *Àjárá*) favour Gun; (*Cotonou, Abomey*) favour Fon.

## Gun (guw) vs. Ewe (ewe)

- Ewe uses the letter **ƒ** (U+0192, bilabial fricative) and **ʋ**; Gun does not use these letters.
- Ewe function words **la, be, eye, nye, yi, wò, nɛ** vs. Gun **ɔ́, ɖò, nyí, lɛ́, ná**.

## Gun / Gbe vs. Yoruba (yor)

- Yoruba uses under-dotted **ẹ, ọ, ṣ** and three-tone marking on every vowel; Gun/Fon use IPA **ɛ, ɔ, ɖ**. The two are visually distinct.
- Yoruba function words **ni, ti, kì, kò, àwọn** are absent in Gun.

## Gun vs. English / French / pcm

- Gun text is rich in **ɛ, ɔ, ɖ** and tone diacritics — rules out English or Nigerian Pidgin. French colonial loans may be present (*politique, école*), but the core function-word inventory disambiguates.
