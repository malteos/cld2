# Gun / Gungbe (guw)

## Summary

Gun (endonyms **Gungbe** or **Gun-gbe**, sometimes **Alada-Gbe**) is a Niger-Congo language of the Gbe cluster (Niger-Congo → Atlantic-Congo → Volta-Niger → Gbe → Phla-Pherá / Eastern Gbe). ISO-639-3 **guw**; no 639-1 code; it is one member of the Gbe dialect continuum that also includes **Fon** (fon), **Ewe** (ewe), **Aja** (ajg), and **Phla-Pherá** (xwl, xwe). Gun is spoken by roughly **500,000–1,000,000** people, mainly in **southern Benin** (Ouémé, Plateau, and around Porto-Novo, the political capital) and in adjacent parts of **southwestern Nigeria** (around Badagry, in Lagos State). It is closely intelligible with Fon.

## Writing system

Gun is written in a Latin alphabet aligned with the pan-Gbe **Africa Alphabet** orthography used for Fon, Ewe, and Aja. Distinctive letters are **ɛ** (U+025B, /ɛ/), **ɔ** (U+0254, /ɔ/), **ɖ** (U+0256, implosive /ɗ/, sometimes written **d**), the digraph **gb** and **kp** for labial-velars, **hw** for /xʷ/, **xw**, and **ny**. Nasal vowels are written with a trailing **n**: *sun*, *tɔn*, *gbɔn*. Three tones exist (high, mid, low); tone marks (acute, grave) are used in dictionaries and religious text but often dropped in informal writing and on the web.

## History and status

Gun is a vehicular language in the Porto-Novo area and is used in radio, Bible translation (Biblica, Jehovah's Witnesses), and local publishing. It is mutually intelligible with Fon to a high degree; many sources treat them as a single Fon-Gbe continuum.
