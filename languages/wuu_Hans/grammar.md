# Wu Chinese grammar

- **Word order**: SVO baseline, but topic-comment structure is even more prominent than in Mandarin; object fronting and bare topics are common: 箇本書我看過了 ('this book, I've already read').
- **Morphology**: Isolating. No inflection. Aspect and modality carried by particles and auxiliaries.
- **Nouns**: No gender, no case. Plural marking via pronoun suffixes 拉 / 哪 (伊拉 'they', 阿拉 'we' in Shanghainese). Classifiers obligatory; the linker 格 often substitutes for Mandarin 的 in possessive/attributive constructions: 我格書.
- **Verbs**: Aspect markers differ from Mandarin. Progressive/locative is often expressed with 垃 / 拉海 / 嗨 ('at [place/state]') rather than 在. Perfective can still surface as 了 but also 好 ('done'). Negation uses 勿 (= Mandarin 不) and 朆 / 呒 (= 没).
- **Phonology**: Wu preserves a three-way voicing contrast (voiced / voiceless unaspirated / voiceless aspirated), unlike Mandarin's two-way contrast. Retains historical final -ʔ glottal stop (checked tones). Tone sandhi is unusually extensive — whole prosodic words re-tonal as a group.
- **Syntax notes**: Copula is 是 (written) or 是个 / 是格 in dialect. Negation 勿 for atelic, 朆 / 呒 / 呒没 for perfective and existential. Classifiers can serve as definite markers (本書 = 'the book'). Double-object order often Recipient–Theme. Final particles 伐 / 啊 / 末 / 哉 / 喏 colour the utterance.
