# Wu Chinese vs. confusable languages

## wuu ↔ cmn / zho (all _Hans) — Wu vs Mandarin

- Both use Simplified Han; shape-level overlap is near-total.
- **Diagnostic lexicon present only in wuu**: 勿 搿 儂 侬 格 垃 拉 嗨 伊 阿拉 朆 呒 哉 伐. Any two or more of these in running text signal Wu.
- Negation: 勿 (wuu) vs 不 (cmn); 朆 / 呒 (wuu) vs 没 / 没有 (cmn).
- Linker/possessive: 格 (wuu, common) vs 的 (cmn, near-universal).
- Demonstrative: 搿 (wuu) vs 这 (cmn); 伊 (wuu 3sg) vs 他 / 她 (cmn).
- Wu text often preserves topic-comment fronting and uses 垃/拉 locatives where Mandarin strictly uses 在.

## wuu ↔ yue — Wu vs Cantonese

- Script variant differs: Simplified (wuu) vs Traditional (yue).
- Different topolect morphemes: wuu has 勿/搿/儂/格/阿拉/伊; yue has 嘅/係/咗/喺/佢/冇/啲/嚟. The two sets do not overlap.

## wuu ↔ jpn — Wu vs Japanese

- Any Hiragana (の は を) or Katakana character rules out Wu. Wu is pure Han + full-width punctuation.

## wuu ↔ kor — Wu vs Korean

- Any Hangul syllable rules out Wu.

## Mixed text caveat

- Wu is most often written in a mixed register with standard Mandarin. A document may contain only a sprinkling of Wu-specific characters surrounded by ordinary Putonghua. Threshold: if ≥2 of {勿, 搿, 儂/侬, 格-as-linker, 阿拉, 哉-final} appear, classify as `wuu` even if most characters look like `cmn`.
