# Wu Chinese (wuu)

## Summary

Wu Chinese, endonym 吳語 / 吴语 (Ngu-gniu / Wúyǔ) — the Shanghainese variety is called 上海話 / 上海闲话 — is a Sinitic language in the Wu branch of Sino-Tibetan. ISO codes: 639-1 — (under `zh`), 639-2 `chi`/`zho`, 639-3 `wuu`. Native speakers number roughly 80–90 million, concentrated in Shanghai, Zhejiang, southern Jiangsu, and parts of Anhui, Jiangxi and Fujian. Major sub-varieties include Shanghainese, Suzhounese, Hangzhounese, Ningbonese, Wenzhounese; mutual intelligibility across the Wu area is limited.

## Writing system

Wu has no official written standard. The `wuu_Hans` row uses **Simplified Han** characters (mainland PRC convention) augmented by a handful of topolect-specific characters that represent Wu morphemes with no direct Mandarin equivalent: 勿 (negator 'not', = Mandarin 不), 搿 (this), 儂 / 侬 (you — Shanghainese 2sg or 'person'), 格 (DE/linker, often for 的 or as classifier marker), 垃 / 拉 (locative/progressive), 伊 (3sg), 阿拉 (1pl, Shanghainese 'we'), 嗨 (at). Written Wu is more commonly mixed with standard written Mandarin in practice; pure Wu prose is rare outside literary experimentation and dialect blogs.

## Status

No official recognition; not taught in schools. Wu is a heritage/home language with strong regional identity in Shanghai and Jiangnan. Romanisation systems (e.g. Shanghainese Wugniu, Qian Nairong's scheme) exist but are not widely used in running text.
