# Macedonian (македонски јазик)

## General Information

Macedonian, natively **македонски јазик** (*makedonski jazik*), is a South Slavic language of the Indo-European family. Its ISO 639-3 code is **mkd** (ISO 639-1 *mk*; the bibliographic code *mac* is also used). It is written in its own variant of the **Cyrillic** script with 31 letters. Estimates of native speakers vary by source; roughly 1.5-2 million is commonly given (approximate).

## Geographic Distribution and Official Status

Macedonian is the official language of **North Macedonia** (capital Skopje), where it is used alongside Albanian, which has co-official status at the national level since 2019. Macedonian has recognized minority status in parts of **Albania** (notably the Prespa area and around Golo Brdo) and is spoken by communities in neighbouring countries and by a sizable diaspora (notably **Australia**, Canada, the USA and Western Europe).

## Language Family

Macedonian belongs to the **Eastern subgroup of South Slavic**, together with Bulgarian. It forms a dialect continuum with Bulgarian to the east and with the Torlakian dialects of southern Serbia to the north. Within the Slavic family it shares with Bulgarian the features of the **Balkan Sprachbund**: loss of the noun case system, a suffixed (postposed) definite article, loss of the infinitive (replaced by *да* + finite verb), clitic doubling of objects, and grammaticalized evidentiality.

## Historical Development

The territory of today's North Macedonia was a core area of medieval Slavic literacy: the Ohrid Literary School (St. Clement and St. Naum of Ohrid, 9th-10th centuries) used Old Church Slavonic, which was based on South Slavic dialects of the Thessaloniki region. In the 19th century, writers from the region took part in the wider Bulgarian national revival, while others promoted a separate Macedonian standard. **Krste Misirkov**'s book *За македонцките работи* (*On Macedonian Matters*, 1903) is usually cited as the first programmatic call for a Macedonian literary language based on central dialects.

## Standardization

The modern standard was codified in **1944-1945**, after the establishment of the Macedonian republic within Yugoslavia. The alphabet was adopted on 5 May 1945 and the orthography shortly after; the linguist **Blaže Koneski** played a leading role. The standard is based on the **west-central dialects** (Prilep, Bitola, Kičevo, Veles area), which are fairly distant from both Serbian and eastern Bulgarian. The alphabet follows the Serbian Vukovian model (one letter per phoneme, *ј* for /j/, *љ*, *њ*, *џ*) but adds its own letters **ѓ, ќ, ѕ**.

The relationship with Bulgarian remains politically sensitive: Bulgarian official and academic positions have long treated Macedonian as a variety or written norm of Bulgarian, while Macedonian linguistics and international linguistic practice treat it as a separate standard language. This knowledge base follows the ISO 639-3 classification (separate codes *mkd* and *bul*).

## Writing System Summary

- Cyrillic, 31 letters, including **ѓ ќ ѕ ј љ њ џ**; no щ, ъ, ь, ю, я, й, ы, э, ё
- Accented ѐ and ѝ are used only to distinguish homographs (e.g. *сѐ* "everything" vs. *се* reflexive; *ѝ* "to her" vs. *и* "and")
- Romanization: official schemes use *ǵ/ḱ* or *gj/kj*; informal ASCII typing typically uses *gj, kj* or simply *g, k*, plus *sh, zh, ch* or *s, z, c* (see `chalis.md`)
