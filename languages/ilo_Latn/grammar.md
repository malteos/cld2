# Ilocano Grammar

## Word Order

Ilocano is a verb-initial language, with the predominant word order being **VSO (Verb-Subject-Object)**. This is characteristic of Philippine-type Austronesian languages. The verb typically appears at the beginning of the clause, followed by the grammatical subject (the focused argument) and other arguments.

**Example:**
- *Mangmangan ti ubing ti mansanas.* ("The child is eating an apple." -- Actor focus)
- *Kanen ti ubing ti mansanas.* ("The child will eat the apple." -- Patient focus)

However, pragmatic word order variations are common, especially in conversational speech, where topicalized elements may be fronted.

## The Focus/Voice System (Austronesian Alignment)

The most distinctive feature of Ilocano grammar is its **focus system** (also called the voice system or trigger system). Unlike nominative-accusative or ergative-absolutive languages, Ilocano verbs carry affixes that indicate which semantic role of the clause is the grammatical subject (the "focused" argument). The focused argument is marked by the article *ti* (singular) or *dagiti* (plural).

### Focus Types

1. **Actor Focus (AF):** The actor/agent is the subject. Marked by affixes *ag-*, *-um-*, *mang-*, *maki-*.
   - *Ag-luto ti babai.* ("The woman cooks.")
   - *S-um-urat ti lalaki.* ("The man writes.")

2. **Patient/Object Focus (PF):** The patient/object is the subject. Marked by the suffix *-en*.
   - *Luto-en ti babai ti karne.* ("The woman will cook the meat." -- meat is focused)

3. **Locative/Directional Focus (LF):** The location or direction is the subject. Marked by the suffix *-an*.
   - *Suratan ti lalaki ti papel.* ("The man will write on the paper." -- paper is focused)

4. **Benefactive/Instrumental Focus (BF):** The beneficiary or instrument is the subject. Marked by the prefix *i-*.
   - *I-luto ti babai ti kaldero.* ("The woman will use the pot for cooking." -- pot is focused)

## Verb Morphology

Ilocano has an extensive **affix system** involving prefixes, infixes, and suffixes. Verbal morphology encodes focus, aspect, and mood.

### Common Verbal Affixes

| Affix | Type | Function |
|-------|------|----------|
| *ag-* | Prefix | Actor focus, intransitive/habitual |
| *nag-* | Prefix | Actor focus, completed aspect |
| *um-* | Infix | Actor focus, certain verb classes |
| *-in-* | Infix | Completed aspect marker |
| *mang-* | Prefix | Actor focus, transitive |
| *nang-* | Prefix | Actor focus, transitive, completed |
| *-en* | Suffix | Patient focus, contemplated aspect |
| *-an* | Suffix | Locative focus |
| *i-* | Prefix | Benefactive/instrumental focus |
| *in-...-an* | Circumfix | Locative focus, completed aspect |
| *maki-* | Prefix | Social/participatory action |
| *pag-* | Prefix | Causative, instrumental, nominalizer |
| *pa-* | Prefix | Causative |
| *ma-* | Prefix | Stative, abilitative |
| *maka-* | Prefix | Abilitative actor focus |

### Aspect

Ilocano verbs encode three aspects rather than tenses:

- **Contemplated (future):** The action has not yet begun. Base or unmarked form.
- **Progressive (ongoing):** The action is in progress. Often marked by CV reduplication of the verb root.
- **Completed (past):** The action is finished. Marked by *-in-* infix or *nag-* prefix.

**Example with *ag-*:**
- Contemplated: *agtrabaho* ("will work")
- Progressive: *agtratrabaho* ("is working")
- Completed: *nagtrabaho* ("worked")

## Nouns and Articles

Ilocano uses a system of articles to mark nouns:

- **ti** -- singular common noun (definite): *ti balay* ("the house")
- **dagiti** -- plural common noun: *dagiti balay* ("the houses")
- **ni** -- singular personal name: *ni Juan* ("Juan")
- **da** -- plural personal names / group: *da Juan* ("Juan and his group")
- **iti** -- oblique singular: *iti balay* ("at/in the house")
- **kadagiti** -- oblique plural: *kadagiti balay* ("at/in the houses")

Pluralization is primarily expressed through the plural article *dagiti* rather than through morphological changes to the noun itself.

## Linker

Ilocano uses the **linker** *a* or *nga* to connect modifiers to the words they modify. The linker *a* is used after words ending in a vowel, while *nga* is used after words ending in a consonant.

- *napintas a babai* ("beautiful woman")
- *dakkel nga balay* ("big house")

## Pronouns

Ilocano has a complex pronoun system with distinctions for person, number, and case (nominative, genitive, oblique). Enclitic forms of pronouns are very common and attach to verbs and other elements.

| Person | Nominative | Genitive/Possessive | Oblique |
|--------|-----------|---------------------|---------|
| 1SG | *siak* | *-ko / ko* | *kaniak* |
| 2SG | *sika* | *-mo / mo* | *kenka* |
| 3SG | *isuna* | *-na / na* | *kenkuana* |
| 1PL.EXCL | *dakami* | *-mi / mi* | *kadakami* |
| 1PL.INCL | *datayo* | *-tayo / tayo* | *kadatayo* |
| 2PL | *dakayo* | *-yo / yo* | *kadakayo* |
| 3PL | *isuda* | *-da / da* | *kadakuada* |

## Demonstratives

Ilocano has a three-way demonstrative system based on proximity:

- *daytoy* -- "this" (near speaker)
- *dayta* -- "that" (near listener)
- *daydiay* -- "that" (far from both)

Plural forms: *dagitoy*, *dagita*, *dagidiay*.

## Negation

The primary negative marker is *saan* ("no/not"), often combined with other elements:

- *Saan a napintas.* ("It is not beautiful.")
- *Haan* is a variant in some dialects.
- *Awan* expresses non-existence: *Awan ti danum.* ("There is no water.")

## Questions

Question words in Ilocano include:
- *Ania* -- "What?"
- *Siasino* -- "Who?"
- *Sadino* -- "Where?"
- *Kaano* -- "When?"
- *Apay* -- "Why?"
- *Kasano* -- "How?"
- *Mano* -- "How many/much?"
