# Standard Latvian vs. confusable languages

Closest confusables in the CommonLID set are the macrolanguage label `lav`
and the sister variety Latgalian (`ltg`). Outside the set, Lithuanian.

## Standard Latvian (lvs) vs. Latgalian (ltg)

- Latgalian uses the letter **y** for a central vowel /ɨ/; Standard Latvian
  **does not use y** at all.
- Latgalian keeps archaic morphology (e.g. different case endings such as
  dative plural `-im/-ym` vs lvs `-iem`; locative `-ā` retained more
  uniformly in lvs).
- Function words: ltg `i, ni, tys, jei, dreižuok, te, tok`; lvs `un, ne, tas,
  ja, drīz, te, tomēr`.
- Latgalian preserves stress-dependent palatalisation and shows different
  vowel reductions (ltg `bōls` ~ lvs `bāls`).
- Latgalian spelling keeps clusters **ct, pt** where lvs has **kt, t**.
- Typical Latgalian letter sequences include **ūo, ōu, ī-** which are rare
  in standard Latvian.

## Standard Latvian vs. Latvian macro (lav)

- The `lvs` label is a subset of `lav`. Prefer `lvs` when text clearly
  follows the 1908/1946 standard orthography and lacks Latgalian markers.
- Text from news outlets, government, Wikipedia lead paragraphs, legal
  texts is almost always `lvs`.

## Standard Latvian vs. Lithuanian (not in CommonLID)

- Lithuanian uses **ą ę į ų ė y** (no macrons); Standard Latvian uses
  **ā ē ī ū** (macrons).
- Lithuanian letters **č š ž** overlap with Latvian, but Lithuanian lacks
  the cedilla consonants **ģ ķ ļ ņ** that are Latvian-diagnostic.
- Lithuanian function words: `ir, yra, aš, tu, jis, ji, bet, ar, kas`; lvs
  `un, ir, es, tu, viņš, viņa, bet, vai, kas`.
