# Standard Latvian (lvs)

## Summary

Standard Latvian (endonym: *latviešu valoda*, more specifically *literārā
latviešu valoda*) is the codified national language of Latvia and the
largest sub-variety of the Latvian macrolanguage (`lav`). It is a Baltic
language in the Indo-European family (Indo-European → Balto-Slavic → Baltic
→ Eastern Baltic → Latvian). ISO codes: 639-3 `lvs` (individual language),
within macro `lav`. Native speakers total about 1.5 million, with diaspora
populations in the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, Ireland,
Russia, Sweden, Australia, Canada and Brazil. It is the sole official state
language of the Republic of Latvia.

## Writing system

Standard Latvian uses the 33-letter Latin alphabet shared with the macro:
a ā b c č d e ē f g ģ h i ī j k ķ l ļ m n ņ o p r s š t u ū v z ž. It
employs macrons on long vowels (**ā ē ī ū**), háček on postalveolars (**č š
ž**), and the distinctive cedilla/comma "mīkstinājums" on **ģ ķ ļ ņ**.
Unlike Latgalian (`ltg`), Standard Latvian **does not use the letter y** and
**does not use the double-dot trema on o**. The 2008 orthography keeps **ch**
out of the standard alphabet. Bicameral casing.

## Status

Standard Latvian is maintained by the State Language Centre (Valsts valodas
centrs). The modern orthography was adopted 1908–1946, replacing the older
German-influenced Gothic (Fraktur) tradition. It is the language of
government, education and media in Latvia and has the status of an official
EU language.
