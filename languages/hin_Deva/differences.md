# Hindi vs confusable languages

## hin vs Marathi (mar)

- Marathi uses the letter **ळ** (retroflex lateral, U+0933) freely — Hindi does not. Even a single **ळ** strongly indicates mar.
- Marathi has a cerebral **ण** in common native vocabulary where Hindi would often have **न**; noun endings **-चा / -ची / -चे** (genitive) are Marathi. Hindi's equivalents are **का / की / के**.
- Marathi copula is **आहे / आहेत** ("is/are"); Hindi is **है / हैं**.
- Hindi uses the ergative marker **ने** on the perfective subject; Marathi uses **ने** but places agreement differently; both overlap, so **ळ** / **आहे** / **-चा** are cleaner diagnostics.

## hin vs Sanskrit (san)

- Sanskrit shows very high density of consonant conjuncts (क्ष, ज्ञ, स्य, र्म, etc.) and frequent visarga **ः** at word-end, neither common in modern Hindi.
- Sanskrit lacks the Hindi postposition set **का/की/के/है/हैं/ने**; its nouns inflect for eight cases instead.
- Sanskrit is verb-final, with no modern loanwords (English/Portuguese/Urdu) and no progressive "**रहा है**" construction.
- Presence of **ॐ**, repeated **एव**, **इति**, **च**, **वा**, **न** (without the *hīn* of *nahīn*) points to san.

## hin vs Urdu (urd)

- Identical underlying Hindustani vocabulary in informal registers, but script-disjoint: hin is Devanagari, urd is Arabic. Any character in one block rules out the other entirely.
- High-register vocabulary diverges (Sanskrit tatsamas in hin, Persian/Arabic in urd), visible only after transliteration.

## hin vs Konkani (gom)

- Konkani in Devanagari shares **ळ** with Marathi and uses **आसा** for "is". Hindi never uses **ळ** or **आसा** natively.

## hin vs Nepali / Bhojpuri / Maithili

Same script, same core postpositions; Hindi is often distinguished by the strict **ने** ergative, **है/हैं** copula and absence of Nepali-specific **छ / छन्** copulas and honorific **तपाईं**.
