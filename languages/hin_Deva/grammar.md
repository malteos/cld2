# Hindi grammar

- **Word order**: Subject–Object–Verb (SOV). Constituent order is fairly free for topicalisation; adjectives and demonstratives precede the noun; postpositions (never prepositions) mark case and semantic role.
- **Morphology**: Moderately fusional. Nouns show two genders (masculine, feminine) and two numbers. Direct vs oblique stems are distinguished before postpositions: *laṛkā* → *laṛke* (before *ne/ko/se*).
- **Postpositions**: Core set is **का / की / के** (genitive, gendered), **को** (accusative/dative), **से** (ablative/instrumental), **में** (locative "in"), **पर** (locative "on"), **तक** (up to), **ने** (ergative marker, obligatory with perfective transitives).
- **Pronouns**: मैं (I), तू / तुम / आप (you, intimate/familiar/polite), वह (he/she/that/he-she/they distal), यह (this/proximal), हम (we), वे (they). Pronouns also inflect for oblique case.
- **Verbs**: Three-stem model (verb root + aspect participle + tense auxiliary). Habitual **-ता / -ती / -ते** + copula, progressive **रहा / रही / रहे** + copula, perfective **-या / -यी / -ये** (with ergative *ne* in transitives). Copula: **है, हैं, था, थी, थे, होगा, होगी, होंगे**.
- **Phonology**: Four-way consonant contrast (voiced/voiceless × aspirated/unaspirated), retroflex series ट ठ ड ढ ण, nasal vowels marked by anusvāra/candrabindu. Stress is non-contrastive and largely predictable.
- **Syntax**: Pro-drop; gender-number agreement on the verb is with the nominative subject, or with the direct object in ergative perfectives. Relative-correlative clauses (*जो … वह*) are common. Negation uses **नहीं** (most uses) or **न** / **मत** (subjunctive / imperative).
