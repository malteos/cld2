# Kyrgyz Grammar (Кыргыз тилинин грамматикасы)

## Word Order

Kyrgyz is a head-final language with a basic Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) word order. The verb typically appears at the end of the sentence, while modifiers precede the elements they modify. Adjectives precede nouns, adverbs precede verbs, and relative clauses precede their head nouns. However, word order in Kyrgyz is relatively flexible due to its rich case marking system, which allows speakers to rearrange constituents for pragmatic purposes such as topicalization or focus. In spoken language and colloquial registers, departures from canonical SOV order are common.

Example: Бала китепти окуду (Bala kitepti okudu) — "The child read the book" (Subject-Object-Verb).

## Agglutinative Morphology

Kyrgyz is a highly agglutinative language, meaning that grammatical relationships and meanings are expressed through the sequential addition of suffixes to word stems. A single word can carry multiple suffixes, each contributing a distinct grammatical meaning. For example, the word "китептеримден" (kitepterimden) can be broken down as: китеп (book) + тер (plural) + им (my) + ден (from) = "from my books." This agglutinative character is one of the defining typological features of Turkic languages and is central to the structure of Kyrgyz.

## Vowel Harmony

Vowel harmony is a fundamental phonological process in Kyrgyz that governs the selection of vowels in suffixes. There are two dimensions of vowel harmony:

### Front-Back Harmony

Vowels are classified as front (э/е, и, ө, ү) or back (а, ы, о, у). Suffixes must contain vowels that agree in frontness or backness with the last vowel of the stem. For example, the plural suffix is -лар after back vowels (балалар, "children") but -лер after front vowels (үйлөр, "houses").

### Rounded-Unrounded Harmony

Within the front-back classes, there is a further distinction between rounded and unrounded vowels. After rounded vowels (о, ө, у, ү), suffixes tend to take rounded variants. For instance, the dative suffix appears as -го/-гө after stems ending in rounded vowels (көлгө, "to the lake") versus -га/-ге after unrounded vowels (балага, "to the child").

The eight vowels of Kyrgyz and their harmonic classification:

| | Front | Back |
|---|---|---|
| **Unrounded** | э/е, и | а, ы |
| **Rounded** | ө, ү | о, у |

## Noun System

### Cases

Kyrgyz has six grammatical cases, each marked by suffixes that vary according to vowel harmony and the final sound of the stem:

1. **Nominative** (Атооч жөндөмө) — unmarked, the citation form: бала (child)
2. **Genitive** (Илик жөндөмө) — marks possession: баланын (of the child), -нын/-нин/-нун/-нүн, -дын/-дин/-дун/-дүн, -тын/-тин/-тун/-түн
3. **Dative** (Барыш жөндөмө) — marks direction or recipient: балага (to the child), -га/-ге/-го/-гө, -ка/-ке/-ко/-кө
4. **Accusative** (Табыш жөндөмө) — marks definite direct objects: баланы (the child [as object]), -ны/-ни/-ну/-нү, -ды/-ди/-ду/-дү, -ты/-ти/-ту/-тү
5. **Locative** (Жатыш жөндөмө) — marks location: балада (at/in the child), -да/-де/-до/-дө, -та/-те/-то/-тө
6. **Ablative** (Чыгыш жөндөмө) — marks source or origin: баладан (from the child), -дан/-ден/-дон/-дөн, -тан/-тен/-тон/-төн

### Number

Kyrgyz has no grammatical gender. The plural is formed with the suffix -лар/-лер/-дар/-дер/-тар/-тер, the specific form determined by vowel harmony and consonant assimilation rules. When a numeral or quantifier is present, the noun typically remains in singular form (беш бала, "five children," not *беш балалар).

### Possession

Possessive suffixes are attached directly to nouns to indicate ownership:

- 1st person singular: -м/-ым/-им/-ум/-үм (китебим, "my book")
- 2nd person singular: -ң/-ың/-иң/-уң/-үң (китебиң, "your book")
- 3rd person singular: -ы/-и/-у/-ү/-сы/-си/-су/-сү (китеби, "his/her book")
- 1st person plural: -быз/-биз/-буз/-бүз (китебибиз, "our book")
- 2nd person plural: -ңыз/-ңиз/-ңуз/-ңүз (китебиңиз, "your [formal/plural] book")
- 3rd person plural: -ы/-и/-у/-ү/-сы/-си/-су/-сү (китеби, "their book")

## Verb System

### Verb Stems and Infinitive

The infinitive (citation form) of Kyrgyz verbs ends in a vowel or consonant, with no single infinitive marker equivalent to English "to." The dictionary form is often given as the imperative second person singular, which equals the bare stem: бар (go), кел (come), жаз (write), оку (read).

### Tense System

Kyrgyz has a rich tense-aspect system:

- **Present continuous**: formed with -(ы)п жат- (жазып жатам, "I am writing")
- **Aorist/habitual present**: -а/-е/-й (жазам, "I write [habitually]")
- **Definite past**: -ды/-ди/-ду/-дү, -ты/-ти/-ту/-тү (жаздым, "I wrote")
- **Indefinite past (evidential)**: -ган/-ген/-кан/-кен (жазган, "I wrote [reportedly / I have written]")
- **Future**: -а/-е/-й + auxiliary or -(а/е)р (жазар, "I will write")
- **Intentional future**: -мак/-мек (жазмакмын, "I intend to write")

### Evidentiality

An important feature of Kyrgyz is grammaticalized evidentiality. The definite past tense (-ды/-ди) indicates that the speaker directly witnessed the event, while the indefinite past (-ган/-ген) signals that the speaker learned about the event indirectly, through hearsay, inference, or discovery. This distinction is obligatory and carries important pragmatic implications.

### Mood

Kyrgyz expresses several moods through verb morphology:

- **Indicative**: the default mood for statements of fact
- **Imperative**: бар! (go!), баргыла! (go! [plural])
- **Optative/Desiderative**: -айын/-ейин (жазайын, "let me write")
- **Conditional**: -са/-се (жазсам, "if I write")
- **Necessitative**: -ыш/-иш керек (жазышым керек, "I must write")

### Voice

- **Passive**: -ыл/-ил, -л (жазылды, "was written")
- **Causative**: -тыр/-тир/-дыр/-дир, -гыз/-гиз (жаздырды, "made [someone] write")
- **Reciprocal**: -ыш/-иш (жазышты, "wrote to each other")
- **Reflexive**: -ын/-ин (жуунду, "washed oneself")

### Negation

Verbs are negated by inserting the suffix -ба/-бе/-бо/-бө or -па/-пе/-по/-пө before the tense suffix: жазбадым (I did not write), окубайм (I do not read).

## Postpositions

Kyrgyz uses postpositions rather than prepositions, consistent with its head-final typology. Postpositions follow the noun they govern and may require the noun to appear in a specific case:

- менен (with) — requires nominative: бала менен (with the child)
- үчүн (for) — requires nominative: бала үчүн (for the child)
- жөнүндө (about) — requires nominative: китеп жөнүндө (about the book)
- тарабынан (by) — requires genitive: баланын тарабынан (by the child)
- кийин (after) — requires ablative: сабактан кийин (after the lesson)
- чейин (until) — requires dative: кечке чейин (until evening)

## Subordination and Complex Sentences

Kyrgyz forms complex sentences primarily through participial and converb (gerund) constructions rather than subordinate clauses with conjunctions. Participles (verbal adjectives) and converbs (verbal adverbs) play a central role in linking clauses. The converb in -(ы)п is particularly productive, chaining sequential or simultaneous actions: ал келип, отурду ("he came and sat down"). Relative clauses are formed by placing a participle before the noun it modifies: мен жазган китеп ("the book that I wrote").

## Questions

Yes/no questions are formed by adding the particle -бы/-би/-бу/-бү (or -пы/-пи/-пу/-пү after voiceless consonants) to the predicate: Сен барасыңбы? ("Are you going?"). Content questions use interrogative words such as ким (who), эмне (what), кайда (where), качан (when), кантип (how), and эмнеге/неге (why), which typically occupy the preverbal position.
