# Cantonese (yue)

## Summary

Cantonese, endonym 粵語 / 廣東話 (Yuhtyúh / Gwóngdūngwá), is a Sinitic language in the Yue branch of Sino-Tibetan. ISO codes: 639-1 — (shares `zh`), 639-2 `chi`/`zho`, 639-3 `yue`. It has roughly 80–85 million L1 speakers, primarily in Guangdong and Guangxi (PRC), Hong Kong, Macau, and large diaspora communities in Southeast Asia, North America, Europe, and Australia. Hong Kong Cantonese is the de facto written standard for the language.

## Writing system

The `yue_Hant` row uses **Traditional Han** characters (as in Hong Kong and Macau), augmented by a set of Cantonese-specific characters that encode morphemes absent from written Mandarin: 嘅 (possessive/DE), 係 (copula), 咗 (perfective), 喺 (locative 'at'), 嚟 (come), 佢 (3rd-person pronoun), 冇 (have-not), 啲 (plural/some), 乜 (what), 嘢 (thing), 氹 (pond/coax), 睇 (see/look), 識 (know). These co-occur with standard Traditional forms 國, 個, 萬, 實, 來, 會, 東, 車, 對. Punctuation is full-width; there is no inter-word spacing. Jyutping and Yale romanisations exist but rarely appear in running text.

## Status

Official (alongside English and written Chinese) in Hong Kong and Macau. In the mainland it is a regional spoken variety without strong official backing. Written Cantonese flourishes in Hong Kong media, subtitles, internet forums, and informal publishing.
