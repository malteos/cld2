# Hungarian Grammar

## Word Order

Hungarian has a relatively flexible word order, but the pragmatically neutral order tends toward SOV (Subject-Object-Verb) or SVO depending on discourse context. The key organizing principle is topic-focus structure: the sentence is divided into a topic (what the sentence is about) and a comment (what is said about the topic). Within the comment, the position immediately before the finite verb is the focus position, which carries the main informational emphasis. This means that word order in Hungarian conveys pragmatic meaning rather than grammatical relations, which are instead expressed through the extensive case system.

Example:
- **Peter megette az almat.** (Peter ate the apple - neutral)
- **Az almat Peter ette meg.** (It was Peter who ate the apple - focus on Peter)
- **Peter az almat ette meg.** (It was the apple that Peter ate - focus on the apple)

## Agglutinative Morphology

Hungarian is a highly agglutinative language, meaning that grammatical relationships are expressed by adding suffixes (and occasionally prefixes) to word stems. A single Hungarian word can carry information that would require an entire phrase or clause in English.

Example: **hazazokert** = haz-a-z-ok-ert (house-POSS.3SG-PL-CAUS) = "for those houses of his/hers"

## Noun Cases

Hungarian has one of the most extensive case systems among European languages, with 18 or more grammatical cases (the exact count varies by analysis). The cases are expressed through suffixes attached to the noun stem:

| Case | Suffix | Example | Meaning |
|------|--------|---------|---------|
| Nominative | -0 | haz | house |
| Accusative | -t | hazat | house (object) |
| Dative | -nak/-nek | haznak | to the house |
| Instrumental | -val/-vel | hazzal | with the house |
| Causal-final | -ert | hazert | for the house |
| Translative | -va/-ve | hazza | becoming a house |
| Terminative | -ig | hazig | until the house |
| Essive-formal | -kent | hazkent | as a house |
| Inessive | -ban/-ben | hazban | in the house |
| Superessive | -n/-on/-en/-on | hazon | on the house |
| Adessive | -nal/-nel | haznal | at the house |
| Sublative | -ra/-re | hazra | onto the house |
| Delative | -rol/-rol | hazrol | from (surface of) house |
| Elative | -bol/-bol | hazbol | out of the house |
| Illative | -ba/-be | hazba | into the house |
| Allative | -hoz/-hez/-hoz | hazhoz | to(wards) the house |
| Ablative | -tol/-tol | haztol | from the house |
| Distributive | -nkent | hazankent | per house |

## Vowel Harmony

Hungarian exhibits a systematic vowel harmony system. Suffixes have front-vowel and back-vowel variants, and the choice depends on the vowels in the stem:

- **Back vowels:** a, a, o, o, u, u
- **Front unrounded vowels:** e, e, i, i
- **Front rounded vowels:** o, o, u, u

Examples:
- **hazban** (in the house - back vowel stem takes -ban)
- **kertben** (in the garden - front vowel stem takes -ben)
- **torben** (in the tower - front rounded vowel stem takes -ben)

Some stems with mixed vowels follow the last vowel or are lexically specified. The front rounded vowels (o, o, u, u) sometimes trigger a third harmonic variant in certain suffixes.

## Verb Conjugation

### Definite vs. Indefinite Conjugation

One of the most distinctive features of Hungarian grammar is the dual conjugation system. Verbs are conjugated differently depending on whether the object is definite or indefinite:

- **Indefinite:** Latok egy hazat. (I see a house.)
- **Definite:** Latom a hazat. (I see the house.)

The definite conjugation is used when the object is a definite noun phrase (with the definite article, a demonstrative, a possessive, or a proper noun). The indefinite conjugation is used when there is no object, or when the object is indefinite.

### Verbal Prefixes (Igekotok)

Hungarian uses a rich system of verbal prefixes (also called preverbs) that modify the meaning of verbs, often indicating direction, completion (perfectivity), or creating entirely new lexical meanings:

- **meg-** (perfective): ir (write) > megir (write completely, finish writing)
- **el-** (away): megy (go) > elmegy (go away)
- **ki-** (out): jon (come) > kijon (come out)
- **be-** (in): megy (go) > bemegy (go in)
- **fel-** (up): all (stand) > felall (stand up)
- **le-** (down): ul (sit) > leul (sit down)
- **vissza-** (back): jon (come) > visszajon (come back)
- **ossze-** (together): gyujt (collect) > osszegyujt (collect together)

These prefixes are separable and move to a post-verbal position in negation, focus constructions, and certain other contexts:
- **Megirtam a levelet.** (I wrote the letter - completed action)
- **Nem irtam meg a levelet.** (I did not write the letter - prefix moves after verb in negation)

## Postpositions

Unlike most European languages, Hungarian uses postpositions rather than prepositions. These follow the noun they modify:

- **az asztal alatt** (under the table, lit. "the table under")
- **a haz mogott** (behind the house, lit. "the house behind")
- **a varos fele** (toward the city, lit. "the city toward")

Some postpositions have three forms expressing location, direction toward, and direction away from:
- **alatt / ala / alol** (under / to under / from under)
- **mogott / moge / mogul** (behind / to behind / from behind)

## Possessive Suffixes

Possession is primarily expressed through suffixes on the possessed noun rather than through possessive pronouns:

- **hazam** (my house)
- **hazad** (your house)
- **haza** (his/her house)
- **hazunk** (our house)
- **hazatok** (your [pl.] house)
- **hazuk** (their house)

## No Grammatical Gender

Hungarian has no grammatical gender. There is no distinction between masculine, feminine, or neuter in nouns, adjectives, pronouns, or verb forms. The third-person singular pronoun **o** means both "he" and "she."

## Phonology

### Vowel Length Distinction

Hungarian distinguishes between short and long vowels, which are phonemically contrastive:
- **a** /ɒ/ vs. **a** /aː/
- **e** /ɛ/ vs. **e** /eː/
- **i** /i/ vs. **i** /iː/
- **o** /o/ vs. **o** /oː/
- **o** /ø/ vs. **o** /øː/
- **u** /u/ vs. **u** /uː/
- **u** /y/ vs. **u** /yː/

### Palatal Consonants

Hungarian has several palatal consonants, many represented by digraphs:
- **gy** /ɟ/ (voiced palatal stop)
- **ty** /c/ (voiceless palatal stop)
- **ny** /ɲ/ (palatal nasal)
- **ly** /j/ (historically a palatal lateral, now pronounced as /j/)

## Derivational Morphology

Hungarian has extremely productive derivational morphology, allowing the creation of new words through suffixation:

- **-as/-es** (nominalization): tanul (learn) > tanulas (learning)
- **-o/-o** (agent): tanul (learn) > tanulo (student/learner)
- **-hat/-het** (potential): ir (write) > irhat (can write)
- **-tat/-tet** (causative): ir (write) > irat (have something written)
- **-kodik/-kedik/-kodik** (reflexive/middle): mos (wash) > mosakodik (wash oneself)
- **-gat/-get** (frequentative): ir (write) > irogat (write occasionally)
- **-tlan/-tlen** (privative): viz (water) > viztelen (waterless)
- **-s** (adjective from noun): viz (water) > vizes (watery)
