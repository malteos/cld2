# Sudanese Arabic (apd)

## Summary

Sudanese Arabic (endonym: **اللهجة السودانية** *al-lahja s-sūdāniyya* or simply
**العامية السودانية**) is the Arabic variety spoken in the Republic of Sudan
and, with dialectal overlap, parts of eastern Chad, Eritrea, and South
Sudan. Family: Afro-Asiatic → Semitic → Central Semitic → Arabic →
Sudanese/Chadian Arabic. ISO: **639-3 `apd`** (macrolanguage `ara`); note the
code `apd` specifically denotes the Sudanese variety, while the closely
related Chadian variety is `shu`. Native speakers ~30–35 million. Principal
region: Sudan (Khartoum, Omdurman, the Nile valley) plus diaspora.

## Writing system

Uses the **Arabic script** (U+0600–U+06FF) with standard MSA orthography.
Sudanese writing tends to be MSA-conforming with dialectal flavour entering
via lexicon and occasional colloquial spellings. No casing; short vowels
unmarked. Some informal texts write **ق** as **ق** even when pronounced [g],
keeping the MSA letter.

## Status

De facto lingua franca across Sudan's many indigenous languages; shares
substantial lexicon with Egyptian and Hijazi Arabic but carries Nubian, Beja,
and English borrowings. Used in Sudanese broadcast media and popular music;
formal writing remains MSA. Social-media Sudanese Arabic is increasingly
visible in corpora.
