# Bengali grammar

- **Word order**: Subject–Object–Verb (SOV); adjectives and demonstratives precede the noun; postpositions only.
- **Morphology**: Moderately fusional / agglutinative in verb forms. No grammatical gender (a major contrast with Hindi and Urdu). Nouns inflect for number (singular, plural), definiteness via classifiers, and case.
- **Nouns**: Animate plural uses **-রা / -এরা** (as in ছেলেরা "boys"), inanimate plural uses **-গুলো / -গুলি**. Classifier clitics **-টা / -টি** (sg) and **-গুলো / -গুলি** (pl) mark definiteness and are highly diagnostic. Four cases in modern usage: nominative, genitive **-এর / -র**, objective/dative **-কে**, locative **-এ / -তে**.
- **Pronouns**: আমি (I), তুমি / আপনি (you, familiar/polite), তুই (you, intimate), সে / তিনি (he/she, neutral/honorific), আমরা (we), তোমরা (you-pl), তারা (they). Demonstratives এই (this) / সেই (that) + classifier.
- **Verbs**: Highly inflected. Agree with subject's person and honorific level (1, 2-intimate, 2-familiar, 2-polite, 3-ordinary, 3-honorific) but *not* with number or gender. Tense–aspect suffix + personal ending. Copula is **আছে / ছিল / ছিলেন / থাকে** or the equational zero-copula present (*o chātro* "he is a student").
- **Phonology**: Seven vowels (inherent /ɔ/ realised as /o/ in some contexts), long–short vowel distinction largely lost in modern Bengali orthography; nasal vowels, retroflex series, and an aspiration distinction remain.
- **Syntax**: Pro-drop; honorific agreement on the verb makes the subject optional. Negation via postposed **না** (indicative) and **নি** (perfect/negative-past). Yes/no questions use sentence-final **কি**.
