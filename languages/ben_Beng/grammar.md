# Bengali Grammar (ben_Beng)

## Word Order

Bengali is a **Subject-Object-Verb (SOV)** language. The canonical word order places the subject first, followed by the object, and the verb at the end of the clause:

- আমি ভাত খাই (Ami bhat khai) - "I rice eat" = "I eat rice"
- সে বই পড়ে (She boi pore) - "He/she book reads" = "He/she reads a book"

Postpositions are used instead of prepositions, and adjectives precede the noun they modify. Relative clauses generally precede the main clause. Word order is relatively flexible for pragmatic purposes (topicalization, focus), but SOV remains the unmarked default.

## Morphological Type

Bengali is primarily an **agglutinative** language with some fusional characteristics. Words are formed by attaching suffixes to stems, with each suffix typically carrying a single grammatical meaning. Verb forms can become quite complex through the stacking of suffixes for tense, aspect, mood, and person/formality.

## Phonology

### Vowels

Bengali has seven oral vowels: /i/, /e/, /æ/, /a/, /ɔ/, /o/, /u/. In addition, Bengali features **nasal vowels** (vowels nasalized through the chandrabindu diacritic চন্দ্রবিন্দু ঁ), for example: চাঁদ (cãd, "moon"), হাঁস (hãs, "duck").

A critical feature of Bengali phonology is the **inherent vowel** /ɔ/ (অ), which is automatically pronounced after a consonant unless suppressed by a hasanta (্) or another vowel sign. This inherent vowel /ɔ/ is distinctive - it contrasts with Hindi's inherent /a/.

### Consonants

Bengali has a rich consonant system organized around several key contrasts:

- **Aspiration contrast**: Bengali distinguishes between aspirated and unaspirated stops at each place of articulation: ক /k/ vs খ /kʰ/, গ /ɡ/ vs ঘ /ɡʱ/, চ /tʃ/ vs ছ /tʃʰ/, etc.

- **Retroflex consonants**: Like other Indo-Aryan languages, Bengali has a series of retroflex consonants (ট /ʈ/, ঠ /ʈʰ/, ড /ɖ/, ঢ /ɖʱ/, ণ /ɳ/) articulated with the tongue curled back against the palate.

- **Voicing contrast**: Voiced and voiceless pairs exist at each point of articulation.

- Notable consonants include the flapped sounds ড় /ɽ/ and ঢ় /ɽʱ/, which developed from the retroflex stops in intervocalic positions.

### Suprasegmental Features

Bengali is a **stress-timed** language with primary stress typically falling on the initial syllable of a word. Intonation plays an important role in distinguishing sentence types (declarative, interrogative, exclamatory).

## Noun System

### Case System

Bengali has a simplified case system with **four cases**:

1. **Nominative** (কর্তৃকারক): Unmarked, used for subjects - ছেলে (chhele, "boy")
2. **Objective/Accusative** (কর্মকারক): Marked with -কে (-ke) for animate objects - ছেলেকে (chheleke, "the boy" as object)
3. **Genitive** (সম্বন্ধ কারক): Marked with -র/-এর (-r/-er) - ছেলের (chheler, "of the boy / the boy's")
4. **Locative** (অধিকরণ কারক): Marked with -এ/-তে/-য় (-e/-te/-y) - ঘরে (ghore, "in the house")

Other case-like relationships are expressed through **postpositions**: দিয়ে (diye, "with/by means of"), থেকে (theke, "from"), জন্য (jonnyo, "for").

### Gender

Bengali has **no grammatical gender**. Unlike Hindi-Urdu, nouns are not classified as masculine or feminine, and adjectives and verbs do not agree in gender. This is a significant typological feature that distinguishes Bengali from many other Indo-Aryan languages.

### Classifiers and Measure Words

Bengali uses an extensive system of **classifiers** (পদাশ্রিত নির্দেশক), similar in function to East Asian classifier systems. When counting or specifying nouns, a classifier must be used between the numeral and the noun:

- একটা বই (ekta boi) - "one-CLF book" = "a book" (general classifier -টা)
- দুটো মানুষ (duto manush) - "two-CLF person" = "two people"
- তিনজন ছাত্র (tinjon chhatro) - "three-CLF(human) student" = "three students"

Common classifiers include:
- **-টা / -টি** (-ta / -ti): General classifiers; -টি is more formal/polite
- **-জন** (-jon): For humans
- **-খানা / -খানি** (-khana / -khani): For flat or thin objects
- **-গাছা / -গাছি** (-gacha / -gachi): For stick-like objects

### Definiteness

Bengali marks definiteness through **suffixes** rather than articles:

- বই (boi) - "book" (indefinite/generic)
- বইটা (boita) - "the book" (definite, informal)
- বইটি (boiti) - "the book" (definite, formal/literary)
- বইগুলো (boigulo) - "the books" (definite plural)

The indefinite article equivalent is expressed with একটা/একটি (ekta/ekti, "one-CLF").

### Pluralization

Plurality can be marked by suffixes such as -রা (-ra, for animate/human), -গুলো/-গুলি (-gulo/-guli, for general definite plural), -সব (-sob), -সকল (-sokol):

- ছেলেরা (chhelera) - "the boys"
- বইগুলো (boigulo) - "the books"

## Verb System

### Tense

Bengali has three primary tenses:

1. **Present**: আমি করি (ami kori) - "I do"
2. **Past**: আমি করলাম (ami korlam) - "I did"
3. **Future**: আমি করব (ami korbo) - "I will do"

### Aspect

Bengali distinguishes several aspects:

- **Simple/Indefinite**: করি (kori) - "do"
- **Progressive/Continuous**: করছি (korchi) - "am doing"
- **Perfect**: করেছি (korechi) - "have done"
- **Habitual Past**: করতাম (kortam) - "used to do"

### Mood

- **Indicative**: Standard statement mood
- **Imperative**: করো (koro, informal), করুন (korun, formal) - "do!"
- **Conditional**: করলে (korle) - "if (one) does"
- **Subjunctive/Optative**: করি (kori) - "let me do"

### Person and Formality Distinctions

A critically important feature of Bengali verbs is the **three-level formality system** in second and third person:

**Second Person (you)**:
- **তুই** (tui) - Intimate/inferior: তুই করিস (tui koris) - very informal, used with close friends, children, or to show contempt
- **তুমি** (tumi) - Familiar: তুমি করো (tumi koro) - standard familiar form, used among equals and friends
- **আপনি** (apni) - Formal/honorific: আপনি করেন (apni koren) - respectful, used with elders, strangers, in formal settings

**Third Person (he/she/they)**:
- **সে** (she) - Ordinary: সে করে (she kore)
- **তিনি** (tini) - Honorific: তিনি করেন (tini koren)

This three-way distinction permeates the entire verb conjugation system and is a fundamental aspect of Bengali social interaction.

### Compound Verbs

Bengali makes extensive use of **compound verbs** (যৌগিক ক্রিয়া), where a main verb in its stem or conjunctive participle form is combined with a light/vector verb to add nuance:

- খেয়ে ফেলা (kheye phela) - "eat up" (completive)
- লিখে রাখা (likhe rakha) - "write and keep" (preservative)
- করে দেওয়া (kore deowa) - "do for someone" (benefactive)
- দেখে নেওয়া (dekhe neowa) - "see/check for oneself" (self-benefactive)
- উঠে পড়া (uthe pora) - "get up suddenly" (sudden action)

Common vector verbs include ফেলা (phela, completive), যাওয়া (jaowa, change of state), নেওয়া (neowa, self-benefit), দেওয়া (deowa, other-benefit), রাখা (rakha, preservative), পড়া (pora, sudden), ওঠা (otha, upward/beginning).

## Syntax

### Negation

Negation is expressed by the particle **না** (na), which typically follows the verb:

- আমি যাব না (ami jabo na) - "I will not go"
- সে আসেনি (she asheni) - "he/she did not come" (negative suffix -নি)

In some tenses, negative forms use special conjugations: করি না (kori na, "don't do") vs. করিনি (korini, "didn't do").

### Question Formation

Yes/no questions are formed by adding the particle **কি** (ki) to a declarative sentence, or simply through rising intonation:

- তুমি যাবে কি? (tumi jabe ki?) - "Will you go?"

Wh-questions use interrogative words such as কে (ke, "who"), কী (ki, "what"), কোথায় (kothay, "where"), কখন (kokhon, "when"), কেন (keno, "why"), কীভাবে (kibhabe, "how").

### Relative-Correlative Constructions

Bengali uses a distinctive **relative-correlative** construction where the relative clause, introduced by a relative pronoun (যে, যা, যেখানে), precedes the main clause with a correlative pronoun (সে, তা, সেখানে):

- যে ছেলেটা এসেছিল, সে আমার বন্ধু (je chheleta eshechilo, she amar bondhu) - "The boy who had come, he is my friend"

### Conjunctive Participle

Bengali frequently uses the **conjunctive participle** (-এ / -ে suffix) to chain sequential actions in a single sentence, reducing the need for conjunctions:

- সে ঘরে এসে বসল (she ghore eshe boslo) - "He, having come home, sat down" = "He came home and sat down"
