# Bengali / Bangla (ben)

## Summary

Bengali (endonym: **বাংলা** *bāṅlā*) is an Indo-European → Indo-Aryan → Eastern-Zone language. ISO codes: `bn` (639-1), `ben` (639-2 / 639-3). It is the official language of Bangladesh and a scheduled language of India (official in West Bengal, Tripura, and the Barak Valley of Assam). L1 speakers number about 230–240 million, and L2 speakers push the total toward 270 million, making Bengali the sixth- or seventh-most-spoken language worldwide.

## Writing system

Bengali is written in the left-to-right **Bangla / Eastern Nāgarī** abugida (Unicode block U+0980–U+09FF), shared with Assamese. It has an overhead bar like Devanagari but the letter shapes are distinctly rounded. Consonants carry inherent /ɔ/; the virāma (hasanta) **্** suppresses this vowel. Dependent vowel signs can be written *before* the consonant (as in **ি**, which stands to the left), *after* it (**া, ী, ু, ূ, ো, ৌ**), or wrapped around it. Anusvāra **ং**, candrabindu **ঁ** and visarga **ঃ** are all present. The script uses its own digits ০ ১ ২ ৩ ৪ ৫ ৬ ৭ ৮ ৯.

## History and status

Bangla has a literary tradition from the 10th-century *Charyāpada* through the Vaishnava poets and the modern renaissance of Rabindranath Tagore (Nobel Laureate 1913), Kazi Nazrul Islam, and Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay. The 1952 Bengali Language Movement in East Pakistan, commemorated by UNESCO's International Mother Language Day, was pivotal to Bangladeshi independence in 1971.
