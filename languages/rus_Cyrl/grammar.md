# Russian Grammar (русский язык)

## Overview

Russian is a fusional, highly inflected East Slavic language. Grammatical relations are expressed mainly by endings on nouns, adjectives, pronouns and verbs, so word order is flexible and used for information structure; the neutral order is SVO. There are no articles. The features below are those most useful for recognising Russian and separating it from Ukrainian, Belarusian, Bulgarian and Serbian.

## Phonology and Spelling

- **Vowel reduction (akanye/ikanye):** unstressed о is pronounced [a] or [ə], unstressed е/я close to [ɪ]. Russian orthography is morphological and does **not** show this: *молоко* is written with three о although pronounced [məlɐˈko]. Belarusian, by contrast, writes the reduction (*малако*). This means written Russian keeps many о in unstressed syllables.
- **Palatalisation:** most consonants have hard and soft counterparts. Softness is shown by the following vowel letter (е, ё, и, ю, я vs. э, о, ы, у, а) or by the soft sign ь (*мать*, *день*).
- **Spelling rules:** after к, г, х, ж, ш, ч, щ the letter и (not ы) is written, and after ж, ш, ц often о/е alternate by stress. Hence *русский*, *книги*, *хорошие*, never *кы*, *гы*, *хы* in native words.
- **Final devoicing and voicing assimilation** are not written (*год* [got]).
- **г** is a plosive [g] (except in a few words like *Бог* in older pronunciation), unlike Ukrainian/Belarusian [ɦ].
- **Pleophony (polnoglasie):** East Slavic *-оро-, -оло-, -ере-* corresponding to South Slavic *-ра-, -ла-, -ре-*: *город* vs. Bulgarian *град*, *голова* vs. *глава*. Russian also keeps Church Slavonic doublets (*глава* "chapter").

## Nouns

- **Three genders** (masculine, feminine, neuter) and two numbers.
- **Six cases:** nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, instrumental, prepositional (locative). A few masculine nouns keep a second genitive (partitive, *чаю*) and second locative (*в лесу*). There is **no productive vocative** (unlike Ukrainian and Serbian); colloquial truncated forms such as *мам!*, *Саш!* exist.
- Typical endings: genitive singular -а/-я, -ы/-и; genitive plural **-ов/-ев**, -ей or zero (*домов*, *людей*, *книг*); instrumental -ом/-ем, -ой/-ей, plural **-ами/-ями**; prepositional -е, plural **-ах/-ях**.
- **Animacy** affects the accusative (animate masculine accusative = genitive).

## Adjectives and Pronouns

- Long adjective endings: masc. nom. **-ый/-ий/-ой** (*новый*, *синий*, *большой*), fem. **-ая/-яя**, neut. **-ое/-ее**, plural **-ые/-ие**; genitive masc./neut. **-ого/-его** (pronounced with [v]), dative **-ому/-ему**, fem. oblique **-ой/-ей**. Uncontracted neuter and feminine endings (*новое*, *новая*) contrast with Ukrainian contracted *нове*, *нова*.
- Short forms are used predicatively (*он готов*, *она рада*).
- Personal pronouns: я, ты, он, она, оно, мы, вы, они; oblique меня, мне, тебя, тебе, его, её, ему, ей, нас, вам, их, им. Reflexive себя/себе, possessive свой.
- Demonstratives этот/эта/это/эти and тот/та/то/те; relative/interrogative который, какой, что, кто, чей.

## Verbs

- **Aspect:** almost every verb has an imperfective/perfective pair (*делать/сделать*, *говорить/сказать*), formed by prefixes, suffixes or suppletion.
- **Infinitive** ends in **-ть** (most), **-ти** (*идти*) or **-чь** (*мочь*).
- **Present/future** personal endings: 1st conjugation -ю/-у, -ешь, **-ет**, -ем, -ете, -ют/-ут; 2nd conjugation -ю/-у, -ишь, **-ит**, -им, -ите, -ят/-ат. Perfective verbs use these endings with future meaning.
- **Compound future** of imperfectives with *буду, будешь, будет*... + infinitive.
- **Past tense** is a former participle agreeing in gender and number, not person: **-л, -ла, -ло, -ли** (*был, была, было, были*; *сказал*). This -л is a key contrast with Ukrainian -в (*був*, *сказав*) and Belarusian -ў.
- **Reflexive** suffix -ся after consonants, -сь after vowels (*учиться*, *учусь*, *учились*). The sequences **-тся** (3sg) and **-ться** (infinitive) are frequent.
- **Conditional/subjunctive** with the particle *бы* plus past form.
- **Participles** (-ущий/-ющий, -ащий/-ящий, -вший, -емый/-имый, -нный/-тый) and **gerunds** (-я/-а, -в/-вши) are common in written registers, giving long words like *являющийся*, *осуществлённый*.
- **Copula:** the present tense of *быть* is normally omitted (*Он врач* "He [is] a doctor"); *есть* appears for existence/possession (*У меня есть...*) and *является* in formal style. Ukrainian, by contrast, uses *є* more often.

## Syntax

- Neutral SVO, with topic-first ordering; the newest information tends to come last.
- **Negation** with *не* before the verb; **negative concord** (*никто ничего не сказал*); genitive of negation (*нет денег*).
- **Possession** typically with *у + genitive + (есть)*: *У меня есть машина*.
- **Numerals** govern case: 2-4 + genitive singular, 5+ + genitive plural (*два года*, *пять лет*).
- **Subordination** with *что*, *чтобы*, *если*, *когда*, *потому что*, *который*; questions with intonation or *ли* (*Знаешь ли ты...*).
- Prepositions frequently paired with cases: *в/во, на* (+acc./prep.), *с/со* (+instr./gen.), *к/ко* (+dat.), *о/об/обо* (+prep.), *от, из, у, для, без, до* (+gen.).

## Summary of Diagnostic Grammar

Past tense in -л, infinitive in -ть, genitive plural -ов, uncontracted adjectives (-ая, -ое, -ые), genitive -ого/-его, 3sg -ет/-ит, zero copula, conjunctions *что/чтобы/если/когда*, and absence of postposed articles together mark Russian against its East and South Slavic neighbours.
