# Russian grammar

- **Word order**: Default SVO but highly free because case morphology
  disambiguates arguments. Information structure (topic–focus) drives surface
  order; inversions SOV, VSO and OSV all occur. Russian is synthetic/fusional.
- **Morphology**: Fusional with heavy suffixation and some prefixation,
  especially on verbs (perfective prefixes `по-, про-, с-, вы-, на-`).
  Nominal suffixes encode gender, number and case in one portmanteau ending.
- **Nouns**: Three genders (masculine, feminine, neuter) and two numbers
  (singular, plural). Six cases: nominative, genitive, dative, accusative,
  instrumental, prepositional (locative). Typical feminine `-а/-я` nominative
  → genitive `-ы/-и`; masculine null → `-а`; neuter `-о/-е` → `-а`.
  Adjectives agree in gender, number, case and animacy; long forms end in
  `-ый/-ий/-ой` (m), `-ая/-яя` (f), `-ое/-ее` (n), `-ые/-ие` (pl).
- **Verbs**: Dominated by the **aspect** opposition (imperfective vs
  perfective), often formed by prefixation or stem alternation (`писать` /
  `написать`). Three tenses (past, present, future); past marked by `-л-`
  agreeing in gender/number rather than person. Non-past conjugation shows
  person/number endings `-ю/-у, -ешь/-ишь, -ет/-ит, -ем/-им, -ете/-ите,
  -ют/-ут/-ят/-ат`. Mood: indicative, imperative, conditional with `бы`.
  Verbs of motion have a directional/non-directional sub-opposition
  (`идти`/`ходить`, `ехать`/`ездить`).
- **Phonology**: 5 vowel phonemes with heavy reduction of unstressed /o/ →
  [ɐ/ə] ("akanye"). Consonants show a systematic palatalised/plain contrast
  (`мать` vs `мять`). Final-obstruent devoicing. Free lexical stress is not
  marked orthographically.
- **Syntax**: Pro-drop is restricted (subject pronouns are usually present in
  non-past, optional in past/imperative). No articles. Present-tense copula
  `быть` is null (`Он врач` "He is a doctor"). Negation by preverbal `не`;
  genitive of negation with existential `нет` + GEN.
