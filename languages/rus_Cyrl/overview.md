# Russian (rus)

## Summary

Russian (endonym: *русский язык*, *russkiy yazyk*) is an East Slavic language
in the Indo-European family (Indo-European → Balto-Slavic → Slavic → East
Slavic). ISO codes: 639-1 `ru`, 639-2 `rus`, 639-3 `rus`. It is written in a
Russian-adapted Cyrillic alphabet of 33 letters. Native speakers number
approximately 150 million, with an additional ~100 million L2 speakers, making
it one of the most widely spoken languages in the world. Russian is the sole
official language of the Russian Federation and a co-official or widely used
language in Belarus, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, and several other post-Soviet
states; it is also one of the six official languages of the United Nations.

## Writing system

Russian uses Cyrillic (Russian civil variant, standardised after the 1917–18
orthographic reform that dropped ѣ, і, ѳ and ѵ). The alphabet contains the
vowels а е ё и о у ы э ю я, the consonants б в г д ж з й к л м н п р с т ф х ц
ч ш щ, and the two sign letters ъ (hard sign) and ь (soft sign). Characters ы,
э, ъ and ё are particularly distinctive: ы and ъ do not occur in Ukrainian,
Belarusian or (as a hard sign) Bulgarian. Casing is bicameral. Russian does
not mark stress in ordinary text, though ё may be written as е in informal
writing.

## Status

Russian has a long literary tradition from the 11th century (Old East Slavic),
codified in its modern form by Lomonosov and Pushkin. It remains a major
language of science, diplomacy and Orthodox Christianity.
