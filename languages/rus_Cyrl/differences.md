# Russian vs. confusable languages

Closest confusables in the CommonLID set are the other Cyrillic Slavic
languages: Ukrainian (`ukr`) and Bulgarian (`bul`).

## Russian vs. Ukrainian

- Russian uses **ы** and **ъ**; Ukrainian does not.
- Ukrainian uses **і**, **ї**, **є**, **ґ** — none of which appear in modern
  Russian orthography.
- Russian `и` ≈ Ukrainian `і`; Russian `е` ≈ Ukrainian `є`/`е`.
- Adjective m.sg.nom: Russian `-ый/-ий` vs Ukrainian `-ий` with genitive
  `-ого` (rus) vs `-ого` pronounced /-oho/ (ukr, letter г is /ɦ/).
- Infinitive: Russian `-ть` vs Ukrainian `-ти` (писать vs писати).
- Function words: Russian `что`, `это`, `очень`; Ukrainian `що`, `це`, `дуже`.

## Russian vs. Bulgarian

- Bulgarian has **no nominal case system** (vestigial pronominal only),
  whereas Russian has six productive cases.
- Bulgarian has a **post-posed definite article** `-ът/-ят` (m), `-та` (f),
  `-то` (n), `-те` (pl); Russian has no articles at all.
- Bulgarian has **no infinitive** (uses да-construction `да работя`);
  Russian infinitive ends in `-ть`/`-ти`.
- Bulgarian has an evidential / renarrative mood; Russian does not.
- Bulgarian uses letter **ъ** as a full vowel [ɤ] in common words (`България`,
  `ъгъл`); Russian **ъ** is only a hard sign, never word-initial or stressed.
