# Grammar (zul)

- **Word order**: SVO, prepositional, modifiers follow the head
  (`umuntu omuhle`, "a good person"). Topic-comment structures are
  common in discourse.
- **Morphology**: highly agglutinative with conjunctive writing — a
  whole clause can be written as a single word (`angazi` "I don't
  know", `ngizokusiza` "I will help you").
- **Nouns**: 15 noun classes in singular/plural pairs. Prefixes
  include `um(u)-/aba-`, `um(u)-/imi-`, `ili-/ama-`, `isi-/izi-`,
  `in-/izin-`, `ulu-/izin-`, `ubu-`, `uku-`. The **augment** vowel
  (`a-`, `i-`, `u-`) is prefixed to the class marker in most
  positions.
- **Agreement**: concord propagates through subject, object,
  adjectives, possessives, demonstratives and copulas, producing long
  chains with rhyming prefixes.
- **Verbs**: obligatory subject concord; optional object concord;
  TAM markers `-ya-` (present disjoint), `-a-/-e-` (recent past),
  `-zo-/-yo-` (future), `-sa-` (still), `-ke-` (ever), `-nga-`
  (potential/negative imperative), negative `a-…-i`. Final vowel
  alternates `-a` (indicative) / `-e` (subjunctive).
- **Phonology**: five vowels, phonemic voicing contrasts, three
  clicks (dental /ǀ/, postalveolar /!/, lateral /ǁ/) each with plain,
  aspirated, breathy-voiced and nasal variants. Lexical tone exists
  but is not marked orthographically. Penultimate length / stress.
- **Syntax**: pro-drop via subject concord; copulas `ng-/y-/u-`
  depending on class; possessive `-a` concord (`incwadi ka-`); no
  definite article — discourse context marks definiteness.
