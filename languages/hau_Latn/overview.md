# Hausa (hau)

## Summary

Hausa (endonym: **Harshen Hausa**, also *Halshen Hausa*) is an Afro-Asiatic language of the Chadic branch (Afro-Asiatic → Chadic → West Chadic → Hausa). ISO codes: **ha** (639-1), **hau** (639-2/T and 639-3). It is one of the largest languages of sub-Saharan Africa, spoken by roughly **50–80 million people** as a first language and an estimated **30–50 million more** as a second language. Its heartland is northern **Nigeria** and southern **Niger**, but it is a major lingua franca across the Sahel and West Africa, including Ghana, Cameroon, Chad, Benin, Togo, Sudan, and diaspora communities. Its regional prestige is comparable to Swahili in East Africa.

## Writing system

Hausa is written in two orthographies. The dominant modern script is **Boko**, a Latin-based alphabet standardised in the 1930s. Boko uses the hooked letters **ɓ**, **ɗ**, **ƙ** (and sometimes **ƴ**) for implosive and ejective consonants, plus the digraphs **sh**, **ts**, **fy**, **gw**, **ky**, **kw**. Long vowels are usually left unmarked in everyday writing but can be indicated with macrons **ā ē ī ō ū** in dictionaries, and tone is only marked pedagogically (low ◌̀, falling ◌̂). The older **Ajami** script (Arabic-based) is still used in religious and traditional contexts but is rare online.

## History and status

Hausa is a national language of Nigeria and Niger, used widely in broadcasting (BBC Hausa, VOA Hausa, DW Hausa), education, film (Kannywood), and publishing.
