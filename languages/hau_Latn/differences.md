# Hausa vs. confusable languages

Hausa shares a West African Latin writing environment with Fulfulde, Kanuri, Yoruba, Igbo, and Nigerian Pidgin, but is typologically distinct (Afro-Asiatic, not Niger-Congo).

## Hausa vs. Fulfulde (fuv, Nigerian Fulfulde)

- Both use hooked letters, but Fulfulde also uses **ŋ** (U+014B) and **ny**; Hausa does not use ŋ at all.
- Fulfulde has an elaborate **noun-class** system with final-vowel concord suffixes (*-o, -ɗo, -ɓe, -ɗi, -ngel, -kon*); Hausa has only masculine/feminine gender.
- Hausa PAC clitics (**ya, ta, sun, mun, ina, yana**) precede the verb and cluster at sentence start; Fulfulde marks person and tense with verb-internal endings (*-mi, -ɗaa, -i, -no*).
- Fulfulde lacks the Hausa copula *ne/ce* and the linker *-n/-r*.
- `ts-` onset and the bipartite negation *ba … ba* are Hausa, not Fulfulde.

## Hausa Boko vs. Ajami (same language, different script)

- Boko = Latin with ɓ ɗ ƙ; Ajami = Arabic script. If the text is Latin, it is Boko.

## Hausa vs. other West African Latin (Yoruba, Igbo, pcm)

- Hausa has **no tone/underdot diacritics** (no á à ā ẹ ọ ị ụ ṣ). If underdots appear, it is not Hausa.
- Yoruba/Igbo show heavy diacritics; Nigerian Pidgin is essentially ASCII English-looking text without ɓ ɗ ƙ.
- Any presence of **ɓ ɗ ƙ** plus function words **ne, ce, da, na, ba** is a near-certain Hausa signal.
