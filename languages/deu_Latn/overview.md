# German (deu)

## Summary

German (endonym: *Deutsch*) is an Indo-European language of the West Germanic branch (Indo-European → Germanic → West → High German). ISO codes: 639-1 `de`, 639-2 `deu` (also `ger`), 639-3 `deu`. It is written in the Latin script. German has approximately 95 million native speakers and a further 80–100 million L2 speakers, making it the most widely spoken native language in the European Union. It is the official or co-official language of Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Belgium (East Cantons), and South Tyrol (Italy). Standard German (*Hochdeutsch*) coexists with a continuum of regional varieties ranging from Low German in the north to Alemannic and Bavarian in the south.

## Writing system

German uses a 26-letter Latin alphabet plus three umlauted vowels `ä`, `ö`, `ü` and the Eszett `ß` (Swiss Standard German substitutes `ss` for `ß`). Every noun — common or proper — is capitalised, which is one of the most visually distinctive features of written German. Compound nouns are written solid (*Donaudampfschifffahrtsgesellschaft*), producing extremely long orthographic words. Punctuation follows Western European norms, with low-high quotation marks `„…"` common in books and newspapers alongside guillemets `»…«` in Swiss usage.

## History

Standard German crystallised around Luther's 1534 Bible translation and was codified by the Duden dictionary from 1880; the 1996/2006 spelling reform standardised `ß`/`ss` distribution and several compound rules.
