# German characteristics (LID signals)

## Script signals

`ß` is effectively unique to German in the CommonLID set and a near-certain positive marker (apart from Swiss Standard German, which omits it). Umlauts `ä`, `ö`, `ü` co-occur strongly with German, Hungarian, Turkish, and a few others, but combined with `ß` they are diagnostic. Ubiquitous mid-sentence capitalised common nouns are a further strong visual cue.

## Top function words

*der, die, das, und, in, zu, den, nicht, von, sie, ist, des, sich, mit, dem, dass, er, es, ein, ich, auf, so, eine, auch, als, an, nach, wie, im, für, man, aber, aus, durch, wenn, nur, war, noch, werden, bei, hat, wir, was, wird, sein, einen, welche, sind, oder, zur*.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- Suffixes: `-ung` (*Bildung, Meinung*), `-keit` (*Einsamkeit*), `-heit` (*Freiheit*), `-schaft` (*Gesellschaft*), `-lich` (*freundlich*), `-isch` (*deutsch, typisch*), `-chen`/`-lein` diminutives.
- Consonant clusters: initial `sch-` (*schön, Schule*), `sp-`/`st-` pronounced /ʃp ʃt/ (*Sprache, Stadt*), `pf-` (*Pfanne*), `tz`, `ck`, `ch` (medial and final).
- Digraphs: `ie` (long /iː/), `eu`/`äu` (/ɔʏ/), `ei` (/aɪ/), `au`.
- Long compounds with multiple internal capitals are extremely productive and visually distinctive.

## Morphology hooks

Inflected articles *der/die/das/den/dem/des* at sentence level are a strong positive cue. Past participles with `ge-` + `-t`/`-en` (*gemacht, gegangen*). Separable prefixes *auf-, aus-, ein-, mit-, vor-, zu-* stranded at clause end.

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

Low-high quotation marks `„…"` in German typography; Swiss publications use `«…»`. Commas are mandatory before subordinate clauses. Decimals use comma (`3,14`) and thousands a period (`1.000.000`).
