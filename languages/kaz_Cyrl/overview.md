# Kazakh / Қазақ тілі

## Basic Information

- **Language name:** Kazakh (Қазақ тілі)
- **ISO 639-3 code:** kaz
- **Language family:** Turkic, Kipchak branch (Northwestern Turkic)
- **Script:** Cyrillic (with additional letters specific to Kazakh)
- **Speakers:** Approximately 13 million native speakers
- **Writing direction:** Left to right

## Geographic Distribution

Kazakh is spoken primarily in Kazakhstan, where it serves as the official state language alongside Russian. Significant Kazakh-speaking communities also exist in:

- **China** (Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region, approximately 1.5 million speakers)
- **Mongolia** (Bayan-Olgii Province, approximately 100,000 speakers)
- **Russia** (Astrakhan, Orenburg, Omsk, and Saratov oblasts)
- **Uzbekistan** (Karakalpakstan and Tashkent region)
- **Smaller diaspora communities** in Turkey, Iran, Afghanistan, Germany, and other countries

## Official Status

Kazakh is the official state language of the Republic of Kazakhstan as established by the 1995 Constitution. It is used in government, education, media, and public life. Russian retains the status of an official language of interethnic communication. The Kazakh language enjoys protected status and active state promotion through language policy programs aimed at increasing its use in all domains of public life.

## Language Classification

Kazakh belongs to the Kipchak (Northwestern) branch of the Turkic language family, specifically to the Kipchak-Nogai subgroup. Its closest relatives are Karakalpak and Nogai, and it shares significant mutual intelligibility with Kyrgyz, which belongs to the Kipchak-Kyrgyz subgroup. More distantly, Kazakh is related to other Turkic languages such as Uzbek, Turkish, Tatar, and Bashkir.

## Script and Writing System

The Kazakh Cyrillic alphabet consists of 42 letters: the 33 letters of the Russian Cyrillic alphabet plus 9 additional characters specific to Kazakh sounds. These additional letters are: Ә ә, Ғ ғ, Қ қ, Ң ң, Ө ө, Ұ ұ, Ү ү, Һ һ, and І і. These letters represent vowel and consonant phonemes that do not exist in Russian, making them highly diagnostic for language identification.

## History and Script Transitions

The Kazakh language has undergone several script changes throughout its history, reflecting broader political and cultural shifts in Central Asia:

1. **Pre-20th century:** Kazakh was written using a modified Arabic script (Төте жазу), adapted by the Kazakh educator and linguist Ахмет Байтұрсынұлы in the early 20th century to better represent Kazakh phonology.

2. **1929-1940 (Latin script):** As part of Soviet language policy, Kazakh transitioned to a Latin-based alphabet (Яңалиф) along with many other Turkic languages of the Soviet Union.

3. **1940-present (Cyrillic script):** Under Soviet directive, Kazakh adopted the Cyrillic script, which has been in use since 1940 and remains the dominant script today.

4. **Planned Latin transition:** In 2017, President Nursultan Nazarbayev signed a decree to transition Kazakh to a Latin-based alphabet by 2025. The proposed Latin alphabet has undergone several revisions. The transition has been gradual and the Cyrillic script continues to be widely used in everyday communication, education, and official documents.

In China, Kazakh speakers continue to use a modified Arabic script, while the Mongolian Kazakh diaspora historically used Cyrillic and has also experimented with Latin and Arabic scripts.

## Linguistic Features

Kazakh is an agglutinative language with SOV (Subject-Object-Verb) word order, vowel harmony, and no grammatical gender. It has a rich case system with seven cases and a complex verbal morphology. These typological features place it firmly within the Turkic language family and distinguish it markedly from the Indo-European languages (such as Russian) that share the Cyrillic script.
