# Kabyle (kab)

## Summary

Kabyle (endonym: **Taqbaylit**) is a Berber (Amazigh) language of the Afro-Asiatic family (Afro-Asiatic → Berber → Northern → Zenatic/Kabyle). ISO codes: **kab** (639-3); Kabyle has no 639-1 code (the macro-label *ber* is 639-2). It is spoken by an estimated **5–7 million people**, mostly in the **Kabylie region** of northern **Algeria** (provinces of Tizi Ouzou, Béjaïa, Bouira, Boumerdès), in **Algiers**, and by a sizeable diaspora in **France** and elsewhere in Europe.

## Writing system

Kabyle is today written most commonly in a **Latin-based Berber alphabet** developed by Mouloud Mammeri and used by institutions such as the HCA and INALCO. The alphabet uses several non-ASCII letters: **ɛ** (pharyngeal, U+025B), **ɣ** (voiced velar fricative, U+0263), **ḥ** (U+1E25), **ḍ** (U+1E0D), **ṛ** (U+1E5B), **ṣ** (U+1E63), **ṭ** (U+1E6D), **ẓ** (U+1E93), plus **č ǧ** in some spellings. Geminate consonants are written double (**ss, tt, mm, nn**). The traditional **Tifinagh** script is used symbolically/publicly but rarely in running text; Arabic script is also occasionally used.

## History and status

Kabyle is the best-documented and most-published Berber variety. Since 2002 Amazigh (Tamazight) is a national language of Algeria, and since 2016 an official one. Kabyle has a vibrant press, music, and online presence (including a full Kabyle Wikipedia).
