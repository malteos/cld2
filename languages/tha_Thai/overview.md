# Thai (tha)

## Summary

Thai, endonym ภาษาไทย (phasa thai), is a Tai-Kadai language in the Southwestern Tai branch, closely related to Lao and to Tai Lue / Shan. ISO codes: 639-1 `th`, 639-2 `tha`, 639-3 `tha`. Native speakers number roughly 20–30 million in Central Thailand (Standard / Central Thai) plus several dialects (Northern/Kham Mueang, Northeastern/Isan — really Lao, Southern) that push total speakers of the wider Thai cluster over 60 million. Thailand is the primary region; smaller communities live in Myanmar, Cambodia, Laos, Malaysia and the diaspora.

## Writing system

Thai is written in the **Thai script** (Unicode block U+0E00–U+0E7F), a Brahmic abugida derived from Old Khmer and ultimately from South Indian Pallava script. Key properties:

- **No spaces between words** within a sentence; spaces mark clause or sentence boundaries.
- **No letter case**.
- Consonants carry an **inherent vowel** (short /a/ or /o/, context-dependent) which is suppressed or modified by surrounding vowel marks.
- Vowels can be written **before, after, above, below, or around** the consonant they attach to (e.g. เ◌, ◌ิ, ◌ุ, ◌า, แ◌, โ◌, ไ◌).
- **Four tone marks**: ่ (mai ek), ้ (mai tho), ๊ (mai tri), ๋ (mai chattawa).
- Digits: Thai digits ๐ ๑ ๒ ๓ ๔ ๕ ๖ ๗ ๘ ๙ coexist with Arabic 0–9.
- Distinctive symbols: ๆ (mai yamok, iteration), ฯ (paiyan noi, abbreviation), ๏ (fongman, paragraph mark), ฯลฯ (et cetera).

## Status

Sole official language of Thailand; used in all government, education, and media.
