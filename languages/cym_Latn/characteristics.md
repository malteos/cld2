# Welsh Identification Characteristics

## The Welsh Alphabet

The Welsh alphabet consists of 29 letters, including eight digraphs that are treated as single letters. The alphabet in order is:

**a, b, c, ch, d, dd, e, f, ff, g, ng, h, i, j, l, ll, m, n, o, p, ph, r, rh, s, t, th, u, w, y**

Notable features:
- **No k, q, v, x, z** in native Welsh words
- **j** is only used in borrowed words
- The digraphs **ch, dd, ff, ng, ll, ph, rh, th** each count as single letters
- **w** and **y** are classified as vowels (the Welsh vowels are a, e, i, o, u, w, y)
- **f** represents /v/ and **ff** represents /f/ (the opposite of English intuition)

## Highly Diagnostic Characters and Digraphs

The following letter combinations are extremely strong indicators of Welsh:

### "ll" - Voiceless Lateral Fricative
The digraph "ll" is perhaps the single most diagnostic feature of Welsh text. It appears with very high frequency in common words: "llawer" (many), "llan" (church/parish, appearing in hundreds of place names), "llywodraeth" (government), "llyfr" (book), "llais" (voice), "llong" (ship), "llwybr" (path), "llwyd" (grey).

### "dd" - Voiced Dental Fricative
The digraph "dd" represents /ð/ and is highly frequent: "bydd" (will be), "sydd" (who is), "newydd" (new), "dydd" (day), "eisteddfod" (cultural festival), "cerdded" (to walk), "gwledd" (feast), "priodas" (wedding uses "dd" in related forms).

### "ff" - Voiceless Labiodental Fricative
The digraph "ff" for /f/ is distinctive: "fferm" (farm), "ffenestr" (window), "ffordd" (road/way), "ffaith" (fact), "ffilm" (film), "ffôn" (phone), "coffi" (coffee), "swyddfa" (office), "trafferth" (trouble).

### "y/yr/'r" - The Definite Article
The definite article "y" (before consonants), "yr" (before vowels and h), "'r" (after vowels) is ubiquitous. Its high frequency and distinctive form make it a strong marker.

## Character Frequency Patterns

Welsh text shows distinctive character frequency patterns:

- Very high frequency of **y** (due to its role as both article and common vowel)
- High frequency of **dd, ll, ff** digraphs
- High frequency of **w** used as a vowel
- Presence of **â, ê, î, ô, û, ŵ, ŷ** (circumflex/to bach marking long vowels)
- Very high frequency of **n** (from "yn," "an," "en" patterns)
- Moderate use of apostrophes in contractions ("mae'r," "does dim," "rydw i'n")
- Absence or extreme rarity of **k, q, v, x, z**

- **th** (part of the digraph)

## Morphological Patterns

### Mutation Patterns as Identification Signals

The presence of initial consonant mutations in text is a strong Welsh identifier. Look for:

- Words beginning with "f" that are mutated forms of "b" or "m" words
- Words beginning with "g" disappearing (soft mutation of "g")
- Words beginning with "ngh," "mh," "nh" (nasal mutation products)
- Words beginning with "ph," "th," "ch" that are aspirate mutation products

### Common Suffixes

- **-aeth** (abstract nouns: "llywodraeth" government, "cymdeithas" > "cymdeithasaeth")
- **-iad** (agent/action nouns: "Cymro" > "Cymreigiad," "darlleniад" reading)
- **-ol** (adjective: "cenedlaethol" national, "hanesyddol" historical)
- **-wr/-ydd** (agent nouns: "gweithiwr" worker, "athro/athrawes" teacher)
- **-au/-iau** (common plural: "geiriau" words, "lleoedd/lleoliadau" locations)
- **-i** (verb ending: "gweld" > "gweli," "canu" > "ceni")

### Common Prefixes

- **an-/af-** (negative: "anhapus" unhappy, "afresymol" unreasonable)
- **cyd-** (co-/together: "cydweithio" cooperate)
- **gor-** (over/super: "gormod" too much)
- **ail-** (re-: "ailadrodd" repeat)
- **rhag-** (pre-/fore-: "rhaglen" programme)

## Distinctive Patterns for Identification

1. **"y/yr/'r" article** appearing with very high frequency
2. **"mae" / "oedd" / "bydd"** forms of "bod" appearing frequently
3. **"yn"** appearing as one of the most common words (multiple grammatical functions)
4. **"ll" and "dd" and "ff"** digraphs at high frequency
5. **"w" and "y"** used as vowels within words (e.g., "cwm," "byd," "dyn," "tywyll")
6. **Circumflex vowels** (to bach): "â, ê, î, ô, û, ŵ, ŷ"
7. **Absence of "k, q, v, x, z"** in native vocabulary
8. **"f" pronounced /v/** and **"ff" pronounced /f/** (affecting borrowing patterns)
