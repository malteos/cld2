# Tosk Albanian (als_Latn) - Identification Characteristics

## Special Characters

Albanian uses the standard Latin alphabet augmented with two special characters that are essential for identification:

- **ë** (e with diaeresis) -- Represents the mid-central vowel /ə/ (schwa). This is extremely frequent in Albanian text, appearing in grammatical endings, function words, and common vocabulary. Its high frequency is a strong indicator of Albanian. Example words: "është" (is), "bërë" (done), "shtëpi" (house), "një" (one/a), "për" (for), "nënë" (mother).

- **ç** (c with cedilla) -- Represents the voiceless postalveolar affricate /tʃ/. While also used in Turkish and French, its combination with ë and Albanian digraphs is distinctive. Example words: "çdo" (every), "çështje" (issue), "shqiptar" (Albanian), "përçarje" (division).

The presence of both ë and ç in a text, especially ë at high frequency, is a strong signal for Albanian. The letter ë is particularly diagnostic because it appears far more frequently in Albanian than in any other Latin-script language.

## Digraphs

Albanian orthography employs nine digraphs that represent single phonemes. These digraphs are treated as single letters in the Albanian alphabet (which has 36 letters total):

1. **dh** -- voiced dental fricative /ð/ (as in English "this"): "dhi" (goat), "dhomë" (room), "i madh" (big)
2. **gj** -- voiced palatal stop /ɟ/: "gjithë" (all), "gjuhë" (language), "gjëndër" (gland)
3. **ll** -- velarized lateral /ɫ/ (dark L): "lloj" (type), "mall" (longing), "i hollë" (thin)
4. **nj** -- palatal nasal /ɲ/: "njeri" (person/man), "njëzet" (twenty), "njohu" (recognized)
5. **rr** -- alveolar trill /r/: "rrugë" (street), "rreth" (around), "arri" (arrived)
6. **sh** -- voiceless postalveolar fricative /ʃ/: "shqip" (Albanian), "shkollë" (school), "shtet" (state)
7. **th** -- voiceless dental fricative /θ/ (as in English "think"): "thonë" (they say), "i thellë" (deep), "thua" (you say)
8. **xh** -- voiced postalveolar affricate /dʒ/: "xhami" (mosque/glass), "xhep" (pocket)
9. **zh** -- voiced postalveolar fricative /ʒ/: "zhvillim" (development), "zhurma" (noise)

The combination of these digraphs, particularly "sh", "gj", "xh", and "zh", alongside the special characters ë and ç, creates a distinctive visual signature for Albanian text.

## Character Frequency Patterns

Albanian text exhibits several distinctive frequency patterns:

- **ë** is among the most frequent characters, typically ranking in the top 5-7 characters by frequency
- The digraph **sh** is extremely common due to the word "shqip/shqiptar" (Albanian) and many common words
- **q** appears more frequently than in most European languages, representing the palatal stop /c/
- **x** is also more frequent than typical, representing the affricate /dz/
- The letters **w** and **y** are rare, appearing almost exclusively in foreign loanwords
- **j** is relatively common, functioning as the glide /j/
- Common consonant clusters include: shk-, sht-, shq-, mbr-, ndr-, str-

## Morphological Patterns

### Suffixed Definite Article

The postposed definite article is one of Albanian's most distinctive and identifiable features:

- **Masculine nouns:** indefinite "libër" (book) > definite "libri" (the book); "mal" (mountain) > "mali" (the mountain); "mik" (friend) > "miku" (the friend)
- **Feminine nouns:** indefinite "vajzë" (girl) > definite "vajza" (the girl); "luft" (war) > "lufta" (the war); "shtëpi" (house) > "shtëpia" (the house)
- **Plural masculine:** "libra" (books) > "librat" (the books)
- **Plural feminine:** "vajza" (girls) > "vajzat" (the girls)

The suffixed -i, -u, -a, -t endings on nouns create recognizable word-final patterns in running text.

### Verb Endings

Albanian verb conjugation produces characteristic endings:
- Present: -j, -n, -t, -jmë, -ni, -jnë (first conjugation)
- Past (aorist): -a, -e, -i, -ëm, -ët, -ën
- Imperfect: -ja, -je, -nte, -nim, -nit, -nin
- Participle: typically ending in -ur or -rë

### Adjectival Agreement

Adjectives in Albanian agree with the noun in gender, number, case, and definiteness. They are typically linked to the noun by a proclitic article (i/e/të/së): "djali i mirë" (the good boy), "vajza e mirë" (the good girl), "djem të mirë" (good boys). This linking article pattern is highly characteristic.

### Common Suffixes

- **-isht** (adverb former): shqip > shqiparisht (in Albanian)
- **-tar/-tare** (agent/nationality): punë > punëtor (worker), Shqipëri > shqiptar (Albanian person)
- **-im/-je** (verbal noun): zhvilloj > zhvillim (development), përpjek > përpjekje (effort)
- **-shëm/-shme** (adjective): mërzit > i mërzitur > i mërzitshëm (boring)

These morphological patterns, combined with the special characters and digraphs, make Albanian text quite distinctive and relatively straightforward to identify among Latin-script languages.
