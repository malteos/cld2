# Tosk Albanian (als_Latn) - Grammar

## Word Order

Albanian follows a basic Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) word order, though it exhibits considerable flexibility due to its rich inflectional morphology. Topic-fronting and focus constructions allow for alternative orders such as OVS or VSO in specific discourse contexts. Adjectives typically follow the noun they modify, connected by a linking article (e.g., "djali i mirë" -- the good boy), though some common adjectives may precede the noun. Adverbs generally appear before the verb or at the end of the clause.

## Morphological Type

Albanian is a highly fusional (inflectional) language. Grammatical relationships are expressed primarily through suffixes and internal modifications to word stems, with a single affix often encoding multiple grammatical categories simultaneously (e.g., case, number, and definiteness in nouns; person, number, tense, and mood in verbs). This fusional character places Albanian typologically alongside other conservative Indo-European languages like Lithuanian, Greek, and Latin.

## Phonology

Standard Albanian (based on Tosk) has a phonemic inventory of approximately 36 phonemes: 7 vowels and 29 consonants.

### Vowels

The vowel system comprises seven phonemes: /a/, /e/, /ɛ/, /i/, /o/, /u/, and /ə/ (written as "ë"). The mid-central vowel /ə/ (ë) is particularly characteristic of Albanian and plays an important role in the morphological system, appearing frequently in unstressed positions and in grammatical endings.

### Consonants

Albanian has a rich consonant inventory including:
- Stops: /p/, /b/, /t/, /d/, /k/, /g/, /c/, /ɟ/ (the last two written "q" and "gj")
- Affricates: /ts/, /dz/, /tʃ/, /dʒ/ (written "c", "x", "ç", "xh")
- Fricatives: /f/, /v/, /θ/, /ð/, /s/, /z/, /ʃ/, /ʒ/, /h/ (θ and ð written "th" and "dh")
- Nasals: /m/, /n/, /ɲ/ (ɲ written "nj")
- Liquids: /l/, /ɫ/, /r/, /ɾ/ (ɫ written "ll", the distinction between trilled /r/ written "rr" and flapped /ɾ/ written "r" is phonemic)
- Glides: /j/

### Tosk-Specific Features

A defining phonological feature of Tosk Albanian (as opposed to Gheg) is **rhotacism**: the historical change of intervocalic /n/ to /r/. For example, where Gheg has "venë" (wine), Tosk has "verë"; Gheg "ranë" corresponds to Tosk "rërë" (sand). This feature has been incorporated into Standard Albanian. Tosk also lacks the nasal vowels that are characteristic of Gheg dialects.

## Noun System

The Albanian noun system is complex and highly inflected, encoding several grammatical categories.

### Gender

Albanian has two grammatical genders: **masculine** and **feminine**. Gender assignment is partly phonological (nouns ending in a consonant or stressed vowel tend to be masculine; those ending in unstressed -ë or -e tend to be feminine) and partly arbitrary. A small class of neuter nouns survives in some contexts, though it has largely merged with the masculine in standard usage.

### Number

Nouns distinguish **singular** and **plural** forms. Plural formation is highly irregular, with numerous patterns including suffixation (-ë, -a, -e, -ra, -nj, etc.), vowel changes, and combinations thereof.

### Case

Albanian retains a five-case system:
1. **Nominative** (subject)
2. **Accusative** (direct object)
3. **Genitive** (possession, typically expressed with a linking particle)
4. **Dative** (indirect object)
5. **Ablative** (source, agent, separation)

The genitive and dative are syncretic in form (share the same endings) but are distinguished by context and by the linking articles that accompany genitive constructions.

### Definiteness - The Suffixed Article

One of the most distinctive features of Albanian grammar is the **postposed (suffixed) definite article**. Rather than using a separate word before the noun (as in English "the"), Albanian attaches the definite article as a suffix to the noun:

- Masculine singular: **-i** (after consonants) or **-u** (after vowels and certain consonants): "libër" (book) > "libri" (the book); "mal" (mountain) > "mali" (the mountain); "djalë" (boy) > "djali" (the boy)
- Feminine singular: **-a**: "vajzë" (girl) > "vajza" (the girl); "shtëpi" (house) > "shtëpia" (the house)

The indefinite form of the noun is the base (unmarked) form, while the definite form is morphologically marked. This suffixed article system is a Balkan areal feature shared with Romanian, Bulgarian, and Macedonian, and is one of the hallmarks of the Balkan Sprachbund.

## Verb System

The Albanian verb system is remarkably rich and complex, with a wide array of tenses, moods, and voices.

### Tenses

Albanian distinguishes the following indicative tenses:
1. **Present** (Koha e tashme)
2. **Imperfect** (Koha e pakryer)
3. **Aorist / Simple Past** (Koha e kryer e thjeshtë)
4. **Perfect** (Koha e kryer)
5. **Pluperfect** (Koha më se e kryer)
6. **Future** (Koha e ardhme) -- formed with the particle "do" + subjunctive

### Moods

Albanian has an exceptionally rich mood system:
- **Indicative** -- statements of fact
- **Subjunctive** -- wishes, doubts, subordinate clauses; formed with the particle "të" + modified verb form
- **Conditional** -- hypothetical situations
- **Optative** -- wishes, curses, blessings
- **Admirative** -- a mood unique to Albanian (and a few other Balkan languages), expressing surprise, wonder, or reporting information the speaker did not directly witness. For example: "Ai punon" (He works -- indicative) vs. "Ai punuaka" (He works! / Apparently he works -- admirative). The admirative is a grammaticalized evidential category, rare among European languages.
- **Imperative** -- commands

### Voice

Albanian verbs distinguish:
- **Active voice**
- **Mediopassive (non-active) voice** -- a single morphological form covering both middle and passive meanings, similar to the Greek middle-passive. The mediopassive is formed with special endings and is used for reflexive actions, reciprocal actions, passive constructions, and verbs that are inherently middle in meaning (such as verbs of emotion or motion).

### Conjugation

Verbs are conjugated for person (1st, 2nd, 3rd) and number (singular, plural). There are several conjugation classes, and irregular verbs are common, particularly among the most frequently used verbs (jam -- to be, kam -- to have, bëj -- to do, shkoj -- to go).

## Syntax

### Clitic Pronouns

Albanian makes extensive use of clitic (unstressed) pronouns, which appear before the verb in specific orders. Both accusative and dative clitics can co-occur, producing clitic clusters: "Ia dhashë" (I gave it to him/her), where "ia" is a fusion of the dative clitic "i" and the accusative clitic "a".

### Subordination

Subordinate clauses are introduced by conjunctions such as "që" (that), "kur" (when), "sepse" (because), "nëse/në qoftë se" (if), and "megjithëse" (although). The subjunctive mood is required in many subordinate contexts, particularly purpose and complement clauses.

### Negation

Negation is expressed with "nuk" (or "s'" in colloquial speech) before the verb in the indicative and conditional, and with "mos" in the subjunctive and imperative: "Nuk e di" (I don't know it), "Mos shko!" (Don't go!).

### Prepositional Phrases

Albanian uses prepositions that govern specific cases. For example, "në" (in) governs the accusative, "me" (with) governs the accusative, "nga" (from) governs the nominative, and "prej" (from/of) governs the ablative.
