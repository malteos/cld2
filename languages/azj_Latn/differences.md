# North Azerbaijani vs. Confusable Languages

## Overview of Confusability

North Azerbaijani is most easily confused with the following languages, listed in order of confusability:

1. **Turkish (tur)** - EXTREMELY confusable (same branch, high mutual intelligibility)
2. **South Azerbaijani (azb)** - Same language, different script (Arabic vs. Latin), so script alone disambiguates
3. **Turkmen (tuk)** - Moderately confusable (Oghuz branch, Latin script)
4. **Crimean Tatar (crh)** - Moderately confusable (Kipchak-Oghuz, Latin script)
5. **Gagauz (gag)** - Somewhat confusable (Oghuz branch, Latin script)
6. **Uzbek (uzb_Latn)** - Low-moderate confusability (Karluk branch, Latin script)

---

## Turkish (tur) vs. North Azerbaijani (azj) -- CRITICAL DISTINCTION

Turkish and Azerbaijani are the most confusable language pair in this analysis. They share the same language branch (Western Oghuz), have approximately 60-70% lexical similarity, nearly identical grammar, and both use Latin-based scripts with overlapping special characters. A short text of just a few words may be genuinely ambiguous between the two languages.

### Alphabet Differences

Both alphabets have 29-32 letters, with significant overlap. Key differences:

| Feature | Azerbaijani | Turkish |
|---|---|---|
| **Schwa letter** | Has **Ə ə** | Does NOT have ə |
| **Q letter** | Has **Q q** (uvular stop) | Does NOT have Q |
| **X letter** | Has **X x** (voiceless velar fricative) | Does NOT have X |
| **W letter** | Does NOT have W | Does NOT have W |
| **Dotless I** | Has **I ı** | Has **I ı** (shared) |
| **Ş, Ç, Ğ, Ö, Ü** | Present | Present (shared) |

**The single strongest discriminator is the letter ə.** If a Latin-script text contains ə, it is almost certainly Azerbaijani, not Turkish. This character alone resolves the majority of ambiguous cases. The letters q and x are also strong Azerbaijani indicators, as they do not exist in the Turkish alphabet.

### Vocabulary Differences

While the core Turkic vocabulary is largely shared, there are systematic lexical differences:

| English | Azerbaijani | Turkish |
|---|---|---|
| what | **nə** | **ne** |
| with | **ilə** | **ile** |
| school | **məktəb** | **okul** (or mektep) |
| week | **həftə** | **hafta** |
| time | **vaxt** | **vakit** / **zaman** |
| now | **indi** | **şimdi** |
| here | **burada** / **burda** | **burada** |
| city | **şəhər** | **şehir** |
| news | **xəbər** | **haber** |
| people | **xalq** | **halk** |
| still | **hələ** | **hala** |
| about | **haqqında** | **hakkında** |
| how | **necə** | **nasıl** |
| why | **niyə** | **neden** / **niçin** |
| want | **istəmək** | **istemek** |
| between | **arasında** | **arasında** |
| thousand | **min** | **bin** |
| country | **ölkə** | **ülke** |
| century | **əsr** | **asır** / **yüzyıl** |
| airplane | **təyyarə** | **uçak** |

Azerbaijani tends to preserve more Arabic and Persian loanwords (məktəb, təyyarə, həftə, xəbər), while Turkish has undergone extensive language reform (dil devrimi) replacing many such loans with native Turkic coinages (okul, uçak, hafta, haber).

Azerbaijani also has a larger layer of Russian loanwords due to the Soviet period:
- **kompüter** (vs. Turkish **bilgisayar**)
- **maşın** (car; vs. Turkish **araba**)
- **avtobus** (vs. Turkish **otobüs**)

### Grammatical Differences

| Feature | Azerbaijani | Turkish |
|---|---|---|
| Present continuous | **-ır/-ir/-ur/-ür** (gəlir) | **-ıyor/-iyor/-uyor/-üyor** (geliyor) |
| Infinitive | **-maq/-mək** (yazmaq) | **-mak/-mek** (yazmak) |
| Future | **-acaq/-əcək** (gələcək) | **-acak/-ecek** (gelecek) |
| Negative aorist | **-maz/-məz** | **-maz/-mez** |
| Question particle | **-mı/-mi/-mu/-mü** | **-mı/-mi/-mu/-mü** (same) |
| Progressive | **-(maqdadır)** | **-(mektedir)** |
| Possessive 3PL | **-(ları/ləri)** | **-(ları/leri)** |

The present continuous tense suffix is the strongest grammatical discriminator: Azerbaijani **-ir** (short) vs. Turkish **-iyor** (long) produces visually very different verb forms.

### Phonological Differences Reflected in Orthography
- Azerbaijani uses **q** where Turkish uses **k** in back-vowel words: **qadın** vs. **kadın** (woman)
- Azerbaijani uses **x** where Turkish uses **h**: **xəstə** vs. **hasta** (sick)
- Azerbaijani uses **ə** where Turkish often uses **e** or **a**: **gəl** vs. **gel** (come)
- Word-final devoicing patterns differ slightly between the two

### Identification Strategy for Turkish vs. Azerbaijani

1. **Check for ə** - present = almost certainly Azerbaijani
2. **Check for q** - present in word-internal/initial position = Azerbaijani
3. **Check for x** - present = Azerbaijani
4. **Check verb endings** - **-ir/-ır** present tense = likely Azerbaijani; **-iyor/-ıyor** = Turkish
5. **Check infinitives** - **-maq/-mək** = Azerbaijani; **-mak/-mek** = Turkish
6. **Check vocabulary** - Persian/Arabic loans (məktəb, xəbər) lean Azerbaijani; Turkish neologisms (okul, haber) lean Turkish

## Turkmen (tuk) vs. North Azerbaijani (azj)

Turkmen is also an Oghuz Turkic language and uses a Latin script (since 1993). However, it differs in several key ways:

- Turkmen has **long vowels** marked by special characters: **ä, ö, ü, ž, ň, ý, ş** and notably **ä** instead of ə
- Turkmen uses **ý** (y with acute) for the palatal approximant in certain positions
- Turkmen uses **ž** (z with caron) which Azerbaijani does not have
- Turkmen lacks **ə**, **q**, and **x** in its alphabet
- Turkmen has distinctive word forms: **we** (and) vs. Azerbaijani **və**; **bilen** (with) vs. Azerbaijani **ilə**

---

## Crimean Tatar (crh) vs. North Azerbaijani (azj)

Crimean Tatar is a Kipchak-Oghuz transitional language also written in Latin script:

- Crimean Tatar uses the digraph **dj** and has some characters not found in Azerbaijani
- Crimean Tatar lacks **ə** (schwa) in its alphabet
- Different function words: **ve** (and), **ile** (with), **için** (for) - more Turkish-like
- The presence of Kipchak features (such as certain consonant correspondences) distinguishes it

---

## Uzbek (uzb_Latn) vs. North Azerbaijani (azj)

Uzbek belongs to the Karluk (Southeastern) branch and is more distant:

- Uzbek Latin script uses **oʻ** (o with turned comma) and **gʻ** (g with turned comma), which are unique identifiers
- Uzbek lacks **ə**, **ş**, **ğ**, and **ç**
- Uzbek uses apostrophe-like modifiers that Azerbaijani does not
- Significantly different core vocabulary and grammar
- Uzbek has lost vowel harmony in its standard form, unlike Azerbaijani
