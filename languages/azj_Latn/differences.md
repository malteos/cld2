# North Azerbaijani vs confusable languages

Closest confusables: Turkish (tur), the aze macrocode, Turkmen
(tuk), Crimean Tatar (crh).

## azj ↔ tur (Turkish)

- **`ə` is the headline diagnostic** — azj writes it in almost every
  sentence (*mən, səhər, gələn, dəfə*); tur never has `ə`.
- azj uses native `q` and `x` (*qardaş, xoş, xalq*); tur uses `k`,
  `h` in the cognates (*kardeş, hoş, halk*).
- Dative: azj `-ə` vs tur `-e`; locative: azj `-də` vs tur `-de`.
- Word-final voiced obstruents: azj keeps `b d g`
  (*kitab, ad, dağ*); tur devoices (*kitap*).

## azj ↔ aze (macro)

- `azj` is the modern post-1993 standard and will be uniformly
  reformed; `aze` macrocode text may contain 1992–1993 transitional
  spellings (occasional `ä`, apostrophes in Arabic loanwords like
  *məs'ul*, alternative hyphenation). For LID purposes they are
  effectively identical.

## azj ↔ tuk (Turkmen)

- Turkmen uses `ä ý ň ž`; azj does not.
- azj has `ə q x`; Turkmen has none of them (Turkmen writes /q/ as
  `k` and /x/ as `h`).
- Turkmen doubles long vowels orthographically; azj does not.

## azj ↔ crh (Crimean Tatar, Latin)

- crh has `ñ`; azj does not.
- azj has `ə`; crh does not.
- Both use `q`, so `q` alone is not enough — combine with `ə`/`ñ`.
