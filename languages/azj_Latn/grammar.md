# North Azerbaijani Grammar (azj_Latn)

## Word Order

North Azerbaijani is a head-final, SOV (Subject-Object-Verb) language. The default word order places the verb at the end of the clause:

- **Mən kitab oxuyuram.** (I book read-PRES-1SG) = "I am reading a book."
- **Uşaq məktəbə gedir.** (Child school-DAT go-PRES-3SG) = "The child goes to school."

Modifiers precede the head noun, adjectives precede nouns, and adverbs precede verbs. Postpositions are used instead of prepositions. While SOV is the canonical order, pragmatic reordering (topicalization, focus) is permitted, with the immediately preverbal position carrying focal stress.

## Morphological Type

Azerbaijani is a highly agglutinative language. Grammatical relations, tense, aspect, mood, person, and number are expressed through suffixes added sequentially to stems. A single verb form can carry multiple suffixes encoding several grammatical categories simultaneously:

- **gəl-mə-yəcək-lər** (come-NEG-FUT-3PL) = "they will not come"
- **yaz-dır-ıl-mış-dır** (write-CAUS-PASS-EVID-COP) = "it is said to have been written"

## Vowel Harmony

Azerbaijani exhibits a robust two-dimensional vowel harmony system that governs suffix vowel selection:

### Back/Front Harmony (Palatal Harmony)
Suffixes with vowels alternate between back and front variants based on the last vowel in the stem:
- Back vowels: a, ı, o, u
- Front vowels: e, i, ö, ü
- The vowel ə patterns with front vowels

Examples:
- **kitab-lar** (books) vs. **ev-lər** (houses)
- **qapı-da** (at the door) vs. **əl-də** (in the hand)

### Rounded/Unrounded Harmony (Labial Harmony)
High vowels in suffixes also alternate for rounding:
- After unrounded vowels: ı, i
- After rounded vowels: u, ü

Examples of four-way alternation (question particle):
- **gəl-di-mi?** (did [he] come?)
- **al-dı-mı?** (did [he] take?)
- **gör-dü-mü?** (did [he] see?)
- **bul-du-mu?** (did [he] find?)

## Phonology

### Vowels (9 vowels)
Azerbaijani has a symmetric nine-vowel system:

| | Front Unrounded | Front Rounded | Back Unrounded | Back Rounded |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High | i | ü | ı | u |
| Mid | e, ə | ö | | o |
| Low | | | a | |

The letter **ə** represents the open front unrounded vowel /æ/, which is one of the most distinctive features of Azerbaijani orthography and a key identifier for the language. It is extremely common and appears in many basic words: **məktəb** (school), **əl** (hand), **dərs** (lesson), **nə** (what).

### Consonants
The consonant inventory includes approximately 23 phonemes:
- Stops: p, b, t, d, k, g, q (uvular)
- Affricates: c /dʒ/, ç /tʃ/
- Fricatives: f, v, s, z, ş /ʃ/, j /ʒ/, x /x/, h, ğ /ɣ/
- Nasals: m, n
- Liquids: l, r
- Glide: y /j/

The uvular stop **q** and the velar fricative **ğ** are phonemically distinctive. The letter **ğ** represents a voiced velar/uvular fricative and is found in words like **dağ** (mountain), **yağış** (rain).

## Noun System

### Cases (6 cases)
Azerbaijani nouns inflect for six grammatical cases:

| Case | Suffix | Example (ev = house) | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | -Ø | ev | Subject |
| Genitive | -(n)ın/-(n)in/-(n)un/-(n)ün | evin | Possession |
| Dative | -(y)a/-(y)ə | evə | Direction, recipient |
| Accusative | -(n)ı/-(n)i/-(n)u/-(n)ü | evi | Definite direct object |
| Locative | -da/-də | evdə | Location |
| Ablative | -dan/-dən | evdən | Source, origin |

After voiceless consonants, the locative and ablative use -ta/-tə and -tan/-tən respectively.

### Number
Plurality is marked with **-lar/-lər** (back/front harmony):
- **kitab-lar** (books)
- **ev-lər** (houses)
- **göz-lər** (eyes)

### No Grammatical Gender
Azerbaijani has no grammatical gender. The third person pronoun **o** covers "he," "she," and "it." There is no noun class or gender agreement system.

### Possessive Suffixes
Nouns take possessive suffixes to indicate ownership:
- 1SG: -(ı)m/-(i)m/-(u)m/-(ü)m — **evim** (my house)
- 2SG: -(ı)n/-(i)n/-(u)n/-(ü)n — **evin** (your house)
- 3SG: -(s)ı/-(s)i/-(s)u/-(s)ü — **evi** (his/her house)
- 1PL: -(ı)mız/-(i)miz/-(u)muz/-(ü)müz — **evimiz** (our house)
- 2PL: -(ı)nız/-(i)niz/-(u)nuz/-(ü)nüz — **eviniz** (your house)
- 3PL: -(s)ı/-(s)i/-(s)u/-(s)ü (+ ları/ləri) — **evləri** (their house)

## Verb System

The Azerbaijani verb system is morphologically rich, encoding tense, aspect, mood, person, number, voice, negation, and evidentiality through suffixes.

### Tense and Aspect
Key tense/aspect forms include:
- **Present continuous:** -(y)ır/-(y)ir/-(y)ur/-(y)ür — **yazıram** (I am writing)
- **Aorist/habitual:** -(a)r/-(ə)r — **yazaram** (I write [habitually])
- **Past definite:** -dı/-di/-du/-dü — **yazdım** (I wrote)
- **Past narrative (evidential):** -mış/-miş/-muş/-müş — **yazmışam** (I apparently wrote / I have written)
- **Future:** -(y)acaq/-(y)əcək — **yazacağam** (I will write)
- **Past imperfect:** -(y)ırdı/-(y)irdi — **yazırdım** (I was writing / I used to write)

### Negation
Verbal negation is formed with the suffix **-ma/-mə** inserted before tense markers:
- **yaz-ma-dım** (I did not write)
- **gəl-mə-yəcək** (he/she will not come)
- **oxu-mu-ram** (I am not reading — note: the vowel harmonizes)

### Question Particle
Yes/no questions are formed with the enclitic particle **-mı/-mi/-mu/-mü** (four-way vowel harmony):
- **Gəldinmi?** (Did you come?)
- **Yazırsanmı?** (Are you writing?)

This particle can also be used with non-verbal predicates:
- **Bu kitabdırmı?** (Is this a book?)

### Evidentiality
Azerbaijani grammaticalizes evidentiality through the **-mış/-miş/-muş/-müş** suffix, which indicates that the speaker did not directly witness the event:
- **O gəldi.** (He came. — speaker witnessed)
- **O gəlmiş.** (He came, apparently / He has come. — speaker infers or was told)

### Voice
- **Passive:** -(ı)l/-(i)l/-(u)l/-(ü)l — **yazılmaq** (to be written)
- **Causative:** -dır/-dir/-dur/-dür (and variants) — **yazdırmaq** (to have [something] written)
- **Reflexive:** -(ı)n/-(i)n/-(u)n/-(ü)n — **yuyunmaq** (to wash oneself)
- **Reciprocal:** -(ı)ş/-(i)ş/-(u)ş/-(ü)ş — **görüşmək** (to meet each other)

### Person Suffixes
Verbs agree with their subjects in person and number:

| Person | Present suffix | Past definite suffix |
|---|---|---|
| 1SG | -(a)m | -m |
| 2SG | -san/-sən | -n |
| 3SG | -Ø | -Ø |
| 1PL | -(ı)q/-(i)k | -q/-k |
| 2PL | -sınız/-siniz | -nız/-niz |
| 3PL | -(lar)/(lər) | -lar/-lər |

## Syntax

### Postpositions
Azerbaijani uses postpositions rather than prepositions:
- **ev-in qabağ-ında** (in front of the house)
- **məktəb-ə görə** (according to the school)
- **kitab üçün** (for the book)

### Subordination
Relative clauses precede the noun they modify and typically use participle forms:
- **Mən oxuyan kitab** (the book that I read)
- **Gələn adam** (the person who is coming)

Complement clauses use the complementizer **ki** (borrowed from Persian) or nominalized verb forms:
- **Bilirəm ki o gələcək.** (I know that he will come.)

### Topic and Focus
Azerbaijani uses word order and particles for information structure. The topic typically appears sentence-initially, while focused constituents appear in the immediately preverbal position. The particle **isə** marks contrastive topics, and **da/də** functions as an additive particle ("also, too").

### Copula
The copular verb in Azerbaijani is expressed through suffixes in the present tense:
- **Mən tələbəyəm.** (I am a student.)
- **O müəllimdir.** (He/She is a teacher.)

In the past tense, the free-standing copula **idi** is used:
- **O müəllim idi.** (He/She was a teacher.)

### Existential Constructions
Existence is expressed with **var** (exists) and **yox** (does not exist):
- **Kitab var.** (There is a book.)
- **Vaxt yox.** (There is no time.)
