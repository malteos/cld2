# North Azerbaijani (azj)

## Summary

North Azerbaijani (endonym: *Azərbaycan dili*, *Azərbaycanca*) is
a Turkic language in the Oghuz branch (Turkic → Common Turkic →
Oghuz → Western Oghuz). ISO codes: 639-3 `azj`, part of the
macrolanguage `aze` (639-1 `az`, 639-2 `aze`). Native speakers
number about 9–13 million, concentrated in the Republic of
Azerbaijan, with communities in Russia (Dagestan), Georgia, and
Turkey. Modern `azj` is the official standard of the Republic of
Azerbaijan and is written in a post-1991 Latin alphabet.

## Writing system

The current Latin alphabet has 32 letters, including the
distinctive `ə ç ğ ı i ö ş ü q x`. The schwa `ə` (U+0259) is the
single most diagnostic character against Turkish. Case-contrasting
dotted/dotless `İ/I` and `i/ı` follow the Turkish model. `q` and
`x` carry phonemic values (uvular/velar stops and velar fricative)
and appear in common native words (*qız, xoş, çox, xalq*). Decimal
separator is the comma.

## History and status

Azerbaijani's script has changed four times: Arabic (pre-1929),
Yañalif Latin (1929–1939), Cyrillic (1939–1991), and the current
Latin (1991–present, finalised 1992). South Azerbaijani (`azb`) in
Iran still uses a Perso-Arabic script and is covered separately.
Within CommonLID, `azj_Latn` is the modern post-1993 Latin standard.
