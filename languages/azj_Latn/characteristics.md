# North Azerbaijani Identification Characteristics (azj_Latn)

## Diagnostic Special Characters

The Azerbaijani Latin alphabet contains several distinctive characters that are highly diagnostic for language identification. The presence of these characters, especially in combination, strongly indicates Azerbaijani text:

| Character | Unicode | Name | Diagnostic Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ə ə** | U+018F, U+0259 | Schwa | **Very high** - Nearly unique to Azerbaijani among Latin-script languages |
| **Ö ö** | U+00D6, U+00F6 | O with diaeresis | Moderate - shared with Turkish, German, Swedish, etc. |
| **Ü ü** | U+00DC, U+00FC | U with diaeresis | Moderate - shared with Turkish, German, etc. |
| **Ç ç** | U+00C7, U+00E7 | C with cedilla | Low-moderate - shared with Turkish, French, Portuguese |
| **Ş ş** | U+015E, U+015F | S with cedilla | Moderate-high - shared with Turkish, Romanian |
| **Ğ ğ** | U+011E, U+011F | G with breve | Moderate-high - shared with Turkish |
| **I ı** | U+0049, U+0131 | Dotless I | Moderate-high - shared with Turkish |

The single most diagnostic character is **ə** (schwa). Its frequent presence in a Latin-script text is an extremely strong signal for Azerbaijani. No other major Latin-script language uses this character with comparable frequency. In Azerbaijani, ə appears in approximately 5-8% of all characters in typical text, making it one of the most frequent vowels.

The combination of **ə** together with **ş**, **ğ**, **ç**, and **ö/ü** is essentially unique to Azerbaijani and serves as a near-certain identifier.

## Character Frequency Patterns

Typical character frequency distribution in Azerbaijani text (approximate percentages):

**Most frequent characters:** a (8-10%), ə (5-8%), i (5-7%), r (5-6%), n (5-6%), l (4-6%), d (4-5%), e (3-5%), s (3-4%), t (3-4%), ı (3-4%), y (3-4%)

**Moderately frequent:** m (2-3%), k (2-3%), u (2-3%), b (2-3%), o (2-3%), q (1-3%), ş (1-2%), z (1-2%), v (1-2%), ç (1-2%)

**Less frequent:** g (1-2%), ö (0.5-1.5%), ü (0.8-1.5%), x (0.5-1%), h (1-2%), ğ (0.5-1%), p (0.5-1%), f (0.3-0.6%), c (0.3-0.6%), j (0.1-0.3%)

Key frequency observations for identification:
- **ə** having very high frequency (top 3-4 characters) is unique to Azerbaijani
- **q** is notably more frequent in Azerbaijani than in Turkish (where it does not exist in the alphabet)
- **x** appears in Azerbaijani but not in the standard Turkish alphabet
- The ratio of ə to e is distinctive: both are common, with ə often more frequent

## Distinctive Function Word Patterns

Several function words are strongly diagnostic for Azerbaijani vs. other Turkic languages:
- **və** (and) - Azerbaijani uses this Persian-origin conjunction; Turkish uses **ve** (similar but without ə)
- **ilə** (with) - Azerbaijani uses this form; Turkish uses **ile**
- **üçün** (for) - Azerbaijani; Turkish uses **için**
- **lakin** (but) - more common in Azerbaijani than Turkish
- **haqqında** (about) - Azerbaijani; Turkish uses **hakkında**
- **var/yox** - both Azerbaijani and Turkish use these, but orthographic context with ə helps

## Morphological Pattern Signatures

### Case suffix patterns
These suffix patterns, governed by vowel harmony, are characteristic of Azerbaijani:
- Locative: **-da/-də/-ta/-tə** after words
- Ablative: **-dan/-dən/-tan/-tən** after words
- Dative: **-a/-ə/-ya/-yə** after words
- Genitive: **-ın/-in/-un/-ün/-nın/-nin/-nun/-nün** after words
- Accusative: **-ı/-i/-u/-ü/-nı/-ni/-nu/-nü** after words

### Verbal suffix patterns
- Present: **-ır/-ir/-ur/-ür** (Azerbaijani form; Turkish uses **-ıyor/-iyor/-uyor/-üyor**)
- Future: **-acaq/-əcək** (Azerbaijani; Turkish uses **-acak/-ecek**)
- Infinitive: **-maq/-mək** (Azerbaijani; Turkish uses **-mak/-mek**)
- Evidential past: **-mış/-miş/-muş/-müş** (shared with Turkish)
- Negation: **-ma/-mə** before tense markers

### Key morphological differences from Turkish
The present continuous tense suffix is a strong discriminator:
- Azerbaijani: **gəl-ir** (comes), **yaz-ır** (writes)
- Turkish: **gel-iyor** (comes), **yaz-ıyor** (writes)

The infinitive suffix also differs:
- Azerbaijani: **-maq/-mək** (e.g., **yazmaq**, **gəlmək**)
- Turkish: **-mak/-mek** (e.g., **yazmak**, **gelmek**)

The future tense suffix shows variation:
- Azerbaijani: **-acaq/-əcək** with the uvular q
- Turkish: **-acak/-ecek** with velar k

## Additional Identification Signals

- The letter **q** is part of the Azerbaijani alphabet but not the Turkish alphabet. Words containing q (qadın, qardaş, qaçmaq) strongly suggest Azerbaijani.
- The letter **x** is in the Azerbaijani alphabet but not Turkish. Words like **xalq** (people), **xəbər** (news) are diagnostic.
- Azerbaijani retains certain Arabic/Persian loanwords that Turkish has replaced with neologisms.
- The word-final cluster patterns differ slightly due to phonological rules.
