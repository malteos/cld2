# Kannada Grammar Reference

Kannada (kannnaDa, ಕನ್ನಡ) is a Dravidian language spoken predominantly in the Indian state of Karnataka. It has a literary tradition spanning over 1,500 years and is one of the scheduled languages of India. This reference covers the core grammatical structures of the language.

---

## Word Order

Kannada follows a strict **SOV (Subject-Object-Verb)** word order. The verb always appears at the end of the clause. Modifiers precede the elements they modify: adjectives precede nouns, and adverbs precede verbs.

- ಅವನು ಪುಸ್ತಕವನ್ನು ಓದುತ್ತಾನೆ (avanu pustakavannnu Oduttaane) -- "He reads a book."
  - ಅವನು (avanu) = he [subject]
  - ಪುಸ್ತಕವನ್ನು (pustakavannnu) = book [object, with accusative suffix]
  - ಓದುತ್ತಾನೆ (Oduttaane) = reads [verb]

While SOV is the canonical order, constituent order can be varied for emphasis or topicalization, though the verb nearly always remains clause-final.

---

## Agglutinative Morphology

Kannada is an **agglutinative language**, meaning grammatical relationships are expressed by adding suffixes sequentially to a stem. A single word can carry information about case, number, tense, aspect, mood, and person through a chain of suffixes.

For example, the word ಮನೆಗಳಲ್ಲಿ (manegaLalli, "in the houses") is built as follows:

- ಮನೆ (mane) = house (stem)
- -ಗಳ (-gaLa) = plural suffix
- -ಲ್ಲಿ (-lli) = locative suffix

This layering of suffixes onto a stem is a hallmark of Dravidian languages and gives Kannada words considerable internal complexity while keeping the system highly regular.

## Phonology

### Vowels

Kannada distinguishes between **short and long vowels**. This length distinction is phonemic -- changing vowel length changes word meaning.

| Short | Long | Example |
|-------|------|---------|
| ಅ (a) | ಆ (aa) | ಅಲ (ala, "there") vs. ಆಲ (aala, "banyan tree") |
| ಇ (i) | ಈ (ii) | -- |
| ಉ (u) | ಊ (uu) | -- |
| ಎ (e) | ಏ (ee) | -- |
| ಒ (o) | ಓ (oo) | -- |

Kannada also has the diphthongs ಐ (ai) and ಔ (au).

### Consonants

Kannada has a rich consonant inventory organized by place and manner of articulation. A critical feature is the series of **retroflex consonants**, produced with the tongue curled back against the hard palate:

- ಟ (Ta), ಠ (Tha), ಡ (Da), ಢ (Dha), ಣ (Na)

These contrast with the dental series:

- ತ (ta), ಥ (tha), ದ (da), ಧ (dha), ನ (na)

The distinction between retroflex and dental sounds is essential and carries semantic weight in many minimal pairs.

### Aspirated vs. Unaspirated Stops

Kannada systematically contrasts **aspirated** and **unaspirated** stops across five places of articulation:

| Place | Unaspirated | Aspirated |
|-------|-------------|-----------|
| Velar | ಕ (ka) | ಖ (kha) |
| Palatal | ಚ (ca) | ಛ (cha) |
| Retroflex | ಟ (Ta) | ಠ (Tha) |
| Dental | ತ (ta) | ಥ (tha) |
| Bilabial | ಪ (pa) | ಫ (pha) |

Each of these also has a voiced counterpart (e.g., ಗ/ga vs. ಘ/gha). This aspiration contrast is maintained in the native vocabulary, though in colloquial speech some speakers may neutralize it in certain environments.

## Noun System

### Gender

Kannada nouns belong to one of **three genders**:

1. **Masculine (pullinga / ಪುಲ್ಲಿಂಗ)**: typically refers to male beings. Example: ಹುಡುಗ (huDuga, "boy").
2. **Feminine (strIlinga / ಸ್ತ್ರೀಲಿಂಗ)**: typically refers to female beings. Example: ಹುಡುಗಿ (huDugi, "girl").
3. **Neuter (napuMsakalinga / ನಪುಂಸಕಲಿಂಗ)**: typically refers to inanimate objects. Example: ಮರ (mara, "tree").

Gender assignment for animate nouns generally follows natural gender, but inanimate nouns must have their gender learned as a lexical property.

### Number

Kannada distinguishes **two numbers**: singular (ekavacana) and plural (bahuvacana). The most common plural suffix is -ಗಳು (-gaLu):

- ಮನೆ (mane, "house") --> ಮನೆಗಳು (manegaLu, "houses")
- ಹುಡುಗ (huDuga, "boy") --> ಹುಡುಗರು (huDugaru, "boys") -- note the alternate plural suffix -ರು (-ru) used for human masculine/feminine nouns.

### Case System

Kannada has **seven grammatical cases**, each marked by a suffix attached to the noun stem (after the plural suffix, if present):

| Case | Suffix | Example (stem: ಮನೆ, "house") |
|------|--------|------|
| Nominative (kartRvibhakti) | -ಉ (-u) / unmarked | ಮನೆಯು (maneyu) |
| Accusative (karmavibhakti) | -ಅನ್ನು (-annu) | ಮನೆಯನ್ನು (maneyannu) |
| Instrumental (karaNavibhakti) | -ಇಂದ (-inda) | ಮನೆಯಿಂದ (maneyinda) |
| Dative (sampradaanavibhakti) | -ಇಗೆ (-ige) / -ಕ್ಕೆ (-kke) | ಮನೆಗೆ (manege) |
| Ablative (apaadaanavibhakti) | -ಇಂದ (-inda) | ಮನೆಯಿಂದ (maneyinda) |
| Genitive (sambandha vibhakti) | -ಅ (-a) / -ಉ (-u) | ಮನೆಯ (maneya) |
| Locative (adhikaraNa vibhakti) | -ಅಲ್ಲಿ (-alli) | ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ (maneyalli) |

Note that the instrumental and ablative cases share the same suffix -inda in Kannada; context determines which interpretation applies.

## Verb System

### Tense

Kannada verbs are conjugated for **three tenses**:

- **Past (bhUtakaala)**: formed with the suffix -ದ (-da) or -ಿದ (-ida). Example: ಮಾಡಿದ (maaDida, "did").
- **Present (vartamaanakaala)**: formed with the suffix -ಉತ್ತ (-utta). Example: ಮಾಡುತ್ತಾನೆ (maaDuttaane, "he does / is doing").
- **Future (bhaviShyatkaala)**: formed with the suffix -ಉವ (-uva) or -ಅವ (-ava). Example: ಮಾಡುವನು (maaDuvanu, "he will do").

### Aspect

Kannada expresses aspectual distinctions through auxiliary verb constructions and participial forms. The **perfective** is marked by combining the past participle with auxiliary verbs, while the **progressive/imperfective** uses the present participle with forms of ಇರು (iru, "to be"):

- ಮಾಡಿದ್ದಾನೆ (maaDiddaane) -- "He has done" (perfective)
- ಮಾಡುತ್ತಿದ್ದಾನೆ (maaDuttiddaane) -- "He was doing" (imperfective/progressive)

### Mood

Kannada verbs express at least three moods:

- **Indicative (nirdeeshaka)**: the default mood for statements of fact. Example: ಅವನು ಬರುತ್ತಾನೆ (avanu baruttaane, "He comes").
- **Imperative (aadeshaarthaka)**: used for commands and requests. Example: ಬಾ (baa, "Come!" -- informal), ಬನ್ನಿ (banni, "Come!" -- polite/plural).
- **Optative (aasheervaadaarthaka)**: expresses wishes, blessings, or desires. Example: ಒಳ್ಳೆಯದಾಗಲಿ (oLLeyadaagali, "May good happen").

### Subject Agreement

Kannada verbs **agree with the subject** in person and number. The verb ending changes to reflect whether the subject is first, second, or third person, and whether it is singular or plural. Gender distinction also appears in third-person forms.

Example with the verb ಬರು (baru, "to come") in present tense:

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|--------|----------|--------|
| 1st | ನಾನು ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (naanu baruttEne) | ನಾವು ಬರುತ್ತೇವೆ (naavu baruttEve) |
| 2nd | ನೀನು ಬರುತ್ತೀಯ (niinu baruttIya) | ನೀವು ಬರುತ್ತೀರಿ (niivu baruttIri) |
| 3rd masc. | ಅವನು ಬರುತ್ತಾನೆ (avanu baruttaane) | ಅವರು ಬರುತ್ತಾರೆ (avaru baruttaare) |
| 3rd fem. | ಅವಳು ಬರುತ್ತಾಳೆ (avaLu baruttaaLe) | ಅವರು ಬರುತ್ತಾರೆ (avaru baruttaare) |
| 3rd neut. | ಅದು ಬರುತ್ತದೆ (adu baruttade) | ಅವು ಬರುತ್ತವೆ (avu baruttave) |

### Negative Verbs

Negation in Kannada is expressed morphologically rather than by a separate word. The negative is formed using specific negative suffixes or by a distinct negative conjugation paradigm. The common pattern uses -ಅಲ್ಲ (-alla) for nominal predicates and negative verb forms with -ಇಲ್ಲ (-illa) or negative stems:

- ಮಾಡುವುದಿಲ್ಲ (maaDuvudilla) -- "does not do" (habitual negative)
- ಮಾಡಲಿಲ್ಲ (maaDalilla) -- "did not do" (past negative)
- ಬರುವುದಿಲ್ಲ (baruvudilla) -- "does not come / will not come"

## Postpositions

Kannada uses **postpositions** rather than prepositions. The postposition follows the noun (which is typically in an oblique or genitive case form):

- ಮನೆಯ ಮೇಲೆ (maneya meele) -- "on the house" (genitive + postposition)
- ಮರದ ಕೆಳಗೆ (marada keLage) -- "under the tree"
- ಊರಿನ ಹೊರಗೆ (uurina horage) -- "outside the town"
- ಶಾಲೆಯ ಮುಂದೆ (shaaleya munde) -- "in front of the school"

Some postpositions govern specific case forms of the preceding noun, while others simply follow the genitive.

---

## Relative Clauses and Participial Constructions

Kannada does not have relative pronouns equivalent to English "who," "which," or "that." Instead, **relative clauses are formed using participial constructions** -- a participle (verbal adjective) placed before the noun it modifies:

- ಬಂದ ಹುಡುಗ (banda huDuga) -- "the boy who came" (past participle + noun)
- ಓದುತ್ತಿರುವ ಹುಡುಗಿ (Oduttiru va huDugi) -- "the girl who is reading" (present participle + noun)
- ಬರುವ ಬಸ್ (baruva bas) -- "the bus that will come" (future participle + noun)

This prenominal participial strategy is characteristic of Dravidian languages and contrasts sharply with the postnominal relative clauses found in Indo-European languages.

---

## Pro-Drop

Kannada is a **pro-drop language**: subject pronouns can be omitted when the referent is clear from the verb morphology or context. Since the verb carries person, number, and (in third person) gender agreement, the subject pronoun is often redundant and its omission is natural and common in both spoken and written Kannada:

- ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ (baruttEne) -- "(I) will come" -- the first-person singular subject is encoded in the verb ending -ಏನೆ (-Ene).
- ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿದ್ಯಾ? (uuTa maaDidyaa?) -- "(Did you) eat?" -- the second-person subject is implicit.

Explicit pronouns are used for emphasis, contrast, or disambiguation.

## Sandhi (Morphophonemic Changes)

**Sandhi** rules govern sound changes that occur at morpheme and word boundaries in Kannada. These changes are systematic and affect both vowels and consonants.

### Vowel Sandhi (svara sandhi)

When two vowels meet at a boundary, one may be deleted or the two may merge:

- ಮನೆ + ಅಲ್ಲಿ --> ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ (maneyalli) -- a glide -ಯ- (-y-) is inserted between the two vowels.
- ಅವನ + ಅಣ್ಣ --> ಅವನಣ್ಣ (avanaNNa) -- the final vowel of the first word is dropped.

### Consonant Sandhi (vyanjana sandhi)

Consonant changes include gemination, voicing assimilation, and nasal assimilation:

- ಕಲ್ + ನಾಡು --> ಕನ್ನಾಡು (kannaaDu, "land of stone / Karnataka") -- the lateral -ಲ- assimilates to the following nasal.
- ಹೊನ್ + ಕಲಶ --> ಹೊಂಕಲಶ (hoMkalasha) -- nasal assimilation before a velar stop.

Sandhi changes are pervasive in both written and spoken Kannada and are an essential aspect of correct word formation and pronunciation.

## Summary

Kannada grammar is characterized by its SOV word order, agglutinative morphology, rich case system, and verb agreement paradigms. The use of participial relative clauses, postpositions, pro-drop, and systematic sandhi rules further define its typological profile as a canonical Dravidian language. Despite its complexity, the system is highly regular, making it amenable to systematic study once the core patterns of suffix attachment and morphophonemic alternation are understood.
