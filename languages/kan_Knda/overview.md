# Kannada (kan)

## Summary

Kannada (endonym: **ಕನ್ನಡ** *Kannaḍa*) is a South Dravidian language spoken natively by roughly 45–55 million people, with another ~15 million L2 speakers. It is the official language of the Indian state of Karnataka and has significant speaker populations in neighbouring Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh/Telangana, Kerala, and Goa, as well as a global diaspora. ISO codes: **639-1 `kn`**, **639-2/639-3 `kan`**. Script: Kannada (ISO 15924 `Knda`).

## Writing system

Kannada is written in the Kannada script, a Brahmic abugida descended (with Telugu) from the Kadamba/Old Kannada tradition. The two scripts share a common ancestor and still look superficially similar, but they occupy disjoint Unicode blocks and are not interchangeable. Kannada has no case distinction. Consonants carry an inherent /a/, which is suppressed by the **virama `್`** (halant, U+0CCD); dependent vowel signs attach around the base letter, and conjuncts stack vertically.

Like Telugu (and unlike Tamil), Kannada preserves the full Sanskrit-style four-way stop contrast (voiceless / voiceless-aspirated / voiced / voiced-aspirated, e.g. ಕ ಖ ಗ ಘ). The top-bar (headmark) on most letters is relatively flat and angular — a visual cue that distinguishes Kannada glyphs from Telugu's more rounded, curled headmarks.

## History and status

Kannada has a literary tradition reaching back to the 9th–10th century CE (Kavirajamarga, c. 850), with inscriptions considerably older. It is one of India's designated classical languages. Modern written Kannada is the standard for education, administration, media, and literature in Karnataka.
