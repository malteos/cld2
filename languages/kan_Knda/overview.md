# Kannada (kan_Knda)

## Basic Information

- **Language Name**: Kannada (ಕನ್ನಡ)
- **ISO 639-3**: kan
- **ISO 639-1**: kn
- **Script**: Kannada script (ಕನ್ನಡ ಲಿಪಿ, Knda)
- **Language Family**: Dravidian > South Dravidian
- **Native Speakers**: ~45 million
- **Total Speakers**: ~55 million (including L2 speakers)

## Geographic Distribution

Kannada is primarily spoken in the southwestern region of India:

- **Karnataka**: Kannada is the official and administrative language of the state of Karnataka, where it serves as the mother tongue of the majority of the population. It is spoken across all districts, with notable dialectal variation between the northern and southern regions of the state.

- **Neighboring States**: Significant Kannada-speaking populations exist in the border areas of neighboring states, including parts of **Maharashtra** (particularly the Belgaum/Belagavi region), **Andhra Pradesh**, **Telangana**, **Tamil Nadu**, and **Kerala**. Smaller communities can also be found in **Goa**.

- **Diaspora**: Kannada-speaking communities are present in the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, the Middle East (particularly the Gulf states), and Singapore, largely driven by migration in the technology and professional sectors.

## Official Status

- Official language of the state of Karnataka, India
- One of the 22 scheduled languages recognized by the Indian Constitution
- Granted **classical language** status by the Government of India in **2008**, in recognition of its antiquity and rich literary heritage
- Used as a medium of instruction in schools and universities across Karnataka
- One of the languages used in administration by the Government of India

## Historical Development

Kannada has one of the longest documented histories among the Dravidian languages, with written records spanning well over 1500 years.

### Old Kannada (450-1200 CE)

The earliest known Kannada inscription is the **Halmidi inscription**, dated to approximately the 5th century CE (c. 450 CE), found in the Hassan district of Karnataka. This inscription, written in Old Kannada script, is widely regarded as the oldest available epigraphic record in the language. Even earlier references to the Kannada-speaking region and its people appear in texts by ancient Greek and Roman geographers.

Old Kannada flourished under the patronage of the **Kadamba**, **Chalukya**, and **Rashtrakuta** dynasties. The **Kavirajamarga** (ಕವಿರಾಜಮಾರ್ಗ), authored during the reign of the Rashtrakuta king Amoghavarsha I in the **9th century CE**, is one of the earliest known works on poetics and literary criticism in any Dravidian language. It provides invaluable evidence of the linguistic and literary landscape of its time, codifying grammatical and aesthetic principles for Kannada poetry.

### Middle Kannada (1200-1700 CE)

The Middle Kannada period saw significant literary output under the **Hoysala**, **Vijayanagara**, and later **Mysore** kingdoms. The Vachana literary movement of the 12th century, led by **Basavanna** and other Shaiva saints (Sharanas), produced a remarkable body of prose-poetry that addressed themes of social equality, devotion, and philosophical inquiry. This movement is considered a watershed moment in Kannada literary and social history. Other notable writers of this era include **Harihara** and **Raghavanka**, who contributed to the development of Kannada narrative poetry.

### Modern Kannada (1700 CE-present)

Modern Kannada literature emerged through the influence of Western literary forms during the colonial period and expanded significantly after Indian independence. The 20th century witnessed an extraordinary flowering of Kannada literature across genres including poetry, fiction, drama, and literary criticism.

## Literary and Cultural Significance

Kannada literature holds a position of exceptional distinction in Indian letters. The language has produced **eight Jnanpith Award winners** -- more than any other language in India. These laureates include **Kuvempu** (1967), **D. R. Bendre** (1973), **Shivaram Karanth** (1977), **Masti Venkatesha Iyengar** (1983), **V. K. Gokak** (1990), **U. R. Ananthamurthy** (1994), **Girish Karnad** (1998), and **Chandrashekhara Kambara** (2010). This unmatched record underscores the depth and vitality of Kannada literary culture.

The literary tradition spans multiple genres and movements, from the early Jain and Shaiva religious literature to the modern Navya (modernist) and Bandaya (protest/activist) literary movements. Kannada cinema, theater, and music (including the Yakshagana folk theater tradition) also form a vibrant part of the cultural landscape of Karnataka.

## Linguistic Features

Kannada is an agglutinative language with a subject-object-verb (SOV) word order. The Kannada script is an alphasyllabary (abugida) descended from the Brahmi script through the Kadamba and Chalukya scripts. It consists of 49 base characters, including 15 vowels and 34 consonants, and is closely related to the Telugu script in structural terms.

The language features a complex system of noun declension with multiple case markers, an elaborate verb morphology encoding tense, aspect, mood, and person, and a distinction between formal and informal registers of speech. Kannada vocabulary has been enriched through historical borrowings from Sanskrit, Prakrit, Persian, Arabic, Portuguese, and English.

## Dialectal Variation

Major dialects of Kannada include **Mysore-Bangalore Kannada** (which forms the basis of the modern standard language), **Dharwad Kannada** (northern Karnataka), **Mangalore Kannada** (coastal), and **Gulbarga Kannada** (northeastern Karnataka). These dialects differ in phonology, vocabulary, and certain grammatical features, though mutual intelligibility is generally maintained across the dialect spectrum.
