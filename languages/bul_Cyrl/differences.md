# Bulgarian vs. confusable languages

Closest confusables in the CommonLID set are the other Cyrillic Slavic
languages Russian (`rus`) and Ukrainian (`ukr`). Outside the CommonLID set,
the nearest relative is Macedonian.

## Bulgarian vs. Russian

- Bulgarian **has no case inflection** on nouns/adjectives; Russian has six
  cases. A flat noun phrase `голямата къща` is Bulgarian; `большому дому`
  (dative) is Russian.
- Bulgarian has a **post-posed definite article** `-ът/-та/-то/-те`; Russian
  has no articles.
- Bulgarian has **no infinitive** — uses `да` + finite verb (`искам да пиша`)
  where Russian uses infinitive `хочу писать`.
- Bulgarian **ъ** is a vowel letter [ɤ] in everyday words (`България`,
  `сън`, `ъгъл`); Russian **ъ** is mute and rare.
- Bulgarian uses aorist/imperfect/renarrative tenses; Russian has only a
  gender-marked past.
- Bulgarian lacks Russian **ё**, **ы**, **э**.

## Bulgarian vs. Ukrainian

- Bulgarian lacks Ukrainian letters **і**, **ї**, **є**, **ґ**.
- Bulgarian uses **ъ** as a vowel; Ukrainian uses **і** for /i/ and does not
  use **ъ** at all.
- Bulgarian has no cases and no infinitive; Ukrainian has seven cases (with
  productive vocative) and an infinitive in `-ти`.
- Bulgarian uses enclitic articles; Ukrainian has no articles.
- Bulgarian future with `ще` + present; Ukrainian synthetic future in `-му`
  attached to infinitive (`робитиму`).
