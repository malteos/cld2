# Bulgarian (bul)

## Summary

Bulgarian (endonym: *български език*, *bǎlgarski ezik*) is a South Slavic
language in the Indo-European family (Indo-European → Balto-Slavic → Slavic →
South Slavic → Eastern South Slavic). ISO codes: 639-1 `bg`, 639-2 `bul`,
639-3 `bul`. Native speakers number around 7–9 million, primarily in
Bulgaria where it is the sole official language, with substantial communities
in Turkey, Ukraine (Bessarabia), Moldova, Greece, Romania and Serbia, and
diaspora populations in Germany, Spain, the UK and North America. It is
closely related to Macedonian, with which it forms an Eastern South Slavic
dialect continuum.

## Writing system

Bulgarian uses a 30-letter Cyrillic alphabet: а б в г д е ж з и й к л м н о п
р с т у ф х ц ч ш щ ъ ь ю я. It notably **lacks** Russian letters ё, ы, э,
and the Ukrainian letters і, ї, є, ґ. Its most distinctive feature is the
use of **ъ** as a full vowel letter (a "big yer") representing a central
mid-back vowel [ɤ], appearing frequently in common words like `България`,
`ъгъл`, `път`, `сън` — whereas in Russian ъ is only a mute hard sign.

## Status

Bulgarian is the earliest attested Slavic language: Old Bulgarian (Old Church
Slavonic) from the 9th century onward, the liturgical language of Orthodox
Slavs. Modern literary Bulgarian was codified in the mid-19th century during
the National Revival.
