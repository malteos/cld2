# Modern Hebrew (heb)

## Summary

Modern Hebrew (endonym: **עברית** *Ivrit*, sometimes **עברית מודרנית** or
**עברית ישראלית** *Ivrit Yisre'elit*) is the revived and modernised form of
Biblical/Mishnaic Hebrew, which has served as the national language of the
State of Israel since 1948. Family: Afro-Asiatic → Semitic → Central Semitic →
Northwest Semitic → Canaanite → Hebrew. ISO codes: **639-1 `he`**,
**639-2/639-3 `heb`**. Native speakers ~7 million in Israel, with ~3 million
additional L2 speakers in Israel and in global Jewish communities. Only
official language of Israel (Arabic has special status).

## Writing system

Uses the **Hebrew script** (U+0590–U+05FF) — a right-to-left abjad of 22
consonant letters. Five letters have final (sofit) forms: **ך ם ן ף ץ** at
word end vs **כ מ נ פ צ** elsewhere. Vowel pointing (niqqud: ַ ָ ֵ ֶ ִ ֹ ֻ ׁ ׂ
ְ) is optional and normally omitted in newsprint and web text; used in
children's texts, poetry, dictionaries, and for disambiguation. No casing.
Cantillation marks (te'amim) are not used in Modern Hebrew.

## Status

Official, administrative, educational, and media language of Israel. Highly
standardised by the **Academy of the Hebrew Language** (האקדמיה ללשון
העברית). Heavy borrowing from English, Arabic, Yiddish, and Russian in
colloquial register.
