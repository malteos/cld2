# Swahili, Coastal (swh)

## Summary

Coastal Swahili (endonym: **Kiswahili**) is the standardised,
prestige-register member of the Swahili macrolanguage (`swa`). Family:
Niger-Congo → Atlantic-Congo → Benue-Congo → Bantu → Sabaki. ISO codes:
639-3 `swh` (individual), within macrocode `swa`; 639-1 `sw`. It is the
form of Swahili taught in schools and used in national media across
Tanzania and Kenya, and is based primarily on the **Unguja (Zanzibar)**
dialect. Native speakers number around 15–20 million with many tens of
millions of L2 users along the Indian Ocean coast and interior East
Africa.

## Writing system

Latin script, 24 letters, no diacritics, no click letters. Bicameral
casing. Key digraphs: `ch`, `sh`, `th`, `dh`, `gh`, `kh`, `ny`, `ng`
and the apostrophe-marked `ng'` for the velar nasal. Spelling is
phonemic and very consistent; Arabic loans are fully romanised
(`kitabu`, `sababu`, `hakika`).

## History

Coastal Swahili crystallised from centuries of Bantu-Arab contact along
the Swahili Coast (Kilwa, Mombasa, Lamu, Zanzibar). After colonisation
it was codified by the Inter-territorial Language Committee in the 1930s
using Unguja as the reference dialect, giving `swh` its distinctive
conservative, Arabic-rich lexicon compared with inland Swahili
varieties.
