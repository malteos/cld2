# Characteristics (swh)

## Script signals

ASCII Latin only; no diacritics, no click letters, no tildes. The
trigraph `ng'` (with ASCII apostrophe) is a hallmark of Swahili
orthography. Presence of `dh`, `th`, `gh`, `kh` in roots points to
Arabic-origin vocabulary typical of the coastal norm.

## Top function words

`na`, `wa`, `ya`, `la`, `cha`, `kwa`, `katika`, `kwenye`, `kwamba`,
`kuwa`, `lakini`, `au`, `pia`, `bado`, `tu`, `sana`, `hivyo`, `hivi`,
`huyo`, `huyu`, `hii`, `hiyo`, `yule`, `nini`, `nani`, `lini`, `wapi`,
`kwa sababu`, `ambayo`, `ambao`, `yote`, `wote`, `tena`, `basi`,
`ndiyo`, `hapana`, `bila`, `juu ya`, `kuhusu`, `tangu`.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- Word-initial class prefixes: `wa-`, `m-`, `mw-`, `ki-`, `vi-`,
  `mi-`, `ma-`, `u-`, `ku-`.
- Verb prefix + TAM sequences: `ana-`, `wana-`, `tuna-`, `uli-`,
  `ali-`, `tuta-`, `wame-`, `ameshak-`.
- Final `-a` on native verbs; `-i`, `-e`, `-u` on Arabic-origin verbs
  (`fikiri`, `samehe`, `tarajia`).
- Frequent digraphs/trigraphs: `ch`, `sh`, `ny`, `ng`, `ng'`, `mb`,
  `nd`, `nj`, `mv`, `dh`, `th`, `gh`.
- Arabic loan markers: `-ibu`, `-ara`, `-isa`, `-ifa` (`jibu`,
  `swala`, `safisha`, `sifa`).

## Morphology hooks

Applicative `-i-/-e-`, causative `-sh-/-z-/-isha-`, passive `-w-`,
stative `-ik-/-ek-`, reciprocal `-an-`. Locative suffix `-ni`.
Reference infix `-o-` for relatives and "of".

## Orthography quirks

Apostrophe only in `ng'`. No capitalisation of nouns beyond sentence
start / proper names. Full stops used as in English; question mark
standard `?`.
