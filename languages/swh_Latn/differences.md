# Differences (swh)

## Confusable languages

### swh vs swa (macrolanguage)

`swh` is the standardised coastal variety inside macrocode `swa`. At
the sentence level the two are often indistinguishable.

- Surface forms, noun classes, TAM markers and concord are identical.
- `swh` adheres tightly to the Unguja/Zanzibar norm: preserves Arabic
  phonemic digraphs (`dh`, `th`, `gh`, `kh`), uses conservative
  copulas (`ni` / `si`) and stricter class agreement.
- `swa` samples mix in inland usage (Kenyan/Tanzanian interior),
  English code-switching, and sometimes Congo Swahili (`swc`)
  features: reduced concord, French loans (`motuka`, `makelele`),
  3rd-person `ana-` generalised.
- When in doubt, richer Arabic lexicon and tight concord → `swh`;
  visible Bantuised English/French loans or loose concord → `swa`.

### swh vs other Bantu in the set

- vs **zul/xho**: `swh` has no clicks (`c/q/x`), no `hl/dl`.
- vs **sna**: `swh` lacks Shona's dense `nd/mb/nh/nz` and uses `na`
  where Shona uses `uye`.
- vs **nso/sot**: `swh` has no `š`, no `hl`, and uses `kwa`/`katika`
  instead of `le`/`ka`.
- vs **lug/nyn**: `swh` has no geminates and uses `wa-` where Luganda
  uses `ba-`.
- vs **lin**: no French loans and no tone diacritics.
