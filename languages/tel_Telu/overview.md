# Telugu (tel)

## Summary

Telugu (endonym: **తెలుగు** *Telugu*) is a South-Central Dravidian language, the most-spoken Dravidian language by native-speaker count (roughly 80–95 million L1 speakers). It is an official language of India and the primary state language of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, with significant diaspora populations in the Gulf states, the United States, Malaysia, and Singapore. ISO codes: **639-1 `te`**, **639-2/639-3 `tel`**. Script: Telugu (ISO 15924 `Telu`).

## Writing system

Telugu is written in the Telugu script, a Brahmic abugida closely related to Kannada (both descended from the Kadamba script). Consonants carry an inherent /a/; dependent vowel signs (mātras) attach around the base letter, and the **virama `్`** (halant, U+0C4D) suppresses the inherent vowel. The script is visually characterised by its roundness and its consistent **talakattu** (headmark, the upward hook `ా` or curl that tops most letters), which gives Telugu text its distinctive "curly" silhouette.

Unlike Tamil, Telugu preserves the full Sanskrit voiced/aspirated stop series (క ఖ గ ఘ / ట ఠ డ ఢ / త థ ద ధ / ప ఫ బ భ), so a four-way contrast per place of articulation is written. There is no letter-case distinction. Conjunct consonants stack vertically below the base letter, with the secondary consonant appearing smaller.

## History and status

Telugu has a continuous literary tradition since the 11th century, with roots reaching back to early medieval inscriptions. It is one of India's designated classical languages and is sometimes nicknamed "the Italian of the East" for its vowel-heavy, open-syllable phonotactics.
