# Belarusian (bel_Cyrl) - Diagnostic Characteristics

## Diagnostic Cyrillic Characters

Belarusian uses a variant of the Cyrillic alphabet with several distinctive features that are critical for language identification:

### Unique and Diagnostic Characters

1. **Ў ў (short u / u-kratkaye)**: This letter is UNIQUE to Belarusian among all Cyrillic-script languages. It represents the non-syllabic [w] sound and appears frequently in native Belarusian text. Its presence is the single strongest indicator that a text is Belarusian. Examples: ён быў (he was), на ўсходзе (in the east), праўда (truth), аўтар (author), галоўны (main).

2. **І і (dotted i)**: Belarusian uses і instead of Russian и. This letter is shared with Ukrainian, so its presence narrows identification to Belarusian or Ukrainian, but does not uniquely identify Belarusian. Examples: і (and), гісторыя (history), ісці (to go).

3. **Apostrophe (')**: Belarusian uses the apostrophe instead of the Russian hard sign ъ to indicate the non-palatalization of the preceding consonant before iotated vowels. Examples: аб'ява (announcement), з'ява (phenomenon), сям'я (family). The ABSENCE of ъ combined with the presence of apostrophes is diagnostic.

### Characters ABSENT from Belarusian

1. **Щ щ**: Belarusian does NOT use this letter. Where Russian uses щ, Belarusian uses the combination **шч**: Russian площадь → Belarusian плошча, Russian ещё → Belarusian яшчэ. The absence of щ combined with presence of шч is a strong diagnostic feature.

2. **Ъ (hard sign)**: Belarusian does NOT use the hard sign. Its function is replaced by the apostrophe ('). The complete absence of ъ in a Cyrillic text is a strong indicator of Belarusian (or Ukrainian).

3. **И и**: Belarusian does NOT use this letter, using і instead.

4. **Э**: While Belarusian does use э, it uses it more frequently than Russian, particularly in native words: гэта (this), гэты (this), цэлы (whole), дзесяць (ten).

## Character Frequency Profile

The most frequent characters in typical Belarusian text (approximate order):

а, е, і, н, о, с, р, т, л, к, д, з, м, у, п, в, г, ы, я, б, ч, ц, ж, ш, х, ў, э, ф, ё, ю, дз

Key frequency observations:
- **а** is the most frequent vowel letter (due to akanne)
- **і** appears with high frequency (replacing Russian и)
- **ў** appears regularly and is unique to Belarusian
- **ц** appears more frequently than in Russian (due to tsekanne)
- **дз** appears as a common digraph (due to dzekanne)
- **э** appears more often than in Russian
- **ё** is always written (unlike in Russian where it is often replaced by е)

### Distinctive Function Words vs. Russian

Several function words strongly distinguish Belarusian from Russian:
- **але** (but) vs. Russian но
- **ці** (whether) vs. Russian ли
- **бо** (because) vs. Russian потому что
- **калі** (when/if) vs. Russian когда/если
- **вельмі** (very) vs. Russian очень
- **таксама** (also) vs. Russian тоже/также
- **пра** (about) vs. Russian о/про
- **ён** (he) vs. Russian он
- **яна** (she) vs. Russian она
- **яны** (they) vs. Russian они
- **гэта** (this) vs. Russian это

- **дзе**, **дзі**, **дзя** (dzekanne patterns)
- **цца**, **ццё**, **ціі** (tsekanne patterns)
- **шчэ**, **шча**, **шчы** (шч replacing щ)
- **аўт**, **оўн**, **аўк** (patterns with ў)

## Morphological Patterns

### Noun Endings
- Nominative masculine: consonant (горад, чалавек)
- Nominative feminine: -а/-я (мова, зямля)
- Nominative neuter: -а/-о/-е (слова, акно, мора)
- Genitive plural often in -аў/-яў (гарадоў, людзей)

### Verb Endings
- Infinitive: -ць/-ці (рабіць, несці)
- 3rd person singular: -е/-а/-іць (чытае, піша, гаворыць)
- 3rd person plural: -уць/-юць/-аць/-яць (чытаюць, гавораць)
- Past masculine: -ў (чытаў, рабіў) -- note this uses ў

### Adjective Endings
- Masculine: -ы/-і (вялікі, добры, беларускі)
- Feminine: -ая/-яя (вялікая, добрая)
- Neuter: -ае/-яе (вялікае, добрае)

### Derivational Patterns
- Abstract nouns: -анне/-енне/-ванне (выхаванне, навучанне)
- Agent nouns: -нік/-чык (працаўнік, лётчык)
- Adjectives from nouns: -ск-/-н- (беларускі, навуковы)
