# Belarusian (bel_Cyrl) - Grammar

## Word Order

Belarusian has a basic SVO (Subject-Verb-Object) word order, but the order is relatively free due to the rich inflectional morphology that marks grammatical relations through case endings rather than position. Word order variation is used extensively for pragmatic purposes such as topic-focus articulation. The most neutral, unmarked order is SVO, but SOV, VSO, OVS, and other arrangements are all grammatically acceptable and convey different information-structural meanings. In questions, word order may remain the same as in declarative sentences, with intonation alone marking the interrogative meaning, or the interrogative word may be fronted. Adjectives typically precede nouns, and possessives may precede or follow depending on emphasis.

## Morphological Type

Belarusian is a fusional, highly inflected language. A single suffix or ending typically encodes multiple grammatical categories simultaneously (e.g., case, number, and gender in a single noun ending). This is characteristic of Slavic languages more broadly. The language has a rich derivational morphology as well, with extensive use of prefixes and suffixes to create new words from existing roots.

## Phonology

Belarusian phonology has several distinctive features that set it apart from other East Slavic languages:

### Dzekanne and Tsekanne

One of the most characteristic phonological features of Belarusian is dzekanne (дзеканне) and tsekanne (цеканне). Before front vowels (і, е, я, ю) and the soft sign (ь), the consonants д and т are palatalized and affricated:

- **д** becomes **дз** [dz']: дзень (day), дзіця (child), медзведзь (bear)
- **т** becomes **ц** [ts']: цёплы (warm), цікавы (interesting), цягнуць (to pull)

This feature is absent from standard Russian and is a strong diagnostic marker for Belarusian text.

### Akanne (Аканне)

Belarusian orthography reflects akanne -- the pronunciation of unstressed /o/ as /a/. Unlike Russian, where akanne is a phonetic phenomenon not reflected in spelling, Belarusian writes words as they are pronounced:

- Russian: молоко (moloko) vs. Belarusian: малако (malako) -- "milk"
- Russian: голова (golova) vs. Belarusian: галава (halava) -- "head"
- Russian: хорошо (khorosho) vs. Belarusian: добра (dobra) -- "well/good"

This phonetic spelling principle is a fundamental feature of Belarusian orthography.

### Hard and Soft Consonant Pairs

Belarusian maintains a system of hard (non-palatalized) and soft (palatalized) consonant pairs, similar to Russian and Ukrainian. However, the specifics differ. Notably, Belarusian has consistently hard р, ч, and ш (unlike Russian where ч is always soft). The consonants дз and ц function as soft counterparts of д and т respectively.

### Consonant Assimilation

Belarusian exhibits extensive consonant assimilation, particularly in voicing. Voiced consonants become voiceless before voiceless consonants and at the end of words, and voiceless consonants become voiced before voiced consonants. This is generally reflected in spelling.

### Vowel System

The Belarusian vowel system consists of six vowel phonemes: /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, /u/, /y/. The vowels are represented by paired letters depending on whether the preceding consonant is hard or soft: а/я, о/ё, у/ю, э/е, ы/і.

## Noun System

### Cases

Belarusian nouns decline in six cases:

1. **Назоўны (Nominative)**: Subject of the sentence -- хлопец чытае (the boy reads)
2. **Родны (Genitive)**: Possession, absence, partitive -- кніга хлопца (the boy's book)
3. **Давальны (Dative)**: Indirect object -- даць хлопцу (to give to the boy)
4. **Вінавальны (Accusative)**: Direct object -- бачу хлопца (I see the boy)
5. **Творны (Instrumental)**: Instrument, accompaniment -- з хлопцам (with the boy)
6. **Месны (Locative/Prepositional)**: Location (always with preposition) -- аб хлопцу (about the boy)

### Gender

Belarusian has three grammatical genders: masculine, feminine, and neuter. Gender is partially predictable from the ending of the nominative singular form:

- **Masculine**: typically ends in a consonant (стол - table, дом - house)
- **Feminine**: typically ends in -а/-я (кніга - book, зямля - earth)
- **Neuter**: typically ends in -а/-я (for soft-stem), -о/-е (акно - window, мора - sea)

### Number

Nouns have singular and plural forms. The plural formation involves changes to the ending and sometimes to the stem. There are also remnants of the old dual number in certain constructions with the numerals 2, 3, and 4.

### Animate/Inanimate Distinction

The animate/inanimate distinction affects the accusative case of masculine nouns (and their modifiers). Animate masculine nouns have an accusative form identical to the genitive, while inanimate masculine nouns have an accusative identical to the nominative. This distinction extends to the plural of all genders.

## Verb System

### Aspect

Like other Slavic languages, Belarusian verbs come in aspectual pairs:

- **Imperfective** (незакончанае): ongoing, habitual, or repeated action -- чытаць (to read/be reading)
- **Perfective** (закончанае): completed, single action -- прачытаць (to read through/finish reading)

Aspect is formed through prefixation, suffixation, or suppletion: пісаць (impf.) / напісаць (pf.) -- to write; рабіць (impf.) / зрабіць (pf.) -- to do.

### Conjugation

Belarusian verbs conjugate for person (1st, 2nd, 3rd) and number (singular, plural) in the present and future tenses. There are two main conjugation classes:

**First conjugation** (e-type): чытаць (to read)
- я чытаю, ты чытаеш, ён/яна чытае
- мы чытаем, вы чытаеце, яны чытаюць

**Second conjugation** (i-type): гаварыць (to speak)
- я гавару, ты гаворыш, ён/яна гаворыць
- мы гаворым, вы гаворыце, яны гавораць

### Tenses

- **Present tense**: formed from imperfective verbs only
- **Past tense**: formed with the suffix -л- plus gender/number endings (ён чытаў, яна чытала, яно чытала, яны чыталі)
- **Future tense**: imperfective uses the auxiliary быць + infinitive (буду чытаць); perfective uses present-tense conjugation forms (прачытаю)

### Mood

- **Indicative**: the default mood for statements of fact
- **Imperative**: formed with specific endings (чытай - read!, чытайце - read! (plural/polite))
- **Conditional**: formed with the past tense + particle бы (я чытаў бы - I would read)

### Participles and Verbal Adverbs

Belarusian has active and passive participles, as well as verbal adverbs (дзеепрыслоўе). Active present participles are formed with -учы/-ючы (чытаючы - reading). Past passive participles are formed with -н-/-т- (напісаны - written, адкрыты - opened). Verbal adverbs indicate simultaneous or prior action: чытаючы кнігу (while reading a book).

## Adjectives

Adjectives agree with nouns in gender, number, and case. They have long (attributive) and short (predicative) forms, though short forms are less common in modern Belarusian than in Russian. Comparative and superlative degrees are formed either synthetically (with suffixes) or analytically (with больш/менш and найбольш/найменш).

## Pronouns

Personal pronouns decline for case and distinguish between familiar (ты) and polite (вы) forms of address. Demonstrative pronouns include гэты (this) and той (that). Relative pronouns such as які (which) and хто (who) introduce relative clauses.

## Syntax

### Negation

Negation is formed by placing the particle не before the verb: я не чытаю (I do not read). Double and multiple negation is standard: ніхто нічога не ведае (nobody knows nothing = nobody knows anything).

### Questions

Yes/no questions are formed by intonation or with the particle ці: Ці ты чытаеш? (Do you read?). Content questions use interrogative words: хто (who), што (what), дзе (where), калі (when), як (how), чаму (why).

### Complex Sentences

Subordinate clauses are introduced by conjunctions such as што (that), каб (in order to), калі (if/when), таму што (because), хаця (although). Relative clauses use the pronoun які (which) that agrees in gender and number with the antecedent.
