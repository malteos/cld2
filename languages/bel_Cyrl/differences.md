# Belarusian (bel_Cyrl) - Differences from Confusable Languages

## Critical Confusable Languages

Belarusian is most easily confused with Russian (extremely confusable) and Ukrainian (also confusable). All three are East Slavic languages written in Cyrillic script with significant lexical and grammatical overlap. Accurate identification requires attention to specific orthographic, phonological, and lexical markers.

## Belarusian vs. Russian (Very Confusable)

### Alphabet Differences

| Feature | Belarusian | Russian |
|---|---|---|
| Short u | **Ў ў** (present) | Absent |
| Dotted i | **І і** (used) | И и (used) |
| Hard sign | Absent (uses **'**) | **Ъ ъ** (used) |
| Shcha | Absent (uses **шч**) | **Щ щ** (used) |
| И и | Absent | Present |
| Ё ё | Always written | Often replaced by е |

The presence of **ў** is the single strongest differentiator. If a Cyrillic text contains ў, it is almost certainly Belarusian. The absence of щ and ъ, combined with the use of і instead of и, further confirms identification.

### Phonological Spelling Differences

**Akanne**: Belarusian spelling reflects the pronunciation of unstressed о as а:
- Russian молоко → Belarusian **малако** (milk)
- Russian хорошо → Belarusian **добра** (well)
- Russian город → Belarusian **горад** (city, but the о is stressed here)

**Dzekanne/Tsekanne**: д→дз and т→ц before front vowels:
- Russian дети → Belarusian **дзеці** (children)
- Russian тело → Belarusian **цела** (body)
- Russian день → Belarusian **дзень** (day)

**Hard р, ч**: Belarusian р and ч are consistently hard, affecting following vowels:
- Russian четыре → Belarusian **чатыры** (four)
- Russian чёрный → Belarusian **чорны** (black)

### Lexical Differences

Many common words differ between Belarusian and Russian:
- but: Belarusian **але** vs. Russian но
- very: Belarusian **вельмі** vs. Russian очень
- when: Belarusian **калі** vs. Russian когда
- because: Belarusian **бо/таму што** vs. Russian потому что
- about: Belarusian **пра** vs. Russian о/про
- he: Belarusian **ён** vs. Russian он
- they: Belarusian **яны** vs. Russian они
- this: Belarusian **гэты/гэта** vs. Russian этот/это
- also: Belarusian **таксама** vs. Russian тоже
- here: Belarusian **тут** vs. Russian тут/здесь
- still/yet: Belarusian **яшчэ** vs. Russian ещё
- only: Belarusian **толькі** vs. Russian только

### Morphological Differences

- Belarusian infinitives end in **-ць/-ці** vs. Russian -ть/-ти: рабіць vs. делать
- Belarusian past masculine ends in **-ў** vs. Russian -л: чытаў vs. читал
- Belarusian has the ending **-аў/-яў** in genitive plural: дамоў vs. домов
- Belarusian adjective masculine ends in **-ы/-і** vs. Russian -ый/-ий: вялікі vs. великий

## Belarusian vs. Ukrainian (Also Confusable)

### Shared Features (making them confusable)

Both Belarusian and Ukrainian:
- Use **і** instead of Russian и
- Do NOT use **ъ** (hard sign)
- Have phonetic spelling principles
- Share some vocabulary distinct from Russian

### Key Differences

| Feature | Belarusian | Ukrainian |
|---|---|---|
| **Ў ў** | Present (unique!) | Absent |
| **Є є** | Absent | Present |
| **Ї ї** | Absent | Present |
| **Ґ ґ** | Absent | Present |
| **Щ щ** | Absent (uses шч) | Present |
| **Э э** | Present | Absent (uses е) |
| **Ы ы** | Present | Absent |

The presence of **ў** immediately identifies Belarusian. Conversely, the presence of **є**, **ї**, or **ґ** immediately identifies Ukrainian. Ukrainian retains **щ**, while Belarusian replaces it with **шч**.

### Lexical Differences

- and: Belarusian **і** vs. Ukrainian і/та
- but: Belarusian **але** vs. Ukrainian але/проте
- he: Belarusian **ён** vs. Ukrainian він
- she: Belarusian **яна** vs. Ukrainian вона
- they: Belarusian **яны** vs. Ukrainian вони
- this: Belarusian **гэта** vs. Ukrainian це
- very: Belarusian **вельмі** vs. Ukrainian дуже
- what: Belarusian **што** vs. Ukrainian що

### Phonological/Spelling Differences

- Dzekanne/tsekanne is specific to Belarusian (absent in Ukrainian)
- Ukrainian has pleophony patterns different from Belarusian: Ukrainian місто vs. Belarusian места (city/place)
- Belarusian akanne is reflected in spelling; Ukrainian has its own vowel alternation patterns (ікання)

## Belarusian vs. Polish (Historical Influence)

While Polish uses Latin script (making script-level confusion unlikely), Polish has significantly influenced Belarusian vocabulary and some grammatical patterns. This is relevant for understanding loanword patterns:

### Polish Lexical Influence on Belarusian

Many Belarusian words show Polish origins or parallel development:
- Belarusian **кватэра** (apartment) cf. Polish kwatera
- Belarusian **кшталт** (shape) cf. Polish kształt
- Belarusian **абсталяванне** (equipment) cf. Polish obstalowanie
- Belarusian **ўлада** (authority) cf. Polish władza
- Belarusian **шкада** (pity) cf. Polish szkoda
- Belarusian **рэч** (thing) cf. Polish rzecz

### Grammatical Influence

Polish influence is visible in certain syntactic constructions and the use of some function words that differ from Russian. The conjunction **але** (but) and certain prepositional usages show parallel development with Polish.

## Summary of Quick Identification Features

To rapidly identify Belarusian Cyrillic text:

1. **Look for ў** -- if present, almost certainly Belarusian
2. **Check for і without є/ї/ґ** -- rules out Russian (uses и) and identifies either Belarusian or Ukrainian; absence of є/ї/ґ rules out Ukrainian
3. **Check for absence of щ** with presence of **шч** -- confirms Belarusian
4. **Look for дз/ц patterns** (dzekanne/tsekanne) -- strongly Belarusian
5. **Check for apostrophe (') instead of ъ** -- consistent with Belarusian
6. **Look for diagnostic words**: ён, яна, яны, але, вельмі, калі, гэта, бо, ці
