# Aragonese LID characteristics

## Script signals

Primary diacritics: `á é í ó ú ü`, optionally `ñ` (Uesca orthography) or digraph `ny` (SLA). No `ç`, no `œ`, no `l·l`. The letters `ll` and `ch` are frequent. Unlike Spanish, Aragonese routinely writes initial `f-` (preserving Latin) rather than `h-`.

## Top function words

`o a os as lo la los las un una uns unas de d' a en con por pa entre sobre y e o pero porque que qui cuán cuánto cómo si no sí más menos muito poco tot tots cada atro mesmo iste ista iste/ese/aquer yo tu el era nusatros vusatros els me te se nos tos lo la los las li les en ne bi i ye son yera yeran ha han habeba habeban`.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- Article `o / a / os / as` before the noun (*o libro, a casa, os diners*). This is the most reliable single diagnostic.
- Initial `f-` retained: *fer, fillo, fumo, farina, fogar, fembra*.
- `ll` for palatal lateral (preserved from Latin `-LL-`): *fillo, muller, caballo*.
- Diphthongs `ue ie` spread widely: *fuella, uello, tiengo, ochanta → güeitanta*.
- Participles `-ato -ito` (SLA) or `-ado -ido` (Uesca).

## Morphology hooks

Articles `o/a/os/as`; clitics `en/ne` and `bi/i`; verb endings `-ar -er -ir`; preterite `-ó/-ió`; 2pl `-atz -etz -itz` in older texts. Diminutive `-et/-eta, -ín/-ina`.

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

Uses `«»` or `""`. No inverted `¿ ¡` (unlike Spanish). Apostrophe is used with preposition `d'` before vowels. Acute accent marks irregular stress similarly to Spanish but over a different vowel inventory due to regular proparoxytone patterns.
