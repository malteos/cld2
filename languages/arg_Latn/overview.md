# Aragonese (arg)

## Summary

Aragonese (endonym: *aragonés* or *fabla*) is a Romance language (Indo-European → Italic → Romance → Ibero-Romance → Pyrenean-Mozarabic), closely related to Spanish and Occitan but distinct from both. ISO codes: 639-1 `an`, 639-2/3 `arg`. Written in the Latin script. Approximately 10,000–25,000 active speakers plus 30,000–50,000 with passive command, concentrated in the Pyrenean valleys of the province of Huesca (Aragon, Spain) — Ansó, Hecho, Benasque, Sobrarbe, Ribagorza, Tensino, Chistau.

## Writing system

Uses the Latin alphabet. Two orthographies compete: the **grafía SLA / Academia de l'Aragonés** (etymological, closer to Catalan/Occitan) and the **grafía EFA / Uesca** (phonetic, closer to Spanish). Common diacritics: acute `á é í ó ú`, grave rarely, diaeresis `ü`. Digraphs `ny` (palatal nasal, SLA) or `ñ` (Uesca), `ll` (lateral palatal), `ch` (/tʃ/). UNESCO classifies Aragonese as *definitely endangered*.

## Status

Co-official in some Aragon municipalities since the 2013 Ley de Lenguas regional statute. Aragonese is on UNESCO's endangered languages list. Actively promoted by the *Academia de l'Aragonés* and local cultural associations. Wikipedia (Aragonese edition) and a small but dedicated media presence exist.
