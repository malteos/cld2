# Aragonese vs. confusable languages

Closest confusables in this set: **Spanish (spa)**, **Catalan (cat)**, **Occitan (oci)**, **Extremaduran (ext)**.

## Aragonese vs. Spanish

- Aragonese articles `o a os as` vs. Spanish `el la los las` — the single cleanest diagnostic.
- Aragonese keeps initial F- (*fer, fillo, fuego, farina*); Spanish has H- or merged (*hacer, hijo, fuego, harina*).
- Aragonese has clitics `en/ne` (partitive) and `bi/i` (locative); Spanish lacks both.
- Aragonese lacks inverted `¿ ¡`; Spanish requires them.
- Aragonese allows `-z` plural in masculine (*chicoz, mozez*); Spanish uses `-s`.

## Aragonese vs. Catalan

- Aragonese `ll` (palatal lateral) vs. Catalan `ll` (same grapheme) but Catalan also has `l·l` and `ny`; Aragonese uses `ny` (SLA) or `ñ` (Uesca).
- Aragonese `o/a/os/as` articles vs. Catalan `el/la/els/les`.
- Catalan endings `-ció -tat -ment`; Aragonese `-zión -dá/-dat -ment`.
- Catalan has periphrastic preterite `vaig + INF` unique to it; Aragonese uses a synthetic preterite.
- Catalan has dense apostrophe-clitic orthography; Aragonese does not.

## Aragonese vs. Occitan

- Both share `nh/ny` palatals and clitics `ne/i` — diagnostics are lexical and article-based.
- Aragonese `o/a/os/as` articles vs. Occitan `lo/la/los/las` or Gascon `eth/era`.
- Occitan `ò` (grave on open o) is routine; Aragonese does not use `ò`.
- Occitan `-cion`; Aragonese `-zión`.

## Aragonese vs. Extremaduran

- Both are Ibero-Romance varieties. Aragonese preserves initial F- (*fer, fillo*); Extremaduran aspirates it to `j/h` (*hazel, hijo*).
- Extremaduran final `-h` for aspirated `-s` (*loh nenoh*); Aragonese keeps `-s/-z`.
- Aragonese articles `o/a/os/as`; Extremaduran keeps Spanish-like `el/la/los/las` (but frequently `l', lu, la`).
- Extremaduran raises unstressed finals to `-u/-i` (*muchu, genti*); Aragonese keeps `-o -e`.
