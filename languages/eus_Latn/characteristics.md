# Basque Language Identification Characteristics

## Special Characters and Orthography

Basque uses the standard Latin alphabet with a few notable conventions. The letter **ñ** appears occasionally, primarily in loanwords and in certain dialectal forms, though the standard orthography favors the digraph **in** for the palatal nasal in many contexts. The most diagnostic orthographic features of Basque are its distinctive consonant digraphs:

- **tx** /t͡ʃ/: postalveolar affricate (e.g., **txakurra** "the dog," **etxea** "the house")
- **ts** /t͡s̺/: apical alveolar affricate (e.g., **matsa** "grape," **hartsa** "bear")
- **tz** /t͡s̻/: laminal alveolar affricate (e.g., **hitza** "the word," **ahotza** "the voice")

These three digraphs are highly characteristic of Basque and rare or absent in neighboring Romance languages. Their presence in a text is a strong diagnostic signal.

Other orthographic conventions include **dd** for the palatal stop, **ll** for the palatal lateral, **tt** for the palatal stop (in some dialects written this way), and **x** for /ʃ/. The letter **h** is written in many words but is only pronounced in northern dialects.

## Character Frequency

Basque text exhibits a distinctive character frequency profile. The most frequent letters in typical Basque text are:

**e, a, i, r, n, t, k, o, z, u, s, d, b, l, g, h, x**

Notable frequency characteristics compared to neighboring languages:
- **k** has very high frequency (much higher than in Spanish or French), as it marks the ergative case and appears in many suffixes
- **z** has high frequency due to its role in sibilants and verbal morphology (-tzen, -tze-)
- **x** appears with moderate frequency (much more than in Spanish/French)
- **h** appears moderately (etymological spelling even when silent)
- **w**, **y** are extremely rare, appearing only in loanwords
- **c**, **q**, **v** do not appear in native Basque words

## Common Bigrams and Trigrams

### Frequent Bigrams
**ak, ek, en, ar, an, al, ko, ka, ta, te, tu, re, ra, er, ea, ia, ik, il, et, on, or, in, at, it, da, du, ze, ri, de, az**

### Frequent Trigrams
**ren, eta, are, ari, zio, tze, eko, ean, era, eko, atu, ite, ala, ika, iku, hor, ain, ire, rak, ore, eko, tik, dut, ene, iza, ari**

These bigrams and trigrams reflect the agglutinative morphology of Basque: frequent case suffixes (-ak, -ek, -en, -ko, -an, -ra, -tik), verbal morphology (-tze-, -ren), and common words (eta, da, du).

## Morphological Patterns for Language Identification

The following suffixes and morphological patterns are highly diagnostic:

### Nominal Morphology
- **-ak**: absolutive plural / ergative singular (extremely frequent)
- **-ek**: ergative plural
- **-aren**: genitive singular
- **-ari**: dative singular
- **-arekin**: comitative singular ("with")
- **-tik**: ablative ("from")
- **-ra**: adlative ("to")
- **-an**: inessive ("in")
- **-ko**: local genitive ("of, from" as adjective)
- **-etan**: inessive plural ("in [plural]")
- **-etara**: adlative plural ("to [plural]")
- **-etatik**: ablative plural ("from [plural]")

### Verbal Morphology
- **-tzen / -tze**: verbal noun, imperfective aspect (e.g., **irakurtzen** "reading," **ikustea** "seeing")
- **-tu / -du**: past participle endings (e.g., **ikasi** "learned," **erabili** "used," **erosi** "bought," **landu** "worked/cultivated")
- **-tuko / -ko**: future marker (e.g., **etorriko** "will come," **egingo** "will do")
- **-tzeko**: purposive ("in order to")
- **-tzean**: temporal ("upon, when")
- **-tzeagatik**: causal ("because of [doing]")

### Highly Diagnostic Consonant Clusters

The digraphs **tx**, **ts**, and **tz** are among the most reliable orthographic markers for Basque identification:

- **tx**: appears in core vocabulary like **etxe** (house), **txakur** (dog), **txiki** (small), **txoko** (corner), **txalupa** (boat)
- **ts**: found in words like **matsa** (grape), **hartsa** (bear), **itsaso** (sea), **utsune** (gap)
- **tz**: common in **hitz** (word), **ahotz** (voice), **hertz**, **gatz** (salt), **motz** (short)

These clusters virtually never appear in Spanish, French, Catalan, or other neighboring languages in the same positions and combinations, making them extremely reliable diagnostic features. A text containing multiple instances of tx, ts, or tz in word-internal or word-final position is almost certainly Basque.

## Additional Diagnostic Signals

- High frequency of **k** at word endings (ergative marker)
- The combination **-eko**, **-etako** as adjectival suffixes
- Verb-final clause structure reflected in text patterns
- Postpositional phrases (noun + case suffix rather than preposition + noun)
- Complete absence of grammatical gender markers
- The conjunctions **eta**, **baina**, **edo** appearing with high frequency
