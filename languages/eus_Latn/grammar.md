# Basque Grammar

## Word Order

Basque is a consistently head-final, SOV (Subject-Object-Verb) language. The basic word order places the verb at the end of the clause, with the subject preceding the object. However, Basque also employs a focus position immediately before the finite verb, meaning that the element receiving emphasis or new information is placed directly before the auxiliary or synthetic verb form. This makes word order somewhat flexible in practice, though SOV remains the default unmarked order.

Example:
- **Mikelek liburua erosi du.** (Mikel-ERG book-ABS buy AUX) "Mikel has bought the book."
- **Liburua erosi du Mikelek.** (book-ABS buy AUX Mikel-ERG) "It's Mikel who bought the book." (focus on Mikel)

## Phonology

### Vowels

Basque has a simple five-vowel system: /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, /u/. There are no phonemic vowel length distinctions in standard Basque, and the vowel inventory is identical to that of Spanish. Diphthongs are common, formed by combinations of high and non-high vowels (ai, ei, oi, au, eu).

### Consonants

The consonant system of Basque is notable for its sibilant series. Standard Basque distinguishes between apical and laminal sibilants:

- **Laminal fricative:** /s̻/ written as **z**
- **Apical fricative:** /s̺/ written as **s**
- **Laminal affricate:** /t͡s̻/ written as **tz**
- **Apical affricate:** /t͡s̺/ written as **ts**
- **Postalveolar affricate:** /t͡ʃ/ written as **tx**
- **Postalveolar fricative:** /ʃ/ written as **x**

This rich sibilant system, particularly the digraphs **tx**, **ts**, and **tz**, is highly characteristic of Basque and serves as a strong diagnostic feature for language identification.

Aspiration (/h/) is phonemically significant in the northern (French Basque) dialects, particularly in Lapurdian and Low Navarrese, but is absent in the southern (Spanish Basque) dialects. In Euskara Batua, the letter **h** is written in many words following the etymological northern forms, even though most speakers from the south do not pronounce it.

Other notable consonants include the palatal series: /ɲ/ written as **n** before **i** or as **ñ**, the palatal lateral /ʎ/ written as **ll**, and the trill /r/ contrasting with the tap /ɾ/ (written **rr** vs **r** respectively).

## Noun System

### Ergative-Absolutive Case Alignment

Basque is one of the few European languages with ergative-absolutive case alignment. In this system:

- The **absolutive** case (unmarked, zero suffix) marks the subject of intransitive verbs and the direct object of transitive verbs.
- The **ergative** case (suffix **-k**) marks the subject (agent) of transitive verbs.

This is fundamentally different from the nominative-accusative alignment found in most European languages. For example:

- **Gizona etorri da.** (man-ABS arrived AUX.INTR) "The man arrived." (gizona = absolutive)
- **Gizonak liburua erosi du.** (man-ERG book-ABS bought AUX.TR) "The man bought the book." (gizonak = ergative; liburua = absolutive)

### Case System

Basque possesses an extensive case system with at least 14 distinct cases:

| Case | Singular (definite) | Plural | Function |
|------|-------------------|--------|----------|
| Absolutive | -a | -ak | Subject of intransitive, object of transitive |
| Ergative | -ak | -ek | Subject of transitive |
| Dative | -ari | -ei | Indirect object |
| Genitive (possessive) | -aren | -en | Possession |
| Comitative | -arekin | -ekin | "with" |
| Instrumental | -az | -ez | "by means of" |
| Inessive | -an | -etan | "in" (location) |
| Adlative | -ra | -etara | "to" (direction) |
| Ablative | -tik | -etatik | "from" |
| Local genitive | -ko | -etako | "of/from" (adjectival) |
| Partitive | -rik | -rik | Partitive (negation, questions) |
| Destinative | -rentzat | -entzat | "for" |
| Motivative | -rengatik | -engatik | "because of" |
| Prolative | -tzat | -tzat | "as, for" |

Cases are suffixed to the last element of the noun phrase, not to each word individually. Definiteness is marked by the article suffix **-a** (singular) or **-ak** (plural), which fuses with case suffixes. There is no grammatical gender in Basque; neither nouns, adjectives, nor articles distinguish masculine from feminine.

### Determiners and Demonstratives

The demonstrative system distinguishes three degrees of distance:
- **hau** (this, proximal)
- **hori** (that, medial)
- **hura** (that, distal)

These decline for all cases, and their plurals are **hauek**, **horiek**, **haiek**.

## Verb System

### Synthetic vs. Periphrastic Conjugation

The Basque verb system is divided into two major types of conjugation:

**Synthetic (trinko) forms:** Only a small handful of verbs (roughly a dozen in modern usage) retain fully conjugated synthetic forms. The most common are:

- **izan** (to be, intransitive auxiliary)
- **ukan / *edun** (to have, transitive auxiliary)
- **joan** (to go)
- **etorri** (to come)
- **egon** (to stay, to be located)
- **ibili** (to walk)
- **jakin** (to know)
- **eduki** (to hold)
- **ekarri** (to bring)
- **eraman** (to carry)

**Periphrastic (adizkera perifrastikoa) forms:** The vast majority of Basque verbs are conjugated periphrastically, combining a non-finite form of the main verb (participle, verbal noun, or future/potential stem) with an auxiliary verb. The auxiliary carries all the agreement information:

- **Liburua irakurri dut.** (book-ABS read AUX.1SG.ERG) "I have read the book."
- **Etxera joan naiz.** (house-to go AUX.1SG.ABS) "I have gone home."

### Verb Agreement

One of the most striking features of Basque verbs is their polypersonal agreement. The auxiliary verb agrees simultaneously with up to three arguments:

1. **Absolutive argument** (subject of intransitive, object of transitive)
2. **Ergative argument** (subject of transitive)
3. **Dative argument** (indirect object)

For example, the auxiliary form **diet** encodes: "I (ergative) - them (absolutive, plural) - to him/her (dative)," meaning "I have [given/said/etc.] them to him/her."

This results in a large paradigm of auxiliary forms. The transitive auxiliary alone has dozens of forms depending on the person and number of the ergative and absolutive arguments, and many more when a dative argument is also present.

### Allocutive Forms

Basque possesses a sociolinguistically remarkable feature called the allocutive (hitano). In informal speech, when addressing someone familiar using the second-person singular pronoun **hi**, the verb form changes to reflect the gender of the addressee, even when that person is not a grammatical argument of the verb. This is the only context in which gender appears in Basque grammar, and it distinguishes between male (**-k** forms) and female (**-n** forms) addressees:

- **Liburu bat erosi diat.** (I bought a book - speaking to a male friend)
- **Liburu bat erosi dinat.** (I bought a book - speaking to a female friend)

## Syntax

### Head-Final Structure

As a consistently head-final language, Basque places modifiers before their heads:

- **Adjectives precede or follow the noun** (postnominal in standard usage with definite article on adjective): **etxe handia** (house big-the, "the big house")
- **Relative clauses precede the noun** they modify: **Mikelek erosi duen liburua** (Mikel-ERG buy AUX-REL book-ABS, "the book that Mikel bought")
- **Postpositions** are used rather than prepositions: **etxearen aurrean** (house-GEN front-in, "in front of the house")
- **Subordinate clauses** generally precede the main clause

### Question Formation

Questions in Basque are formed by placing the questioned element in the focus position (immediately before the verb) and optionally adding question particles:

- **al**: general question particle, placed after the verb: **Etorriko zara al?** ("Will you come?")
- **ote**: expresses doubt or uncertainty: **Etorriko ote da?** ("I wonder if he/she will come?")

Content questions use interrogative words such as **zer** (what), **nor** (who), **non** (where), **nola** (how), **zergatik** (why), **noiz** (when), and **zein** (which), placed in the focus position before the verb.

### Subordination

Basque uses a variety of suffixes and particles for subordination:
- **-(e)la**: complementizer for declarative clauses ("that")
- **-(e)n**: relative clause marker; also used for indirect questions
- **-(e)lako**: causal ("because")
- **ba-**: conditional prefix ("if")
- **-tzen**: imperfective/habitual verbal noun
- **-tzeko**: purposive ("in order to")
- **-tzean**: temporal ("when, upon")
