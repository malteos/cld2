# English (eng)

## Summary

English (endonym: *English*) is an Indo-European language of the West Germanic branch (Indo-European → Germanic → West → Anglo-Frisian → Anglic). ISO codes: 639-1 `en`, 639-2 `eng`, 639-3 `eng`. It is written in the Latin script. With roughly 380 million native speakers and over 1.1 billion additional L2 speakers, it is the most widely used language globally. Principal first-language regions include the United Kingdom, Ireland, the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa, and English serves as an official or working language in dozens of further countries.

## Writing system

English uses a 26-letter Latin alphabet with case distinctions (A–Z / a–z) and almost no native diacritics. Loanwords may retain marks such as *café*, *naïve*, *résumé*, *façade*, but these are optional in most registers. The apostrophe is used for contractions (*don't*, *it's*) and the Saxon genitive (*John's*, *dogs'*). Sentence-initial and proper-noun capitalisation apply; the first-person pronoun *I* is always capitalised. Punctuation follows standard Western conventions (`. , ; : ? !`) with straight or curly quotes.

## History

English evolved from Old English (Anglo-Saxon) through Middle English and Early Modern English; heavy lexical borrowing from Old Norse, Norman French, Latin, and Greek has produced a famously mixed vocabulary, and its orthography preserves many historical spellings.
