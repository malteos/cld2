# English characteristics (LID signals)

## Script signals

Plain 26-letter Latin with no productive diacritics and no `ß`, `ij`, `å`, `ø`, `ñ`, `ç`, `ä/ö/ü`. Apostrophes in contractions (*'s*, *n't*, *'re*, *'ve*, *'ll*, *'d*) are a strong English marker. Text with no accented characters, with frequent apostrophes, and with many short function words is almost certainly English.

## Top function words

*the, of, and, to, a, in, is, it, you, that, he, was, for, on, are, as, with, his, they, I, at, be, this, have, from, or, one, had, by, but, not, what, all, we, when, your, can, said, there, an, each, which, she, do, how, their, if, will, about, out*. The single-letter words *a* and *I*, and the bigram *to the*, are very high-precision English cues.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- Suffixes: `-tion`, `-sion`, `-ing`, `-ed`, `-ly`, `-ment`, `-ness`, `-ity`, `-ance/-ence`, `-able/-ible`, `-ful`, `-less`, `-er/-or`.
- Digraphs: `th` (extremely frequent word-initially in *the, this, that, they, there*), `sh`, `ch`, `wh`, `gh` (often silent, as in *night*, *through*), `qu`, `ough`.
- Double consonants medially (*letter*, *running*).
- Prefixes: `un-`, `re-`, `pre-`, `dis-`, `mis-`, `over-`, `under-`.
- No `ij`, no initial `sch-` clusters (unlike German/Dutch), no word-final `-keit`, `-ung`, `-tje`, `-heid`.

## Morphology hooks

Auxiliary + bare infinitive sequences (*do not know*, *did say*, *has gone*, *will take*). Possessive clitic `'s` attaches to any noun phrase. The progressive *be + V-ing* construction is near-unique among the Germanic languages in this set and a very strong positive cue.

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

Standard Latin punctuation, straight double quotes `"..."` or curly `"..."`, Oxford/serial comma optional. No inverted question or exclamation marks. Titles and headings often use title-case capitalisation.
