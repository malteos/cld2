The British Isles lie off the northwestern coast of continental Europe, separated from France by the narrow waters of the English Channel and from Scandinavia by the North Sea. The largest island, Great Britain, encompasses the nations of England, Scotland, and Wales, while the second largest, Ireland, is divided between the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland, which is part of the United Kingdom. The landscape of these islands is remarkably varied for their modest size, ranging from the flat fens of East Anglia and the rolling chalk downs of southern England to the rugged highlands of Scotland and the dramatic coastal cliffs of western Ireland.

The geography of the English-speaking world extends far beyond the British Isles. The United States spans an entire continent, from the rocky shores of New England to the sun-drenched beaches of California, with the vast Great Plains, the towering Rocky Mountains, the arid deserts of the Southwest, and the subtropical wetlands of Florida in between. Canada, the second largest country in the world by area, stretches from the fertile prairies of the interior to the frozen tundra of the Arctic, with dense boreal forests covering much of its northern expanse. Australia presents yet another geographic profile, with its enormous interior desert surrounded by a narrow coastal fringe of more temperate and tropical landscapes where the vast majority of the population lives.

New Zealand, situated in the southwestern Pacific Ocean, offers extraordinary geographic diversity within a relatively small area. The country's two main islands feature snow-capped mountains, active volcanoes, ancient glaciers, subtropical forests, and a stunning coastline of beaches, fjords, and harbours. The geographic range of English-speaking territories also includes tropical islands in the Caribbean, the volcanic landscapes of Hawaii, and the coral atolls of the Pacific, reflecting the global reach of the language and the diverse environments in which English-speaking communities have established themselves over the centuries.
