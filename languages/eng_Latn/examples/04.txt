Every morning, Sarah wakes up at half past six, brews a pot of strong coffee, and spends a few quiet minutes reading the news on her phone before the rest of the household begins to stir. Her two children, aged eight and eleven, need to be dressed, fed, and out the door by quarter past eight to catch the school bus, which means that mornings are always a carefully orchestrated race against the clock. Breakfast is usually something simple -- cereal with milk, toast with butter and jam, or occasionally scrambled eggs if there is enough time.

After dropping the children off, Sarah drives to the office where she works as an account manager for a mid-sized marketing firm. Her commute takes roughly twenty-five minutes depending on traffic, and she often uses the time to listen to podcasts about business strategy or current affairs. The workday is filled with meetings, email correspondence, client calls, and the endless task of keeping projects on track and within budget. She usually eats lunch at her desk, a salad or a sandwich brought from home, while catching up on messages she missed during the morning rush.

The evenings are devoted to family life. Dinner is prepared and eaten together around the kitchen table, where each person shares something about their day. After the dishes are washed and homework is completed, the family might watch a programme on television, play a board game, or simply read in the living room. The children are in bed by half past eight, and Sarah and her husband spend the remaining hours of the evening talking, planning the week ahead, or enjoying the peaceful quiet that comes only after the children have finally fallen asleep. Weekends bring their own rhythm of grocery shopping, laundry, sports practices, birthday parties, and the occasional family outing to a park, museum, or cinema.
