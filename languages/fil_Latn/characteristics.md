# Filipino LID characteristics

## Script signals

28-letter Latin extended alphabet. Presence of `f`, `j`, `v`, `z`, `c`,
`ñ` in loanwords and names is more acceptable in Filipino than in pure
Tagalog (`abakada`-era texts avoid them). `ñ` appears in Spanish names
(`Señor`, `Niño`, `Año`). No vowel diacritics in normal prose.

## Top function words

`ang`, `ng`, `sa`, `mga`, `na`, `ay`, `at`, `o`, `pa`, `rin`/`din`,
`lang`/`lamang`, `ba`, `daw`/`raw`, `po`, `opo`, `kasi`, `kaya`, `para`,
`dahil`, `kung`, `pero`, `hindi`, `huwag`, `oo`, `may`, `mayroon`, `wala`,
`ako`, `ikaw`, `siya`, `kami`, `kayo`, `sila`, `tayo`, `ito`, `iyan`,
`iyon`, `dito`, `diyan`, `doon`.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- `ng` as a standalone word (the genitive particle) is extremely frequent
  and is a near-perfect Tagalog/Filipino signature.
- `mga` (plural marker, pronounced *manga*) is similarly distinctive.
- Infix `-um-`, `-in-` inside verbs: `bumili`, `binili`, `kinain`,
  `sumulat`.
- CV reduplication for imperfective/contemplated aspect: `bumibili`,
  `bibili`, `kumakain`.
- Linker `na`/`-ng` joining noun phrases: `magandang umaga`,
  `mabait na bata`.
- Demonstrative triad `ito`/`iyan`/`iyon` and locative
  `dito`/`diyan`/`doon`.

## Morphology hooks

Voice affixes: `mag-`, `-um-`, `-in`, `-an`, `i-`, `ipang-`. Aspect via
reduplication + `-in-`. Enclitic chain after verbs: `…na ba lang rin
talaga?` — the presence of `lang`, `na`, `ba`, `po`, `rin`/`din` in
short clitic strings is very Filipino.

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

Standard ASCII. Honorific `po` / `opo` at clause end is very common in
polite registers. Peso symbol `₱` or `Php` precedes amounts.
