# Filipino (fil)

## Summary

Filipino (endonym: *Filipino* or *Wikang Filipino*) is the standardised
national language of the Republic of the Philippines, in practice based
on Tagalog with increased borrowing from English, Spanish, and other
Philippine languages. Austronesian → Malayo-Polynesian → Philippine →
Central Philippine → Tagalog. ISO codes: 639-1 none, 639-2 `fil`,
639-3 `fil`. Native Tagalog speakers number 25–30 million, with Filipino
understood by the large majority of the country's 110 million people
as a second language.

## Writing system

Filipino uses the 28-letter *Makabagong Alpabetong Filipino* introduced in
1987, which extends the older 20-letter *Abakada* by adding `c, f, j, ñ,
q, v, x, z` to accommodate loanwords and indigenous names. Tagalog/Abakada
never had these; their presence in a Filipino text strongly suggests the
modern Filipino standard rather than pure Tagalog. No diacritics are
written in mainstream modern text, though older literature marks stress
with acute/grave/circumflex over vowels.

## History and status

Declared the national language in the 1987 Constitution as a successor
to "Pilipino" (which was itself a renaming of the Tagalog-based
national language). Filipino is taught nationwide and is the medium of
domestic broadcast, film, and government alongside English (the other
official language).
