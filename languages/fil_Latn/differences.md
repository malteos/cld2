# Filipino vs confusable languages

Filipino and Tagalog are essentially the same language from a LID
perspective; many corpora use the tags interchangeably. Filipino is the
officially standardised national variety, Tagalog (`tgl`) is the
underlying ethnic language.

## vs Tagalog (tgl)

- **Alphabet reach**: Filipino tolerates `f, j, v, z, c, ñ` in loanwords
  and proper names; pure Tagalog/Abakada avoids them (nativises to `p,
  b, s, k, ny`). Text heavy in untransliterated English/Spanish loanwords
  (`cellphone`, `juice`, `vlog`, `zero`, `Niño`) is more likely to be
  labelled Filipino than Tagalog.
- **Register**: Filipino is the urban, Manila-centric national standard;
  Tagalog is often tagged for regional or older literary text.
- **Grammar and function words are essentially identical**: `ang`, `ng`,
  `sa`, `mga`, `na`, `ay`, `hindi`, the focus-voice verb system — no
  diagnostic difference.
- **Demonstratives**: colloquial Filipino often uses `nyan` (< `iyan`)
  and `nito` in spoken/chat text; written Tagalog literature prefers
  full `iyan`, `ito`.
- **Practical advice**: for short web snippets, `fil` and `tgl` are
  essentially indistinguishable. Pick by corpus convention or default to
  `fil` for modern web and `tgl` for pre-1987 or linguistic materials.

## vs other Philippine languages

Against Bikol (`bcl`/`bik`), Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon: the particle
set `ang`/`ng`/`sa`/`mga` plus pronouns `ako`/`siya`/`kami`/`kayo` is
decisive. Bikol uses `an`/`kan`/`sa`/`sa mga` instead of `ang`/`ng`.
