# Oromo (orm, macro-language)

## Summary

Oromo (endonym: **Afaan Oromoo**) is a Cushitic language of the Afro-Asiatic family (Afro-Asiatic → Cushitic → East Cushitic → Oromoid). ISO codes: **om** (639-1), **orm** (639-2/T), and **orm** as a 639-3 **macrolanguage** that covers several individual codes: **gaz** West Central Oromo, **hae** Eastern Oromo, **gax** Borana-Arsi-Guji Oromo, and **orc** Orma. Total Oromo speakers are about **35–40 million**, making it one of the largest languages in the Horn of Africa. Oromo is spoken by the Oromo people across **Ethiopia** (where it is the most widely spoken single language), northern **Kenya**, and parts of **Somalia**.

## Writing system

Oromo is written in **Qubee**, a Latin-based alphabet adopted officially in 1991. Qubee uses only ASCII letters plus the apostrophe (glottal stop **'**). Key digraphs are **ch** (/tʃ/), **dh** (/ɗ/), **ny** (/ɲ/), **ph** (/pʼ/), **sh** (/ʃ/); the letter **x** represents the ejective /tʼ/ and **q** the ejective /kʼ/. Long vowels and geminate consonants are written by doubling (*afaan*, *hedduu*, *bitootessa*). Tone is not marked.

## History and status

Before 1991 Oromo was written sporadically in the Ge'ez (Ethiopic) script. Qubee is now the standard orthography and the language is a working language of Ethiopia's federal government and the official language of the Oromia Regional State. The label **orm** in datasets typically denotes general/standardised Oromo text, which in practice is West Central Oromo (gaz).
