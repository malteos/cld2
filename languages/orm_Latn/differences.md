# Oromo (macro) vs. confusable languages

## orm vs. gaz (macro vs. West Central)

- **orm** is the 639-3 macrolanguage; **gaz** is its largest member (West Central Oromo). Standard written Oromo is gaz, so `orm` and `gaz` text look essentially identical.
- Other Oromo varieties under the macro (Eastern **hae**, Borana **gax**, Orma **orc**) are primarily oral; written material on the web is rare and usually labelled at the macro level.
- If a dataset offers both **orm** and **gaz**, treat gaz as the "specific" tag and orm as the "macro / variety-unknown" tag. Diagnostic features between the two are lexical (place and clan names) rather than structural.

## Oromo vs. Somali (som)

- Both Cushitic SOV with doubled vowels, but Somali uses **c** for a consonant (pharyngeal /ʕ/) and **x** for /ħ/; Oromo does not use **c** except in the digraph **ch**, and uses **x** for ejective /tʼ/.
- Somali has **dh** as a retroflex, Oromo **dh** as an implosive — the surface is the same but Somali co-occurs with **c** and **x** consonants, Oromo with **ny** and ejective **q**.
- Oromo preverbal **hin-** negation and **ykn** for "or" have no Somali equivalent.

## Oromo vs. Amharic (amh) / Tigrinya (tir)

- Amharic and Tigrinya use Ethiopic script. If Ethiopic appears, it is not Oromo.
- Romanised Amharic lacks the doubled-vowel, geminate-heavy Qubee look.
