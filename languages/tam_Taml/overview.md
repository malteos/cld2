# Tamil (tam)

## Summary

Tamil (endonym: **தமிழ்** *Tamiḻ*) is a Dravidian language of the Southern branch, spoken natively by roughly 75–85 million people. It is an official language of India (state language of Tamil Nadu and Puducherry), Sri Lanka, and Singapore, and has large diaspora populations in Malaysia, Mauritius, Fiji, South Africa, Canada, and the Gulf states. ISO codes: **639-1 `ta`**, **639-2/639-3 `tam`**. Script: Tamil (ISO 15924 `Taml`).

## Writing system

Tamil is written in the Tamil script, a Brahmic abugida with no case distinction. Consonants carry an inherent /a/ that is suppressed by the virama **் (puḷḷi)**, a dot above the consonant; vowel signs attach on the left, right, above, or around the base glyph. The script is famously minimalist among Dravidian scripts: it has only a single series of stops (no aspirated/voiced letters) and encodes voicing allophonically by position. Distinctive letters include **ழ** (retroflex approximant /ɻ/, unique to Tamil among living scripts), **ள** (retroflex lateral), **ற** (alveolar trill/tap), and **ண** (retroflex nasal). A separate series of **Grantha** letters (ஜ ஷ ஸ ஹ க்ஷ ஶ) is used for Sanskrit and English loanwords.

## History and status

Tamil has a continuous literary tradition spanning more than two millennia (Sangam literature dates to c. 300 BCE–300 CE) and is one of India's classical languages. Modern written Tamil preserves a strong diglossia between literary (*centamiḻ*) and colloquial (*koṭuntamiḻ*) registers.
