# Tatar vs confusable languages

Closest confusables (Cyrillic Turkic): Bashkir (bak), Kazakh (kaz),
Crimean Tatar Cyrillic (crh_Cyrl), and Russian (rus) for
off-the-shelf distinction.

## tat ↔ bak (Bashkir)

- **`ҙ ҫ ҡ ғ` are Bashkir-specific**; Tatar does not use them.
- **`җ` is Tatar-specific**; Bashkir has no `җ` — where Tatar has
  `җ` Bashkir typically has `й` (*Tatar* **җ**ир ↔ *Bashkir* **й**ер
  "earth"; *Tatar* **җ**итте ↔ *Bashkir* **й**етте).
- Tatar *с з* vs Bashkir `ҫ ҙ` in many cognates.
- Tatar *к г* vs Bashkir `ҡ ғ` in many cognates.
- Otherwise the two alphabets look nearly identical, so `җ` vs
  `ҙ/ҫ/ҡ/ғ` is the crucial diagnostic.

## tat ↔ kaz (Kazakh)

- Kazakh uses `ә ө ү ң` but also `ғ қ ұ і һ`; Tatar uses `ә ө ү ң
  җ һ` but has no `ғ қ ұ і`.
- Tatar `җ` is absent in Kazakh (Kazakh uses `ж` for the same
  sound).

## tat ↔ rus (Russian)

- Any occurrence of `ә ө ү җ ң һ` in a Cyrillic text rules out
  Russian.
- Tatar agglutinative suffix chains (`-ларыбыздан`,
  `-леккә кадәр`) look unlike Russian morphology.

## tat ↔ crh (Crimean Tatar Cyrillic)

- Crimean Tatar Cyrillic uses `къ гъ нъ дж` digraphs rather than
  single extras; absence of `җ ә ө ү` or presence of `къ` / `нъ` /
  `гъ` flags Crimean Tatar.
