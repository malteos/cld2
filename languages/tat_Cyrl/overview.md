# Tatar (tat)

## Summary

Tatar (endonym: *татар теле*, *татарча*) is a Turkic language of
the Kipchak branch (Turkic → Common Turkic → Kipchak → Kipchak-Bulgar
/ Volga-Kipchak). ISO codes: 639-1 `tt`, 639-2/639-3 `tat`. Native
speakers number approximately 4.3–5.2 million, concentrated in the
Republic of Tatarstan (Russia) and surrounding Volga region, with
significant diaspora in Bashkortostan, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and
Turkey. In CLD2/CommonLID this folder covers the Cyrillic orthography
(`tat_Cyrl`), which is the dominant script in use today.

## Writing system

Tatar Cyrillic uses all 33 standard Russian Cyrillic letters plus
six additional characters: **`ә ө ү җ ң һ`**. These extras mark
front low `ä`, front round-mid `ö`, front high round `ü`, voiced
palatal affricate `җ`, velar nasal `ң`, and voiceless glottal
fricative `һ`. Tatar was written in Perso-Arabic until 1928, then
Latin (*Yañalif*, 1928–1939), then Cyrillic (1939–present). A
parallel Latin standard (*Zamanälif*) was defined in 1999 and the
Uniform Turkic Alphabet in 2012 but has limited official use.

## History and status

Tatar is the co-official language of the Republic of Tatarstan
alongside Russian. It is one of the largest minority languages of
the Russian Federation.
