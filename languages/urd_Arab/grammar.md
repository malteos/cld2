# Urdu grammar

- **Word order**: SOV with quite free ordering of arguments; adjectives and demonstratives precede the noun; postpositions (not prepositions) mark case and role.
- **Morphology**: Mildly fusional / partly agglutinative. Core relationships are shown by postpositions **نے، کو، سے، میں، پر، کا/کی/کے، تک**. Nouns have two genders (masculine, feminine) and two numbers.
- **Nouns**: Genitive is inflected: **کا** (m.sg) / **کی** (f.) / **کے** (m.pl/oblique). Oblique-case stems differ from direct (e.g. *laṛkā* → *laṛkē*). No grammatical articles; definiteness is pragmatic.
- **Pronouns**: میں (I), تم / آپ (you, fam/pol), وہ (he/she/it/they, distal), یہ (this / proximal), ہم (we). Honorifics use plural agreement.
- **Verbs**: Agree with the gender-and-number of the nominative subject (or of the direct object in ergative past-tense constructions with **نے**). Copula **ہے / ہیں / تھا / تھی**. Progressive is formed with **رہا / رہی / رہے ہیں**; perfective uses the stem plus gender/number endings.
- **Phonology**: 10 vowel qualities (3 long/short pairs plus /ɛ ɔ/), distinctive aspiration (voiced and voiceless), retroflex series /ʈ ɖ ɽ/ written **ٹ ڈ ڑ**. Nasalisation is contrastive and marked with **ں** word-finally or **٘** elsewhere.
- **Syntax**: Ergative alignment in the perfective (agent marked by **نے**). Pro-drop of subjects in conversational register; copula may be dropped colloquially but is present in writing. SOV relative-correlative constructions (*jo…voh…*) are common.
