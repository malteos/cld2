# Northern Sotho / Sepedi (nso)

## Summary

Northern Sotho (endonym: **Sesotho sa Leboa** or **Sepedi**) is a Bantu
language of the Sotho-Tswana group (Niger-Congo → Atlantic-Congo →
Benue-Congo → Southern Bantu → Sotho-Tswana). ISO codes: 639-1 `nso`
(used), 639-2 `nso`, 639-3 `nso`. It has roughly 4–5 million first-
language speakers, almost entirely in Limpopo, Gauteng and Mpumalanga
provinces of South Africa. It is one of the eleven official languages
of South Africa.

## Writing system

Latin script, bicameral. The defining diacritic is **`š`** (s with
háček), the voiceless postalveolar fricative /ʃ/, used in native
vocabulary (`šupa`, `leše`, `gošo`). Otherwise plain Latin. Common
digraphs: `hl`, `tlh`, `kg`, `tš`, `tšh`, `ph`, `th`, `kh`, `sh`,
`ng`, `ny`, `mp`, `nk`. Disjunctive spelling: verb auxiliaries and
concords are written as separate words (`ke be ke dira`, "I was
doing").

## History

Northern Sotho was standardised by the Lutheran Berlin Mission in the
late 19th century on the Pedi (Bapedi) dialect; "Sepedi" and "Sesotho
sa Leboa" are used interchangeably though the latter is the formally
preferred umbrella term. It is closely related to but distinct from
Southern Sotho (`sot`) and Tswana (`tsn`).
