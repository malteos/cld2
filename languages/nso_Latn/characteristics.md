# Characteristics (nso)

## Script signals

The single strongest cue is the letter **`š`** (s with háček), used
in native words (`šupa`, `leša`, `lešaba`, `gore ba šoma`). `š` does
**not** appear in Southern Sotho (`sot`). Also: digraphs `hl`,
`tlh`, `kg`, `tš`, `tšh`, `ng`, `ny`, `mp`, `nk`. No clicks, no
augments. Disjunctive spelling gives a short-word rhythm.

## Top function words

`gore` (that/so that), `ge` (when/if), `efela` / `eupša` (but),
`goba` (or), `ka gobane` (because), `ka ge` (since), `ka baka la`
(because of), `le` (and/with/also), `ka` (with/by), `go` (to/of),
`ga` (at/of), `bjale` (now), `gona` (there/really), `mo` (here/in),
`fao` (there), `fa` (here), `ke` (it is / 1sg), `ga se` (is not),
`se` (this), `seo` / `yoo` / `bao` (that), `eng` (what), `mang`
(who), `neng` (when), `kae` (where), `kamoka` (all), `fela` (only),
`gape` (again/also), `ee` (yes), `aowa` (no).

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- Presence of `š` (and `tš`, `tšh`) in mid-word positions.
- Class prefixes: `mo-`, `ba-`, `me-`, `le-`, `ma-`, `se-`, `di-`,
  `bo-`, `go-`, `n-`.
- Verb concord + TAM written as separate words: `ke a`, `o a`,
  `re tla`, `ba be ba`, `ke ba`, `ga ke`, `ga a`.
- Final `-ile` (perfective), `-a` (indicative), `-e` (subjunctive /
  negative).
- Characteristic digraphs/trigraphs: `hl`, `tlh`, `kg`, `tš`, `tšh`,
  `ng`, `ny`, `kh`, `ph`, `th`.
- `ga` as negative / locative is ubiquitous (`ga ke`, `ga ba`,
  `gae`, `gagwe`).

## Morphology hooks

Productive extensions: applicative `-el-/-ed-`, causative `-iš-`
(note the `š`!), passive `-w-`, reciprocal `-an-`, stative `-eg-`.
Locative suffix `-ng` (`toropong`, "in town").

## Orthography quirks

Disjunctive writing. The háček `š` must be preserved; sometimes
ASCII-only text substitutes `sh`, but canonical nso uses `š`. No
augments, no clicks.
