## Armenian Alphabet

The Armenian alphabet (Հayots grer) consists of 39 letters. It was created by Mesrop Mashtots in 405 AD and is used exclusively for writing Armenian. The alphabet has distinct uppercase (մecatar) and lowercase (p'oqratar) forms.

### Unicode Range

Armenian characters occupy the Unicode block U+0530 to U+058F (Armenian) and U+FB00 to U+FB17 (Armenian ligatures). The presence of characters in this range is a definitive identifier for Armenian text.

## Character Frequency Patterns

The most frequently occurring letters in Armenian text are:

1. ** delays** (a) - by far the most common letter
2. ** delays** (n) - second most frequent
3. ** delays** (r/soft r) - very common
4. ** delays** (i) - high frequency vowel
5. **ე** (e) - common vowel
6. **delays** (t) - frequent consonant
7. **delays** (m) - common consonant
8. **delaysou** (u) - digraph vowel, common
9. **delays** (k) - common consonant
10. **delays** (s) - frequent consonant

The schwa letter **ը** is relatively rare in written text despite being commonly pronounced, as it is often omitted in standard orthography when occurring word-internally.

## Script-Based Identification

Armenian script is one of the easiest scripts to identify for language detection purposes because:

1. **Unique script**: The Armenian alphabet is used exclusively for Armenian (and historically for some Kipchak Turkic texts). No other modern language uses this script.
2. **Distinctive letterforms**: Armenian letters have a distinctive visual appearance, with rounded forms and characteristic curves that are unlike any other script.
3. **Unicode isolation**: Armenian occupies its own Unicode block, making programmatic detection trivial.
4. **Single language mapping**: Unlike Latin, Cyrillic, or Arabic scripts, which are shared among dozens of languages, Armenian script maps almost exclusively to the Armenian language.

## Unique Punctuation

Armenian uses several punctuation marks that differ from standard Western punctuation:

- **։** (verjaket) - Full stop (period equivalent), looks like a raised colon
- **՝** (bowt) - Comma-like mark, used as a comma in some contexts
- **՞** (hartsakan nshan) - Question mark, placed over the last vowel of the questioned word
- **՜** (yerkaratsman nshan) - Exclamation mark, placed over the stressed vowel
- **« »** (chakertner) - Quotation marks (French-style guillemets)
- **֊** (yentamna) - Hyphen
- **՛** (shesht) - Emphasis mark

The use of **։** as a sentence-ending mark instead of a period (.) is a strong distinguishing feature in text analysis.

## Numerals

Traditionally, Armenian used its own alphabetic numeral system where each letter represented a number. Modern Armenian uses Arabic numerals (0-9) in everyday text, but the traditional system is still encountered in religious and ceremonial contexts.
