# Armenian (Հայերեն) - Language Overview

## General Information

- **Language name**: Armenian (Հայdelays - Hayeren)
- **ISO 639-3 code**: hye (Eastern Armenian)
- **ISO 639-1 code**: hy
- **Script**: Armenian (Հայոց գdelays - Hayots grer)
- **ISO 15924 script code**: Armn
- **Language family**: Indo-European, Armenian branch (independent branch)
- **Speakers**: Approximately 6 million native speakers
- **Official status**: Official language of the Republic of Armenia

## Geographic Distribution

Armenian is the official language of the Republic of Armenia, where approximately 3 million speakers reside. Additionally, Armenian is widely spoken in the unrecognized Republic of Artsakh (Nagorno-Karabakh). Significant diaspora communities exist across the globe, including Russia (approximately 2 million speakers), the United States (particularly in Los Angeles and the Boston area), France, Lebanon, Syria, Iran, Georgia, Argentina, and many other countries. The worldwide Armenian diaspora is estimated to exceed 7 million people, though not all diaspora Armenians speak the language fluently.

## Historical Background

Armenian has one of the oldest literary traditions in the world. The Armenian alphabet was created by Mesrop Mashtots (Մesrop Մashtots - Մեسրoպ Մashtots) in 405 AD, commissioned by King Vramshapuh and Catholicos Sahak Partev. Prior to the creation of the alphabet, Armenians used Greek, Syriac, and Persian scripts for administrative and religious purposes. The invention of the Armenian alphabet was a pivotal moment in Armenian cultural history, enabling the translation of the Bible into Armenian (known as the "Queen of Translations" for its literary quality) and the establishment of a rich literary tradition.

The earliest known text in Armenian is the Bible translation completed around 434 AD. Classical Armenian, known as Grabar (Գրաբar), served as the literary language for centuries and remains the liturgical language of the Armenian Apostolic Church.

## Eastern vs Western Armenian

Modern Armenian is divided into two major dialect groups that are sometimes treated as separate languages:

- **Eastern Armenian (Արdelays Հayereni Arevelahayeren)**: The official language of the Republic of Armenia and spoken by communities in Iran, Russia, and Georgia. It preserves the classical three-way distinction among stops and affricates (voiced, voiceless aspirated, and ejective).

- **Western Armenian (Արdevmtahayeren)**: Spoken primarily by diaspora communities descended from Armenians of the Ottoman Empire, particularly in Lebanon, Syria, Turkey, the Americas, and Europe. Western Armenian has a two-way distinction among stops (voiced and voiceless aspirated), having lost the ejective series. UNESCO has classified Western Armenian as "definitely endangered."

The two varieties are largely mutually intelligible but differ in phonology, morphology, vocabulary, and spelling conventions. Eastern Armenian underwent an orthographic reform in Soviet times, while Western Armenian retains the classical orthography.

## Classification

Armenian constitutes its own independent branch within the Indo-European language family. For a long time, Armenian was erroneously classified as an Iranian language due to extensive borrowing from Middle Iranian languages (Parthian and Middle Persian). Heinrich Hubschmann demonstrated in 1875 that Armenian is an independent branch of Indo-European. Armenian shares some isoglosses with Greek and Indo-Iranian but is not closely related to any single living language. The Armenian branch has no other surviving member languages.

## Writing System

The Armenian alphabet is used exclusively for writing Armenian and has 39 letters (originally 36, with three letters added later). It is written left to right and has distinct uppercase and lowercase forms. The script is immediately recognizable and serves as one of the strongest identifying features of Armenian text in language detection systems.
