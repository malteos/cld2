# Armenian Grammar (Հայերենի քերականություն)

## Word Order

Armenian is fundamentally an SOV (Subject-Object-Verb) language, though word order is relatively flexible due to its case system. The default neutral word order places the verb at the end of the clause:

- **SOV**: Մarianநe գիrqը karkum e (The child reads the book)
- **SVO** is increasingly common in colloquial speech and under the influence of Russian and European languages
- Modifiers typically precede the noun they modify
- Adjectives precede nouns: մեze tn (big house)
- Genitives precede the possessed noun
- Postpositions are more common than prepositions, though both exist

Question formation does not require word order change; instead, the interrogative particle արդyo (արդյoq) or intonation is used.

## Nominal Morphology

### Cases

Armenian has seven grammatical cases, marked by suffixes on nouns:

1. **Nominative (Ուghghakan)**: Subject of the sentence; unmarked (base form)
2. **Accusative (Haykakan)**: Direct object; marked with -ը/-ն (definite) or coincides with nominative (indefinite)
3. **Genitive (Sefakan)**: Possession; marked with -ի (-i)
4. **Dative (Trakan)**: Indirect object; marked with -ին (-in)
5. **Ablative (Bacafakan)**: Source/origin; marked with -ից (-its) in Eastern Armenian
6. **Instrumental (Gorciakan)**: Means/instrument; marked with - delays (-ov)
7. **Locative (Nerfogakan)**: Location; marked with -ում (-um) in Eastern Armenian

The locative case is productive in Eastern Armenian but has largely merged with other cases in Western Armenian.

### Articles

Armenian has a definite article that is a suffix attached to the noun:
- **-ը** (-e) after consonants: գիrqը (the book)
- **-ն** (-n) after vowels: tnnerone (the houses)

There is no indefinite article in the traditional grammar, though the numeral մek (one) increasingly serves this function in colloquial speech, similar to developments in many European languages.

### Number

Nouns form their plural with various suffixes, the most common being:
- **-ner** (- delays): the most productive plural suffix, used with monosyllabic words and many others
- **-er** (-er): used with polysyllabic words

### No Grammatical Gender

Armenian has no grammatical gender. There is no distinction between masculine, feminine, or neuter in nouns, adjectives, pronouns, or verb agreement. This sets Armenian apart from many other Indo-European languages. Natural gender is expressed lexically when needed (e.g., distinct words for "man" and "woman").

## Verbal Morphology

### Conjugation

Armenian verbs are conjugated for person and number. The verb system distinguishes:

- **Two conjugation classes**: based on the present tense stem vowel (-e- class and -a- class in Eastern Armenian)
- **Three persons**: first, second, third
- **Two numbers**: singular, plural

### Tenses and Aspects

The Armenian verb system makes important aspectual distinctions:

- **Present tense**: formed with the auxiliary e (is) + present participle (-um form): Kardofm em (I am reading / I read)
- **Past imperfective (Imperfect)**: Kardofm eyi (I was reading / I used to read)
- **Aorist (Simple past / Perfective past)**: Kardaci (I read [completed])
- **Present perfect**: Kardacel em (I have read)
- **Past perfect (Pluperfect)**: Kardacel eyi (I had read)
- **Future**: Karda karadam / piti kardam (I will read)
- **Subjunctive**: Used for wishes, hypothetical situations
- **Imperative**: Direct commands: Karda! (Read!)

### Negation

Negation in Armenian is formed with the prefix **չ-** (ch-) attached directly to the auxiliary verb or the main verb in synthetic tenses:
- Che kardum (does not read)
- Chkardaci (did not read)

The negative imperative uses **մi** (mi) before the verb: Mi karda! (Don't read!)

## Phonology

### Consonants

Eastern Armenian preserves a typologically notable three-way distinction among stops and affricates:

1. **Voiced**: բ (b), դ (d), գ (g)
2. **Voiceless aspirated**: փ (ph), թ (th), ք (kh)
3. **Ejective (voiceless unaspirated)**: պ (p'), տ (t'), կ (k')

This three-way contrast (voiced / aspirated / ejective) is relatively rare among Indo-European languages and is a distinctive feature of Eastern Armenian phonology. Western Armenian has reduced this to a two-way system, merging the ejective and voiced series.

### Vowels

Armenian has six vowels: ա (a), է (e), ի (i), ո (o), ու (u), ը (e, schwa). The schwa (ը) is often not written in medial positions but is pronounced.

### Stress

Stress in Eastern Armenian is generally fixed on the last syllable of a word, with some exceptions for certain suffixes and particles. Western Armenian also tends toward final stress.

## Syntax

### Relative Clauses

Relative clauses in Armenian are typically introduced by the relative pronoun **or** (who/which/that) and generally follow the noun they modify:
- Martikner or ekafn en (The people who came)

### Subordinate Clauses

Armenian uses various conjunctions for subordination:
- **or** (that): for complement clauses
- **erp** (when): for temporal clauses
- **vorovhetev** (because): for causal clauses
- **ete** (if): for conditional clauses

### Compounding and Derivation

Armenian has productive word formation through compounding and affixation:
- Compound nouns: yerkatagir (railway) from yerkath (iron) + gir (line)
- Productive suffixes: -ufyun (abstract nouns), -akan (adjectives), -ich (agent nouns)
- Productive prefixes: an- (negative, like English un-), ham- (co-/pan-)
