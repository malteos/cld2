# Differences (swa)

## Confusable languages

### swa vs swh (Coastal/Standard Swahili)

`swa` is the ISO macrolanguage; `swh` is the coastal/standardised
member. In CommonLID both codes coexist and are very hard to separate
at the sentence level.

- Surface orthography, noun-class system and core function words
  (`na`, `wa`, `ya`, `kwa`, `kwamba`) are identical.
- `swh` stays close to the Zanzibar (Unguja) norm: slightly more
  Arabic-origin vocabulary (`sababu`, `hakika`, `tafadhali`) and
  conservative copula `ni`.
- `swa` samples include inland / L2 usage: more English loans, looser
  concord, and some Congo Swahili (`swc`) features such as reduced
  agreement and French loans (`motuka`, `makelele`) when they leak into
  the macrocode bucket.

### swa vs other Bantu in the set

- vs **zul/xho**: no click letters, no `hl/dl`; Nguni languages have
  both.
- vs **sna**: less density of prenasalised `nd/mb/nh/nz`; uses
  `na/kwa` where Shona uses `uye/asi`.
- vs **nso/sot**: no `š`, no `hl`; uses `na` rather than `le`.
- vs **lin**: no French loans (`mpe`, `likolo`); lacks Lingala tone
  diacritics `ó é á`.
- vs **lug/nyn**: no geminate consonants and different class-prefix
  shapes (`wa-/m-` vs Luganda `ba-/mu-`).
