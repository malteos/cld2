# Characteristics (swa)

## Script signals

Pure ASCII Latin, no diacritics, no click letters. `c`, `q`, `x` are
rare in native roots. The apostrophe in `ng'` (velar nasal) is a strong
Swahili cue. Absence of `š`, `ê`, `ô`, tildes `ĩ/ũ`, and double-letter
geminates rules out most other Bantu languages in the CommonLID set.

## Top function words

`na` (and/with/by), `wa` (of — cl. 2), `ya` (of — cl. 9), `la` (of —
cl. 5), `kwa` (to/by/at), `katika` (in), `kwamba` (that), `kuwa` (to be
/ that), `lakini` (but), `au` (or), `pia` (also), `tu` (only/we), `sana`
(very), `hivyo` / `hivi` (thus/these), `huyo` / `huyu` (that/this
person), `hii` / `hiyo` (this/that), `nini` (what), `nani` (who), `lini`
(when), `wapi` (where), `kwa sababu` (because), `ambayo` / `ambao`
(which/who), `yote` / `wote` (all), `tena` (again), `basi` (so/then),
`ndiyo` (yes), `hapana` (no).

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- Word-initial `m-`, `mw-`, `wa-`, `ki-`, `vi-`, `mi-`, `ma-`, `u-`,
  `ku-` abound due to noun-class prefixes.
- Verb-initial `ni-`, `u-`, `a-`, `tu-`, `m-`, `wa-` + TAM `-na-`,
  `-li-`, `-ta-`, `-me-` (`anasema`, `tulikuwa`, `watakwenda`).
- Most verbs end in `-a`; Arabic-origin verbs end in `-i` / `-e`
  (`rudi`, `samehe`, `jaribu`).
- Frequent digraphs: `ch`, `sh`, `ny`, `ng`, `ng'`, `mb`, `nd`, `nj`.

## Morphology hooks

Applicative `-i-/-e-`, causative `-sh-/-z-`, passive `-w-`, stative
`-k-/-ik-`, reciprocal `-an-`. Locative suffix `-ni` (`nyumbani`,
`shuleni`). Object infix sits between TAM and root.

## Orthography quirks

No diacritics. Arabic loans are nativised in spelling (`sababu`, not
`sabab`). Foreign proper nouns keep `q`, `x`, `c` when borrowed.
