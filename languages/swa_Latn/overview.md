# Swahili (swa)

## Summary

Swahili (endonym: **Kiswahili**) is a Bantu language of the Niger-Congo
family (Niger-Congo → Atlantic-Congo → Benue-Congo → Bantu → Sabaki).
ISO codes: 639-1 `sw`, 639-2 `swa`, 639-3 `swa` (macrolanguage). The
`swa` code covers the macrolanguage and groups the standardised coastal
variety (`swh`), Congo Swahili (`swc`) and other regional varieties. It
is spoken by roughly 80–150 million people as a first or second language
across East and Central Africa, particularly in Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda,
Rwanda, Burundi and the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo. It is
an official language of Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda and the African Union.

## Writing system

Swahili uses the Latin script with 24 letters; `q` and `x` are almost
absent from native vocabulary. There are no diacritics and no click
letters. Bicameral casing is standard. Digraphs `ch`, `sh`, `th`, `dh`,
`gh`, `kh`, `ng'`, `ny` are frequent; the apostrophe in `ng'`
distinguishes the velar nasal from `ng`.

## History

Swahili grew along the Indian Ocean coast through centuries of Bantu–Arab
contact, leaving a thick Arabic loan stratum (`kitabu`, `dunia`, `sababu`,
`safari`). Standard Swahili was codified in the 1930s on the Zanzibar
(Unguja) dialect; the `swa` macrocode therefore spans considerable
regional variation in vocabulary and concord.
