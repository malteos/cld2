# Moroccan Arabic — Darija (ary)

## Summary

Moroccan Arabic (endonym: **الدارجة المغربية** *ad-dārija l-maġribiyya*,
commonly just *dārija*) is the Maghrebi Arabic variety spoken natively in
Morocco. Family: Afro-Asiatic → Semitic → Central Semitic → Arabic → Maghrebi
Arabic. ISO: **639-3 `ary`** (macrolanguage `ara`). Native speakers ~30–35
million in Morocco plus diaspora in France, Belgium, Spain, the Netherlands,
Italy, and Germany. Intelligibility with eastern Arabic varieties is low
without exposure.

## Writing system

Written in the **Arabic script** (U+0600–U+06FF) with MSA-compatible letters;
some social-media, advertising, and song-lyric writing uses the Latin "Arabizi"
convention with digits 2, 3, 7, 9 for hamza/ʿayn/ḥāʾ/qāf, though only the
Arabic-script form is targeted by the `ary_Arab` label. Moroccan script
sometimes employs extended letters for loan sounds: **ڤ / ڥ** for /v/, **پ**
for /p/, **ڭ / گ** for /g/ (esp. Berber loans, French loans). No casing.

## Status

National vernacular of Morocco and increasingly visible written register
(Facebook, WhatsApp, ad copy, novels such as Youssef Fadel's). Coexists in
diglossia with MSA (school, administration) and French (business, higher
education). Substrate influence from Amazigh (Berber) languages and heavy
borrowing from French and Spanish.
