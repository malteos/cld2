# Spanish Grammar

## Typology

- **Basic word order:** SVO, with flexible VS order for focus, in questions and with unaccusative verbs (*Llegó el tren* "The train arrived").
- **Morphology:** fusional; rich verbal inflection, modest nominal inflection (gender and number only, no case on nouns).
- **Pro-drop:** subject pronouns are usually omitted (*Hablo español* "I speak Spanish"); overt pronouns signal contrast.
- **Alignment:** nominative-accusative; animate/specific direct objects take the preposition *a* ("personal *a*"): *Vi a María*.

## Phonology (brief)

- **Five vowels** /a e i o u/, no phonemic length, no vowel reduction, no nasal vowels (contrast Portuguese and Catalan).
- **Consonants:** tap /ɾ/ vs trill /r/ (*pero* vs *perro*); palatal nasal /ɲ/ written **ñ**; velar fricative /x/ written **j** or **g** before e/i (*jamás*, *gente*).
- **Distinción vs seseo:** north/central Spain distinguishes /θ/ (*z*, *ce/ci*) from /s/; most of the Spanish-speaking world merges them into /s/. **Yeísmo** (merging *ll* and *y*) is now the majority pronunciation.
- **Stress** is phonemic and predictable from spelling: words ending in a vowel, *n* or *s* are stressed on the penultimate syllable, others on the last; exceptions carry an acute accent (*canción*, *árbol*, *teléfono*).
- Diphthongization of Latin stressed short e/o into **ie/ue** is a hallmark (*tiempo*, *tiene*, *puede*, *nuevo*) that Portuguese, Galician and Catalan lack (*tempo*, *pode*, *novo*; Cat. *temps*, *pot*, *nou*).

## Nouns, Articles, Adjectives

- **Gender:** masculine/feminine; typically *-o* masc., *-a* fem., but *-e* and consonant-final nouns vary (*el puente*, *la noche*, *la ciudad*).
- **Plural:** *-s* after vowels, *-es* after consonants (*casa → casas*, *ciudad → ciudades*, *canción → canciones*; *-z → -ces*: *luz → luces*).
- **Definite article:** *el, la, los, las*; neuter *lo* (*lo bueno* "what is good"). Only two obligatory contractions: *a + el = al*, *de + el = del*. (Portuguese/Galician contract almost every preposition with the article.)
- **Indefinite article:** *un, una, unos, unas*.
- Adjectives agree in gender and number and usually follow the noun (*una casa blanca*); some shorten before nouns (*buen*, *gran*, *primer*).

## Pronouns

- Subject: *yo, tú (vos), él, ella, usted, nosotros/-as, vosotros/-as, ellos/-as, ustedes*.
- Clitics: *me, te, lo, la, le, se, nos, os, los, las, les*. Proclitic with finite verbs (*lo veo*, *se lo dije*), enclitic with infinitives, gerunds and affirmative imperatives, written as one word (*verlo*, *diciéndole*, *dímelo*). Spain shows *leísmo* (*le vi* for "I saw him").

## Verbs

Three conjugations: **-ar, -er, -ir** (*hablar, comer, vivir*). Verbs inflect for person/number, tense, aspect and mood.

| | hablar, present | preterite | imperfect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1sg | hablo | hablé | hablaba |
| 2sg | hablas | hablaste | hablabas |
| 3sg | habla | habló | hablaba |
| 1pl | hablamos | hablamos | hablábamos |
| 2pl (Spain) | habláis | hablasteis | hablabais |
| 3pl | hablan | hablaron | hablaban |

- **Preterite vs imperfect** aspect contrast (*habló* vs *hablaba*).
- **Perfect tenses** with *haber* + participle (*he hablado*, *había comido*) — Portuguese and Galician use *ter* instead. *Tener* + participle exists but is resultative, not a general auxiliary.
- **Subjunctive** is fully alive (*quiero que vengas*; past subjunctive in *-ra* and *-se*: *hablara/hablase*). The future subjunctive (*hablare*) survives only in legal/archaic texts, unlike Portuguese.
- **No personal (inflected) infinitive** — a feature of Portuguese and Galician.
- **Ser vs estar:** essential vs stative "be" (*es médico*, *está cansado*). *Hay* is the existential "there is" (Port. *há/tem*, Gal. *hai*, Cat. *hi ha*).
- Progressive: *estar* + gerund in *-ando/-iendo* (*está hablando*).
- Future and conditional synthetic (*hablaré*, *hablaría*); periphrastic future *ir a* + infinitive is very common (*voy a hablar*). Note this is a future, unlike the Catalan *vaig parlar* (past).
- Reflexive/impersonal *se* (*se venden casas*, *se dice que...*).

## Syntax

- Negation: preverbal *no*; negative concord (*No vino nadie*, *No dije nada*).
- Questions and exclamations are framed with inverted marks **¿...?** and **¡...!** — a uniquely Spanish orthographic convention.
- Relative and complementizer *que*; interrogatives carry an accent (*qué, quién, cómo, dónde, cuándo*).
- Clitic doubling is common (*A Juan le di el libro*).

## Short Examples

- *Mañana voy a ver a mi hermana en Sevilla.* "Tomorrow I'm going to see my sister in Seville." (personal *a*, *ir a* future, ñ)
- *¿Ya has comido? — No, todavía no he tenido tiempo.* "Have you eaten yet? — No, I haven't had time yet." (*haber* perfect, inverted ¿, diphthong in *tiempo*)
- *Los niños estaban jugando cuando empezó a llover.* "The children were playing when it started to rain." (imperfect + preterite, *los*, *cuando*)
