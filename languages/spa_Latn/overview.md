# Spanish (spa_Latn)

## Names and Codes

- **English name:** Spanish (also Castilian)
- **Endonyms:** *español*, *castellano*. Both are used; *castellano* is preferred in some Spanish regions with other co-official languages (Catalonia, Galicia, Basque Country) and in parts of South America (e.g. Argentina, Chile), while *español* dominates elsewhere.
- **ISO 639-1:** `es` · **ISO 639-2/3:** `spa` · **Script:** Latin (`Latn`)
- **Glottolog:** `stan1288` (Standard Spanish)

## Classification

Indo-European > Italic > Romance > Western Romance > Ibero-Romance > West Iberian > Castilian. Its closest relatives are Asturian-Leonese and (more distantly) Galician and Portuguese; Catalan belongs to a different branch (Occitano-Romance in many classifications) but shares centuries of contact and much vocabulary with Spanish.

## Speakers and Distribution

Spanish is commonly cited as the world's second language by number of native speakers, after Mandarin. Estimates vary by source and year; figures of roughly 480-500 million native speakers and close to 600 million total speakers (including L2 learners) are typical (Instituto Cervantes yearbooks). Treat these as approximate.

It is official or de facto national language in about 20 sovereign states:

- **Europe:** Spain (co-official with Catalan/Valencian, Galician, Basque and Aranese Occitan in their respective autonomous communities).
- **Americas:** Mexico (largest Spanish-speaking population), Colombia, Argentina, Peru, Venezuela, Chile, Ecuador, Guatemala, Cuba, Bolivia, Dominican Republic, Honduras, Paraguay (with Guaraní), El Salvador, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Panama, Uruguay, plus Puerto Rico (US territory).
- **Africa:** Equatorial Guinea; also used in Ceuta and Melilla and among Sahrawi communities.
- **United States:** tens of millions of home speakers, making the US one of the largest Spanish-speaking countries in absolute terms.

Spanish is an official language of the United Nations, the European Union, the Organization of American States, Mercosur and the African Union, among others.

## History

Spanish arose from Vulgar Latin in the County of Castile (north-central Iberia) around the 9th-10th centuries. Glosses in Latin manuscripts (the *Glosas Emilianenses* and *Silenses*, c. 10th-11th century) are often cited as early written evidence of Ibero-Romance. The language expanded southward with the Reconquista, absorbing a substantial Arabic lexical layer (e.g. *aceite*, *almohada*, *ojalá*, many words in *al-*). Under Alfonso X (13th century) Castilian became a language of prose, law and science.

1492 is a symbolic year: Antonio de Nebrija published his Castilian grammar, the Jewish expulsion created the diaspora of Judaeo-Spanish (Ladino, ISO `lad`), and Columbus's voyage began the expansion to the Americas. The 16th-17th-century *Siglo de Oro* (Cervantes, Lope de Vega) fixed much of the literary standard, while a sibilant merger produced today's /θ/ vs /s/ distinction in central Spain and *seseo* elsewhere.

## Standardization

The **Real Academia Española** (RAE, founded 1713) and the **Asociación de Academias de la Lengua Española** (ASALE, 1951; 23 national academies) jointly publish the normative works: the *Diccionario de la lengua española*, the *Nueva gramática* (2009) and the *Ortografía de la lengua española* (2010). The standard is pluricentric: Peninsular, Mexican, Rioplatense and other norms coexist, differing mainly in pronunciation, second-person address (*vosotros* in Spain vs *ustedes* elsewhere; *voseo* in Argentina, Uruguay, Central America) and lexis, while the written standard is highly unified. The 2010 Ortografía, for example, recommends writing *solo* and demonstratives (*este*, *ese*) without the old diacritic accent, though *sólo* remains common in real text.

## Status

Spanish is a safe, institutionally dominant language with very large online presence. In Spain it coexists with regional languages and frequently appears mixed with them (Catalan, Galician), which matters for language identification.
