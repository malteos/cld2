# Spanish vs. confusable languages

Closest confusables in this set: **Portuguese (por)**, **Catalan (cat)**, **Aragonese (arg)**, **Extremaduran (ext)**. Galician is not in this set but shares many features with Portuguese.

## Spanish vs. Portuguese

- Spanish `ñ` vs. Portuguese `nh` (*España* vs. *Espanha*).
- Spanish `ll` vs. Portuguese `lh` (*brillar* vs. *brilhar*).
- Portuguese has nasal vowels `ã õ` and circumflex `â ê ô`, plus `ç`; Spanish has none of these.
- Portuguese contracts preposition + article (`do/da/dos/das/no/na/pelo/pela`); Spanish only contracts `al/del`.
- Endings: Spanish `-ción -dad -mente`; Portuguese `-ção -dade -mente`.
- Spanish inverted `¿ ¡`; Portuguese never uses them.

## Spanish vs. Catalan

- Catalan digraph `ny` for Spanish `ñ` (*España* → *Espanya*); Catalan `·l` (geminate L) unique.
- Catalan definite articles `el/la/els/les`, `l'` before vowels; Spanish `el/la/los/las`.
- Catalan endings `-ció, -tat, -ment` vs. Spanish `-ción, -dad, -mente`.
- Catalan uses `ç` and `è ò í ú à` — wider diacritic set than Spanish (which only uses acute + `ü` + `ñ`).
- Catalan has weak pronouns `em/et/es/ens/us/els` vs. Spanish `me/te/se/nos/os/les`.

## Spanish vs. Aragonese

- Aragonese preserves Latin initial F (*fer, fillo, foc*) vs. Spanish *hacer, hijo, fuego*.
- Aragonese uses `ll` but writes definite article `o/a/os/as` (not `el/la`).
- Aragonese diphthongs `ua ie` extended (*cuatro, nueu*); Spanish `cuatro, nueve`.
- Aragonese `-ar/-er/-ir` infinitives but often final `-z` plural (*chicoz, mozez*).

## Spanish vs. Extremaduran

- Extremaduran aspirates final `-s` to `-h` in writing (*loh, tréh, muchah*); Spanish keeps `-s`.
- Extremaduran neutralises final `-r/-l` and writes `-l` (*comel, salil* for *comer, salir*).
- Extremaduran uses `-i -u` for Spanish `-e -o` in unstressed finals (*muchu, genti*); Spanish keeps `-o -e`.
