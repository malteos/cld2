# Punjabi (Gurmukhi Script) - Language Overview

## Basic Information

- **Language Name:** Punjabi (ਪੰਜਾਬੀ)
- **ISO 639-3 Code:** pan
- **ISO 639-1 Code:** pa
- **Script:** Gurmukhi (ਗੁਰਮੁਖੀ)
- **Language Family:** Indo-European > Indo-Iranian > Indo-Aryan > Northwestern Zone > Punjabi
- **Estimated Speakers:** ~125 million (native), ~150 million (total including L2)
- **Writing Direction:** Left to right

## Geographic Distribution

Punjabi is the native language of the Punjab region, which spans the border between India and Pakistan. In India, it is primarily spoken in the state of Punjab, as well as in parts of Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Delhi, and Rajasthan. Significant Punjabi-speaking diaspora communities exist in Canada, the United Kingdom, the United States, Australia, and various countries in the Middle East. Canada, in particular, has a large Punjabi-speaking population, making Punjabi one of the most spoken languages in the country after English and French.

## Official Status

Punjabi holds official language status in the Indian state of Punjab, where it serves as the primary language of administration, education, and daily communication. It is one of the 22 scheduled languages recognized by the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution. Punjabi is also recognized as an additional official language in the states of Haryana, Delhi, and Himachal Pradesh. In Pakistan, while Punjabi is the most spoken language by native speakers, it does not have official status at the national level, where Urdu and English serve as official languages. However, it has some recognition at the provincial level in Pakistani Punjab.

## Script

The Gurmukhi script (ਗੁਰਮੁਖੀ, literally "from the mouth of the Guru") is an abugida used to write the Punjabi language in India. It was standardized by Guru Angad Dev Ji, the second Sikh Guru, in the 16th century, building upon earlier Landa scripts used in the region. The script is closely associated with Sikhism and the Sikh religious tradition, as the Guru Granth Sahib, the central religious scripture of Sikhism, is written in Gurmukhi. The script consists of 35 basic consonant characters (ਅੱਖਰ), 10 vowel symbols (ਲਗਾਂ ਮਾਤਰਾਂ), and additional diacritical marks for tonal distinctions and nasal sounds. It is important to note that Punjabi in Pakistan is typically written in the Shahmukhi script, a variant of the Perso-Arabic script, creating a digraphic situation for the language.

## Brief History

Punjabi has a rich literary and cultural history dating back several centuries. The language evolved from the Shauraseni Prakrit and Apabhramsha languages through medieval developments. The earliest Punjabi literature includes the works of Sufi poets such as Baba Farid (1173-1266 CE), whose compositions are included in the Guru Granth Sahib. The Sikh Gurus composed extensively in Punjabi, and the language became closely intertwined with the Sikh religious and cultural identity.

During the Mughal period, Punjabi literature flourished with the works of poets like Shah Hussain, Bulleh Shah, and Waris Shah, whose epic romance "Heer Ranjha" (1766) is considered one of the masterpieces of Punjabi literature. The language continued to develop through the colonial period, when efforts were made to standardize its grammar and create educational materials.

In the post-independence era, Punjabi has undergone significant modernization. The Punjabi Suba movement in the 1950s and 1960s led to the creation of a Punjabi-majority state in India in 1966. Today, Punjabi is a vibrant language with a thriving media ecosystem including newspapers, television channels, a large film industry (Pollywood), and a globally influential music scene. The language continues to evolve in the digital age, with growing online content and technological support for the Gurmukhi script.
