# Amharic (amh_Ethi) - Script and Identification Characteristics

## Ethiopic Script (Fidel)

Amharic is written in the Ethiopic script, known as ፊደል (Fidel), which is an abugida (alphasyllabary) writing system. Unlike a true alphabet where consonants and vowels are separate letters, in the Ethiopic script each character represents a consonant-vowel syllable. The script is derived from the ancient South Arabian script and was originally developed for writing Ge'ez (Classical Ethiopic).

### Structure of the Script

The Ethiopic script is organized into a matrix of consonant rows and vowel orders (columns). Each base consonant character has seven forms (orders), each representing the consonant combined with one of seven vowels:

1. **First order (Ge'ez):** Consonant + /a/ (or schwa /ə/)
2. **Second order (Ka'ib):** Consonant + /u/
3. **Third order (Salis):** Consonant + /i/
4. **Fourth order (Rabi'):** Consonant + /a/
5. **Fifth order (Hamis):** Consonant + /e/
6. **Sixth order (Sadis):** Consonant + (no vowel or /i/)
7. **Seventh order (Sabi'):** Consonant + /o/

For example, the consonant h (ሀ):
- ሀ (ha), ሁ (hu), ሂ (hi), ሃ (ha), ሄ (he), ህ (h), ሆ (ho)

The complete Amharic Fidel contains approximately 230+ distinct characters, including base consonants with their seven orders, labialized (rounded) consonant variants, and additional characters for sounds specific to Amharic.

### Unicode Range

Amharic text uses the following Unicode blocks:
- **Ethiopic:** U+1200 to U+137F (primary block, 384 code points)
- **Ethiopic Supplement:** U+1380 to U+139F
- **Ethiopic Extended:** U+2D80 to U+2DDF
- **Ethiopic Extended-A:** U+AB00 to U+AB2F

The presence of characters in the U+1200-U+137F range is a strong indicator of an Ethiopic-script language (Amharic, Tigrinya, Ge'ez, Tigre, or others).

### Ethiopic Punctuation

Amharic uses distinctive Ethiopic punctuation marks:
- **፡** (U+1361) - Word separator (hulet netib), historically used to separate words; in modern usage, spaces are increasingly preferred
- **።** (U+1362) - Full stop (arat netib), equivalent to a period
- **፣** (U+1363) - Comma (ye-Amharic serez)
- **፤** (U+1364) - Semicolon
- **፥** (U+1365) - Colon
- **፦** (U+1366) - Preface colon (used before speech or quotations)
- **፧** (U+1367) - Question mark
- **፨** (U+1368) - Paragraph separator

Modern Amharic writing frequently mixes Ethiopic and Latin punctuation, using standard Western commas, periods, question marks, and quotation marks alongside or instead of their Ethiopic equivalents.

## Character Frequency Patterns

In Amharic text, certain characters and character groups appear with notably high frequency:

### Most Frequent Characters
The most commonly occurring Ethiopic characters in typical Amharic text include:
- የ (ye) - extremely frequent due to its role as the genitive/relative prefix
- ን (n/in) - frequent as accusative marker and part of common morphemes
- ው (w/uw) - frequent in definite article suffixes and pronouns
- ም (m/im) - common in negation, conjunction, and morphological suffixes
- ት (t/it) - frequent in feminine markers and various morphemes
- ል (l/il) - common in benefactive, purposive, and infinitive markers
- ር (r/ir) - frequent consonant in roots
- ስ (s/is) - common in various grammatical morphemes
- ብ (b/ib) - common in instrumental/locative prefix
- ክ (k/ik) - common in second person markers and various roots
- ለ (le) - high frequency as the dative/benefactive preposition
- በ (be) - high frequency as the instrumental/locative preposition
- ከ (ke) - high frequency as the ablative preposition
- እ (i/'i) - very frequent as initial element in many pronouns and conjunctions

## Common Character Sequences and Morphological Patterns

### Identifying Prefixes
- የ- (ye-): genitive/relative marker, extremely high frequency
- በ- (be-): locative/instrumental, appears at word beginnings
- ለ- (le-): dative/benefactive
- ከ- (ke-): ablative ("from")
- እን- (in-): first person plural prefix on verbs
- ይ- (yi-): third person masculine imperfective prefix
- ት- (ti-): second/third person feminine imperfective prefix
- አ- (a-): causative prefix

### Identifying Suffixes
- -ው (-w/-ew): masculine definite article / 3sg.m object
- -ዋ (-wa): feminine definite article
- -ኦች (-oCH): regular plural
- -ን (-n): accusative marker
- -ም (-m): "also" / negative suffix
- -ኝ (-gn): 1sg object suffix
- -ት (-t): feminine marker / 2sg marker

### Characteristic Bigrams and Trigrams
High-frequency Ethiopic character sequences in Amharic include:
- የ + noun (genitive constructions)
- ነው / ናት / ናቸው (copula forms)
- እና (conjunction "and")
- ውስጥ (postposition "inside")
- ብሎ / ብላ / ብለው (converb of "say")

These patterns, especially the high frequency of የ, the copula ነው and its variants, and the conjunction እና, serve as strong markers distinguishing Amharic text from other Ethiopic-script languages.
