# Amharic (amh)

## Summary

Amharic (endonym: **አማርኛ** *Amarəñña*) is an Ethio-Semitic language and the
working language of the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia. Family:
Afro-Asiatic → Semitic → South Semitic → Ethio-Semitic → South →
Transversal South → Amharic-Argobba → Amharic. ISO codes: **639-1 `am`**,
**639-2/639-3 `amh`**. Native speakers ~32–35 million, L2 speakers ~25
million more, primarily in Ethiopia (Addis Ababa, Amhara, Gondar, Gojjam,
Shewa, Wollo), with diaspora communities in Israel, the USA, and the Gulf.

## Writing system

Uses the **Ethiopic script** (Ge'ez abugida, ፊደል *Fidəl*), Unicode block
U+1200–U+137F (Ethiopic) plus U+1380–U+139F (Supplement), U+2D80–U+2DDF
(Extended), U+AB00–U+AB2F (Extended-A). Abugida: each base consonant has
seven vowel-bearing orders (ä, u, i, a, e, ə, o), produced by systematic
modification of the base shape. Written left-to-right. No casing. Word
separator is the traditional two-dot colon **፡** (U+1361) in older texts;
the ASCII space is now dominant, while **፡** is retained stylistically.

## Status

Official working language of the Ethiopian federal government, lingua franca
of much of urban Ethiopia, and major literary/media language with extensive
publishing, TV, radio, and web presence. It is the only Ethiopic-script
language likely to dominate amh CommonLID samples, but Tigrinya (tir), Ge'ez
(gez), Tigre (tig), and others share the script.
