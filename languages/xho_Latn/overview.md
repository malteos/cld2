# Xhosa (xho)

## Summary

Xhosa (endonym: **isiXhosa**) is a Southern Bantu language of the Nguni
group (Niger-Congo → Atlantic-Congo → Benue-Congo → Southern Bantu →
Nguni). ISO codes: 639-1 `xh`, 639-2 `xho`, 639-3 `xho`. It has roughly
8–9 million first-language speakers, mostly in the Eastern Cape and
Western Cape provinces of South Africa, with L2 use throughout the
country. Xhosa is one of South Africa's eleven official languages.

## Writing system

Latin script, bicameral, no diacritics in native words. Xhosa has
**three click consonant series** written `c` (dental), `q`
(post-alveolar) and `x` (lateral). Each click has plain, aspirated
(`ch`, `qh`, `xh`), nasal (`nc`, `nq`, `nx`), voiced (`gc`, `gq`,
`gx`), slack-voiced (`ngc`, `ngq`, `ngx`) and other variants,
producing a rich inventory of click digraphs and trigraphs. Common
non-click digraphs: `hl`, `dl`, `ny`, `ng`, `mb`, `nd`, `nk`, `mp`,
`tsh`, `tyh`, `bh`, `ph`, `th`, `kh`, `sh`.

## History

Xhosa developed from the southern Nguni dialect continuum and acquired
its extensive click inventory through long contact with Khoe and San
peoples — giving xho more click density than Zulu. The literary
tradition goes back to mid-19th-century mission presses.
