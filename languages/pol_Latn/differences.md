# Polish vs. confusable languages

Closest confusable in the CommonLID set is Czech (`ces`); both are West
Slavic with Latin script. Outside CommonLID, Slovak, Silesian and Kashubian
are the nearest relatives.

## Polish vs. Czech

- **Nasal vowels** `ą`, `ę` occur in Polish only; Czech has no nasal vowels.
- **ł** (/w/) is Polish only; Czech has no ł.
- **ó** (= /u/) is Polish; Czech writes long /uː/ as **ů** or **ú**, never ó.
- Czech has **ř** (/r̝/), **ě**, **háček** diacritics on `č š ž ť ď ň`, and
  **kroužek** `ů`; Polish has none of these.
- Polish uses **digraphs** `sz, cz, rz, dz, dź, dż, ch`; Czech prefers single
  letters with háček `š, č, ž` (plus `ch` digraph shared).
- Polish `-ość` ≈ Czech `-ost` (no final soft sign, no palatal i).
- Stress: Polish penultimate; Czech initial.
- Function words: pol `i, w, nie, że, się, jest`; ces `a, v, ne, že, se, je`.
- Negative particle: pol `nie` (separate word) vs ces `ne-` (attached
  prefix, `nemám`).
- Past tense: Polish has enclitic person endings on the l-participle
  (`pisałem`); Czech uses auxiliary `být` (`psal jsem`).

## Polish vs. other CommonLID languages

- Distinguishing from all Cyrillic-script neighbours is trivial (script).
- Among Latin-script Slavic candidates outside this set, Polish **ł + ogonek
  vowels** are the single best composite signal.
