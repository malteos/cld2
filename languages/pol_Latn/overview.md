# Polish (pol)

## Summary

Polish (endonym: *język polski*, *polszczyzna*) is a West Slavic language in
the Indo-European family (Indo-European → Balto-Slavic → Slavic → West
Slavic → Lechitic). ISO codes: 639-1 `pl`, 639-2 `pol`, 639-3 `pol`. Native
speakers total roughly 40–45 million, mostly in Poland where Polish is the
sole official language, with large diaspora communities in the United States,
Germany, the United Kingdom, Canada, France, Brazil, Australia, Ukraine,
Belarus, Lithuania and Israel. Polish is the most widely spoken West Slavic
language and is mutually intelligible to a limited extent with Czech and
Slovak, and more closely with Kashubian and Silesian.

## Writing system

Polish uses a 32-letter Latin alphabet augmented with diacritics: a **ą** b c
**ć** d e **ę** f g h i j k l **ł** m n **ń** o **ó** p r s **ś** t u w y z
**ź** **ż** (the letters q, v, x occur only in foreign names). Key features
are the ogonek vowels **ą** and **ę** (nasal), the stroked consonant **ł**
(pronounced /w/), the acute-diacritic palatals **ć ń ś ź**, the kropka-top
**ż**, and the accented **ó** (pronounced like **u**). Digraphs **cz, sz,
rz, dz, dź, dż, ch** represent single sounds. Casing is bicameral.

## Status

Polish has a continuous literary tradition since the 14th century (Bogurodzica
hymn). Standardisation occurred during the Renaissance and Enlightenment; the
current orthography was set by the 1936 reform and is maintained by the
Rada Języka Polskiego.
