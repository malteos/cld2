# Persian / Farsi (fas)

## Summary

Persian (endonym: **فارسی** *fārsī*) is an Indo-European → Indo-Iranian → Iranian → Western Iranian language. ISO codes: `fa` (639-1), `per` / `fas` (639-2/B and 639-2/T), `fas` (639-3). It is the official language of Iran (as *Fārsī*), Afghanistan (as *Darī*) and Tajikistan (as *Tojikī*, but written in Cyrillic there). Total speakers, combining L1 and L2, are estimated at 110–130 million. The CommonLID tag `fas_Arab` covers the Iranian and Afghan (Arabic-script) varieties.

## Writing system

Persian is written in a right-to-left Perso-Arabic abjad that extends the Arabic alphabet with four distinctively Iranian letters: **پ** (pe), **چ** (če), **ژ** (že), **گ** (gāf). Persian prefers the Iranian shapes **ک** (U+06A9, *not* the Arabic ك U+0643) and **ی** (U+06CC, *not* Arabic ي U+064A); ه word-finally often represents a final /e/ and carries the *ezāfe* hamza ۀ or written kasra `ـِ`. Digits may be Eastern Arabic-Indic (۰۱۲۳۴۵۶۷۸۹). Short vowels are normally unwritten. Persian does not use the Urdu retroflex letters ٹ ڈ ڑ or Uzbek vowels ۉ ې.

## History and status

Modern Persian (*Fārsī-ye now*) descends from Middle Persian (Pahlavi) and has been a major literary language since the 9th century (Rudaki, Ferdowsi, Hafez, Rumi). It was the court language of the Mughal Empire and a lingua franca across western, central and south Asia until the 19th century, leaving heavy lexical layers in Urdu, Turkish, Hindi and Uzbek.
