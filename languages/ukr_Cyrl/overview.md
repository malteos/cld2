# Ukrainian (ukr)

## Summary

Ukrainian (endonym: *українська мова*, *ukrayins'ka mova*) is an East Slavic
language in the Indo-European family (Indo-European → Balto-Slavic → Slavic →
East Slavic). ISO codes: 639-1 `uk`, 639-2 `ukr`, 639-3 `ukr`. Native speakers
number approximately 30–40 million, mostly in Ukraine where it is the sole
official state language; substantial diaspora communities exist in Canada,
the United States, Poland, Germany, Brazil, Argentina and Kazakhstan.
Ukrainian is mutually somewhat intelligible with Belarusian and partially
with Russian and Polish, reflecting its intermediate position in the Slavic
dialect continuum.

## Writing system

Ukrainian uses a 33-letter Cyrillic alphabet distinct from Russian. It
contains four letters not present in modern Russian: **і**, **ї**, **є**,
**ґ**, and notably **lacks** Russian **ы**, **ъ** and **э**. The full
inventory: а б в г ґ д е є ж з и і ї й к л м н о п р с т у ф х ц ч ш щ ь ю я.
The apostrophe `’` is used where Russian would use the hard sign ъ (e.g.
`п'ять` "five"). Casing is bicameral. The letter **г** represents /ɦ/ (voiced
pharyngeal fricative), while **ґ** represents the stop /g/.

## Status

Ukrainian emerged from Old East Slavic through the Ruthenian literary tradition
and was codified in the 19th century (Shevchenko, Kotliarevskyi). It became
the sole state language of independent Ukraine in 1991 and underwent a
significant orthographic update in 2019.
