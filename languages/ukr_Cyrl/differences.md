# Ukrainian vs. confusable languages

Closest confusables in the CommonLID set are the other Cyrillic Slavic
languages: Russian (`rus`) and Bulgarian (`bul`).

## Ukrainian vs. Russian

- Ukrainian has **і**, **ї**, **є**, **ґ**; Russian does not.
- Russian has **ы**, **ъ**, **э**; Ukrainian does not.
- Ukrainian infinitive `-ти` (`писати`), Russian `-ть` (`писать`).
- Ukrainian past m.sg. in `-в` (`був, писав, ходив`); Russian in `-л`
  (`был, писал, ходил`).
- Function words: ukr `що, це, і, й, дуже, тільки, але` vs rus `что, это, и,
  очень, только, но`.
- Ukrainian letter `г` = /ɦ/ — many common words differ (ukr `голова, газета`
  pronounced with /ɦ/; rus `голова` with /g/). Stop /g/ spelled `ґ` only in
  Ukrainian.
- Ukrainian has a productive **vocative** case (`друже`, `мамо`); Russian has
  only fossilised vocatives (`Боже`, `Господи`).
- Apostrophe `’` before я/ю/є/ї is Ukrainian-only in this role.

## Ukrainian vs. Bulgarian

- Bulgarian has **no case system** and uses **post-posed definite articles**
  `-ът/-та/-то/-те`; Ukrainian has seven cases and no articles.
- Bulgarian lacks an infinitive; Ukrainian has productive `-ти`.
- Bulgarian uses **ъ** as a vowel letter; Ukrainian does not use **ъ** at all.
- Bulgarian lacks Ukrainian-only **і**, **ї**, **є**, **ґ**.
- Bulgarian past tenses include aorist and imperfect; Ukrainian has a single
  synthetic past marked for gender but not person.
