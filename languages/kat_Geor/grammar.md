# Georgian Grammar (ქართული გრამატიკა)

## Word Order

Georgian is fundamentally an SOV (Subject-Object-Verb) language, though word order is relatively flexible due to its rich case-marking system. The canonical order is Subject-Indirect Object-Direct Object-Verb. Pragmatic factors such as focus and topic heavily influence actual word order in both spoken and written Georgian. New or emphasized information tends to appear in pre-verbal position, while given information may be moved to post-verbal position or omitted entirely through pro-drop.

Examples of word order flexibility:
- **SOV (canonical)**: ბავშვმა წიგნი წაიკითხა (bavshvma tsigni ts'aik'itxa, "the child read a book")
- **SVO**: ბავშვმა წაიკითხა წიგნი (bavshvma ts'aik'itxa tsigni)
- **OVS**: წიგნი წაიკითხა ბავშვმა (tsigni ts'aik'itxa bavshvma)

All three convey the same proposition but differ in information structure and pragmatic emphasis.

## Morphological Typology

Georgian exhibits a complex morphological system that combines features of both agglutinative and fusional typologies. Verbs are highly synthetic, with multiple prefixes and suffixes encoding person, number, tense, aspect, mood, version, causation, and directionality. Nouns display fusional case marking with seven distinct cases. The language also makes extensive use of derivational morphology, with a productive system of prefixes and suffixes that create new words from existing roots.

## Noun System

### Cases

Georgian has seven grammatical cases, each marked by a suffix on the noun:

| Case | Suffix | Function | Example (კაცი, "man") |
|------|--------|----------|----------------------|
| Nominative | -ი (-i) | Subject (present tenses) | კაცი (k'atsi) |
| Ergative | -მა (-ma) | Subject (aorist tenses) | კაცმა (k'atsma) |
| Dative | -ს (-s) | Indirect object, subject (perfect tenses) | კაცს (k'atss) |
| Genitive | -ის (-is) | Possession | კაცის (k'atsis) |
| Instrumental | -ით (-it) | Means, instrument | კაცით (k'atsit) |
| Adverbial | -ად (-ad) | Manner, transformation | კაცად (k'atsad) |
| Vocative | -ო (-o) | Direct address | კაცო (k'atso) |

### Gender and Articles

Georgian has no grammatical gender. Nouns are not classified as masculine, feminine, or neuter, and there is no agreement for gender on adjectives, verbs, or any other part of speech. The third-person pronoun ის (is) is used for all genders ("he," "she," or "it").

Georgian also has no definite or indefinite articles. Definiteness is determined by context, word order, and occasionally by demonstratives. The numeral ერთი (erti, "one") can sometimes function as an indefinite marker in colloquial speech.

### Number

Nouns form plurals with the suffix -ები (-ebi) for most nouns, though some older or irregular patterns exist. The plural suffix is added before the case marker: კაცები (k'atsebi, "men"), კაცებმა (k'atsebma, "men" in ergative). When a numeral is present, the noun typically remains in the singular: ხუთი კაცი (xuti k'atsi, "five men," literally "five man").

### Postpositions

Georgian primarily uses postpositions rather than prepositions. These follow the noun and typically govern specific cases:
- -ზე (on, about) — governs dative
- -ში (in, into) — governs dative
- -დან (from) — governs dative
- -თვის (for) — governs genitive
- -თან (near, with, at) — governs dative

## Verb System

The Georgian verb system is one of the most complex in the world and is the central feature of the language's grammar. Georgian verbs are polypersonal, meaning they agree with both the subject and the object in person and number.

### Screeve System

Georgian verbs are organized into groups of tense-aspect-mood forms called screeves (მწკრივი, mts'k'rivi). There are eleven screeves organized into four series:

**Series I — Present (აწმყო, ats'mqo)**
1. Present Indicative: ვწერ (vts'er, "I write")
2. Imperfect: ვწერდი (vts'erdi, "I was writing")
3. Present Subjunctive: ვწერდე (vts'erde, "I would write")

**Series II — Aorist (წყვეტილი, ts'qvetili)**
4. Aorist Indicative: დავწერე (davts'ere, "I wrote")
5. Aorist Subjunctive/Optative: დავწერო (davts'ero, "that I write")

**Series III — Perfect (პერფექტი, p'erp'ek'ti)**
6. Present Perfect: დამიწერია (damits'eria, "I have apparently written")
7. Pluperfect: დამეწერა (damets'era, "I had apparently written")
8. Perfect Subjunctive: დამეწეროს (damets'eros, "I would have apparently written")

**Series IV — Resultative**
9-11. Additional screeves expressing resultant states

### Version

Version (ქცევა, ktseva) is a morphological category that indicates the relationship between the verb action and the participant who benefits from or is affected by it. Georgian has four versions:

- **Subjective version** (-ი-, -i-): the subject acts for their own benefit
- **Objective version** (-უ-, -u-): the action benefits someone else
- **Locative version** (-ა-, -a-): relates the action to a location
- **Neutral version** (no marker): no special benefactive meaning

### Preverbs

Georgian verbs use a system of preverbs (პრევერბი, p'reverbi) — prefixes that add directional, aspectual, or lexical meaning to the verb. Common preverbs include:

- შე- (she-): inward, together
- გა- (ga-): outward, completion
- და- (da-): downward, completion
- ა- (a-): upward
- წა- (ts'a-): away, forward
- მო- (mo-): toward the speaker
- ჩა- (cha-): downward into
- გადა- (gada-): across, over

Preverbs are essential for forming the aorist and perfect series and often distinguish perfective from imperfective aspect.

### Polypersonal Agreement

Georgian verbs agree with multiple arguments simultaneously. A single verb form can mark the person and number of both the subject and the direct or indirect object through a combination of prefixes and suffixes:

- Subject markers (prefixes): ვ- (v-, 1st person), ø- (2nd person), ø- or ს-/ა-/ო- (3rd person)
- Object markers (prefixes): მ- (m-, 1st person object), გ- (g-, 2nd person object), ø-/ს-/უ-/ჰ- (3rd person object)
- Number markers (suffixes): -თ (-t, 1st/2nd person plural), -ენ/-ეს/-ან (-en/-es/-an, 3rd person plural)

Example: გვიყვარხართ (gviqvarxart) means "we love you (plural)" — a single word encoding subject person/number (1st plural), object person/number (2nd plural), and tense.

## Ergativity (Split Ergative)

Georgian has a split-ergative case alignment system that is unique among the world's languages. The case marking of the subject changes depending on the screeve series of the verb:

- **Series I (present)**: Nominative-Accusative alignment — the subject takes the nominative case
- **Series II (aorist)**: Ergative-Absolutive alignment — the subject of a transitive verb takes the ergative case, while the object takes the nominative
- **Series III (perfect)**: Dative alignment — the subject takes the dative case, the object takes the nominative

This three-way split is sometimes called "active alignment" and is a defining typological feature of Georgian.

| Series | Subject case | Object case |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| Present | Nominative (-ი) | Dative (-ს) |
| Aorist | Ergative (-მა) | Nominative (-ი) |
| Perfect | Dative (-ს) | Nominative (-ი) |

## Syntax

### Relative Clauses

Georgian uses the relative pronoun რომელიც (romelic, "which/who") to form relative clauses. The relative pronoun declines for case. Relative clauses typically follow the noun they modify but can precede it in formal registers.

### Negation

Negation is formed with the particle არ (ar) placed before the verb: არ ვიცი (ar vitsi, "I don't know"). In the imperative mood, the negative particle ნუ (nu) is used instead: ნუ წერ (nu ts'er, "don't write"). The particle ვერ (ver) expresses inability: ვერ ვხედავ (ver vxedav, "I cannot see").

### Coordination and Subordination

The most common coordinating conjunction is და (da, "and"). Other conjunctions include მაგრამ (magram, "but"), ან (an, "or"), and თუმცა (tumtsa, "however"). Subordinating conjunctions include რომ (rom, "that"), თუ (tu, "if"), რადგან (radgan, "because"), and როცა (rotsa, "when").

### Pro-drop

Georgian is a pro-drop language. Since the verb encodes both subject and object person/number, independent pronouns are typically omitted unless emphasis or contrast is needed. The sentence ვხედავ (vxedav) alone means "I see (him/her/it)" without requiring any overt pronouns.
