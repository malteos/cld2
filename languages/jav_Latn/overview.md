# Javanese (Basa Jawa) - Language Overview

## Classification

Javanese (Basa Jawa) is an Austronesian language belonging to the Malayo-Polynesian branch, specifically within the Western Malayo-Polynesian subgroup. Its ISO 639-3 code is **jav**. Within the Austronesian family, Javanese sits in the Javanese subgroup alongside several closely related languages such as Osing and Tenggerese, which are sometimes treated as dialects.

## Speakers and Distribution

With approximately **82 million native speakers**, Javanese is the largest Austronesian language by number of native speakers and one of the most widely spoken languages in the world. The majority of speakers reside on the island of Java, Indonesia, primarily in the provinces of Central Java (Jawa Tengah), East Java (Jawa Timur), and the Yogyakarta Special Region (Daerah Istimewa Yogyakarta). Significant Javanese-speaking diaspora communities exist in Suriname, New Caledonia, Malaysia, and other parts of the Indonesian archipelago, particularly in Lampung (Sumatra) and parts of Kalimantan, where transmigration programs relocated Javanese families.

## Writing Systems

Javanese has historically been written in the **Javanese script** (Aksara Jawa or Hanacaraka), an abugida descended from the Brahmi script via Kawi. The Javanese script has a long literary history dating to inscriptions from the 9th century CE. However, in modern usage, the **Latin script** (Aksara Latin) has become the dominant writing system for everyday communication, education, media, and digital content. The Javanese script is still taught in schools in Central Java and Yogyakarta and appears on street signs and official documents as a cultural preservation measure, but functional literacy in it is declining.

## Official Status

Javanese holds **no official status at the national level** in Indonesia, where Bahasa Indonesia (Indonesian) serves as the sole national and official language. However, Javanese functions as a **regional language** recognized under Indonesian law. It is used in regional broadcasting, local newspapers, and some local government contexts. Javanese is taught as a subject (muatan lokal) in schools across Central and East Java.

## Literary Tradition

Javanese possesses one of the richest literary traditions in Southeast Asia. Classical Javanese literature includes **kakawin** (Old Javanese poetry modeled on Sanskrit kavya), **kidung** (Middle Javanese poetry), and **babad** (historical chronicles). Major works include the Kakawin Ramayana, Kakawin Arjunawiwaha, Serat Centhini, and Babad Tanah Jawi. The wayang (shadow puppet) tradition preserves and transmits literary narratives from the Ramayana and Mahabharata adapted into Javanese cultural contexts.

## Speech Levels (Unggah-Ungguh)

A defining sociolinguistic feature of Javanese is its elaborate system of **speech levels** (unggah-ungguh basa). The three primary levels are:

- **Ngoko**: The informal, familiar register used among close friends, family, and when addressing social inferiors. This is the base level of Javanese vocabulary.
- **Madya**: A middle register that blends ngoko and krama elements. It is considered semi-formal and is common in everyday polite interaction.
- **Krama**: The formal, refined register used to show respect to elders, social superiors, and strangers. Krama involves extensive vocabulary substitution (e.g., ngoko *mangan* "eat" becomes krama *dhahar*).

Additional sub-registers include **krama inggil** (high krama, used to honor the addressee) and **krama andhap** (humble krama, used to lower oneself). The speech level system deeply affects word choice, and to a lesser extent, affixation. Mastery of these levels is considered a marker of cultural competence and refinement (Javanese: *njawani*).

## Dialects

Major dialectal divisions include Western Javanese (Banyumasan or "ngapak"), Central Javanese (Solo-Yogya, considered the prestige dialect), and Eastern Javanese (Surabayan and Malangan varieties). These dialects differ in phonology, vocabulary, and the extent to which speech levels are maintained.
