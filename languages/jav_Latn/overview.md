# Javanese (jav)

## Summary

Javanese (endonym: *Basa Jawa*) is an Austronesian language in the
Malayo-Polynesian → Javanese branch. ISO codes: 639-1 `jv`, 639-2/3 `jav`.
It is the largest Austronesian language by native speakers — roughly
80–85 million native speakers — spoken primarily in Central Java, East
Java, Yogyakarta and Banten provinces of Indonesia, with substantial
diaspora communities in Suriname, New Caledonia, the Netherlands and
Malaysia. It is the national language of none, but is a regional and
cultural language of Indonesia.

## Writing system

Javanese historically uses the *Aksara Jawa* Brahmic abugida (also called
Hanacaraka) and, in Islamic contexts, *Pegon* (modified Arabic). On the
web and in CommonLID, Javanese is almost exclusively written in Latin
script (`jav_Latn`) using an orthography close to Indonesian Rumi. The
Latin orthography marks the low-mid front vowel with `é` or `è`, the
pepet schwa with `ê`, and the retroflex `ḍ`/`ṭ` in some spellings, though
most web text drops diacritics and writes plain `e`, `d`, `t`.

## History and status

Javanese has a continuous literary tradition from the 9th-century Old
Javanese (*Kawi*) through Middle and Modern Javanese. It operates a
complex speech-level system (*ngoko*, *madya*, *krama*) distinguishing
familiar versus polite/honorific registers; this affects lexical choice
far more than grammar.
