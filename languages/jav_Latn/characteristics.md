# Javanese LID characteristics

## Script signals

In Latin orthography, careful Javanese text uses `é`, `è`, `ê`
(sometimes `ȇ`) to distinguish the three mid-front/central vowels. These
diacritics are rare in Indonesian/Malay and are a strong positive signal.
Retroflex digraphs or diacritics (`dh`, `th`, or `ḍ`, `ṭ`) in words like
`gedhé`, `wedhus`, `bathik` are also Javanese-specific. Web text often
drops diacritics; then rely on function words and lexicon.

## Top function words

- *Ngoko*: `aku`, `kowé`, `ora`, `wis`, `arep`, `durung`, `apa`, `iki`,
  `iku`, `karo`, `neng`, `saka`, `marang`, `uga`, `nanging`, `lan`,
  `utawa`, `banjur`, `bar`.
- *Krama*: `kula`, `sampeyan`, `panjenengan`, `mboten`, `saged`,
  `kagungan`, `dalem`, `inggih`, `menika`, `kaliyan`, `saking`,
  `dhumateng`, `ananging`, `utawi`.
- Common to both: `iki`, `iku`, `ana`, `ing`.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- `mboten` (krama "not"), `sampeyan`, `panjenengan`, `saged`, `menika`,
  `ingkang`, `dhateng`, `kaliyan` are near-perfect Javanese tells.
- `ora` (ngoko "not") plus `wis` / `durung` / `arep` clustering is
  diagnostic.
- `dh` and `th` digraphs in content words: `gedhé`, `Ngayogyakarta`,
  `bathara`.
- Place names and honorifics: `Ngayogyakarta`, `Surakarta`, `Kraton`,
  `Raden`.

## Morphology hooks

- 1SG agent prefix `tak-`/`dak-`: `tak-tuku`, `tak-gawa`.
- Applicative `-aké` or shortened `-ké`: `nggawé-aké`, `njupukké`.
- Definitising `-é`/`-né`: `bukune`, `omahé`.
- Nasal active prefix `N-` merging with stem: `nulis`, `nggawa`,
  `mbantu`.

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

Plain ASCII punctuation. Uses Indonesian-style numerals and rupiah `Rp`.
Diacritics `é è ê` appear in careful publications but are dropped on
social media.
