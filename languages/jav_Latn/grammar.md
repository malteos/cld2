# Javanese grammar

- **Word order**: SVO, analytic with agglutinative affixation.
  Modifier-after-head like other Malayo-Polynesian languages
  (`omah gedhé`).
- **Morphology**: Same general Austronesian template as Indonesian/Malay
  but with different allomorphy and an extra layer of speech-level
  lexical substitution. Prefixes `N-` (nasal active), `di-` (passive),
  `ke-` (accidental/potential), `tak-`/`dak-` (1sg agent), `mbok-`,
  `ma-`, `sa-`. Suffixes `-aké`/`-ké` (applicative/causative), `-i`,
  `-an`, `-é` (definitising). Reduplication is productive:
  `omah-omah`, `turu-turu`.
- **Nouns**: No gender, no number, no case. Possession: noun + pronoun
  enclitic `-ku`, `-mu`, `-é`/`-né` (`omahku`, `omahé`).
- **Verbs**: No tense. Aspect/mood periphrastic: `wis` (already),
  `lagi`/`lagek` (progressive), `arep`/`bakal` (future), `durung` (not
  yet). Voice active vs passive as in Malay.
- **Phonology**: Six vowels plus the mid-vowel distinction `é/è/ê`
  marked in careful orthography. Consonants include a dental/retroflex
  contrast `t/ṭ` and `d/ḍ` (often merged in web spelling). Stress is
  penultimate.
- **Syntax notes**: Three main speech levels — *ngoko* (familiar:
  `aku`, `kowé`, `ora`), *madya* (middle), *krama* (polite: `kula`,
  `sampeyan`/`panjenengan`, `mboten`, `saged`). The choice of register
  is a very strong Javanese marker regardless of grammar.
