# Catalan vs. confusable languages

Closest confusables in this set: **Spanish (spa)**, **Occitan (oci)**, **Aragonese (arg)**, and to a lesser extent **Extremaduran (ext)**.

## Catalan vs. Spanish

- Catalan `ny` vs. Spanish `ñ` (*Espanya* vs. *España*); Catalan `l·l` has no Spanish equivalent.
- Catalan uses `ç è ò à í ú ï ü`; Spanish uses only `ñ á é í ó ú ü`.
- Endings: `-ció -tat -ment` (cat) vs. `-ción -dad -mente` (spa).
- Catalan has clitics `hi en em et es` and productive apostrophe elision; Spanish does not elide.
- No inverted `¿ ¡` in Catalan.

## Catalan vs. Occitan

- Both share much vocabulary and the ending `-cion/-ció`. Occitan keeps `-cion` (*nacion*); Catalan writes `-ció` (*nació*).
- Catalan has `l·l` and `ny`; Occitan uses `lh` and `nh` (like Portuguese).
- Occitan uses `ò ç` freely and has article `lo/la/los/las` or Gascon `eth/era`; Catalan `el/la/els/les` or Balearic `es/sa`.
- Catalan preserves `v/b` distinction orthographically (varies dialectally); Occitan merges.

## Catalan vs. Aragonese

- Aragonese articles `o/a/os/as`; Catalan `el/la/els/les`.
- Aragonese keeps Latin initial F (*fillo, fer*); Catalan has it too (*fill, fer*) but uses `ll`, not `ll`/-palatal contrast differs.
- Aragonese lacks `l·l`, `ny` (writes `ny` sporadically or `ñ`), and has no standardised middle-dot.
- Catalan's apostrophe-heavy orthography contrasts with Aragonese cleaner word boundaries.

## Catalan vs. Extremaduran

- Extremaduran shows final `-h` aspiration, `-l/-r` neutralisation, and `u/i` in unstressed finals; Catalan keeps full forms with vowel reduction in pronunciation, not writing.
- Extremaduran shares Spanish-style `ñ`; Catalan uses `ny`.
