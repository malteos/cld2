# Distinguishing Catalan from Confusable Languages

Catalan can be confused with several other Romance languages, particularly Spanish, Occitan, French, Portuguese, and Italian. This document outlines the key distinguishing features for language identification purposes.

## Catalan vs Spanish (Castilian)

Spanish is the most commonly confused language with Catalan, as they coexist in the same geographic territory and share a large amount of vocabulary.

### Key Differentiators

**Articles (highly diagnostic):**
- Catalan plural articles: **els** (masc.), **les** (fem.)
- Spanish plural articles: **los** (masc.), **las** (fem.)
- The presence of *els* or *les* as articles is a strong Catalan indicator

**Preposition "with":**
- Catalan: **amb** (highly diagnostic)
- Spanish: **con**

**Conjunction "and":**
- Catalan: **i**
- Spanish: **y** (or **e** before /i/)

**The punt volat (l·l):**
- Catalan: *col·laborar*, *intel·ligent*
- Spanish: *colaborar*, *inteligente*
- The presence of l·l is virtually conclusive for Catalan

**Periphrastic past tense:**
- Catalan: *vaig cantar* (I sang) -- uses "anar" (to go) + infinitive
- Spanish: *cante* -- simple synthetic form
- This construction is unique to Catalan among Romance languages

**Demonstratives:**
- Catalan: *aquest/aquesta/aquests/aquestes* (this/these)
- Spanish: *este/esta/estos/estas*

**Common vocabulary differences:**
| Catalan | Spanish | English |
|---------|---------|---------|
| molt | muy/mucho | very/much |
| pero | pero | but |
| tambe | tambien | also |
| sempre | siempre | always |
| menjar | comer | to eat |
| parlar | hablar | to speak |
| trobar | encontrar | to find |
| sortir | salir | to go out |
| fer | hacer | to do/make |
| dir | decir | to say |

**Cedilla usage:**
- Catalan uses **ç** frequently: *plaça*, *començar*, *coneixença*
- Spanish does not use ç at all

**Clitic pronouns en/hi:**
- Catalan has *en* and *hi* clitics (shared with French/Occitan)
- Spanish has no direct equivalents

## Catalan vs Occitan

Occitan is historically the closest language to Catalan. The two languages share a common ancestor and were sometimes considered dialects of the same language in the medieval period.

### Key Differentiators

**Articles:**
- Catalan: *el, la, els, les*
- Occitan: *lo, la, los, las* (most dialects)

**Verb morphology:**
- Catalan periphrastic past: *vaig cantar*
- Occitan simple past: *cantèri* or *cantèi* (depending on dialect)

**Preposition "with":**
- Catalan: *amb*
- Occitan: *amb/embe/dab* (varies by dialect; *amb* is shared in some varieties)

**Characteristic endings:**
- Catalan: *-ment* (adverb), *-tat* (abstract noun)
- Occitan: *-ment* (shared), *-tat* (shared but often *-tat/-dat*)

**Orthographic conventions:**
- Catalan uses the punt volat: *l·l*
- Occitan uses *lh*, *nh* digraphs (like Portuguese): *trabalhar*, *senhor*
- Occitan uses *ò, ó* like Catalan but also **ç** with different distribution

**Function words:**
- Catalan *aquest* vs Occitan *aqueste/aquel*
- Catalan *amb* vs Occitan *amb/dab*
- Catalan *pero* vs Occitan *mas/mes*

The Occitan *lh* and *nh* digraphs are strong differentiators, as Catalan uses *ll* and *ny* for the same sounds.

## Catalan vs French

Despite both being Western Romance languages with some shared features, Catalan and French are generally distinguishable.

### Key Differentiators

**Articles:**
- Catalan: *el, la, els, les*
- French: *le, la, les, les*
- Note: feminine plural *les* is shared but masculine forms differ

**Negation:**
- Catalan: single negation with *no*: *No parlo catala*
- French: two-part negation *ne...pas*: *Je ne parle pas catalan*

**Orthography:**
- Catalan uses the **punt volat** (l·l): absent in French
- French uses circumflex accent (â, ê, î, ô, û): rare or absent in Catalan
- French uses extensive silent final consonants; Catalan pronounces most final consonants
- French uses **-tion** ending; Catalan uses **-cio**

**Shared features (can cause confusion):**
- Both use ç (cedilla)
- Both have *en* and *hi/y* pronominal clitics
- Both have vowel reduction (though the specific patterns differ)

**Distinguishing function words:**
- Catalan *amb* vs French *avec*
- Catalan *el/els* vs French *le/les*
- Catalan *pero* vs French *mais*
- Catalan *molt* vs French *tres/beaucoup*

## Catalan vs Portuguese

Portuguese and Catalan share several orthographic features, including the cedilla, which can cause confusion in short text samples.

### Key Differentiators

**Nasal vowels:**
- Portuguese has nasal vowels marked with tilde: *ã, õ* (*não*, *informação*)
- Catalan has no nasal vowels or tilde usage

**Articles:**
- Catalan: *el, la, els, les*
- Portuguese: *o, a, os, as*
- Article forms are completely different and highly diagnostic

**Digraphs:**
- Portuguese uses *lh* and *nh*: *trabalho*, *senhor*
- Catalan uses *ll* and *ny*: *treball*, *senyor*

**Function words:**
- Catalan *i* (and) vs Portuguese *e*
- Catalan *amb* (with) vs Portuguese *com*
- Catalan *que* vs Portuguese *que* (shared)
- Catalan *es* vs Portuguese *e* (is)

## Catalan vs Italian

Italian and Catalan are less commonly confused but share some structural similarities.

### Key Differentiators

**Articles:**
- Catalan: *el, la, els, les*
- Italian: *il/lo, la, i/gli, le*

**Double consonants:**
- Italian extensively uses geminate consonants: *anno*, *bello*, *tutto*
- Catalan only has geminate l (l·l); other consonants are not geminated

**Final vowels:**
- Italian words almost always end in vowels: *informazione*, *collaborare*
- Catalan words frequently end in consonants: *informacio*, *col·laborar*

## Summary of Diagnostic Features (Ranked by Reliability)

1. **l·l (punt volat)** -- near-certain Catalan indicator
2. **Periphrastic past with "anar"** -- *vaig, vas, va, vam, vau, van* + infinitive
3. **Function word "amb"** (with) -- unique to Catalan (and some Occitan dialects)
4. **Plural articles "els/les"** combined with singular "el/la" -- rules out Spanish, Portuguese, Italian
5. **Demonstratives "aquest/aquesta"** -- distinct from all confusable languages
6. **Clitic pronouns "en" and "hi"** -- shared only with French and Occitan
7. **Digraphs "ll" (word-initial) and "ny"** -- distinguishes from Occitan and Portuguese (lh/nh)
8. **Suffix "-cio"** -- vs Spanish "-cion", French "-tion", Italian "-zione"
9. **Conjunction "i"** (and) -- vs Spanish "y", French "et", Portuguese "e", Italian "e"
10. **Grave + acute accent distinction on e/o** -- shared with some languages but distribution is characteristic
