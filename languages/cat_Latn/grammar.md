# Catalan Grammar

## Typological Overview

- **Word order:** SVO (Subject-Verb-Object)
- **Morphological type:** Fusional
- **Head direction:** Head-initial (prepositions, noun-adjective order generally)
- **Alignment:** Nominative-accusative

## Phonology

### Vowels

Catalan has a rich vowel system that varies significantly between dialects. The two major dialectal groups, Eastern and Western Catalan, differ primarily in their vowel systems.

**Eastern Catalan (Central, Balearic, Rossellonese, Alguerese):**
- Stressed vowels: 7 vowels /a, e, ɛ, i, o, ɔ, u/
- Unstressed vowels undergo significant **vowel reduction**: the seven stressed vowels collapse into three unstressed vowels /ə, i, u/. Specifically:
  - /a/, /e/, /ɛ/ all reduce to schwa /ə/ in unstressed position
  - /o/, /ɔ/ reduce to /u/ in unstressed position
  - /i/ remains /i/
- This vowel reduction is one of the most distinctive phonological features of Eastern Catalan and strongly differentiates it from Spanish

**Western Catalan (Northwestern, Valencian):**
- Stressed vowels: 7 vowels /a, e, ɛ, i, o, ɔ, u/
- Unstressed vowels: 5 vowels /a, e, i, o, u/ (no vowel reduction to schwa)
- The Western system more closely resembles that of Spanish

The contrast between open and closed mid vowels (/e/ vs /ɛ/, /o/ vs /ɔ/) is phonemic and distinguishes meaning: "be" /be/ (well) vs "be" /bɛ/ (good/asset), "os" /os/ (bone) vs "os" /ɔs/ (bear).

### Consonants

Catalan has approximately 23 consonant phonemes:

- **Stops:** /p, b, t, d, k, ɡ/
- **Fricatives:** /f, v, s, z, ʃ, ʒ/ (the /v/ phoneme is maintained in Balearic and some Valencian dialects but merges with /b/ in Central Catalan)
- **Affricates:** /ts, dz, tʃ, dʒ/
- **Nasals:** /m, n, ɲ, ŋ/
- **Laterals:** /l, ʎ, lː/ (the geminate /lː/, written as l·l, is distinctive)
- **Rhotics:** /r, ɾ/
- **Approximants:** /j, w/

Notable phonological features:
- **Final obstruent devoicing:** voiced obstruents become voiceless at the end of a word: "fred" (cold) is pronounced /fɾɛt/
- **Voicing assimilation in sibilants:** sibilants assimilate voicing before voiced consonants
- **Geminate l (l·l):** The presence of a geminate lateral /lː/, written with the "punt volat" (raised dot) as l·l, is unique among Romance languages and highly diagnostic for Catalan identification

## Noun System

### Gender

Catalan has two grammatical genders: **masculine** and **feminine**. Gender assignment follows typical Romance language patterns:

- Nouns ending in **-a** are generally feminine: *casa* (house), *taula* (table)
- Nouns ending in a consonant or **-e**, **-o** are generally masculine: *llibre* (book), *gat* (cat)
- Numerous exceptions exist and must be learned individually

### Number

Plural formation follows several patterns:
- Add **-s** to words ending in an unstressed vowel: *casa > cases*, *home > homes*
- Add **-os** to words ending in a stressed vowel or certain consonants: *pis > pisos*
- Add **-s** to words ending in a stressed vowel with accent shift: *cafe > cafes*
- Words ending in **-s**, **-x**, **-ç** remain unchanged or add **-os**: *peix > peixos*

### Articles

**Definite articles:**
| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | el | els |
| Feminine | la | les |

- Before vowels: *l'* (elision): *l'home* (the man), *l'escola* (the school)
- Contracted forms with prepositions: *de + el = del*, *a + el = al*, *per + el = pel*

**Personal articles (highly distinctive):**
Catalan uses personal articles before proper names, a feature not found in Spanish:
- **en** (masculine): *en Joan* (Joan, a man)
- **na** (feminine): *na Maria* (Maria)
- These are reduced to **n'** before vowels: *n'Anna*
- In some dialects, **el/la** are used instead before names

**Indefinite articles:**
| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine | un | uns |
| Feminine | una | unes |

## Verb System

### Conjugation Classes

Catalan verbs are traditionally divided into three conjugation classes, though the second class has two sub-groups:

1. **First conjugation (-ar):** *cantar* (to sing), *parlar* (to speak) -- the most productive class
2. **Second conjugation:**
   - **-er/-re subgroup (pure):** *perdre* (to lose), *prendre* (to take)
   - **-er inceptive subgroup:** *coneixer* (to know), *neixer* (to be born) -- these insert the infix *-eix-* in certain forms
3. **Third conjugation (-ir):**
   - **Pure:** *dormir* (to sleep), *sentir* (to feel)
   - **Inceptive:** *servir* (to serve), *llegir* (to read) -- these also use the *-eix-* infix

### Tenses and Moods

Catalan has a full range of tenses and moods typical of Romance languages:

**Indicative mood:**
- Present: *canto, cantes, canta, cantem, canteu, canten*
- Imperfect: *cantava, cantaves, cantava, cantavem, cantaveu, cantaven*
- Preterite (simple past): *canti, cantares, canta, cantarem, cantareu, cantaren* (largely literary)
- **Periphrastic past (passat perifràstic):** *vaig cantar, vas/vares cantar, va cantar, vam/varem cantar, vau/vareu cantar, van/varen cantar* -- This is **unique to Catalan** among Romance languages. It uses the verb "anar" (to go) as an auxiliary followed by the infinitive to express the simple past. Despite using the verb "to go," this construction refers to past events, not future ones. This is one of the most diagnostic features of Catalan.
- Future: *cantare, cantaras, cantara, cantarem, cantareu, cantaran*
- Conditional: *cantaria, cantaries, cantaria, cantariem, cantarieu, cantarien*
- Present perfect: *he cantat, has cantat, ha cantat, hem cantat, heu cantat, han cantat*
- Pluperfect: *havia cantat*

**Subjunctive mood:**
- Present subjunctive: *canti, cantis, canti, cantem, canteu, cantin*
- Imperfect subjunctive: *cantes, cantessis, cantes, cantessim, cantessiu, cantessin*

**Imperative:**
- *canta (tu), canti (voste), cantem (nosaltres), canteu (vosaltres), cantin (vostes)*

### Auxiliary Verbs

- **haver** is used to form compound tenses: *he parlat* (I have spoken)
- **ser/estar** distinction exists but differs somewhat from Spanish usage
- **anar** serves as the auxiliary for the periphrastic past, a uniquely Catalan construction

## Syntax Features

### Clitic Pronouns

Catalan has a rich system of clitic (weak) pronouns that attach to verbs:

| Person | Direct Object | Indirect Object |
|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | em (me) | em (me) |
| 2nd sg. | et (te) | et (te) |
| 3rd sg. | el/la (lo/la) | li |
| 1st pl. | ens (nos) | ens (nos) |
| 2nd pl. | us (vos) | us (vos) |
| 3rd pl. | els/les (los/les) | els (los) |
| Reflexive | es (se) | es (se) |

**Special clitics (highly diagnostic):**
- **en** (partitive/locative): replaces partitive noun phrases or complements introduced by *de*. Equivalent to French "en": *Vols pa? -- Si, en vull* (Do you want bread? -- Yes, I want some)
- **hi** (locative/other): replaces locative complements or complements introduced by *a*, *en*, *amb*. Equivalent to French "y": *Vas a Barcelona? -- Si, hi vaig* (Are you going to Barcelona? -- Yes, I'm going there)

The presence and frequent use of *en* and *hi* clitics distinguishes Catalan from Spanish (which lacks direct equivalents) and aligns it with French and Occitan.

### Clitic Placement

- **Proclisis** (before the verb): with conjugated verbs in declarative sentences: *Ho veig* (I see it)
- **Enclisis** (after the verb): with infinitives, gerunds, and affirmative imperatives: *veure-ho* (to see it), *veient-ho* (seeing it), *mira-ho* (look at it)
- Clitic combinations follow specific ordering rules and undergo phonological changes

### Negation

Negation is formed with **no** placed before the verb: *No parlo catala* (I don't speak Catalan). Unlike French, Catalan does not use a two-part negation system (no equivalent of *ne...pas*), though negative polarity items like *pas* exist in some northern dialects influenced by French and Occitan.

### Question Formation

Questions can be formed through:
- Intonation alone (most common in colloquial speech)
- Subject-verb inversion
- Use of the interrogative particle **que** (in some dialects): *Que vens?* (Are you coming?)

### Relative Clauses

The general relative pronoun is **que**. The compound forms **el qual, la qual, els quals, les quals** are used in more formal contexts and after prepositions.

### "Ser" vs "Estar"

Like Spanish and Portuguese, Catalan distinguishes between *ser* (permanent qualities, identity, time) and *estar* (temporary states, locations), but the distribution differs in several cases from Spanish, with *ser* being used more broadly in Catalan for locations of events and certain states.
