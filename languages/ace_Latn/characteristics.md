# Acehnese (ace_Latn) - Language Identification Characteristics

## Unique Orthographic Characters

Acehnese uses the standard Latin alphabet with several additional diacritical characters that are critical markers for language identification:

- **è** (e with grave accent) - represents the open-mid front unrounded vowel /ɛ/
- **é** (e with acute accent) - represents the close-mid front unrounded vowel /e/
- **ë** (e with diaeresis) - represents the mid central vowel /ə/ (schwa), also written as "eu"
- **ô** (o with circumflex) - represents the close-mid back rounded vowel /o/
- **û** (u with circumflex) - used in some orthographic conventions

These diacritics are a strong distinguishing signal. Indonesian, Malay, Minangkabau, and Sundanese do not use è, é, ë, or ô in their standard orthographies. The presence of these characters in a text is a near-definitive indicator of Acehnese. However, many informal Acehnese texts written online omit the diacritics, making identification more challenging.

The digraph **eu** (representing /ɯ/ or /ə/) is extremely common and distinctive. Words like *ureueng* ("person"), *teungoh* ("in the middle of"), *meunyo* ("if"), *peugah* ("to say"), and *reubee* ("thousand") feature this digraph prominently.

## Character Frequency Patterns

The following character-level patterns distinguish Acehnese from its confusable neighbors:

- Very high frequency of the digraph **eu**: far more common than in Indonesian or Malay
- High frequency of **ng** (velar nasal): appears in many common words (*nyang*, *ureueng*, *teungoh*, *gampong*)
- High frequency of **euh** and **euk** sequences at word endings
- The letter **h** appears more frequently than in Indonesian, particularly in word-final position
- Relatively high frequency of **ny** (palatal nasal): *nyang*, *nyan*, *beunyoe*
- Lower frequency of **f** and **v** compared to Indonesian (these sounds are not native to Acehnese)
- The sequence **oe** appears in older orthographic forms (e.g., *kamoe*, *tanyoe*)

## Common Bigrams

The following character bigrams appear with distinctively high frequency in Acehnese:

- **eu** - the most distinctive Acehnese bigram
- **ng** - velar nasal, very common
- **ny** - palatal nasal
- **an** - common word ending
- **ak** - common in function words (*bak*, *jak*)
- **oh** - common in words like *rumoh*, *pajoh*
- **eu** - same as above, extremely frequent
- **ya** - in *nyang* and related forms
- **la** - in *lam*, *laen*
- **on** - in *lon*, *gampong*

## Common Trigrams

- **nya** - from *nyang*, the most frequent function word
- **eun** - common suffix and word-internal sequence
- **eur** - in *ureueng* and related words
- **ang** - common word ending
- **ong** - in *gampong*, *dong*
- **euh** - common word ending
- **euk** - common word ending
- **meu** - the ubiquitous verbal prefix
- **peu** - causative prefix
- **teu** - accidental/involuntary prefix
- **geu** - third person formal prefix
- **lam** - preposition, also common as word-internal sequence

## Morphological Patterns for Identification

### Distinctive Prefixes
- **meu-** : middle/reflexive/reciprocal voice (*meusaboh*, *meulawan*, *meunasah*)
- **peu-** : causative (*peumate*, *peugah*, *peuhah*)
- **teu-** : involuntary/accidental (*teudong*, *teupeugah*)
- **geu-** : 3rd person formal agent (*geucok*, *geujak*, *geupajoh*)
- **ta-** : 1st inclusive / 2nd person agent (*tajak*, *tawoe*, *tapajoh*)
- **ku-** : 1st person singular agent (*kujak*, *kuturi*)
- **neu-** : 2nd person formal agent (*neujak*, *neupajoh*)

### Distinctive Word Patterns
- Words ending in **-eung**: *ureueng*, *teungoh*, *reubeueng*
- Words ending in **-ong**: *gampong*, *rumong*
- Words ending in **-oh**: *rumoh*, *pajoh*, *gadoh*
- The word **ureueng** ("person") is extremely frequent and highly distinctive
- The word **gampong** ("village") is characteristic of Acehnese

### Key Discriminative Features vs. Indonesian
- **eu** digraph frequency (rare in Indonesian, ubiquitous in Acehnese)
- Diacritics è, é, ë, ô (absent in Indonesian)
- Function words *nyang*, *bak*, *lam*, *keu*, *han*, *hana* (not Indonesian)
- Pronominal forms *lon*, *gopnyan*, *gata*, *kamoe*, *tanyoe* (not Indonesian)
- Verbal prefixes *meu-*, *peu-*, *teu-*, *geu-* (not Indonesian; Indonesian uses *me-*, *pe-*, *ter-*, *di-*)

## Script Features

Acehnese uses the Latin script (ISO 15924: Latn) as its primary modern writing system. The alphabet consists of the 26 standard Latin letters plus the diacritical characters listed above. There is no single universally standardized orthography; multiple spelling conventions coexist. The most widely used system is based on the Indonesian Latin orthography with the addition of Acehnese-specific diacritics and digraphs.

Key script-level features:
- No capitalization rules differ from standard Latin conventions
- The digraph **eu** is treated as a single phonological unit
- The digraph **ng** represents a single sound (velar nasal)
- The digraph **ny** represents a single sound (palatal nasal)
- Word boundaries follow standard Latin spacing conventions
- Loanwords from Arabic are adapted to Acehnese phonology and orthography
- Reduplication is marked with a hyphen: *rumoh-rumoh*
