# Acehnese (ace_Latn) - Grammar

## Typological Overview

Acehnese is an agglutinative language with some isolating features. It uses a combination of affixation, cliticization, and word order to express grammatical relationships. The basic word order is Subject-Verb-Object (SVO), though pragmatic factors such as topicalization and focus can alter this order. Acehnese is notable among Western Austronesian languages for its relatively rich system of pronominal clitics and its register-based vowel system inherited from the Chamic branch.

## Phonology

### Vowels

Acehnese has a rich vowel inventory that is distinctive among Austronesian languages. The language distinguishes oral and nasal vowels, a feature relatively rare in the region. The oral vowels include:

- Front: /i/, /e/ (written as "é"), /ɛ/ (written as "è")
- Central: /ə/ (written as "eu" or "ë"), /a/
- Back: /u/, /o/ (written as "ô"), /ɔ/ (written as "o")

Additionally, Acehnese has a set of nasal vowels corresponding to most oral vowels. Nasalized vowels are phonemic and create minimal pairs with their oral counterparts. In the Latin orthography, nasal vowels are typically not distinctly marked, relying on context and the presence of nasal consonants in the environment.

The high back unrounded vowel /ɯ/ (sometimes written as "eu") is a particularly distinctive feature of Acehnese phonology that helps differentiate it from related languages.

### Consonants

The consonant inventory includes:

- Stops: /p/, /b/, /t/, /d/, /c/ (palatal), /j/ (voiced palatal), /k/, /g/, /ʔ/ (glottal stop)
- Nasals: /m/, /n/, /ɲ/ (written "ny"), /ŋ/ (written "ng")
- Fricatives: /s/, /h/
- Liquids: /l/, /r/
- Glides: /w/, /y/

Notable features include the frequent use of the glottal stop in word-final position and between vowels, and the prevalence of nasal consonants. The distinction between dental/alveolar and retroflex consonants found in some descriptions reflects dialectal variation.

### Syllable Structure

The basic syllable structure is (C)V(C), with consonant clusters generally restricted to the onset position and typically involving a nasal + stop sequence (e.g., /mb/, /nd/, /ŋg/). Final consonants are limited to stops, nasals, and the glottal stop. Stress is generally penultimate in polysyllabic words.

## Morphology

### Noun System

Acehnese nouns are not inflected for grammatical gender or case. Number is generally not marked on the noun itself; plurality can be indicated through reduplication, numerals, or quantifiers. Classifiers are used with numerals when counting objects, similar to many other Southeast Asian languages.

Common classifiers include:
- **droe** - for people (e.g., *lhee droe ureueng* "three people")
- **boh** - for round objects and general items (e.g., *duwa boh rumoh* "two houses")
- **blah** - for flat objects
- **batang** - for long objects
- **bak** - for trees and plants

Possession is expressed through juxtaposition (possessor follows the possessed noun) or with the possessive marker. Pronominal possession uses clitic forms attached to the noun.

### Pronoun System

Acehnese has a complex system of personal pronouns and pronominal clitics. Full pronouns are used for emphasis, while clitics are the default in most constructions. The pronominal system distinguishes:

| Person | Full Form | Clitic |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| 1st singular | lon/ulôn | -lôn |
| 2nd singular | gata/droeneuh | -kah |
| 3rd singular | gobnyan/jih | -geuh/-jih |
| 1st plural (inclusive) | tanyoe | -teu |
| 1st plural (exclusive) | kamoe | -meu |
| 2nd plural | gata bandum | -kah |
| 3rd plural | awak nyan | -geuh |

The clitic pronouns attach to verbs and function as agreement markers. Acehnese is notable for its split-ergative clitic system: in intransitive clauses, the single argument is marked differently depending on whether the verb is agentive or stative.

### Verb System

Acehnese verbs do not conjugate for tense. Tense and aspect are expressed through auxiliary words, adverbs, and context. Key tense/aspect markers include:

- **ka** - completive/perfective aspect (past)
- **teungoh** - progressive aspect (ongoing action)
- **kheun** - future marker
- **geuneuk/jeuet** - prospective/ability

#### Verbal Affixation

Acehnese has a productive system of verbal affixes that modify the meaning and argument structure of verbs:

**Prefixes:**
- **meu-** - intransitive/middle voice marker; indicates an action performed by the subject, often reflexive or reciprocal (*meulawan* "to fight each other", *meusaboh* "to unite")
- **peu-** - causative prefix; makes an intransitive verb transitive (*mate* "to die" > *peumate* "to kill")
- **teu-** - accidental/involuntary action (*teupeugah* "accidentally said", *teudong* "to stand inadvertently")
- **geu-** - third person agent marker used in literary/formal register (*geucok* "he/she takes", *geujak* "he/she goes")
- **ta-** - first person inclusive or second person polite agent marker (*tajak* "we go / you go")
- **ku-** - first person singular agent marker (*kujak* "I go")
- **neu-** - second person polite agent marker (*neujak* "you go")

**Suffixes:**
- **-eun** - objective/goal-oriented suffix
- **-geuh** - third person agreement clitic

**Circumfixes:**
- **peu-...-eun** - benefactive causative

#### Voice System

Acehnese has a complex voice system that distinguishes active, passive, and middle constructions. The active voice uses agent-marking prefixes on the verb. The passive is formed by the absence of an agent prefix or by placing the patient in subject position. The middle voice, marked by *meu-*, indicates actions where the subject is both agent and patient or where the action is reciprocal.

## Syntax

### Basic Clause Structure

The default word order is SVO:
- *Gopnyan ka pajoh bu* ("He/she already ate rice")
- Subject: *gopnyan* (he/she), Verb: *pajoh* (eat), Object: *bu* (rice)

### Negation

Negation is expressed with particles placed before the verb:
- **han/hana** - general negation ("not")
- **bèk** - prohibitive negation ("don't")
- **kon** - contrastive negation ("not [X but Y]")
- **hantom** - "never"

### Question Formation

Questions are formed through:
- Question particles: **peu** ("what"), **soe** ("who"), **pat/dipat** ("where"), **pakon** ("why"), **pakiban** ("how"), **pajan** ("when")
- Sentence-final question particle **-koh** or rising intonation for yes/no questions

### Relative Clauses

Relative clauses are introduced by **nyang** (relativizer/complementizer), which is one of the most frequent function words in Acehnese:
- *Ureueng nyang ka jak u Banda Aceh* ("The person who went to Banda Aceh")

### Serial Verb Constructions

Like many Southeast Asian languages, Acehnese makes extensive use of serial verb constructions, where two or more verbs are strung together without any conjunction:
- *Gopnyan jak bloe bu* ("He/she went [to] buy rice")

### Coordination and Subordination

Coordination uses conjunctions such as **dan** ("and"), **atawa** ("or"), **tapi** ("but"). Subordination employs complementizers and adverbial markers like **nyang** ("that/which"), **bahwa** ("that"), **watee** ("when"), **kareuna** ("because"), **meunyo** ("if").

## Reduplication

Reduplication is a productive morphological process in Acehnese used for several purposes:
- **Full reduplication** for plurality: *rumoh-rumoh* ("houses")
- **Full reduplication** for intensity or distribution: *manyang-manyang* ("very tall / tall ones")
- **Partial reduplication** for various semantic effects
