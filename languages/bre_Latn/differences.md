# Differentiating Breton from Confusable Languages

## Overview of Confusable Languages

Breton can potentially be confused with several languages due to shared ancestry, geographic proximity, or superficial orthographic similarities:

1. **Welsh (Cymraeg)** - fellow Brythonic Celtic language
2. **Cornish (Kernewek)** - closest Brythonic relative
3. **French (Fran-ais)** - dominant contact language, source of many loanwords
4. **Irish (Gaeilge)** / **Scottish Gaelic (Gaidhlig)** - fellow Celtic but Goidelic branch

## Breton vs. Welsh

Welsh and Breton are both Brythonic Celtic languages and share significant structural similarities, including initial consonant mutations, V2 word order, and conjugated prepositions. However, they are readily distinguishable by several features:

### Orthographic Differences
- **c'h**: Breton uses the unique digraph **c'h** for the voiceless velar fricative; Welsh uses **ch**. This is the single most reliable distinguishing feature.
- **k vs c**: Breton consistently uses **k** (*ker*, *kig*, *kan*); Welsh uses **c** before a, o, u (*car*, *cig*, *can*).
- **w**: Breton uses **w** more broadly; Welsh also uses **w** but additionally employs **u** as a vowel in ways Breton does not.
- **zh**: Breton's **zh** digraph does not exist in Welsh.
- Welsh uses **dd** (voiced dental fricative), **ff** (voiceless f), **ll** (voiceless lateral fricative), and **rh** (voiceless r), none of which appear in Breton.
- Welsh **y** is extremely common as a vowel; Breton uses **y** much less frequently.

### Function Word Differences
| English | Breton | Welsh |
|---------|--------|-------|
| the | ar/an/al | y/yr/'r |
| and | ha/hag | a/ac |

### Grammatical Differences
- Welsh has more inflected prepositions than Breton and conjugates them slightly differently.
- Welsh uses **yn** as a predicative particle; Breton has no direct equivalent.
- Welsh uses **bod** (to be); Breton uses **bezañ**.
- Welsh mutation system has three types (soft, nasal, aspirate); Breton has four (lenition, spirantization, provection, mixed).

## Breton vs. Cornish

Cornish is Breton's closest relative, and the two languages were likely mutually intelligible until the medieval period. Distinguishing them requires attention to specific orthographic and lexical differences:

### Orthographic Differences
- Cornish (in Standard Written Form) uses **dh** for the voiced dental fricative; Breton does not use this digraph.
- Cornish uses **gh** for the velar fricative; Breton uses **c'h**.
- Cornish retains **wh** in words like *whel* (work); Breton lost this distinction.
- Breton **zh** has no Cornish equivalent.
- The **-añ** verbal noun ending in Breton corresponds to **-a** or **-ya** in Cornish.

### Function Word Differences
| English | Breton | Cornish |
|---------|--------|---------|
| the | ar/an/al | an |
| and | ha/hag | ha/hag |
| is | eo | yw/os |
| in | e/en | yn |
| not | ne...ket | ny/nyns |
| of | a/eus | a |

### Lexical Differences
While much core vocabulary is shared, centuries of separate development have led to divergences. Breton has absorbed far more French loanwords, while Cornish has absorbed more English ones. Some cognates have shifted in form:
- Breton *skol* vs Cornish *skol* (school - same)
- Breton *ti* vs Cornish *chi* (house)
- Breton *dour* vs Cornish *dowr* (water)

## Breton vs. French

Although Breton and French are unrelated (Celtic vs. Romance), Breton has borrowed extensively from French over centuries of contact. This can occasionally cause short text segments to appear ambiguous.

### Key Structural Differences
- **Mutations**: Breton's initial consonant mutations are absent in French.
- **Word order**: Breton's V2 and VSO tendencies contrast with French SVO.
- **Articles**: Breton *ar/an/al* vs French *le/la/les*.
- **Verb morphology**: Breton synthetic verb forms and conjugated prepositions have no French parallel.
- **Vocabulary**: Despite borrowings, core function words are entirely different.
- **Orthography**: The **c'h** digraph, **zh**, **ñ**, and frequent use of **k** and **w** immediately distinguish Breton from French.

### Distinguishing from French Loanwords in Breton
Breton frequently adapts French loanwords to its own phonology and orthography:
- French *telephone* > Breton *pellgomz* (calque) or *telefon*
- French *ordinateur* > Breton *urzhiataer*
- French *universite* > Breton *skol-veur* (calque: great-school)

## Breton vs. Irish / Scottish Gaelic

Though all are Celtic, the Goidelic languages are quite distinct from Breton:
- Goidelic uses a completely different orthographic system with extensive silent letters and different digraphs (**bh**, **mh**, **fh**, **dh**, **gh**, **sh**, **th**).
- Irish/Scottish Gaelic use the dot-above (historically) or **h** after consonants for lenition; Breton uses different initial letters.
- Function words are entirely different: Irish *an* (the), *agus* (and), *ar* (on), *le* (with).
- Word-final clusters differ: Irish frequently ends words in vowels or broad/slender consonants; Breton ends words in consonant clusters and **-añ**.

## Quick Diagnostic Decision Tree

1. Does the text contain **c'h**? --> Almost certainly **Breton**
2. Does the text contain **ll**, **dd**, **ff**, **rh** and function words like *y/yr*, *mae*, *yn*? --> **Welsh**
3. Does the text contain **dh**, **gh** and function words like *yw*, *yn*, *nyns*? --> **Cornish**
4. Does the text contain *le*, *la*, *les*, *de*, *du*, *est*, *dans*, *que*? --> **French**
5. Does the text contain **bh**, **mh**, **fh** and words like *agus*, *na*, *le*? --> **Irish/Scottish Gaelic**
6. Does the text contain **ar/an/al** articles, **ha/hag**, **ne...ket**, **-añ** endings, **zh**, **k** instead of **c**? --> **Breton**
