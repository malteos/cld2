# Breton Grammar

## Word Order

Breton has a complex word order system. The basic underlying word order is VSO (Verb-Subject-Object), a feature shared with other Celtic languages. However, in practice, modern Breton frequently uses SVO order, particularly in colloquial speech, partly due to French influence.

A defining feature of Breton syntax is the V2 (verb-second) constraint in main clauses. The conjugated verb or auxiliary must appear in the second position, with one constituent fronted before it for emphasis or focus. This means the first position can be filled by the subject, object, adverbial, or other constituent, depending on what the speaker wishes to emphasize:

- **Subject focus**: *Yann a lenn ul levr.* (Yann reads a book.)
- **Object focus**: *Ul levr a lenn Yann.* (A book Yann reads.)
- **Verb focus**: *Lenn a ra Yann ul levr.* (Read does Yann a book - emphasis on reading.)

The preverbal particles **a** and **e** play crucial roles. The particle **a** is used when a noun phrase or focused element precedes the verb, while **e** is used with adverbial phrases or in subordinate clauses.

## Phonology and Mutations

### Consonant Mutations

Initial consonant mutation is one of the most distinctive features of Breton grammar and is essential for correct speech and writing. There are four types of mutation:

#### 1. Lenition (Kemmadurezh dre vlotaat)
The most common mutation, triggered by numerous grammatical contexts:

| Base | Lenited |
|------|---------|
| p | b |
| t | d |
| k | g |
| b | v |
| d | z |
| g | c'h |
| gw | w |
| m | v |

Triggers include: after the article with feminine singular nouns, after certain prepositions (da, dre, hep, a), after the possessive adjectives da (your) and e (his), after the particle a, and after certain numerals.

#### 2. Spirantization (Kemmadurezh dre c'hwezhadenniñ)
Affects only voiceless plosives:

| Base | Spirantized |
|------|-------------|
| p | f |
| t | z |
| k | c'h |

Triggered by: the possessive adjective he/ho (her), the numeral tri/teir (three), the conjunction ma (if), and a few other contexts.

#### 3. Provection (Kemmadurezh dre galetaat / hardening)
Voices become voiceless:

| Base | Provected |
|------|-----------|
| b | p |
| d | t |
| g | k |
| gw | kw |

Triggered by: the possessive adjective ho (your, plural), the conjunction ha/hag (and), certain verb forms, and the preposition da in certain constructions.

#### 4. Mixed Mutation
A combination pattern triggered primarily by the article with plural nouns after certain prepositions and by the verbal particle e:

| Base | Mixed |
|------|-------|
| p | b |
| t | d |
| k | g |
| b | v |
| d | t |
| g | c'h |
| gw | w |
| m | v |

### Vowel System

Breton has a rich vowel system including oral and nasal vowels. The orthography represents these with various diacritical marks:
- **ê** = open e [ɛː]
- **ù** = [y] (similar to French u)
- **û** = long [yː]
- **ñ** = nasalization of preceding vowel
- **ë** = schwa or indicates separate pronunciation

## Noun System

### Gender
Breton has two grammatical genders: masculine and feminine. Gender assignment is largely arbitrary for inanimate nouns, though there are some patterns (e.g., trees are generally feminine, while their fruits may be masculine). Gender determines which mutations are triggered after the article.

- *an ti* (the house, masculine, no mutation)
- *ar gador* (the chair, feminine, lenition of k > g in *kador*)

### Number
Breton distinguishes singular and plural. Plural formation is diverse:
- **-où**: the most common plural suffix (*levr* > *levrioù*, book > books)
- **-ioù**: variant of the above (*marc'h* > *merc'hier* or *marc'hoù*)
- **-ed**: for animate beings (*bugel* > *bugale*, child > children)
- **-ien**: for people (*Breizhad* > *Breizhiz*, Breton person > Breton people)
- Internal vowel change and other irregular patterns also occur.

Breton also retains a singulative/collective distinction for some nouns, particularly natural objects: *gwez* (trees, collective) > *gwezenn* (a single tree, singulative with -enn suffix).

### Articles
- **an** before n, d, t, h, and vowels
- **al** before l
- **ar** before all other consonants
- **un/ul/ur** (indefinite, same distribution as definite)

There is no indefinite plural article; bare plurals are used.

### Cases
Breton has no case system. Grammatical relations are indicated by word order, prepositions, and mutations.

## Verb System

### Synthetic and Analytic Forms

Breton verbs have two conjugation strategies:

1. **Synthetic (inflected) forms**: The verb itself carries person/number markers. These are more common in literary Breton and in certain tenses.
   - *Kanañ a ran.* (I sing. — *ran* is the 1sg present of *ober*, to do, used as auxiliary)
   - *Lenn a rez.* (You read.)

2. **Analytic (periphrastic) forms**: Using the auxiliary verb *ober* (to do) or *bezañ* (to be) with the verbal noun (equivalent to an infinitive).
   - *Me a zo o lenn.* (I am reading. — progressive with *bezañ* + *o* + verbal noun)

### Key Verbs

**Bezañ** (to be) is the most important verb and has two present tense paradigms:
- **Situational** (location/state): *emaon, emaout, emañ, emaomp, emaoc'h, emaint*
- **Identificational/habitual**: *on, out, eo, omp, oc'h, int*

**Ober** (to do/make) serves as a general auxiliary:
- Present: *ran, rez, ra, reomp, rit, reont*

**Kaout/Endevout** (to have) is used for possession and as a perfect auxiliary:
- *Am eus ul levr.* (I have a book.)

### Tenses and Moods

Breton has a range of tenses including present, imperfect, preterite (simple past), future, conditional, and pluperfect. The subjunctive exists but is rarely used in modern speech. The imperative is used for commands.

### Verbal Particles

Two preverbal particles govern clause structure:
- **a**: used when a noun phrase, pronoun, or focused element precedes the verb (triggers lenition)
- **e**: used when an adverbial element precedes the verb or in subordinate clauses (triggers mixed mutation in some dialects)

### Negation

Negation uses a discontinuous morpheme: **ne... ket** (similar to French *ne... pas*):
- *Ne ganañ ket.* (I do not sing.)
- In colloquial speech, *ne* is often dropped: *Ganañ ket.*

## Prepositions

Breton prepositions are conjugated (inflected for person and number), a feature shared with other Celtic languages:
- *da* (to): *din* (to me), *dit* (to you), *dezhañ* (to him), *dezhi* (to her), *deomp* (to us), *deoc'h* (to you pl.), *dezho* (to them)
- *gant* (with): *ganin* (with me), *ganit* (with you), *gantañ* (with him), etc.
- *evit* (for), *diwar* (from/off), *war* (on), *e* (in), *eus/a* (of/from)

## Adjectives

Adjectives generally follow the noun they modify:
- *un ti bras* (a big house)

Some common adjectives can precede the noun:
- *ur brav ti* (a fine house)

Comparison uses *-oc'h* (comparative) and *-añ* (superlative):
- *bras* (big) > *brasoc'h* (bigger) > *brasañ* (biggest)

## Numerals

Breton uses a vigesimal (base-20) counting system for traditional numbers, similar to French and other Celtic languages:
- 1-10: *unan, daou/div, tri/teir, pevar/peder, pemp, c'hwec'h, seizh, eizh, nav, dek*
- 20: *ugent*
- 40: *daou-ugent* (two-twenty)
- 60: *tri-ugent* (three-twenty)

Gender distinction exists in numerals 2, 3, and 4 (masculine/feminine forms).
