# Bambara (Bamanankan) - Identification Characteristics

## Diagnostic Special Characters

The most distinctive orthographic feature of Bambara text is the presence of the following special characters, which are highly diagnostic:

- **ɛ** (open e / epsilon) - extremely common, appears in many core words
- **ɔ** (open o) - very common, fundamental to the vowel system
- **ɲ** (palatal nasal) - common, as in "ɲɔgɔn" (each other), "ɲuman" (good)
- **ŋ** (velar nasal) - less frequent but diagnostic, used word-medially and word-finally

These four characters together constitute a strong fingerprint for Bambara and related Manding languages written in the Latin script. The co-occurrence of ɛ and ɔ in a text is particularly telling, as few other Latin-script African languages use both with the same frequency.

In some informal or older texts, the following substitutions may appear:
- "è" for "ɛ"
- "ò" for "ɔ"
- "ny" for "ɲ"
- "ng" for "ŋ"

## Morphological Patterns

### Reduplication
Bambara makes extensive use of reduplication for emphasis, distribution, or modification:
- "dɔɔnin dɔɔnin" (little by little)
- "kelen kelen" (one by one)
- "caman caman" (very many)

### Compound Words
Bambara forms many compound words, often noun+noun or noun+verb:
- "lakɔli" (la + ecole, from French "ecole") = school
- "baarakɛla" (baara + kɛ + la) = worker
- "dumunike" (dumuni + ke) = eating

### Derivational Suffixes
- **-la/-na** (agent): "kalan" -> "kalandenw" (students)
- **-li/-ni** (action noun): "kalan" -> "kalanni" (education)
- **-ya** (abstract noun): "cɛ" -> "cɛya" (manhood)
- **-man** (possessing quality): "hakili" -> "hakiliman" (intelligent)

## Tonal Patterns

Although tone is not regularly marked in standard Bambara orthography, tonal patterns are a fundamental structural feature. In language identification, tone is not directly visible in text, but its effects can be inferred from:

1. **Homographs that would be disambiguated by tone**: Many common words have different meanings based on tone, which means context-dependent interpretation is crucial.
2. **Auxiliary verb selection**: The choice of auxiliary (bɛ, ye, ka, etc.) creates characteristic syntactic patterns visible in text.
3. **Grammatical tone in verb constructions**: Certain verbal constructions are marked by tone change rather than segmental change.

## French Loanwords

Due to the colonial history and ongoing influence of French in Mali, Bambara text frequently contains French loanwords adapted to Bambara phonology:
- "lakɔli" (ecole = school)
- "lɛkɔli" (variant of above)
- "gafɛ" or "gafe" (cafe)
- "mobili" (automobile = car)
- "telefɔni" (telephone)
- "porogaramu" (programme)
- "ɲɛnajɛ" (native word for entertainment, but shows typical Bambara phonology)

These loanwords undergo Bambara phonological adaptation, resulting in characteristic spelling patterns.
