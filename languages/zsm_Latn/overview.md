# Standard Malay (zsm)

## Summary

Standard Malay (endonym: *Bahasa Melayu* or officially *Bahasa Malaysia*)
is the Malaysian national standard variety within the Malay macrolanguage.
Austronesian → Malayo-Polynesian → Malayic. ISO codes: 639-1 none, 639-3
`zsm`. Roughly 20 million native speakers in Peninsular Malaysia, Sabah
and Sarawak, plus over 15 million Malaysian second-language users. It is
the co-official language of Malaysia and the main medium of education,
government, and broadcast media. `zsm` is a subtag of the `msa`
macrolanguage but refers specifically to the codified Malaysian written
norm overseen by *Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka* (DBP).

## Writing system

Modern *zsm* uses the Rumi (Latin) alphabet — 26 letters, no diacritics —
following the MABBIM 1972 reform harmonised with Indonesian. The Arabic-
derived *Jawi* script is retained for ceremonial, Islamic-studies, and
some signage use, but essentially all web and news content is Rumi.
Hyphen marks reduplication (`kanak-kanak`, `cepat-cepat`). Loanwords from
English are adapted phonetically (`stesen`, `bas`, `kaunter`, `sains`,
`teknologi`).

## History and status

Standardised by DBP from the 1950s on Johor-Riau Malay, *zsm* is the
codified successor to Classical Malay used in Malaysia today. It is
extremely close to both Indonesian (shared Rumi orthography) and Brunei
Malay (Brunei uses *zsm* as one of its official written forms alongside
*Bahasa Melayu Brunei*).
