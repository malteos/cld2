# Standard Malay LID characteristics

## Script signals

Rumi (26-letter Latin) with no diacritics. Hyphenated reduplication
(`kanak-kanak`, `sama-sama`, `lain-lain`, `tiba-tiba`) is a strong
Malay/Indonesian signal. The digraphs `ng`, `ny`, `sy`, `kh` are
productive; `f`, `v`, `z` appear only in loanwords.

## Top function words

`yang`, `dan`, `di`, `ke`, `dari`, `untuk`, `pada`, `dengan`, `dalam`,
`akan`, `sudah`, `belum`, `tidak`, `bukan`, `ini`, `itu`, `ialah`,
`adalah`, `ada`, `tetapi`, `atau`, `juga`, `saya`, `awak`, `anda`,
`kami`, `kita`, `mereka`, `dia`, `oleh`, `bagi`, `kerana`, `sebagai`,
`boleh`, `mahu`, `iaitu`, `hanya`.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- `kerana` (never `karena`) is a near-perfect Malaysian tell.
- `mahu` (desiderative) contrasts with Indonesian `mau`.
- `ialah` copula is very Malaysia-leaning; `adalah` is possible but
  marked.
- `-nya` enclitic is high-frequency word-finally.
- `meN-` allomorphy: `mem-`, `men-`, `meng-`, `meny-`.
- Suffixed verbs `-kan`, `-i`: `memberikan`, `mendekati`.
- `pe(N)-...-an` nominalisation: `pembangunan`, `perhubungan`.

## Morphology hooks

Same productive template as Malay macrolanguage:
`{me|di|ber|ter|pe|se|ke}STEM{kan|i|an|nya}`. Bound pronouns `-ku`,
`-mu`, `-nya` are high-frequency word-final n-grams.

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

Plain ASCII punctuation. Malaysian currency `RM` precedes digits. Month
names `Januari … Disember` (with `Disember` — not `Desember`). Day names
`Isnin, Selasa, Rabu, Khamis, Jumaat, Sabtu, Ahad`; Malaysian forms
`Jumaat` and `Ahad` differ from Indonesian `Jumat` and `Minggu`.
