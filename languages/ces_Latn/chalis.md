# Czech (ces_Latn) under CHALIS conditions

CHALIS (Tichý & Libovický 2026, arXiv 2606.06088) tests Czech against its cousin **Slovak (slk)**, **without diacritics**, and with **homoglyphs** (Latin letters replaced by Cyrillic/Greek look-alikes). It also contains **Russian typed in Czech-style Latin**, which can superficially resemble Czech. The gold label is the ISO 639-3 code **ces**. Local reference material:

- `../chalis/discriminators.md`, `../chalis/discriminators/ces-slk.tsv`, `../chalis/discriminators/ces-slk_nodiac.tsv`
- `variants/no_diacritics/` and `variants/homoglyphs/` (synthetic transforms of real UD text; they model, but are not, the CHALIS data)
- `../rus_Cyrl/variants/romanized_cs/` (Russian in Czech-style Latin)

## 1. Cousin-language cues robust to noise

1. **Reflexive *se*** (rank 2 in the Czech list) vs. Slovak **sa**. ASCII-only, survives stripping, appears in almost every sentence. (Careful: *se* is also the Czech preposition "with", which is still Czech.)
2. **Copula with *js-***: *jsem, jsi, jsme, jste, jsou* vs. Slovak *som, si, sme, ste, sú*. The onset *js-* is practically exclusive to Czech.
3. **Relative stem *kter-*** (*ktery, ktera, ktere, kteri*) vs. Slovak *ktor-*.
4. **Past tense *byl, byla, bylo, byli*** vs. Slovak *bol, bola, bolo, boli*.
5. **Conditional *bych, bys, bychom, byste*** vs. Slovak *by som, by si, by sme*.
6. **Conjunctions/adverbs**: *jako, kdyz, protoze, pokud, nebo, jeste, take/taky, jen/pouze, vice/vic, mezi, proc, kdo, ted, pak* vs. Slovak *ako, ked, pretoze, ak, alebo, este, tiez, len/iba, viac, medzi, preco, kto, teraz, potom*.
7. **Vocalized prepositions** *ve, ze, ke* vs. Slovak *vo, zo, ku*.
8. **Endings**: *-em* (instr.), *-ou* in 3pl verbs (*budou, mohou, jsou*), *-ni* verbal nouns (*reseni*), *-ove* (anim. pl.), 1sg *-u/-i* (*chci, muzu, jdu*) vs. Slovak *-om, -u/-ia, -nie, -ovia, -m*.

## 2. Czech without diacritics

Stripping removes *ě ř ů* and all háčky/čárky (*ě→e, ř→r, ů→u, č→c*). Example from `variants/no_diacritics/ud_01.txt`:

> *Kdyz jsem si k babicce v nedeli vecer pro ni prisla, myslela jsem, ze omdlim.*

Surviving cues: *kdyz, jsem* (twice), *pro*. In `ud_03.txt`: *Proc se babicka smeje? Asi jsem rekl neco veseleho* → *proc, se, jsem, rekl, neco*.

Top Czech markers in stripped form (from `ces-slk_nodiac.tsv`): *jsem, jako, jsou, kdyz, byl, jsme, ktere, ktery, bylo, jeste, byla, pak, ktera, bych, protoze, prvni, podle, pokud, neco, ted, mezi, jste, proc, sve, jejich*. Slovak counterparts: *ako, som, bol, pre, ak, alebo, nie, ich, len, vo, ked, ktory, ktore, bola, ktora, bolo, este, zo, podla, viac, medzi, moze, tiez, pozri, pocas*.

Other traces of Czech that survive: *-ou* verb endings (*budou, mohou*), the superlative prefix *nej-* (*nejlepsi, nejvetsi*) vs. Slovak *naj-* (*najlepsi*), and Czech *i/u* where Slovak has *ie/o* (*misto/miesto, muze/moze, kun/kon*). Lexical pairs also survive: *rekl/povedal, mluvit/hovorit, opravdu/naozaj, diky/vdaka*.

**Ambiguity cases** (same form in both languages after stripping): *co, pri, pred, byt, ze, uz, az, velmi, mam, ma, nic, tri, dobre, rok, roku*, infinitives in *-at/-it/-ovat*. A short string made only of such words (*Uz je to dobre.*) cannot be decided reliably; report the most likely label only on the basis of the markers above.

## 3. Homoglyph handling

In this repo's simulation (`variants/homoglyphs/`, generated by `../scripts/build_chalis_variants.py`) about half of the eligible letters are swapped: a→а U+0430, c→с U+0441, e→е U+0435, o→о U+043E, p→р U+0440, x→х U+0445, y→у U+0443, i→і U+0456, j→ј U+0458, s→ѕ U+0455, v→ν (Greek nu) U+03BD, plus capitals А В С Е Н К М О Р Т Х. The actual CHALIS substitution table may differ (not verified here).

1. **Normalize first**: map all Cyrillic/Greek look-alikes to Latin, then decide.
2. A mostly-Latin word with one or two Cyrillic letters (*јѕem, nеděli*) is a **homoglyph attack, not Russian**. Real Russian has entire words in Cyrillic and letters with no Latin twin (*ы, ж, ш, щ, я, ю, ь, ъ, д, л, п, б*).
3. Precomposed Czech letters (*ě, ř, ů, č, š, ž, á, é, í, ý*) are untouched in the simulation and stay fully diagnostic. *ř* or *ů* next to Cyrillic *е/о* → Czech.

Raw example (`variants/homoglyphs/ud_01.txt`): *Když јѕem ѕi k bаbіčce v nеděli vеčer pro ni рřišla, myѕlеlа jsem, žе omdlím.* → normalized *Když jsem si k babičce v neděli večer pro ni přišla, myslela jsem, že omdlím.* → **ces**.

## 4. Czech vs. Russian in Czech-style Latin

Russian romanized with Czech orthography (`../rus_Cyrl/variants/romanized_cs/`) uses *č, š, ž, ch* and therefore looks Czech-like. Distinguish by vocabulary and morphology: Russian *eto, čto, kak, tolko, očen, kotoryj, segodnja, ja* and endings *-ogo, -ego, -yj, -ij, -aja, -yje/-ye* vs. Czech *to, co, jak, je, jsem, jen, velmi, který, dnes, já* and endings *-ého, -ý, -á*. Romanized Russian is full of *ja/ju* sequences (*sostavljalo, desjati, tysjač, Armeniju*) and lacks *ě, ř, ů* and Czech length marks (*á, í, ý*). Note *byl, kak/jak, on, ona* overlap, so weigh several words.

## 5. Decision checklist

1. Normalize homoglyphs.
2. Recurring *ě, ř, ů* → **ces**; recurring *ä, ô, ľ, ĺ, ŕ* → **slk**.
3. Count markers: *se, jsem/jsou/jsme, kter-, byl/byla/bylo, bych, jako, kdyz, protoze, pokud, nebo, jeste, take, mezi, proc, ve/ze/ke* (Czech) vs. *sa, som/su/sme, ktor-, bol/bola/bolo, by som, ako, ked, pretoze, ak, alebo, este, tiez, medzi, preco, vo/zo/ku* (Slovak).
4. Check Russian-in-Latin markers (*čto/chto, eto, očen, -ogo, -yj*) and Polish (*ł, ę, ą, się*).
5. If only shared words remain, the sample is ambiguous; prefer the language with any single high-precision marker.

## 6. Illustrative noisy snippets

1. *Vcera jsem byl ve meste, ale obchod uz byl zavreny, takze jsme sli domu.* → **ces** (*jsem, byl, ve, jsme, sli*).
2. *Myѕlím, žе tо nеní рrаνdа, рrоtоžе tаm nіkdо nеbуl.* → normalize → *Myslím, že to není pravda, protože tam nikdo nebyl.* → **ces** (*není, protože, nikdo*).
3. *To je dobre.* → ambiguous between **ces** and **slk** (all three words are shared); no marker present.
