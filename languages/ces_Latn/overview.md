# Czech (ces)

## Summary

Czech (endonym: *čeština*, *český jazyk*) is a West Slavic language in the
Indo-European family (Indo-European → Balto-Slavic → Slavic → West Slavic →
Czech–Slovak). ISO codes: 639-1 `cs`, 639-2/T `ces`, 639-2/B `cze`, 639-3
`ces`. Native speakers number approximately 10–11 million, almost all in the
Czech Republic where it is the sole official language; additional communities
exist in Slovakia, Austria, Germany, Poland, Canada, the United States and
Argentina. Czech is mutually highly intelligible with Slovak and partially
intelligible with Polish and other West Slavic languages.

## Writing system

Czech uses a Latin alphabet with 42 graphemes (including digraphs ch). The
alphabet: a á b c č d ď e é ě f g h ch i í j k l m n ň o ó p q r ř s š t ť u
ú ů v w x y ý z ž. Diacritics include the **háček** (caron) on č, š, ž, ř,
ť, ď, ň (ě is háček over e); the **čárka** (acute) marking long vowels á é í
ó ú ý; and the **kroužek** (ring) on **ů** (always long /uː/, never
word-initial). The letter **ř** (/r̝/, a raised-alveolar trilled fricative)
is unique to Czech. Casing is bicameral.

## Status

Czech has a literary record from the 12th century. Standard modern Czech was
codified by Jan Hus (diacritic orthography, ca. 1410) and revived in the
19th-century National Revival (Josef Dobrovský, Josef Jungmann). A distinct
colloquial register, Common Czech (obecná čeština), coexists with the
literary standard.
