# Czech LID characteristics

## Script signals

Latin with rich diacritics. The single strongest Czech signal is **ř** —
unique to Czech worldwide. Other highly distinctive letters: **ů** (the
kroužek/ring), **ě**, and the háček set **č š ž ť ď ň**. Czech also has long
vowels **á é í ó ú ý**. Czech **does not** have Polish **ł**, **ą**, **ę**,
**ś**, **ć**, **ń**, **ź**, or the digraphs **sz**, **cz**, **rz**.

## Top function words

a, i, v, ve, na, do, z, ze, za, o, u, k, ke, ku, s, se, po, před, pod, nad,
při, mezi, o, ale, ani, nebo, či, že, aby, když, kde, jak, jako, co, kdo,
který, která, které, je, jsou, byl, byla, bylo, byli, bylo by, ne, ano, už,
ještě, také, jen, velmi, každý, tento, tato, toto, ty, tyto.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- **ř** is the clearest signal; appears in everyday words (`řeka, přes, tři,
  dveře`). After voiceless consonants ř = /r̝̊/.
- **ů** always word-medial/final, never initial (`dům, stůl, bůh, původní`).
- **ě** always after consonant (`dítě, věc, pěkný, měsíc`).
- Long-vowel endings **-á, -é, -í, -ý, -ou** in noun/adjective paradigms.
- Adjective endings: m. `-ý/-í`, f. `-á/-í`, n. `-é/-í`; pl. `-í/-é/-á`.
- Abstract noun ending **-ost** (`radost, bolest, možnost`) parallels Polish
  `-ość` but without final acute.
- Word-initial `nej-` (superlative) and `ne-` (negation).
- Syllabic r/l in clusters: `vlk, krk, prst, srna, hltal`.

## Morphology hooks

- Auxiliary clitics `jsem, jsi, jsme, jste` attached to l-participle
  (`viděl jsem, koupili jsme`).
- Conditional clitics `bych, bys, by, bychom, byste, by`.
- Reflexive `se` and dative `si` as second-position (Wackernagel) clitics.
- Vocative `-e, -u, -i, -o` (`pane!, příteli!`).

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

- Czech uses low-high quotation marks `„…"`, with guillemets `»…«` as
  alternatives.
- Non-breaking space after single-letter prepositions/conjunctions in
  typography (`v Praze`, `a také`).
- Capitalisation conservative: months, weekdays, nationalities lowercase.
