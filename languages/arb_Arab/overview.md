# Modern Standard Arabic (arb)

## Summary

Modern Standard Arabic (endonym: العربية الفصحى *al-ʿarabiyya al-fuṣḥā*,
also اللغة العربية الحديثة) is the contemporary literary and broadcast standard
derived from Classical Arabic. Family: Afro-Asiatic → Semitic → Central
Semitic → Arabic. ISO codes: **639-3 `arb`**, under the 639-1 `ar` and 639-2
`ara` umbrellas. It has essentially no native speakers but ~270 million
functional users and is co-official in 25+ states of the Arab League, as well
as a working language of the UN, the African Union, and the OIC.

## Writing system

Uses the **Arabic script** (U+0600–U+06FF) as an abjad: 28 consonants, three
long vowels, right-to-left, cursive with contextual joining forms. Optional
diacritics (harakat: ـَ ـِ ـُ ـْ ـّ) vocalise text; fully vocalised MSA is
standard in Qur'ānic material, children's texts, and dictionaries, but absent
from most news/web prose. No letter casing. Default orthography uses ا أ إ آ ى
ة ء.

## Status

MSA is the target register for formal news (Al Jazeera, BBC Arabic), modern
literature, academic writing, government communications, and religious
discourse outside liturgy. It coexists with regional dialects in a
**diglossic** relationship — speakers use the dialect at home and MSA for
reading/writing and pan-Arab media. In LID pipelines, `arb` content is largely
indistinguishable from the macrolanguage `ara` tag.
