# Modern Standard Arabic grammar

- **Word order**: Predominantly **VSO** in formal prose; SVO is also well
  attested, especially when topicalising the subject. Highly synthetic via
  templatic non-concatenative morphology.
- **Morphology**: Root-and-pattern system, typically triconsonantal roots
  interleaved with vocalic melodies and CV templates — k-t-b → *kataba* (wrote),
  *kitāb* (book), *maktab* (office), *maktaba* (library), *kātib* (writer).
  Ten productive verbal derivation forms (Form I–X) add valency/aspect
  semantics (Form II intensive, Form III reciprocal, Form VII passive, etc.).
- **Nouns**: Masculine vs feminine gender (fem. usually marked ة). Three
  numbers: singular, dual (ـانِ / ـينِ), plural (sound masc ـونَ/ـينَ, sound
  fem ـاتٌ, broken plurals via vowel melody). Three cases: nominative ـُ,
  accusative ـَ, genitive ـِ — fully vocalised in formal writing, often
  dropped in unvocalised text. Definiteness with الـ.
- **Verbs**: Two aspects (perfective / imperfective), derived jussive and
  subjunctive moods, imperative, active/passive voices. Agreement in person,
  number, and gender; dual forms preserved. Future via سـ or سوف + imperfective.
- **Phonology**: 28 consonant phonemes including pharyngealised emphatic
  consonants ص ض ط ظ, pharyngeals ع ح, uvular ق, velar fricatives خ غ. Three
  short (a i u) and three long (ā ī ū) vowels, plus diphthongs ay aw.
- **Syntax notes**: Pro-drop; subject agreement marked on verb. No overt
  present copula — a nominal sentence ُمحمد طالب means "Muhammad is a student."
  Negation particles diverge by tense: لا (generic imperfective), ما (past),
  لم + jussive (past negation), لن + subjunctive (future negation), ليس
  (non-verbal negation). Pronominal object/possessive suffixes attach to verbs
  and nouns.
