# Malayalam (mal)

## Summary

Malayalam (endonym: **മലയാളം** *Malayāḷam*) is a South Dravidian language, the mother tongue of roughly 35–40 million people, primarily in the Indian state of Kerala and the union territory of Lakshadweep, with large diaspora communities in the Gulf states, Malaysia, Singapore, the United Kingdom, and the Americas. ISO codes: **639-1 `ml`**, **639-2/639-3 `mal`**. Script: Malayalam (ISO 15924 `Mlym`).

## Writing system

Malayalam is written in the Malayalam script, a Brahmic abugida derived from the ancient Grantha/Vatteluttu tradition. It has no case distinction. Consonants carry an inherent /a/, suppressed by the virama **്** (U+0D4D, *candrakkala*); dependent vowel signs sit around the base letter. Malayalam preserves the full Sanskrit inventory of voiced and aspirated stops as distinct letters (ക ഖ ഗ ഘ, etc.) so all four series per place of articulation are written.

The script is famous for:

- **Chillu letters** — atomic "pure" consonant forms without a visible virama: **ൻ** (chillu-n), **ൾ** (chillu-ḷ), **ർ** (chillu-r), **ൽ** (chillu-l), **ൺ** (chillu-ṇ), **ൿ** (chillu-k). These are unique to Malayalam.
- Extensive **ligatured conjuncts**, with many traditional stacked forms (e.g. **ന്ത**, **ണ്ട**, **സ്ത്ര**) that are visually denser than in Tamil or Kannada.
- Two spelling standards: the **traditional (old) orthography**, common before 1971, which uses more stacked conjuncts; and the **reformed orthography**, which splits many conjuncts horizontally using explicit chandrakkala.

## History and status

Malayalam emerged as distinct from Tamil by roughly the 9th–10th century CE and has a strong literary tradition from the medieval *maṇipravāḷam* style onward. It is an official language of India and of Kerala.
