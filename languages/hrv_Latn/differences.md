# Differences Between Croatian and Related Languages

## Croatian vs. Serbian

Croatian (hrv_Latn) and Serbian (srp_Latn / srp_Cyrl) are the two most closely related standard languages that require careful differentiation for language identification. While they share a common Neo-Shtokavian base and are largely mutually intelligible, they differ systematically in script usage, lexicon, phonology, and certain grammatical preferences.

### Script Differences

The most immediately visible difference is script: Croatian exclusively uses the Latin script (Gaj's Latin alphabet), whereas Serbian uses both the Cyrillic script (the primary official script) and a Latin script variant. When Serbian is written in Latin script, distinguishing it from Croatian requires attention to lexical and morphological features rather than orthographic ones.

### Lexical Differences

This is the most reliable domain for distinguishing Croatian from Serbian. Croatian language policy has historically favored native Slavic word formation and calques, while Serbian has been more open to international borrowings, particularly from Turkish, French, German, and Greek. ### Phonological / Reflex Differences

The most systematic phonological difference lies in the Yat reflex:

- **Croatian**: uses the **Ijekavian** pronunciation and spelling (*ije* for long, *je* for short): *mlijeko* (milk), *rijeka* (river), *lijepo* (beautifully), *dijete* (child), *bjelina* (whiteness)
- **Serbian**: predominantly uses the **Ekavian** reflex: *mleko*, *reka*, *lepo*, *dete*, *belina* (though Serbian also has an Ijekavian variant in some regions)

This reflex difference permeates the entire lexicon and is one of the most reliable indicators for language identification between Croatian and Ekavian Serbian.

### Morphological and Syntactic Differences

- **Infinitive vs. da-construction**: Croatian strongly prefers the infinitive (*Zelim ici* "I want to go"), while Serbian more commonly uses *da* + present tense (*Zelim da idem*).
- **Future tense formation**: Croatian uses the short form of *htjeti* cliticized to the infinitive (*Radit cu* or *Cu raditi*), whereas Serbian typically uses *cu da radim*.
- **Interrogative/indefinite pronouns**: Croatian uses *tko*, *netko*, *nitko*, *svatko*; Serbian uses *ko*, *neko*, *niko*, *svako*.
- **Relative pronoun**: Croatian *koji* is used broadly; Serbian often uses *sto* in colloquial registers where Croatian would use *koji*.

### Orthographic Conventions

- Croatian writes assimilated consonant clusters phonetically but maintains some morphological spellings: *predsjednik* (president), keeping the *d* before *s*.
- Serbian spelling is more strictly phonetic: *predsednik*.

## Croatian vs. Bosnian

Bosnian (bos_Latn) is the closest standard language to Croatian, and the two are extremely difficult to distinguish in short text samples. Both use the Latin script and both are Ijekavian. Key differences:

### Lexical Preferences

Bosnian retains more Turkish (Ottoman) loanwords than Croatian due to the historical influence of Ottoman rule in Bosnia. Examples:

| Croatian | Bosnian | English |
|----------|---------|---------|
| tisuca | hiljada | thousand |
| tjedan | sedmica | week |
| vlak | voz | train |
| kruh | hljeb | bread |
| nogomet | fudbal | football |
| kazaliste | pozoriste | theater |
| kolodvor | stanica | station |

Bosnian also uses some words of Arabic and Turkish origin (*rahmet*, *bajram*, *dzamija*, *avlija*, *mahala*, *carsija*) more frequently, reflecting the cultural and religious context of the Bosniak population.

### Phonological Features

- Bosnian preserves the phoneme **/x/** (h) in positions where Croatian may omit it: Bosnian *lahko* vs. Croatian *lako* (easily), Bosnian *mehko* vs. Croatian *meko* (soft).
- Both are Ijekavian, reducing phonological differentiation.

### Grammatical Differences

Differences are minimal. Bosnian, like Serbian, tends to use *da* + present tense alongside the infinitive more freely than Croatian, which has a stronger preference for the infinitive.

## Croatian vs. Slovenian

Slovenian (slv_Latn) is a Western South Slavic language, more distantly related to Croatian than Serbian or Bosnian. Mutual intelligibility is limited compared to the Croatian-Serbian pair.

### Key Differences

- **Dual number**: Slovenian retains the dual grammatical number (for exactly two entities), which Croatian lost centuries ago. The presence of dual forms (*midva*, *vidva*, *onadva*; verb forms like *delava*, *delata*) is a strong Slovenian indicator.
- **Lexicon**: Significant lexical divergence. Slovenian *dekle* (girl) vs. Croatian *djevojka*; Slovenian *pes* (dog) vs. Croatian *pas*; Slovenian *druzina* (family) vs. Croatian *obitelj*.
- **Orthography**: Slovenian uses *c*, *s*, *z* (with carons) but does **not** use *c* (c with acute) or *d* (d with stroke), which are characteristic of Croatian/Bosnian/Serbian. This is a highly reliable script-level distinguisher.
- **Phonology**: Slovenian has vowel reduction and a different prosodic system. It lacks the pitch accent system of standard Croatian.
- **Morphology**: Slovenian has six cases (no vocative in standard use), compared to Croatian's seven. Verb conjugation patterns also differ.

### Reliable Distinguishing Markers

The absence of **c** and **d** in Slovenian text, combined with the presence of dual forms and distinct core vocabulary, makes Croatian-Slovenian differentiation relatively straightforward for language identification systems compared to the Croatian-Serbian-Bosnian triangle.
