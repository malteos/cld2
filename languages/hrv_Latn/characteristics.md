# Croatian Language Characteristics for Language Identification (hrv_Latn)

## Script and Special Characters

Croatian uses the Latin script with the following special characters and digraphs that are distinctive markers for language identification:

### Unique Characters with Diacritics
- **c** (c with caron) -- voiceless postalveolar affricate /tS/
- **c** (c with acute) -- voiceless palatal affricate /tc/
- **d** (d with stroke) -- voiced palatal affricate /dj/
- **s** (s with caron) -- voiceless postalveolar fricative /S/
- **z** (z with caron) -- voiced postalveolar fricative /Z/

### Distinctive Digraphs
- **dz** (dz with caron) -- voiced postalveolar affricate /dZ/
- **lj** -- palatal lateral /L/
- **nj** -- palatal nasal /J/

The presence of **c** (c with acute) and **d** (d with stroke) is a strong indicator for Croatian/Bosnian/Serbian, as these characters are rare in other Latin-script languages. The combination of c, c, d, s, z in a single text strongly suggests a South Slavic language written in Latin script.

## Character Frequency Patterns

In Croatian text, the most frequent letters are: a, i, o, e, n, r, s, t, j, u, k, l, d, p, m, v, z, g, b, c, h. The letter **j** has notably high frequency compared to most other European languages, as it appears in the Ijekavian reflex (e.g., *lijep*, *dijete*, *mlijeko*), in digraphs (*lj*, *nj*), and as a glide in various morphological contexts. The letters **q**, **w**, **x**, and **y** do not belong to the native Croatian alphabet and appear only in foreign words.

## Morphological Patterns for Language Identification

Croatian's rich derivational morphology produces characteristic suffixes that serve as reliable indicators:

### Noun Suffixes
- **-ost**: abstract quality nouns (*mogucnost* "possibility", *slobodnost* "freedom")
- **-nje / -anje / -enje**: verbal nouns (*ucenje* "learning", *pisanje* "writing", *hodanje* "walking")
- **-stvo**: collective/abstract nouns (*drustvo* "society", *vodstvo* "leadership")
- **-ica**: feminine agent/diminutive (*prijateljica* "female friend", *ulica* "street")
- **-ac**: masculine agent nouns (*pisac* "writer", *igrac* "player")
- **-ina**: augmentative/quality (*velicina* "size", *tisina* "silence")

### Adjective Suffixes
- **-icki**: relational (*politicki* "political", *turisticki* "touristic")
- **-ski / -cki**: relational/ethnic (*hrvatski* "Croatian", *gradski* "urban")
- **-an / -na / -no**: qualitative (*vazan* "important", *slozen* "complex")
- **-ni / -na / -no**: relational (*drzavni* "state [adj.]", *narodni* "national")

### Verb Suffixes
- **-ati**: infinitive marker, most common class (*raditi* "to work", *gledati* "to watch")
- **-iti**: second most common infinitive ending (*govoriti* "to speak")
- **-uje-**: present tense marker for -ovati verbs (*pokazuje* "shows")

## Distinguishing Features from Close Languages

Key features that help distinguish Croatian from the closely related Bosnian and Serbian (Latin script):

- Higher frequency of words with the Ijekavian reflex (*ije/je*) compared to Serbian Ekavian (*e*)
- Preference for native Slavic vocabulary over loanwords (e.g., *tisuca* instead of *hiljada*)
- Use of infinitive constructions where Serbian prefers *da* + present tense
- Specific lexical items unique to Croatian standard (*opce*, *uopce*, *opcenito* vs. Serbian *uopste*)
- Word-final *-tko* (e.g., *netko*, *nitko*, *svatko*) vs. Serbian *-ko* (*neko*, *niko*, *svako*)
