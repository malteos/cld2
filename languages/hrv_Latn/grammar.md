# Croatian Grammar (hrv_Latn)

## Word Order

Croatian is fundamentally an SVO (Subject-Verb-Object) language, but its rich inflectional morphology permits considerable flexibility in word order. Variations such as VSO, OVS, and other arrangements are used for emphasis, topicalization, or stylistic purposes without ambiguity, since grammatical relations are marked by case endings rather than positional syntax. For example, "Ivan vidi Mariju" (Ivan sees Marija) and "Mariju vidi Ivan" both convey the same meaning, though the latter places emphasis on the object.

## Nominal Morphology

### Cases

Croatian has seven grammatical cases, one of the richest case systems among modern European languages:

1. **Nominative** (nominativ): marks the subject. *Student uci.* (The student studies.)
2. **Genitive** (genitiv): marks possession, partitive, and various prepositional complements. *Knjiga studenta.* (The student's book.)
3. **Dative** (dativ): marks the indirect object and beneficiary. *Dajem studentu knjigu.* (I give the student a book.)
4. **Accusative** (akuzativ): marks the direct object. *Vidim studenta.* (I see the student.)
5. **Vocative** (vokativ): marks direct address. *Studente!* (Student!)
6. **Locative** (lokativ): used with prepositions to indicate location. *U skoli.* (In school.)
7. **Instrumental** (instrumental): marks the instrument or means, and accompaniment. *Pisem olovkom.* (I write with a pencil.)

### Gender and Number

Croatian nouns belong to one of three grammatical genders: masculine, feminine, or neuter. Gender assignment is partly predictable from morphological form (e.g., most nouns ending in a consonant are masculine, those in -a are typically feminine, those in -o or -e are typically neuter), but there are exceptions. Each noun declines for two numbers: singular and plural. The combination of 7 cases, 3 genders, and 2 numbers produces a large number of distinct inflectional forms.

### Adjective Agreement

Adjectives agree with their head noun in gender, number, and case. Furthermore, Croatian adjectives have two forms: a definite (long) form and an indefinite (short) form, distinguished by the presence or absence of a pronominal suffix. The definite form is used when the referent is known or specific (e.g., *novi auto* "the new car"), while the indefinite form appears in predicative constructions or with indefinite referents (e.g., *auto je nov* "the car is new").

## Verbal Morphology

### Aspect

The Croatian verb system is organized around the category of aspect, a fundamental feature of Slavic languages. Most verbs come in aspectual pairs:

- **Imperfective** (nesvrseni vid): expresses ongoing, habitual, or repeated actions. *Pisati* (to write, imperfective).
- **Perfective** (svrseni vid): expresses completed, bounded actions. *Napisati* (to write / to have written, perfective).

Aspect is encoded lexically, often through prefixation (e.g., *citati* -> *procitati*) or suffixation (e.g., *baciti* -> *bacati*), and determines which tense forms a verb can take.

### Tenses

Croatian has three main tenses in active use:

- **Present** (prezent): formed with personal endings added to the verb stem. Used for current and habitual actions with imperfective verbs, and for future reference with perfective verbs.
- **Perfect** (perfekt): the most common past tense, formed with the present tense of *biti* (to be) and the past participle (l-participle). *Ja sam pisao.* (I wrote / I was writing.)
- **Future I** (futur prvi): formed with the short forms of *htjeti* (to want) and the infinitive. *Ja cu pisati.* (I will write.)

Additional tenses exist but are rare in contemporary spoken language: the aorist, imperfect, pluperfect, and future II (futur drugi), which appears in conditional temporal clauses.

### Mood

- **Imperative** (imperativ): *Pisi!* (Write!), *Pisimo!* (Let us write!), *Pisite!* (Write! [plural/formal])
- **Conditional** (kondicional): formed with the conditional auxiliary *bih, bi, bi, bismo, biste, bi* and the l-participle. *Pisao bih.* (I would write.)

## Clitics

Croatian has a complex system of clitics -- unstressed words that must appear in a specific order within the sentence, typically in the second position (Wackernagel's position). Clitics include short forms of the auxiliary *biti* (sam, si, je, smo, ste, su), the reflexive pronoun *se*, short dative and accusative pronouns (mi, ti, mu, joj, nam, vam, im; me, te, ga, je/ju, nas, vas, ih), and the interrogative particle *li*. The fixed ordering of clitics is a characteristic feature of Croatian syntax:

*Dao sam mu je.* (I gave it to him.) -- auxiliary, dative pronoun, accusative pronoun.

## Phonology

### Vowels

Croatian has a simple five-vowel system: /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, /u/. Vowel length is phonemic, and the language also has a syllabic /r/ that can function as a vowel nucleus (e.g., *krv* "blood", *prst* "finger").

### Consonants

The consonant inventory includes several notable features:

- **Palatal consonants**: /c/ (written c), /dz/ (written dz), /nj/ (written nj), /lj/ (written lj)
- **Postalveolar affricates and fricatives**: /ts/ (c), /tS/ (c with caron), /dZ/ (dz with caron), /S/ (s with caron), /Z/ (z with caron)
- **Palatal affricates**: /tc/ (c with acute, a voiceless palatal affricate) and /dj/ (d with stroke, a voiced palatal affricate)

The distinction between the postalveolar pair (c/dz) and the palatal pair (c/d) is a notable feature of Croatian phonology, marking it distinctly from Serbian, which tends to merge these in some dialects.

### Pitch Accent

Standard Croatian, based on the Neo-Shtokavian dialect, has a pitch accent system with four distinct accent types: short falling, short rising, long falling, and long rising. Accent placement is phonemic and can distinguish otherwise identical words. This prosodic system is a characteristic feature of the Shtokavian dialect group.

## Derivational Morphology

Croatian has extremely productive derivational morphology. Common processes include:

- **Prefixation** for verbs: *pisati* -> *napisati*, *prepisati*, *dopisati*, *ispisati*, *upisati*
- **Suffixation** for nouns: *-ost* (abstract quality: *mladost* "youth"), *-nje/-anje/-enje* (verbal nouns: *pisanje* "writing"), *-ac/-ica* (agent nouns: *pisac* "writer", *uciteljica* "female teacher")
- **Diminutives and augmentatives**: *kuca* -> *kucica* (little house), *ruka* -> *rucetina* (big hand)
- **Adjective formation**: *-ski/-cki/-icki* (relational: *hrvatski* "Croatian"), *-an/-na/-no* (qualitative)
