# Khmer Grammar Reference

## 1. Overview

Khmer (ភាសាខ្មែរ, /pʰiːəsaː kʰmaːe/) is the official language of Cambodia, spoken by approximately 16 million people. It belongs to the Austroasiatic language family, specifically the Mon-Khmer branch. Unlike its neighbors Thai and Vietnamese, Khmer is not a tonal language; instead, it employs a register system to distinguish meaning. The language is overwhelmingly analytic and isolating in its morphology, relying on word order, particles, and auxiliary words rather than inflectional affixes to encode grammatical relationships.

## 2. Word Order: Subject-Verb-Object

Khmer follows a basic Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) word order, which is consistent across declarative statements. Modifiers such as adjectives and relative clauses follow the noun they modify, and adverbs typically follow the verb.

- កូនប្រុសអានសៀវភៅ (koun prah aan siəvpʰɨv) -- "The boy reads a book."
- ស: កូនប្រុស (the boy, subject), វ: អាន (reads, verb), អ: សៀវភៅ (book, object).

Possessors follow the possessed noun:

- ផ្ទះខ្ញុំ (pteah kɲom) -- "my house" (literally "house I").

Adjectives likewise follow the noun:

- ផ្ទះធំ (pteah thom) -- "big house" (literally "house big").

Demonstratives also appear after the noun:

- សៀវភៅនេះ (siəvpʰɨv nih) -- "this book" (literally "book this").

While SVO is the default, Khmer also permits topic-fronting and other rearrangements for pragmatic emphasis (see Section 7).

## 3. Analytic and Isolating Morphology

Khmer is classified as an analytic or isolating language. Words are predominantly monosyllabic or sesquisyllabic (one-and-a-half syllables, where an unstressed minor syllable precedes a stressed major syllable). Grammatical relationships are expressed through separate function words, word order, and context rather than through bound morphemes attached to lexical stems.

There is some derivational morphology in Khmer, inherited from older Mon-Khmer patterns, involving infixes and prefixes. For example:

- ដើរ (daə) "to walk" can be related to ដំណើរ (dɑmnaə) "a journey, a trip" through an infix.
- លក់ (lʊək) "to sell" relates to ផ្សារ (psaa) "market" through broader lexical derivation.

However, these derivational processes are largely fossilized and not productive in modern spoken Khmer. Speakers do not freely create new words using infixation the way agglutinative or fusional languages form new words through regular morphological processes.

## 4. No Inflectional Morphology

Khmer lacks inflectional morphology entirely. Verbs do not conjugate for tense, aspect, mood, person, or number. Nouns do not decline for case, number, or gender. Consequently, the same verb form appears regardless of the subject or time reference:

- ខ្ញុំទៅ (kɲom tɨv) -- "I go."
- គាត់ទៅ (koat tɨv) -- "He/she goes."
- ខ្ញុំទៅម្សិលមិញ (kɲom tɨv msəl mɨɲ) -- "I went yesterday."
- ខ្ញុំនឹងទៅ (kɲom nɨng tɨv) -- "I will go."

In the above examples, the verb ទៅ (tɨv, "go") remains unchanged. Temporal meaning is conveyed by time adverbials like ម្សិលមិញ (msəl mɨɲ, "yesterday") or by the future marker នឹង (nɨng). Similarly, plurality is indicated by context or by adding words like ជាច្រើន (cie craən, "many") rather than by modifying the noun itself.

## 5. Phonology: The Register System

Khmer is notable among Southeast Asian languages for being non-tonal. Instead, it uses a phonation-based register system. The Khmer script has two series of consonant symbols, traditionally called the first series (អក្សរ​ទី​មួយ) and the second series (អក្សរ​ទី​ពីរ). Each series determines the inherent vowel quality of the syllable:

- First series consonants produce vowels with a breathy or lower phonation.
- Second series consonants produce vowels with a clear or higher phonation.

For example, the consonant letter ក in the first series gives the inherent vowel /ɑː/, while the same letter in the second series context yields /ɔː/. This system effectively doubles the vowel inventory, giving Khmer one of the largest vowel systems in the world, with estimates ranging from 18 to over 30 distinct vowel nuclei depending on the analysis (counting short/long distinctions and diphthongs).

Khmer also features a rich system of initial consonant clusters, many of which have been simplified in colloquial speech but are preserved in the orthography. Common cluster types include:

- ក្រ (kr-), as in ក្រុង (krong, "city")
- ស្រ (sr-), as in ស្រុក (srok, "district, country")
- ព្រ (pr-), as in ព្រៃ (prey, "forest")

These clusters are a distinguishing feature of Khmer compared to Thai and Lao, which have fewer permissible initial clusters.

## 6. Classifier System

Khmer uses a system of classifiers (also called numeral classifiers or measure words) when counting or quantifying nouns. The classifier is placed after the numeral, and the entire numeral-classifier phrase follows the noun:

- សៀវភៅបីក្បាល (siəvpʰɨv bəy kbaal) -- "three books" (literally "book three CLF").

Here ក្បាល (kbaal, whose literal meaning is "head") serves as the classifier for books. Different semantic classes of nouns require different classifiers:

- នាក់ (neak) for people: មនុស្សបីនាក់ (mɔnuh bəy neak) -- "three people."
- ក្បាល (kbaal) for animals and bound volumes: ឆ្កែពីរក្បាល (cʰkae pii kbaal) -- "two dogs."
- ដើម (daəm) for trees and plants: ដើមឈើបួនដើម (daəm cʰəə buən daəm) -- "four trees."
- គ្រឿង (krɨəng) for machines and vehicles: ឡានមួយគ្រឿង (laan muəy krɨəng) -- "one car."

The classifier is obligatory in formal or careful speech when a numeral is present. In casual speech, some classifiers may be omitted, but their use is considered grammatically correct and standard.

## 7. Topic-Comment Structure

While Khmer is fundamentally SVO, it frequently employs topic-comment constructions in which a constituent is fronted to the beginning of the sentence to establish the discourse topic, followed by a comment about that topic:

- សៀវភៅនេះ ខ្ញុំអានហើយ (siəvpʰɨv nih kɲom aan haəy) -- "This book, I have already read (it)."

In this sentence, សៀវភៅនេះ ("this book") is the topic, and the rest is the comment. The object has been fronted from its canonical post-verbal position. Topic-comment constructions are pervasive in spoken Khmer and are used to signal emphasis, contrast, or given information.

Another example:

- រឿងនោះ គាត់មិនដឹងទេ (rɨəng nuh koat mɨn dəng teː) -- "That matter, he does not know."

## 8. Serial Verb Constructions

Khmer makes extensive use of serial verb constructions (SVCs), in which two or more verbs are strung together in a single clause without overt coordination or subordination markers. The verbs share a common subject and describe components of a single complex event:

- គាត់ដើរទៅផ្សារ (koat daə tɨv psaa) -- "He walks (to) go (to the) market."

Here ដើរ (daə, "walk") and ទៅ (tɨv, "go") combine to describe the manner and direction of movement. Other examples:

- យកសៀវភៅមកអោយខ្ញុំ (yɔːk siəvpʰɨv mɔːk aoy kɲom) -- "Take the book, come, give (it to) me."

This chain of verbs -- យក ("take"), មក ("come"), អោយ ("give") -- functions as a single predicate expressing the complex action of bringing something to someone. Serial verb constructions are a core feature of Khmer syntax and account for many expressions that would require prepositions, conjunctions, or subordinate clauses in European languages.

## 9. Particles and Their Grammatical Roles

Khmer relies heavily on particles -- short, uninflected function words -- to convey grammatical meaning including tense, aspect, mood, politeness, and discourse function.

**Aspect and Tense Particles:**

- កំពុង (kɑmpung) marks progressive aspect: ខ្ញុំកំពុងធ្វើការ (kɲom kɑmpung tvəə kaa) -- "I am working."
- បាន (baan) marks completed action or past reference: គាត់បានទៅ (koat baan tɨv) -- "He went / He has gone."
- នឹង (nɨng) marks future intention: យើងនឹងទៅ (yəəng nɨng tɨv) -- "We will go."
- ហើយ (haəy) marks completion or change of state: ខ្ញុំញ៉ាំហើយ (kɲom ɲam haəy) -- "I have (already) eaten."

**Politeness Particles:**

- Khmer has sentence-final particles that index social register. For example, បាទ (baat) is used by male speakers and ចាស (caah) by female speakers to express politeness and affirmation.

**Discourse Particles:**

- ណា (naa) can soften a statement or add a persuasive tone.
- ណាស់ (nah) intensifies an adjective: ល្អណាស់ (lʔɑː nah) -- "very good."

These particles are essential to natural Khmer expression and carry substantial communicative weight despite their minimal phonological form.

## 10. Negation Patterns

Negation in Khmer is formed by placing the negative marker មិន (mɨn) before the verb and the particle ទេ (teː) at the end of the clause. This circumfixal negation pattern is one of the most recognizable features of Khmer syntax:

- ខ្ញុំមិនដឹងទេ (kɲom mɨn dəng teː) -- "I do not know."
- គាត់មិនមកទេ (koat mɨn mɔːk teː) -- "He/she does not come."

The particle ទេ is generally required in standard negation and serves to complete the negative construction. Omitting ទេ can sound abrupt or incomplete, though it does occur in rapid informal speech.

For prohibitive or imperative negation ("don't"), the form អត់ (ʔɑt) or កុំ (kom) is used:

- កុំទៅ (kom tɨv) -- "Don't go."
- អត់មានទេ (ʔɑt mien teː) -- "There is none / (I) don't have (any)."

The word អត់ (ʔɑt) is widely used in colloquial Khmer as an alternative to មិន, often implying non-existence or lack:

- ខ្ញុំអត់ចេះទេ (kɲom ʔɑt ceh teː) -- "I don't know how to (do it)."

## 11. Question Formation

Khmer forms questions without altering the word order of the declarative sentence. Instead, question particles or question words are inserted into the sentence.

**Yes/No Questions:**

The particle ទេ (teː) at the end of a statement, often preceded by an optional rising intonation, can form a yes/no question. More explicitly, the construction ...ឬទេ (rɨɨ teː) is used:

- គាត់មកឬទេ? (koat mɔːk rɨɨ teː?) -- "Is he/she coming (or not)?"

An alternative is the particle ទេ? alone with appropriate intonation:

- អ្នកចេះភាសាខ្មែរទេ? (neak ceh pʰiːəsaː kʰmaːe teː?) -- "Do you know Khmer?"

**Content (WH-) Questions:**

Khmer question words typically appear in situ -- that is, in the same position as the constituent they replace, rather than being fronted as in English:

- អ្នកទៅណា? (neak tɨv naa?) -- "Where are you going?" (literally "you go where?")
- នេះជាអ្វី? (nih cie ʔvəy?) -- "What is this?" (literally "this is what?")
- អ្នកណាមក? (neak naa mɔːk?) -- "Who is coming?" (literally "who come?")
- ថ្លៃប៉ុន្មាន? (tlay ponmaan?) -- "How much does it cost?" (literally "expensive how-much?")

Common question words include:

| Khmer | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| អ្វី | ʔvəy | what |
| ណា | naa | where / which |
| អ្នកណា | neak naa | who |
| ពេលណា | peel naa | when |
| យ៉ាងម៉េច | yaang maec | how |
| ប៉ុន្មាន | ponmaan | how many / how much |
| ហេតុអ្វី | haet ʔvəy | why |

## 12. Summary

Khmer grammar is characterized by its analytic structure, fixed SVO word order with pragmatic flexibility through topicalization, absence of inflectional morphology, and reliance on particles and serial verb constructions to express meanings that other languages encode morphologically. Its non-tonal register-based phonology, extensive vowel inventory, and classifier system further distinguish it typologically. Understanding these features is essential for accurate linguistic analysis and for natural language processing of Khmer text.
