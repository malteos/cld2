# Chinese — macrolanguage (zho)

## Summary

`zho` is the ISO-639-3 **macrolanguage** code covering the Sinitic branch of Sino-Tibetan: Mandarin (cmn), Cantonese (yue), Wu (wuu), Min (nan/hak cluster), Xiang (hsn), Gan (gan), Hakka (hak), and others. ISO codes: 639-1 `zh`, 639-2 `chi`/`zho`, 639-3 `zho` (macro). The Chinese name is 中文 (Zhōngwén) or 汉语 (Hànyǔ). Combined L1 speakers exceed 1.3 billion. Principal regions are China, Taiwan, Singapore, Hong Kong, Macau, Malaysia, and global diaspora.

## Writing system

The `zho_Hans` row is **Simplified Han**, the script used in mainland China, Singapore and Malaysia. Characters appear without word spacing. Core simplified markers: 国 个 会 来 为 万 实 这 关 学 发 东 车 电 书 门. Traditional equivalents 國 個 會 來 為 萬 實 這 關 學 發 東 車 電 書 門 are absent from Simplified text. Full-width punctuation (。 ， 、 ； ： ！ ？ 「」 《》 （）) is standard. Arabic and Chinese numerals co-occur.

## Status vs. cmn

In practice, written `zho_Hans` corpora are dominated by standard Mandarin (Putonghua); the row is essentially identical to `cmn_Hans` at sentence level. The macrocode exists for corpora tagged generically "Chinese" where the speaker variety is unknown or mixed. Choose `zho` when the source did not specify a variety, `cmn` when Putonghua is explicit.
