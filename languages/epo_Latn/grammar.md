# Esperanto Grammar

## Word Order

Esperanto has a default Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) word order, but because the accusative case marker (-n) clearly identifies the direct object, word order is relatively free. All of the following sentences are grammatically correct and mean "The cat catches the mouse":

- **La kato kaptas la muson.** (SVO - most common)
- **La muson kaptas la kato.** (OVS)
- **Kaptas la kato la muson.** (VSO)

This flexibility allows speakers to adjust word order for emphasis, style, or poetic effect. Despite this freedom, SVO remains the unmarked and most frequent order in everyday speech and writing.

## Phonology

Esperanto has a straightforward phonological system:

- **Vowels (5):** a, e, i, o, u - pronounced as in Spanish or Italian (pure monophthongs)
- **Consonants (23):** b, c (ts), ĉ (tʃ), d, f, g (always hard), ĝ (dʒ), h, ĥ (x, voiceless velar fricative), j (j as in "yes"), ĵ (ʒ), k, l, m, n, o, p, r (trilled or tapped), s (always voiceless), ŝ (ʃ), t, ŭ (w, used in diphthongs aŭ, eŭ), v, z
- **Diphthongs:** aŭ (as in "how"), eŭ (no English equivalent; approximately "eh-oo" said quickly)
- **Tone:** None. Esperanto is not a tonal language.
- **Stress:** Always on the penultimate (second-to-last) syllable, without exception. This is one of the most rigid rules of Esperanto. Examples: es-PE-ran-to, lin-GVO, do-MO, in-ter-NA-ci-a.

Each letter represents exactly one sound, and each sound is represented by exactly one letter. There are no silent letters and no ambiguous pronunciations.

## Noun System

Esperanto nouns are remarkably regular:

- **Base form:** All nouns end in **-o** (domo = house, libro = book, kato = cat)
- **Plural:** Add **-j** (domoj = houses, libroj = books, katoj = cats). Pronounced as a diphthong: /oj/.
- **Accusative:** Add **-n** to mark the direct object (domon = house [as object], libron = book [as object])
- **Plural accusative:** Both suffixes combine: **-ojn** (domojn, librojn, katojn)
- **Article:** There is one definite article: **la** (invariable for number, case, or gender). There is no indefinite article; a bare noun is indefinite: *domo* = "a house," *la domo* = "the house."
- **Gender:** Esperanto has no grammatical gender. Nouns do not belong to masculine, feminine, or neuter classes. Natural sex can be indicated with suffixes: *patro* (father), *patrino* (mother), using the suffix *-in-* for feminine. The modern community also uses *-iĉ-* (unofficial) for explicitly masculine marking.

## Verb System

The Esperanto verb system is completely regular with no exceptions, no irregular verbs, and no conjugation by person or number:

### Tense and Mood Suffixes

| Suffix | Function | Example (with *lerni* = to learn) |
|--------|----------|-----------------------------------|
| **-i** | Infinitive | lerni (to learn) |
| **-as** | Present tense | mi lernas (I learn / I am learning) |
| **-is** | Past tense | mi lernis (I learned / I was learning) |
| **-os** | Future tense | mi lernos (I will learn) |
| **-us** | Conditional | mi lernus (I would learn) |
| **-u** | Imperative/Jussive | lernu! (learn!) |

Verbs do not change for person or number: *mi lernas, vi lernas, li lernas, ni lernas, ili lernas* (I learn, you learn, he learns, we learn, they learn).

### Participles

Esperanto has six participles, formed from combinations of tense (past, present, future) and voice (active, passive):

**Active participles:**
- **-anta** (present active): lernanta = learning
- **-inta** (past active): lerninta = having learned
- **-onta** (future active): lernonta = about to learn

**Passive participles:**
- **-ata** (present passive): lernata = being learned
- **-ita** (past passive): lernita = (having been) learned
- **-ota** (future passive): lernota = about to be learned

Participles can function as adjectives (ending in -a), nouns (ending in -o), or adverbs (ending in -e). They also combine with the verb *esti* (to be) to form compound tenses: *mi estas lernanta* (I am learning), *la leciono estas lernita* (the lesson has been learned).

## Adjectives and Adverbs

- **Adjectives** end in **-a** and agree with their noun in number and case: *bona libro* (good book), *bonaj libroj* (good books), *bonan libron* (good book, accusative).
- **Adverbs** end in **-e**: *bone* (well), *rapide* (quickly), *feliĉe* (happily).
- **Comparison:** *pli... ol* (more... than), *plej* (most), *malpli* (less), *malplej* (least). Example: *pli granda ol* (bigger than), *la plej granda* (the biggest).

## Word Building

Esperanto's agglutinative morphology allows extensive word building through prefixes and suffixes applied to roots. This is one of the language's most powerful features, allowing speakers to derive many words from a small number of roots.

### Common Prefixes

| Prefix | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **mal-** | Opposite | bona (good) -> malbona (bad) |
| **ek-** | Beginning of action | kanti (to sing) -> ekkanti (to start singing) |
| **re-** | Again / back | veni (to come) -> reveni (to come back) |
| **ge-** | Both sexes | patroj (fathers) -> gepatroj (parents) |
| **dis-** | Dispersal | semi (to sow) -> dissemi (to disseminate) |
| **mis-** | Wrongly | kompreni (to understand) -> miskompreni (to misunderstand) |

### Common Suffixes

| Suffix | Meaning | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **-ej-** | Place | lerni (to learn) -> lernejo (school) |
| **-ist-** | Professional / adherent | dento (tooth) -> dentisto (dentist) |
| **-ig-** | To cause to be | pura (clean) -> purigi (to clean) |
| **-iĝ-** | To become | ruĝa (red) -> ruĝiĝi (to become red, to blush) |
| **-ul-** | Person characterized by | juna (young) -> junulo (young person) |
| **-aĵ-** | Concrete thing | manĝi (to eat) -> manĝaĵo (food) |
| **-ec-** | Abstract quality | bela (beautiful) -> beleco (beauty) |
| **-ar-** | Collection | arbo (tree) -> arbaro (forest) |
| **-an-** | Member / inhabitant | klubo (club) -> klubano (club member) |
| **-il-** | Tool / instrument | tranĉi (to cut) -> tranĉilo (knife) |
| **-in-** | Feminine | patro (father) -> patrino (mother) |
| **-ebl-** | Possibility | manĝi (to eat) -> manĝebla (edible) |
| **-ind-** | Worthy of | laŭdi (to praise) -> laŭdinda (praiseworthy) |

## Correlative Table

Esperanto features a systematic table of 45 correlative words formed by combining 5 beginnings with 9 endings:

| | -o (thing) | -u (person) | -a (kind) | -e (place) | -am (time) | -al (reason) | -el (manner) | -om (quantity) | -es (possession) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ki-** (question) | kio | kiu | kia | kie | kiam | kial | kiel | kiom | kies |
| **ti-** (demonstrative) | tio | tiu | tia | tie | tiam | tial | tiel | tiom | ties |
| **i-** (indefinite) | io | iu | ia | ie | iam | ial | iel | iom | ies |
| **ĉi-** (universal) | ĉio | ĉiu | ĉia | ĉie | ĉiam | ĉial | ĉiel | ĉiom | ĉies |
| **neni-** (negative) | nenio | neniu | nenia | nenie | neniam | nenial | neniel | neniom | nenies |

## Syntax Notes

- The accusative marker **-n** can also indicate direction of motion: *mi iras hejmen* (I go homeward).
- Prepositions govern the nominative case, except when showing direction: *mi estas en la domo* (I am in the house) vs. *mi iras en la domon* (I go into the house).
- Double negatives are not used: *mi neniam vidas neniun* is incorrect; use *mi neniam vidas iun* (I never see anyone).
- Questions are formed with the particle **ĉu** for yes/no questions: *Ĉu vi parolas Esperanton?* (Do you speak Esperanto?)
