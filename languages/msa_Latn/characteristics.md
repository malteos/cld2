# Malay LID characteristics

## Script signals

26-letter Latin, no diacritics. Hyphenated reduplication (`kanak-kanak`,
`tiba-tiba`, `orang-orang`, `sama-sama`) is a strong Malay/Indonesian
signal. Digraphs `ng`, `ny`, `sy`, `kh` are common. Native words rarely
use `f`, `v`, `z`, `x`, `q`.

## Top function words

`yang`, `dan`, `di`, `ke`, `dari`, `untuk`, `pada`, `dengan`, `dalam`,
`akan`, `sudah`, `belum`, `tidak`, `bukan`, `ini`, `itu`, `ialah`,
`adalah`, `ada`, `tetapi`, `atau`, `juga`, `saya`, `awak`, `kami`,
`kita`, `mereka`, `dia`, `oleh`, `bagi`, `kerana`, `sebagai`, `boleh`,
`mahu`, `iaitu`.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- High frequency of `yang`, `-nya` enclitic, and `kerana` (not `karena`).
- `meN-` allomorphy: `mem-`, `men-`, `meng-`, `meny-` (`membaca`,
  `menulis`, `mengambil`, `menyapu`).
- `ber-` stative/reciprocal verbs: `berjalan`, `bermain`, `bersama`.
- `pe(N)-...-an` nominalisation: `pembangunan`, `perhubungan`,
  `pengajaran`.
- `ke-...-an` abstract nouns: `kebebasan`, `keadilan`, `keselamatan`.
- Suffixal verbs `-kan`, `-i`: `memberikan`, `menghampiri`.

## Morphology hooks

The derivational template `{me|di|ber|ter|pe|se|ke}STEM{kan|i|an|nya}` is
the single most productive morphological footprint. Bound pronouns
`-ku`/`-mu`/`-nya` are frequent word-final n-grams.

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

Plain ASCII punctuation. Malaysian texts use `RM` (ringgit) before digits;
Brunei uses `B$`. Month names: `Januari … Disember` (Malay spells
December as `Disember`, Indonesian as `Desember` — a useful orthographic
tell).
