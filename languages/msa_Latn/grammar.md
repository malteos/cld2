# Malay grammar

- **Word order**: SVO, analytic. Modifiers follow head nouns
  (`rumah besar`). Existential and locative clauses commonly front locative
  PPs (`Di sini ada banyak orang`).
- **Morphology**: Mildly agglutinative. Productive prefixes `meN-`, `di-`,
  `ber-`, `peN-`, `ter-`, `se-` combine with suffixes `-kan`, `-i`, `-an`
  and circumfixes `ke-...-an`, `peN-...-an`. Reduplication (`budak-budak`,
  `cantik-cantik`) marks plurality, variety, intensity.
- **Nouns**: No grammatical gender, number, or case. Classifiers
  (`seorang`, `seekor`, `sebuah`, `sebatang`, `sekeping`) quantify.
  Possession is expressed by juxtaposition (`buku saya` "my book") or the
  enclitic `-nya`.
- **Verbs**: No tense/person agreement. Aspect/mood particles: `sudah`
  (perfective), `sedang` (progressive), `akan` (future), `belum` (not
  yet), `pernah` (ever), `masih` (still). Voice opposition active `meN-` /
  patient-focus `di-` / accidental `ter-`.
- **Phonology**: Six vowels `a e i o u ə`; open syllables dominant;
  penultimate stress. Native inventory lacks /f/, /v/, /z/; these appear
  only in loanwords and are sometimes reduced to /p/, /b/, /s/.
- **Syntax notes**: Pro-drop; zero copula (`Dia guru` "He is a teacher");
  equative `ialah` vs attributive `adalah`; relativiser `yang`; negation
  `tidak`/`tak` (verbs, adjectives) and `bukan` (nouns). Wh-in-situ is
  typical: `Awak nak pergi mana?` "Where do you want to go?"
