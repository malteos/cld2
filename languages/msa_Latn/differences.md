# Malay (macro) vs confusable languages

Malay (`msa`) is the macrolanguage covering Malaysian Standard (`zsm`),
Brunei, Riau, Kedah, Kelantan and several other varieties. It is in the
CLD2 **Indonesian / Malay** close-set together with `ind` and `zsm`.

## vs Indonesian (ind)

- **Lexicon** is the dominant signal: Malay `mahu`, `kerana`, `ahli`,
  `boleh`, `ialah`, `iaitu`, `bilik`, `percuma`, `pejabat`, `farmasi`,
  `universiti` vs Indonesian `mau`, `karena`, `anggota`, `bisa`,
  `adalah`, `yaitu`, `kamar`, `gratis`, `kantor`, `apotek`, `universitas`.
- **Month name**: `Disember` (MY) vs `Desember` (ID).
- **"car"**: Malay `kereta`, Indonesian `mobil`; "train" is `kereta api`
  (MY) but just `kereta` is ambiguous.
- **Loan origin**: Malay takes from English (`stesen`, `bas`, `kaunter`);
  Indonesian from Dutch (`stasiun`, `bus`/`bis`, `loket`).

## vs Standard Malay (zsm)

`zsm` is the Malaysia-specific written norm inside `msa`. When text
contains Malaysian-only lexemes (`kaunter`, `bas`, `stesen`, `kompaun`,
`cadangan kerajaan Persekutuan`), prefer `zsm`. Brunei-specific tokens
(`Titah`, `Kebawah Duli Yang Maha Mulia`) push toward `msa` macro.

## vs Javanese / Sundanese

Web text in Javanese or Sundanese often carries heavy Malay borrowing;
demand language-internal markers (`mboten`, `sampeyan` for Javanese;
`abdi`, `teu`, `aya` for Sundanese) before deciding against Malay.
