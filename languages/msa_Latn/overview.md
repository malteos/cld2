# Malay macro-language (msa)

## Summary

Malay (endonym: *Bahasa Melayu*) is an Austronesian language in the
Malayo-Polynesian → Malayic branch. ISO codes: 639-1 `ms`, 639-2 `may`
(bibliographic) / `msa` (terminological), 639-3 `msa` (macrolanguage).
The macrolanguage covers Malaysian Standard Malay (`zsm`), Brunei Malay,
Riau Malay, Kedah, Kelantan-Pattani, and several regional lects. Together
the group has roughly 200–250 million speakers when second-language users
across Malaysia, Brunei, Singapore, southern Thailand and Sumatra are
included. Malay has been the maritime lingua franca of insular Southeast
Asia for well over a millennium.

## Writing system

Modern Malay uses the 26-letter Latin alphabet ("Rumi") with no diacritics
after the 1972 joint spelling reform (MABBIM) that aligned Malaysian and
Indonesian orthographies. The hyphen marks reduplication (`budak-budak`,
`cepat-cepat`). A separate Arabic-derived script called *Jawi* is still
used in religious and cultural contexts (Brunei officially uses both), but
CommonLID data and the vast majority of web text is in Rumi.

## History and status

Classical Malay served as the administrative and literary language of the
Malacca, Johor and Aceh sultanates from the 15th century onward. It is the
parent of standardised Indonesian (`ind`) and Malaysian Malay (`zsm`), and
the `msa` macro-tag is typically applied when the specific variety is
unknown or when content is regionally mixed.
