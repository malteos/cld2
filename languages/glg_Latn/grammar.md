# Galician Grammar

## Typological Overview

- **Word order**: SVO (Subject-Verb-Object) as the default, but with considerable flexibility due to fusional morphology; VSO and OVS orders are common in questions, focus constructions, and subordinate clauses
- **Morphological type**: Fusional/inflectional, with rich verbal morphology and agreement marking
- **Alignment**: Nominative-accusative

## Phonology

### Vowels

Galician has a seven-vowel system in stressed position: /a/, /e/, /E/ (open e), /i/, /o/, /O/ (open o), /u/. This distinction between open and close mid vowels is phonemic and distinguishes meaning (e.g., "oso" /oso/ "bear" vs. "oso" /Oso/ "bone", or "pe" /pe/ "foot" vs. "pe" /pE/ used in set phrases). In unstressed positions, the system reduces somewhat, though not as dramatically as in Portuguese.

Some western and central dialects preserve nasal vowels, a feature inherited from Galician-Portuguese and still present in modern Portuguese but largely absent from the standard Galician norm. The diphthong system includes both falling diphthongs (ei, ou, oi, ai, eu, ui) and rising diphthongs (ia, ie, io, ua, ue, uo).

### Consonants

The consonant inventory includes a notable dialectal feature called the gheada, in which the voiced velar stop /g/ is realized as a fricative [h], [x], or [G] in certain positions. While widespread in spoken Galician across large areas of western and central Galicia, the gheada is not reflected in standard orthography and is sometimes stigmatized.

Another important dialectal feature is seseo (merging of /s/ and /T/ into [s]), found in western coastal areas. The standard norm maintains the distinction between "s" /s/ and "z/c(e,i)" /T/.

Galician does not have the phoneme /x/ (Spanish "j" sound) in inherited vocabulary; words with this sound are typically borrowings from Spanish.

## Noun System

### Gender

Galician has two grammatical genders: masculine and feminine. Gender assignment follows patterns similar to other Romance languages, with some notable differences from Spanish and Portuguese. Most nouns ending in "-o" are masculine, and most ending in "-a" are feminine, but there are exceptions. Some nouns have different genders than their Spanish or Portuguese cognates (e.g., "a ponte" is feminine in Galician, while "el puente" is masculine in Spanish).

### Number

Plural formation follows regular patterns: nouns ending in a vowel add "-s" (casa > casas), nouns ending in "-r," "-z," or "-n" typically add "-es" (animal > animais, luz > luces, can > cans). Nouns ending in "-l" drop the "-l" and add "-is" (animal > animais, papel > papeis), which is a distinctive feature compared to Spanish (animales, papeles).

### Articles

The definite articles are: **o** (masc. sg.), **a** (fem. sg.), **os** (masc. pl.), **as** (fem. pl.). These are identical to Portuguese and differ from Spanish (el, la, los, las).

The indefinite articles are: **un** (masc. sg.), **unha** (fem. sg.), **uns** (masc. pl.), **unhas** (fem. pl.). The feminine form "unha" is distinctive and different from both Spanish "una" and Portuguese "uma."

### Article Contractions

Galician has extensive contractions of prepositions with articles:

- de + o = **do**, de + a = **da**, de + os = **dos**, de + as = **das**
- en + o = **no**, en + a = **na**, en + os = **nos**, en + as = **nas**
- a + o = **ao**, a + a = **a** (written with grave accent in some contexts)
- por + o = **polo**, por + a = **pola**, por + os = **polos**, por + as = **polas**
- con + o = **co**, con + a = **coa**, con + os = **cos**, con + as = **coas**

The contractions with "con" (co, coa, cos, coas) are highly distinctive of Galician and do not exist in either Spanish or Portuguese.

## Verb System

### Overview

The Galician verb system is highly inflected, with conjugations encoding person, number, tense, aspect, and mood. There are three conjugation classes based on the infinitive ending: -ar (first), -er (second), -ir (third).

### Personal Infinitive

Like Portuguese (and unlike Spanish), Galician has a **personal infinitive** (infinitivo conxugado) that can be inflected for person and number. This allows infinitive clauses to have overt subjects:

- falar (to speak), falares (for you to speak), falar (for him/her to speak), falarmos (for us to speak), falardes (for you pl. to speak), falaren (for them to speak)

This is a major structural feature that distinguishes Galician from Spanish and aligns it with Portuguese.

### Future Subjunctive

Galician retains the **future subjunctive** (subxuntivo futuro), a form that has been lost in most other Romance languages except Portuguese:

- "Cando eu falar..." (When I speak/will speak...)
- "Se ti quixeres..." (If you want/will want...)

### Indicative Tenses

Key indicative tenses include:

- **Present**: falo, falas, fala, falamos, falades, falan
- **Preterite** (simple past): falei, falaches, falou, falamos, falastes, falaron
- **Imperfect**: falaba, falabas, falaba, falabamos, falabades, falaban
- **Synthetic pluperfect** (mais-que-perfecto sintetico): falara, falaras, falara, falaramos, falarades, falaran. This synthetic form is used in literary and formal registers and functions as a pluperfect or as a conditional in some contexts.
- **Future**: falarei, falaras, falara, falaremos, falaredes, falaran
- **Conditional**: falaria, falarias, falaria, falariamos, falariades, falarian

### Compound Tenses

Compound past tenses are formed with the auxiliary verb **ter** (not "haber" as in Spanish):

- "Teño falado" (I have spoken)
- "Tina falado" (I had spoken)

The use of "ter" as the primary auxiliary is shared with Portuguese and contrasts with Spanish, which uses "haber."

### Clitic Placement

Clitic pronoun placement in Galician follows specific rules:

- **Enclisis** (clitic after the verb) is the default in affirmative main clauses: "Douche o libro" (I gave him the book)
- **Proclisis** (clitic before the verb) occurs after negation, subordinating conjunctions, adverbs, and in subordinate clauses: "Non che dou o libro" (I don't give you the book)
- **Mesoclisis** (clitic inserted within the verb) can occur with future and conditional forms in some literary or archaic registers: "Darache o libro" (I will give you the book), though this is rare in modern usage

### Other Notable Features

- The verb "haber" in its impersonal form "hai" is used for existential constructions ("Hai moita xente" = There are many people)
- The gerund ends in "-ndo" (falando, comendo, vivindo), as in Spanish and Portuguese
- Reflexive constructions are widespread, as in other Ibero-Romance languages
- The periphrastic construction "ir + infinitive" is used for the near future, and "estar a + infinitive" for the progressive aspect, paralleling Portuguese usage

## Syntax

### Relative Clauses

Galician uses "que" as the primary relative pronoun, with "quen" for animate referents in oblique positions. Prepositional relative clauses use article + "que" or article + "cal/cales."

### Question Formation

Questions can be formed by inversion (VSO order) or simply by intonation, as in other Romance languages. Interrogative pronouns include "que" (what), "quen" (who), "cal/cales" (which), "canto/a/os/as" (how much/many), "como" (how), "onde" (where), "cando" (when), "por que" (why).

### Negation

Simple negation uses "non" before the verb. Double negation is standard: "Non veu ninguén" (Nobody came, lit. "Not came nobody"). This parallels both Spanish and Portuguese patterns.

## Key Differences from Spanish

- Personal infinitive (absent in Spanish)
- Future subjunctive (lost in Spanish)
- Articles o/a/os/as (vs. el/la/los/las)
- Auxiliary "ter" for compound tenses (vs. "haber")
- Plural "-is" for words ending in "-l" (animais vs. animales)
- Contractions with "con" (co, coa vs. con el, con la)
- Clitic placement rules differ in details

## Key Differences from Portuguese

- Spelling conventions differ (Galician follows more Spanish-influenced orthographic norms)
- "-cion" endings (vs. Portuguese "-cao")
- "unha" (vs. Portuguese "uma")
- "non" (vs. Portuguese "nao")
- Less vowel reduction in unstressed syllables
- No nasal diphthongs in the standard norm (vs. Portuguese -ao, -oe, -ae)
- Different treatment of some consonant clusters
