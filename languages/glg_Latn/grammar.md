# Galician Grammar

## Typological Overview

- **Word order**: SVO (Subject-Verb-Object) as the default, but with considerable flexibility due to fusional morphology; VSO and OVS orders are common in questions, focus constructions, and subordinate clauses
- **Morphological type**: Fusional/inflectional, with rich verbal morphology and agreement marking
- **Alignment**: Nominative-accusative

## Phonology

### Vowels

Galician has a seven-vowel system in stressed position: /a/, /e/, /ɛ/ (open e), /i/, /o/, /ɔ/ (open o), /u/. This distinction between open and close mid vowels is phonemic and distinguishes meaning (e.g., "oso" /ˈoso/ "bear" vs. "óso" /ˈɔso/ "bone", or "pé" /pɛ/ "foot" vs. "pe" /pe/ "the letter p"). In a few such pairs the standard spelling marks the difference with a diacritic accent (óso, pé, é "is" vs. e "and", nós vs. nos, fóra vs. fora); otherwise the open/close contrast is not written. In unstressed positions, the system reduces somewhat, though not as dramatically as in Portuguese.

Some western and central dialects preserve nasal vowels, a feature inherited from Galician-Portuguese and still present in modern Portuguese but largely absent from the standard Galician norm. The diphthong system includes both falling diphthongs (ei, ou, oi, ai, eu, ui) and rising diphthongs (ia, ie, io, ua, ue, uo).

### Consonants

The consonant inventory includes a notable dialectal feature called the gheada, in which the voiced velar stop /g/ is realized as a fricative [ħ], [h], [x], or [ɣ] in certain positions. While widespread in spoken Galician across large areas of western and central Galicia, the gheada is not reflected in standard orthography and is sometimes stigmatized.

Another important dialectal feature is seseo (merging of /s/ and /θ/ into [s]), found in western coastal areas. The standard norm maintains the distinction between "s" /s/ and "z/c(e,i)" /θ/.

Galician does not have the phoneme /x/ (Spanish "j" sound) in inherited vocabulary; words with this sound are typically borrowings from Spanish. The letter "x" in standard Galician spells /ʃ/ (xente, xa, xuño), and "ñ" spells /ɲ/ (teño, viño, España).

## Noun System

### Gender

Galician has two grammatical genders: masculine and feminine. Gender assignment follows patterns similar to other Romance languages, with some notable differences from Spanish and Portuguese. Most nouns ending in "-o" are masculine, and most ending in "-a" are feminine, but there are exceptions. Some nouns have different genders than their Spanish or Portuguese cognates (e.g., "a ponte" is feminine in Galician, while "el puente" is masculine in Spanish).

### Number

Plural formation follows regular patterns: nouns ending in a vowel add "-s" (casa > casas), nouns ending in "-r" or "-z" add "-es" (flor > flores, luz > luces), and nouns ending in "-n" add "-s" (can > cans, camión > camións). Nouns ending in "-l" drop the "-l" and add "-is" (animal > animais, papel > papeis), which is a distinctive feature compared to Spanish (animales, papeles).

### Articles

The definite articles are: **o** (masc. sg.), **a** (fem. sg.), **os** (masc. pl.), **as** (fem. pl.). These are identical to Portuguese and differ from Spanish (el, la, los, las).

The indefinite articles are: **un** (masc. sg.), **unha** (fem. sg.), **uns** (masc. pl.), **unhas** (fem. pl.). The feminine form "unha" is distinctive and different from both Spanish "una" and Portuguese "uma."

### Article Contractions

Galician has extensive contractions of prepositions with articles:

- de + o = **do**, de + a = **da**, de + os = **dos**, de + as = **das**
- en + o = **no**, en + a = **na**, en + os = **nos**, en + as = **nas**
- a + o = **ao**, a + a = **á**, a + os = **aos**, a + as = **ás** (written with an acute accent in Galician, unlike Portuguese "à, às" with a grave accent)
- por + o = **polo**, por + a = **pola**, por + os = **polos**, por + as = **polas**
- con + o = **co**, con + a = **coa**, con + os = **cos**, con + as = **coas**

The contractions with "con" (co, coa, cos, coas) are standard written forms in Galician and are very frequent in running text. Spanish has no such forms (con el, con la). Standard written Portuguese normally keeps "com o, com a", although contracted forms such as "co", "coa", "cos", "coas" (also written c'o, c'a) do occur in colloquial, regional and poetic Portuguese. They are therefore a strong, but not absolute, Galician cue; frequent "co/coa" together with "unha", "non" and "polo/pola" is much more reliable.

## Verb System

### Overview

The Galician verb system is highly inflected, with conjugations encoding person, number, tense, aspect, and mood. There are three conjugation classes based on the infinitive ending: -ar (first), -er (second), -ir (third).

### Personal Infinitive

Like Portuguese (and unlike Spanish), Galician has a **personal infinitive** (infinitivo conxugado) that can be inflected for person and number. This allows infinitive clauses to have overt subjects:

- falar (to speak), falares (for you to speak), falar (for him/her to speak), falarmos (for us to speak), falardes (for you pl. to speak), falaren (for them to speak)

This is a major structural feature that distinguishes Galician from Spanish and aligns it with Portuguese.

### Future Subjunctive

Galician retains the **future subjunctive** (subxuntivo futuro), a form that has been lost in most other Romance languages except Portuguese, although in modern Galician it is mainly literary or formal:

- "Cando eu falar..." (When I speak/will speak...)
- "Se ti quixeres..." (If you want/will want...)

### Indicative Tenses

Key indicative tenses include:

- **Present**: falo, falas, fala, falamos, falades, falan
- **Preterite** (simple past): falei, falaches, falou, falamos, falastes, falaron
- **Imperfect**: falaba, falabas, falaba, falabamos, falabades, falaban
- **Synthetic pluperfect** (antepretérito / pretérito pluscuamperfecto): falara, falaras, falara, falaramos, falarades, falaran. This synthetic form is used in literary and formal registers and functions as a pluperfect or as a conditional in some contexts.
- **Future**: falarei, falarás, falará, falaremos, falaredes, falarán
- **Conditional**: falaría, falarías, falaría, falariamos, falariades, falarían

Note that the 1st and 2nd person plural forms of the imperfect, pluperfect and conditional (falabamos, falaramos, falariamos, falabades...) are stressed on the penultimate syllable in Galician and carry no written accent, unlike Spanish "hablábamos, hablaríamos" and Portuguese "falávamos, falaríamos".

### Compound Tenses

Compound past tenses are formed with the auxiliary verb **ter** (not "haber" as in Spanish):

- "Teño falado" (I have spoken - usually with a repeated or durative sense, "I have been speaking")
- "Tiña falado" (I had spoken)

The use of "ter" as the primary auxiliary is shared with Portuguese and contrasts with Spanish, which uses "haber." Galician uses these periphrases less than Portuguese, preferring the simple preterite (falei) for most past events.

### Clitic Placement

Clitic pronoun placement in Galician follows specific rules:

- **Enclisis** (clitic after the verb) is the default in affirmative main clauses: "Douche o libro" (I give you the book; dou + che). The preterite equivalent is "Deiche o libro" (I gave you the book). The clitic "che" is 2nd person singular (to you), not 3rd person.
- **Proclisis** (clitic before the verb) occurs after negation, subordinating conjunctions, many adverbs, and in subordinate clauses: "Non che dou o libro" (I don't give you the book)
- **Mesoclisis** (clitic inserted within the verb, as in archaic "dar-che-á") survives only in some literary or archaic registers; modern Galician writes the clitic attached to the end of the future/conditional form: "Darache o libro" (He/she will give you the book), "Dareiche o libro" (I will give you the book)

### Other Notable Features

- The verb "haber" in its impersonal form "hai" is used for existential constructions ("Hai moita xente" = There are many people)
- The gerund ends in "-ndo" (falando, comendo, vivindo), as in Spanish and Portuguese
- Reflexive constructions are widespread, as in other Ibero-Romance languages
- The periphrastic construction "ir + infinitive" is used for the near future, and "estar a + infinitive" for the progressive aspect, paralleling European Portuguese usage

## Syntax

### Relative Clauses

Galician uses "que" as the primary relative pronoun, with "quen" for animate referents in oblique positions. Prepositional relative clauses use article + "que" or article + "cal/cales."

### Question Formation

Questions can be formed by inversion (VSO order) or simply by intonation, as in other Romance languages. Interrogative pronouns include "que" (what), "quen" (who), "cal/cales" (which), "canto/a/os/as" (how much/many), "como" (how), "onde" (where), "cando" (when), "por que" (why).

### Negation

Simple negation uses "non" before the verb. Double negation is standard: "Non veu ninguén" (Nobody came, lit. "Not came nobody"). This parallels both Spanish and Portuguese patterns.

## Key Differences from Spanish

- Personal infinitive (absent in Spanish)
- Future subjunctive (lost in Spanish)
- Articles o/a/os/as (vs. el/la/los/las)
- Auxiliary "ter" for compound tenses (vs. "haber")
- Plural "-is" for words ending in "-l" (animais vs. animales)
- Contractions with "con" (co, coa vs. con el, con la)
- Letter "x" for /ʃ/ (xente, xa) where Spanish has "g/j" (gente, ya)
- Clitic placement rules differ in details

## Key Differences from Portuguese

- Spelling conventions differ (Galician follows more Spanish-influenced orthographic norms: ñ instead of nh, ll instead of lh, x for /ʃ/, no tilde on vowels)
- "-ción" endings (vs. Portuguese "-ção"): información vs. informação
- "unha" (vs. Portuguese "uma")
- "non" (vs. Portuguese "não")
- "á, ás" contractions with acute accent (vs. Portuguese "à, às")
- Less vowel reduction in unstressed syllables
- No nasal vowels or diphthongs in the standard norm (vs. Portuguese -ão, -õe, -ãe): Galician "man, cans, nación" vs. Portuguese "mão, cães, nação"
- Different treatment of some consonant clusters
