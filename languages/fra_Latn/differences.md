# French vs. confusable languages

Closest confusables in this set: **Occitan (oci)** and the French-based Creoles **rcf, gcf, gcr, acf**. Italian/Catalan share some vocabulary but are clearly distinct by diacritics.

## French vs. Occitan

- French writes `-tion` (*nation, solution*); Occitan writes `-cion` (*nacion, solucion*).
- Occitan uses `ò ç` freely (`ò` is absent in French); French uses `œ`, `â`, `ê` that Occitan avoids.
- Occitan definite articles `lo la los las` / Gascon `eth era` vs. French `le la les`.
- Occitan preserves `-a` feminines (*la taula*); French centralises to `-e` (*la table*).
- Negation `non / pas` (postverbal) in Occitan vs. obligatory `ne…pas` in French.

## French vs. French-based creoles (rcf / gcf / gcr / acf)

- Creoles drop most French diacritics (`à ç â ê` rarely appear; only `é` persists in some orthographies); French is diacritic-heavy.
- Creole subject clitics `mwen/mi/an/nou/zòt/yo` replace French `je tu il nous vous ils`.
- Creole determiners `la, a, sé, yo` follow the noun; French articles `le la les` precede.
- Creoles write phonetic `ou` for closed `u` sounds; kreyòl orthographies use `k` for `qu` (*ki* vs. *qui*) and `w` for semivowel `u` (*mwen*).
- Compound tenses with *avoir/être* are absent in creoles; they use preverbal TAM particles (*ka, té, ké, ap*).

## French vs. Italian/Catalan

- Italian has double consonants (*tt, zz, ll*), no `ç`, no `œ`, vowel-final words. Catalan uses `·l` (geminate dot), `ny`, and terminal `-ció`.
