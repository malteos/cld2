# French grammar

- **Word order**: SVO in declaratives; inversion in formal questions (*parlez-vous?*); clitic pronouns precede the finite verb (*je le vois*). Largely analytic with some residual fusion.
- **Morphology**: Fusional but heavily eroded in speech; orthography preserves silent endings (`-s`, `-ent`, `-e`) that are rarely pronounced. Written morphology is richer than spoken.
- **Nouns**: Two genders (masculine, feminine); number marked mainly on determiners since most plural `-s` are silent. Definite articles `le la les l'`; indefinite `un une des`; partitive `du de la des`.
- **Adjectives**: Agree in gender and number; usually postnominal but a closed class is prenominal (*bon, petit, grand, jeune, vieux, beau*). Liaison and elision (*l'homme, d'abord*) are productive.
- **Verbs**: Three conjugation classes (`-er`, `-ir`, `-re`), plus many irregular verbs. Rich TAM system: present, imperfect, simple past (mostly written), future, conditional, present/past subjunctive, imperative. Compound tenses use auxiliaries `avoir` and `être` plus past participle. Periphrastic future *aller + INF* dominates in speech.
- **Pronouns**: Strong subject/object distinction; obligatory subject pronouns (not pro-drop). Clitic chain follows a strict order (*me te se le la lui y en*). Polite `vous` vs. familiar `tu`.
- **Phonology**: ~13 oral + 3–4 nasal vowels (`an on in un`); silent final consonants; liaison across word boundaries; uvular `r`; fixed phrase-final stress.
- **Syntax notes**: Non-pro-drop. Bipartite negation *ne…pas* (*ne* often dropped in speech). Questions via intonation, *est-ce que*, or inversion. Copula `être`; possession with `avoir`.
