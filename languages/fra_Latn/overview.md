# French (fra_Latn)

## General Information

- **Language name:** French (Francais)
- **ISO 639-3 code:** fra
- **ISO 639-1 code:** fr
- **Language family:** Indo-European > Italic > Romance > Western Romance > Gallo-Romance > Oil
- **Script:** Latin
- **Speakers:** Approximately 300 million total speakers worldwide; roughly 80 million native speakers
- **Writing direction:** Left to right

## Geographic Distribution

French is spoken across multiple continents, making it one of the most geographically widespread languages in the world. Its primary regions of use include:

- **Europe:** France (metropolitan), Belgium (Wallonia and Brussels), Switzerland (Romandy), Luxembourg, Monaco, and the Channel Islands.
- **North America:** Canada (particularly Quebec, New Brunswick, and Ontario), as well as communities in the United States (Louisiana, New England, parts of Florida).
- **Africa:** A vast number of countries across West, Central, and North Africa, including the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Cameroon, Ivory Coast, Madagascar, Senegal, Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Guinea, Chad, Rwanda, Burundi, Benin, Togo, the Central African Republic, the Republic of the Congo, Gabon, Djibouti, and the Comoros, among others.
- **Caribbean and Oceania:** Haiti, Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, New Caledonia, French Polynesia, Vanuatu, and other territories.

## Official Status

French holds official or co-official status in 29 countries. It is one of the six official languages of the United Nations and serves as a working language of many international organizations, including the European Union, NATO, the International Olympic Committee, the International Red Cross, and the African Union. The Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF) brings together 88 member states and governments that share the use of French or have significant francophone populations. French also serves as an official language of numerous international courts and legal institutions, including the International Court of Justice.

## Historical Development

French evolved from Vulgar Latin, the colloquial form of Latin spoken by Roman soldiers, settlers, and merchants throughout Gaul following the Roman conquest in the first century BCE. As the Roman Empire declined, the Latin spoken in Gaul was increasingly influenced by the languages of Germanic tribes, particularly the Franks, who gave their name to both France and the French language.

### Old French (9th-14th centuries)

The earliest known document in Old French is the Strasbourg Oaths of 842, a political alliance text. Old French was characterized by a two-case system inherited from Latin, significant dialectal variation (including the distinction between the langue d'oil in the north and the langue d'oc in the south), and extensive borrowing from Frankish and other Germanic languages.

### Middle French (14th-17th centuries)

Middle French saw the gradual loss of the case system, the simplification of verb conjugations, and the beginning of standardization. The Ordinance of Villers-Cotterets (1539), issued by King Francis I, mandated the use of French instead of Latin in all legal and official documents, a landmark moment in the language's history.

### Modern French (17th century-present)

The founding of the Academie francaise in 1635 by Cardinal Richelieu marked the beginning of systematic efforts to regulate and standardize the French language. The Academy published its first dictionary in 1694 and has continued to serve as the guardian of French linguistic norms. The French Revolution further promoted the spread of standard French across France, where regional languages and dialects had long remained dominant in everyday speech. The colonial expansion of the 18th and 19th centuries spread French to Africa, the Americas, Asia, and the Pacific. In the modern era, French continues to evolve while maintaining a strong tradition of formal language planning and standardization through institutions in France, Belgium, Switzerland, Canada, and across the Francophonie.
