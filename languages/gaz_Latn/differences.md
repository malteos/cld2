# West Central Oromo vs. confusable languages

## gaz vs. orm (Oromo macrocode)

- **gaz** is the specific West Central Oromo variety (the largest, standardised for writing in Ethiopia). **orm** is the 639-2 macrocode that covers all Oromo lects: gaz (West Central), **hae** (Eastern), **orc** (Orma), **gax** (Borana-Arsi-Guji).
- In practice, written standard Oromo text is gaz. A document labelled **orm** may be gaz or a mix; CLD2/commonLID often uses orm as the catch-all.
- Diagnostics that could distinguish Borana (gax) from gaz: Borana is more conservative in retaining **-dh-**, has some distinct lexical items (*horii* "cattle/money"), and a stronger **-o/-oo** final bias. At the sentence level the two are hard to tell apart without metadata.

## Oromo vs. Somali (som)

- Both Cushitic and SOV, but Somali orthography uses **c** for pharyngeal /ʕ/, **x** for /ħ/ and **q** for uvular — Oromo uses **x** for /tʼ/ and **q** for /kʼ/. The presence of `c` as a consonant letter inside words (*caasimad, ciyaal, caruur*) tends to signal Somali.
- Somali uses double vowels too but has no **dh** implosive digraph; instead it uses **dh** as a retroflex stop.
- Oromo words ending in **-tti/-rra/-rraa/-iin** and the negator **hin-** are strongly Oromo, not Somali.

## Oromo vs. Amharic (amh) / Tigrinya (tir)

- Amharic and Tigrinya use Ethiopic/Geʿez script. Any text in Latin that discusses Ethiopia is much more likely Oromo (Qubee). If Ethiopic script appears, it is not Oromo.
