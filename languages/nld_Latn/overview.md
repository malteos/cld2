# Dutch (nld)

## Summary

Dutch (endonym: *Nederlands*) is an Indo-European language of the West Germanic branch (Indo-European → Germanic → West → Low Franconian). ISO codes: 639-1 `nl`, 639-2 `nld` (also `dut`), 639-3 `nld`. It is written in the Latin script. Dutch has roughly 24–25 million native speakers and about 5 million L2 speakers. It is the sole official language of the Netherlands, one of three in Belgium (Flanders), and official in Suriname, Aruba, Curaçao, and Sint Maarten. The Flemish written standard is essentially identical to the Dutch of the Netherlands, while spoken Belgian Dutch (Flemish) differs in prosody and some lexicon.

## Writing system

Dutch uses the 26-letter Latin alphabet plus the digraph `ij`, traditionally treated as a single letter and capitalised as `IJ` (e.g. *IJsland*). Acute accents appear for emphasis or to disambiguate (*één, vóór*) and diaereses separate vowels across a syllable boundary (*coördinatie, geïnstalleerd*). Only proper nouns and sentence-initial words are capitalised. Double-written vowels `aa`, `ee`, `oo`, `uu` mark length in closed syllables. Punctuation follows standard Western conventions.

## History

Dutch descends from Old Low Franconian via Middle Dutch; the 1947–1954 and 1995 spelling reforms standardised `ij`/`y` usage and the treatment of compound loanwords. Afrikaans, a daughter language in South Africa, split from 17th-century Dutch settler speech.
