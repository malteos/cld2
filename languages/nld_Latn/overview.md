# Dutch (nld_Latn) - Language Overview

## Basic Information

- **Language Name:** Dutch (Nederlands)
- **ISO 639-3 Code:** nld
- **ISO 639-1 Code:** nl
- **Script:** Latin (Latn)
- **Language Family:** Indo-European > Germanic > West Germanic > Low Franconian
- **Morphological Type:** Fusional
- **Number of Native Speakers:** Approximately 25 million
- **Total Speakers (including L2):** Approximately 30 million

## Geographic Distribution

Dutch is the official language of the **Netherlands** and one of three official languages of **Belgium** (spoken primarily in the Flanders region and the Brussels-Capital Region). It is also an official language of **Suriname** in South America and of the Caribbean constituent countries of the Kingdom of the Netherlands: **Aruba**, **Curacao**, and **Sint Maarten**. Historically, Dutch colonial influence spread the language to parts of Indonesia, South Africa, and other regions.

In Belgium, the Dutch-speaking community comprises roughly 60% of the population, concentrated in the northern half of the country known as Flanders. The variety of Dutch spoken in Belgium is often referred to as Flemish (Vlaams), though linguistically it is considered part of the Dutch language continuum rather than a separate language. The differences between Netherlandic Dutch and Belgian Dutch are largely matters of pronunciation, vocabulary, and certain idiomatic expressions, comparable in scope to the differences between British and American English.

## Official Status

Dutch holds official status at multiple levels. It is the sole official language of the Netherlands at the national level, alongside Frisian which has co-official status in the province of Friesland. In Belgium, Dutch is the official language of the Flemish Community and the Flemish Region. At the European Union level, Dutch is one of the 24 official languages. The **Nederlandse Taalunie** (Dutch Language Union), a treaty organization comprising the Netherlands, Belgium (Flanders), and Suriname, coordinates Dutch language policy, orthography, and education across national borders.

## Brief History

Dutch evolved from Old Frankish (Old Low Franconian) dialects spoken in the Low Countries during the early medieval period. The earliest known Dutch text is the "Hebban olla vogala" fragment, dating to approximately 1100 CE, although older Franconian texts exist from the 9th century.

During the **Middle Dutch** period (roughly 1150-1500), a rich literary tradition developed, particularly in Flanders and Brabant, producing works such as "Van den vos Reynaerde" (Reynard the Fox) and the writings of the mystic Hadewijch. The political and economic center of the Dutch-speaking world shifted northward during the 16th and 17th centuries as the Dutch Republic rose to prominence.

The **Dutch Golden Age** (17th century) saw the standardization of the language accelerate, driven by the publication of the States Bible (Statenvertaling) in 1637, which played a role for Dutch comparable to that of the King James Bible for English. Trade and colonial expansion during this era spread Dutch influence across the globe, from New Amsterdam (later New York) to the Dutch East Indies, the Cape Colony in South Africa, and various Caribbean territories.

Modern standard Dutch (Algemeen Beschaafd Nederlands, now called Standaardnederlands) was further codified in the 19th and 20th centuries. The spelling was officially standardized by the Woordenlijst Nederlandse Taal (the "Green Booklet"), first published in 1954 and periodically updated. The most recent major spelling reform took effect in 2005.

Dutch is notable as the parent language of **Afrikaans**, which developed from 17th-century Dutch dialects spoken by settlers in South Africa and diverged sufficiently to be considered a separate language by the early 20th century. Afrikaans retains mutual intelligibility with Dutch to a significant degree, particularly in written form.

## Resource Level

Dutch is a **high-resource language** with extensive digital presence, large text corpora, comprehensive dictionaries, well-developed NLP tools, and broad representation in machine translation systems and other language technologies.
