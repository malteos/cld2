# Dutch - Differences from Similar Languages

Dutch is confused mainly with **Afrikaans** and **West Frisian**, less often with **German**, Low German and Luxembourgish, and - in CHALIS - it serves as the West Germanic contrast to **Danish**. Belgian Dutch (Flemish) is the same language (`nld`) and must not be labelled separately.

## Dutch vs Afrikaans (afr) - Very Close

Afrikaans developed from 17th-century Dutch; written texts share much vocabulary. See also `afr_Latn/differences.md`.

| Cue | Dutch | Afrikaans |
|---|---|---|
| Digraph | **ij**: *mij, zijn, tijd, bij* | **y**: *my, sy, tyd, by* |
| Initial cluster | **sch**: *school, schip* | **sk**: *skool, skip* |
| Articles | **de / het**, **een** | **die**, **'n** |
| Negation | single **niet/geen** | double **nie ... nie** (*Ek kan nie swem nie*) |
| "is/are" | *ben, bent, is, zijn* | *is* for all persons |
| I / you / she / they | *ik, jij, zij, zij/ze* | *ek, jy, sy, hulle* |
| for / very | *voor, erg/heel* | *vir, baie* |
| Verb inflection | *ik werk, hij werkt, wij werken* | *werk* for all persons |
| Simple past | common: *was, had, liep, kwam* | almost only perfect; a few preterites survive (*was, kon, wou, moes, sou*) |
| -tie / -sie | *politie, informatie* | *polisie, informasie* (also *inligting*) |
| *z-* | *zon, zee, zeggen* | *son, see, sê* |
| Final -d/-g/-cht | *goed, nacht, acht* | *goed, nag, ag* |
| Diminutive | *-je/-tje* (*meisje*) | *-ie/-tjie* (*meisie*) |

Key trap: *het* is also the Afrikaans auxiliary "has/have" (*ek het gesien*); in Dutch it is the neuter article or "it". Afrikaans has circumflex/diaeresis letters *ê, ô, ë* (*sê, môre*); a Dutch text normally has no *ê/ô*.

## Dutch vs West Frisian (fry)

Frisian is official in the province of Fryslân and is written with its own orthography.

| Cue | Dutch | West Frisian |
|---|---|---|
| Neuter article | **het** | **it** |
| not | **niet** | **net** |
| in / of / with / for | *in, van, met, voor* | *yn, fan, mei, foar* |
| I am / have | *ik ben, ik heb* | *ik bin, ik haw* |
| also / we / our | *ook, wij, ons* | *ek, wy, ús* |
| become | *worden* | *wurde* |
| Letters | no circumflex | **â, ê, ô, û, ú**, and *y* where Dutch has *ij/i* |

Frisian *sk-* (*skoalle*) and *-ea-, -oa-, -ue-* digraphs (*hearre, boat, muoike*) are absent from Dutch.

## Dutch vs German (deu)

See also `deu_Latn/differences.md`.

- German **capitalises all nouns**; Dutch does not.
- German **ä, ö, ü, ß**; Dutch none (Dutch *ë/ï* are tremas, not umlauts).
- Dutch **ij** vs German **ei** (*mijn/mein, tijd/Zeit*); Dutch doubled vowels *aa/ee/oo* vs German *ah/eh/oh* or single vowels.
- Articles *de/het/een* vs *der/die/das/ein*; Dutch has no case-marked articles (*den/dem/des* are German; Dutch *den* is archaic or in names).
- Function words: *niet/nicht, ook/auch, met/mit, van/von, voor/für, wat/was, maar/aber, zijn/sein, heeft/hat, wordt/wird*.
- Initial clusters: Dutch *zw-, sl-, sm-, sn-* (*zwart, slapen, smal, sneeuw*) vs German *schw-, schl-, schm-, schn-* (*schwarz, schlafen, schmal, Schnee*); Dutch *schr-* (*schrijven*) matches German *schr-*.
- Dutch *-heid, -lijk, -tje* vs German *-heit, -lich, -chen*.

## Dutch vs Danish (CHALIS contrast)

The two are not mutually intelligible in writing, but short texts share some function-word spellings. From `chalis/discriminators.md`:

- **Shared spellings (not diagnostic):** *de, en, op, er, om, dit, over, kan, nu, hun, dag, alle, ja, man, af, hele*. Several mean different things: Dutch *en* "and" vs Danish *en* "a"; Dutch *er* "there" vs Danish *er* "is"; Dutch *hun* "their" vs Danish *hun* "she"; Dutch *dit* "this" vs Danish *dit* "your (neut.)".
- **Dutch markers:** *het, dat, voor, je, met, niet, zijn, maar, aan, hij, ook, ze, door, uit, bij, was, wat, heeft, nog, deze, hebben, meer, zo, geen, mijn*.
- **Danish markers:** *og, det, til, på, jeg, har, ikke, med, så, fra, skal, ved, vil, også, være, hvor, hvis, sig, efter, mig, eller, hvad, ud, godt, meget*.
- Letters: æ, ø, å exclude Dutch; *ij, aa, oo, uu, oe* (when not standing for ø/å) point to Dutch. Danish *hv-* initial (*hvad, hvor*) and *-hed* never occur in native Dutch words.
- Danish suffixed articles (*-en, -et, -ene*) vs Dutch preposed *de/het*.

## Netherlandic vs Belgian Dutch (Flemish) - Same Label

Both use the same standard spelling, so the label is always `nld`. Belgian texts may show:

- Pronouns **gij/ge** "you" (ranks 3521/3940 in `vocabulary.json`), **u** used more widely, and **ne/nen** articles in dialect-coloured writing.
- Vocabulary: *gsm* (NL *mobiel*), *goesting, amai, allee, plezant, seffens, ambetant, frieten* (NL *patat*), *schepen* (alderman), *beenhouwer* (NL *slager*). Many of these are colloquial and absent from the 10k list.
- Diminutive *-ke(n)* in dialect writing (*manneke*).

Surinamese Dutch likewise remains `nld`.

## Dutch vs English

English has *the, and, of, is, to* and no *ij*; shared spellings (*in, is, over, was, have/heb*) rarely mislead beyond a few words. Dutch *-en* plural verbs and *ge-* participles are absent from English.
