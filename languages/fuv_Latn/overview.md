# Nigerian Fulfulde (fuv)

## Summary

Nigerian Fulfulde (endonym: **Fulfulde**; speakers are called **Fulɓe**, a single Pullo) is a Niger-Congo language of the Atlantic branch (Niger-Congo → Atlantic-Congo → North Atlantic → Fula). ISO-639-3 **fuv** covers the eastern Nigerian/Cameroonian variety; the Fula macro-language is **ff** (639-1) / **ful** (639-2). fuv has roughly **15–20 million** speakers across **northern Nigeria**, **northern Cameroon**, **Chad**, and adjacent regions, with smaller communities in Sudan. Other major Fula varieties have their own codes (e.g. *fuc* Pulaar, *fuf* Pular, *ffm* Maasina).

## Writing system

Fulfulde is written in a Latin alphabet with the hooked consonants **ɓ**, **ɗ**, **ƴ** (and sometimes **ɠ**) shared with Hausa, plus the velar nasal **ŋ** (U+014B) and the digraphs **mb**, **nd**, **ng**, **nj**, **ny**. Long vowels are written as double letters: **aa ee ii oo uu**. Geminate consonants are also written double (*mb mb*, *dd*, *bb*). Tone is non-phonemic and unwritten. An older **Ajami** (Arabic) orthography exists but is uncommon on the web. Nigerian Fulfulde uses no tone marks and no underdots.

## History and status

Fulfulde is a regional lingua franca across the Sahel. In Nigeria it is a recognised language of instruction and broadcasting in several northern states. The Fulɓe are traditionally pastoralists, and the language is spread along a vast east–west axis from Senegal to Sudan.
