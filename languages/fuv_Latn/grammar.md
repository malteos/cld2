# Nigerian Fulfulde grammar

- **Word order**: Basic **SVO**, head-initial. Possessor and adjective follow the head noun, which carries the class-concord suffix.
- **Morphology**: Strongly **agglutinative** with extensive suffixing. Characteristic of Atlantic languages: consonant **mutation** at the initial of a stem tracks singular/plural and noun class, e.g. *pullo* (sg.) ↔ *fulɓe* (pl.), *gorko* ↔ *worɓe* "man/men".
- **Nouns**: A rich **noun-class** system (often called "gender") with ~20–25 classes, each signalled by a characteristic final suffix on the noun. Typical class markers include **-o** (human sg.), **-ɓe** (human pl.), **-ɗo / -ɗum**, **-ngal / -kal**, **-ngel / -kon** (diminutive), **-nde / -de**, **-ndu / -ɗi**, **-ka / -ki**, **-ngol / -ɗi**. Determiners, adjectives and relative pronouns agree in class.
- **Verbs**: Voice-rich. Three major voices encoded by suffix: **active** *-de/-ude*, **middle** *-aade/-ɗaade*, **passive** *-ɗeede/-ee*. Subject-person is suffixed: 1sg *-mi*, 2sg *-ɗa/-ɗaa*, 3sg *-i / -∅*, 1pl inclusive *-en*, 1pl exclusive *-min*, 2pl *-ɗon*, 3pl *-ɓe/-i*. Aspect markers include perfective *-i*, imperfective *-a*, progressive *no … -a*.
- **Phonology**: Implosives **ɓ ɗ ƴ**, nasal **ŋ**, prenasalised **mb nd ng nj**. Five vowels with phonemic length. No tone.
- **Syntax notes**: Pro-drop is normal because person is on the verb. Negation uses distinct verb paradigms (e.g. negative perfective *-aali*, *-aaki*) rather than a single particle. Copula / focus particle **ko** is frequent ("it is …").
