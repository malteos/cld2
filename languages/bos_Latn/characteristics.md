# Bosnian Language Identification Characteristics

## Distinctive Characters

The Bosnian Latin alphabet contains 30 letters, including several that are distinctive for language identification:

### Letters with Diacritics
- **č** (c with caron) — voiceless postalveolar affricate /tʃ/
- **ć** (c with acute) — voiceless alveolopalatal affricate /tɕ/
- **đ** (d with stroke) — voiced alveolopalatal affricate /dʑ/
- **š** (s with caron) — voiceless postalveolar fricative /ʃ/
- **ž** (z with caron) — voiced postalveolar fricative /ʒ/

### Digraphs (treated as single letters)
- **dž** — voiced postalveolar affricate /dʒ/
- **lj** — palatal lateral /ʎ/
- **nj** — palatal nasal /ɲ/

The presence of these characters (č, ć, đ, š, ž, and digraphs dž, lj, nj) immediately narrows identification to the Bosnian/Croatian/Serbian/Montenegrin group. The absence of the Montenegrin-specific letters **ś** and **ź** helps distinguish Bosnian from Montenegrin.

## Character Frequency Patterns

The most frequent characters in Bosnian text (excluding spaces) are:

1. **a** (~11.0%)
2. **i** (~9.5%)
3. **o** (~8.8%)
4. **e** (~8.2%)
5. **n** (~6.5%)
6. **s** (~5.5%)
7. **r** (~5.0%)
8. **j** (~4.8%)
9. **t** (~4.5%)
10. **u** (~4.2%)

The relatively high frequency of **j** is notable and reflects the ijekavian reflex (e.g., *lijep*, *mlijeko*, *rijeka*) as well as its frequent use in verb endings and pronoun forms. The letter **h** appears with higher frequency in Bosnian compared to colloquial Serbian due to h-retention in words like *hljeb*, *kahva*, *lahko*, *mehko*.

### Bosnian-Specific Function Word Notes

- **također** (also) is preferred over Croatian **takoder** (same spelling, sometimes different pronunciation preferences)
- **sa** (with) is strongly preferred in Bosnian (Croatian often uses **s** before consonants)
- **šta** (what) is typical for Bosnian and Serbian, versus Croatian **što** for "what" in questions
- **lahko** (easily) is a Bosnian preference, versus Croatian **lako**
- **prije** (before) — ijekavian form, versus Serbian ekavian **pre**
- **između** (between) — ijekavian, versus Serbian **između** (same spelling but the root vowel pattern differs in related forms)

The most frequent character bigrams in Bosnian text:

- **je** (extremely common — copula, reflexes, suffixes)
- **na** (preposition, prefix)
- **an** (common in loanwords, native words)
- **st** (common cluster)
- **os** (common in abstract nouns)
- **ra** (common combination)
- **ij** (characteristic of ijekavian — *prije, lijep, svijet*)
- **ni** (negation prefix, common combination)
- **pr** (prefix/preposition)
- **ko** (common combination)
- **en** (common in loanwords)
- **ti** (infinitive ending)
- **no** (neuter ending, adverb)
- **da** (conjunction/preposition)
- **ri** (common combination)

## Common Trigrams

- **ije** (highly diagnostic — ijekavian reflex: *mlijeko, svijet, vrijeme*)
- **ost** (abstract noun suffix: *mogućnost, slobodnost*)
- **sta** (various morphological contexts)
- **nje** (verbal noun suffix: *čitanje, pisanje*)
- **pri** (prefix: *prije, priča*)
- **ati** (infinitive ending: *čitati, pisati*)
- **iti** (infinitive ending: *raditi, živiti*)
- **ova** (suffix pattern: *gradova, osnova*)
- **pre** (less frequent than *pri*/*pri-je* in Bosnian)
- **ani** (participle patterns)
- **dže** (Turkish loanword marker: *džamija, džezva*)

## Morphological Patterns for LID

### Turkish and Arabic Loanwords

This is the single most important discriminator for Bosnian versus Croatian and Serbian. Bosnian retains and actively uses a larger inventory of Turkish and Arabic loanwords than either Croatian or Serbian:

- **džamija** (mosque) — Turkish *cami*
- **avlija** (courtyard) — Turkish *avlu*
- **sokak** (street, narrow) — Turkish *sokak*
- **čaršija** (bazaar/market) — Turkish *çarşı*
- **kahva** (coffee) — Turkish *kahve* (Croatian: *kava*, Serbian: *kafa*)
- **rahatluk** (comfort) — Turkish *rahatlık*
- **ćilim** (rug) — Turkish *kilim*
- **dućan** (shop) — Turkish *dükkan*
- **mahala** (neighborhood) — Turkish *mahalle*
- **komšija** (neighbor) — Turkish *komşu*
- **bajram** (holiday/festival) — Arabic via Turkish
- **džezva** (coffee pot) — Turkish *cezve*
- **ćevap** (kebab) — Turkish *kebap*
- **sarma** (stuffed cabbage rolls) — Turkish *sarma*
- **sedmica** (week) — used vs Croatian *tjedan*

### H-Retention

Bosnian is more conservative in retaining the phoneme /h/ in positions where it has been lost in Serbian and some Croatian dialects:

- **hljeb** (bread) — Serbian *hleb*, Croatian *kruh*
- **lahko** (easily) — Serbian/Croatian *lako*
- **mehko** (softly) — Serbian/Croatian *meko*
- **hrđa** (rust) — retained consistently
- **historija** (history) — versus Croatian *povijest*, Serbian *istorija*

### Ijekavian Reflex

Like Croatian and Montenegrin, Bosnian uses the ijekavian reflex of Common Slavic *yat*:

- **mlijeko** (milk) — Serbian ekavian *mleko*
- **lijep** (beautiful) — Serbian *lep*
- **svijet** (world) — Serbian *svet*
- **vrijeme** (time/weather) — Serbian *vreme*
- **rijeka** (river) — Serbian *reka*

This immediately distinguishes Bosnian from Serbian (ekavian) text but does NOT distinguish it from Croatian or Montenegrin, which also use ijekavian.

## Script-Specific Features

For Latin-script Bosnian:
- Uses the full Gaj Latin alphabet with diacritics
- Does NOT use the Montenegrin extensions ś and ź
- Digraphs dž, lj, nj are sorted as single letters
- No use of Hungarian-style diacritics (unlike some historical South Slavic writing)
- ASCII-degraded text may show c for č/ć, s for š, z for ž, d for đ, dz for dž — complicating LID but the degradation pattern itself is a signal

## Key Distinguishing Combinations for LID

When multiple features co-occur, identification confidence increases:

1. **Ijekavian reflex** (ije/je alternations) + **Turkish loanwords** (dž-, č-initial loans) = strong Bosnian signal
2. **H-retention** in common words (*hljeb*, *lahko*) + ijekavian = very strong Bosnian signal
3. High frequency of **dž** digraph in content words = Bosnian or Montenegrin
4. Use of **šta** (not **što** for interrogative "what") + ijekavian = Bosnian (not Croatian)
5. Absence of **ś** and **ź** = not Montenegrin
