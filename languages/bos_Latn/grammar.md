# Bosnian Grammar (bosanska gramatika)

## Typological Overview

Bosnian is a fusional (inflectional) language with a relatively free word order. The dominant word order is Subject-Verb-Object (SVO), but because grammatical relations are largely encoded through case morphology, other orders (SOV, VSO, OVS, etc.) are possible and used for pragmatic purposes such as topicalization, focus, and emphasis. The language exhibits pro-drop behavior, meaning that subject pronouns can be (and frequently are) omitted when the verb morphology makes the subject clear.

## Phonology

### Vowels

Bosnian has a simple five-vowel system:

| Vowel | Front | Central | Back |
|-------|-------|---------|------|
| High  | i     |         | u    |
| Mid   | e     |         | o    |
| Low   |       | a       |      |

All five vowels can be phonemically long or short, yielding ten distinct vowel phonemes in terms of quantity. Vowel length is contrastive and can distinguish meaning: *grad* (city) vs. *grȃd* (hail), though in standard orthography length is not marked.

### Consonants

Bosnian has approximately 25 consonant phonemes:

- **Stops:** p, b, t, d, k, g
- **Affricates:** c /ts/, č /tʃ/, ć /tɕ/, dž /dʒ/, đ /dʑ/
- **Fricatives:** f, s, z, š /ʃ/, ž /ʒ/, h /x/
- **Nasals:** m, n, nj /ɲ/
- **Liquids:** l, lj /ʎ/, r (also syllabic)
- **Glides:** v, j

The distinction between the palatal affricates č/dž (postalveolar) and ć/đ (alveolopalatal) is a characteristic feature of Bosnian and other Western South Slavic languages. The phoneme /x/ (written as *h*) is notably more consistently preserved in Bosnian than in colloquial Serbian, where it is often dropped or replaced.

### Pitch Accent

Bosnian is a pitch-accent language with a four-accent system inherited from Neo-Stokavian. The four accents are:

1. **Short falling** (kratak silazni): ȁ
2. **Long falling** (dugi silazni): ȃ
3. **Short rising** (kratak uzlazni): à
4. **Long rising** (dugi uzlazni): á

In standard pronunciation, falling accents can only occur on the first syllable, while rising accents occur on non-final syllables. Additionally, post-accentual length (non-accented long vowels) can occur after the accented syllable. The accent system is largely predictable but carries lexical and grammatical significance in certain minimal pairs.

## Nominal Morphology

### Nouns

Bosnian nouns are inflected for:

- **Gender:** masculine, feminine, neuter (3 genders)
- **Number:** singular, plural (2 numbers)
- **Case:** nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, vocative, instrumental, locative (7 cases)

This yields a rich paradigm with numerous declension classes. The major declension types are:

**Masculine nouns** (typically ending in a consonant in nominative singular):
- *grad* (city): grad, grada, gradu, grad, grade, gradom, gradu (sg.); gradovi, gradova, gradovima, gradove, gradovi, gradovima, gradovima (pl.)

**Feminine nouns** (typically ending in -a in nominative singular):
- *knjiga* (book): knjiga, knjige, knjizi, knjigu, knjigo, knjigom, knjizi (sg.); knjige, knjiga, knjigama, knjige, knjige, knjigama, knjigama (pl.)

**Neuter nouns** (typically ending in -o or -e in nominative singular):
- *selo* (village): selo, sela, selu, selo, selo, selom, selu (sg.); sela, sela, selima, sela, sela, selima, selima (pl.)

The animate/inanimate distinction is relevant for masculine nouns in the accusative singular, where animate nouns take the genitive form (accusative = genitive) while inanimate nouns retain the nominative form.

### Adjectives

Adjectives agree with their head noun in gender, number, and case. Bosnian adjectives have two forms:

- **Indefinite (short) form:** used predicatively and in indefinite contexts (*nov grad* — a new city)
- **Definite (long) form:** used attributively in definite contexts (*novi grad* — the new city)

Adjectives have comparative and superlative degrees, formed with the suffix *-iji/-ji* (comparative) and the prefix *naj-* plus the comparative form (superlative): *star* (old), *stariji* (older), *najstariji* (oldest).

### Pronouns

Personal pronouns exist in full (stressed) and clitic (unstressed) forms:

| Person | Nominative | Accusative (full/clitic) | Dative (full/clitic) |
|--------|-----------|-------------------------|---------------------|
| 1sg    | ja        | mene / me               | meni / mi           |
| 2sg    | ti        | tebe / te               | tebi / ti           |
| 3sg.m  | on        | njega / ga              | njemu / mu          |
| 3sg.f  | ona       | nju / je/ju             | njoj / joj          |
| 3sg.n  | ono       | njega / ga              | njemu / mu          |
| 1pl    | mi        | nas / nas               | nama / nam          |
| 2pl    | vi        | vas / vas               | vama / vam          |
| 3pl    | oni/one/ona | njih / ih             | njima / im          |

## Verbal Morphology

### Verb System

Bosnian verbs are conjugated for person (1st, 2nd, 3rd) and number (singular, plural), and are characterized by a complex system of tense, aspect, and mood.

**Aspect** is a fundamental category: most verbs come in imperfective/perfective pairs:
- *pisati* (to write, imperfective) / *napisati* (to write, perfective)
- *čitati* (to read, imperfective) / *pročitati* (to read, perfective)

### Tenses

The main tenses in active use are:

1. **Present tense** (prezent): *ja čitam, ti čitaš, on čita, mi čitamo, vi čitate, oni čitaju*
2. **Perfect tense** (perfekat): formed with the present of *biti* (to be) + past participle: *ja sam čitao/čitala*
3. **Future I** (futur I): formed with the clitic of *htjeti* (will) + infinitive: *ja ću čitati* or contracted *čitaću*
4. **Aorist** (aorist): synthetic past tense, more common in literary language: *ja čitah*
5. **Imperfect** (imperfekat): past habitual/progressive, literary: *ja čitah* (from imperfective verbs)
6. **Pluperfect** (pluskvamperfekat): *ja sam bio čitao / ja bijah čitao*
7. **Future II** (futur II / egzaktni futur): *ja budem čitao*

In everyday speech, the perfect tense has largely replaced the aorist and imperfect, which are restricted to literary, narrative, and formal registers.

### Moods

- **Indicative:** the default mood for factual statements
- **Imperative:** *čitaj!* (read!, 2sg), *čitajmo!* (let's read!, 1pl), *čitajte!* (read!, 2pl)
- **Conditional:** formed with the aorist of *biti* (*bih, bi, bi, bismo, biste, bi*) + past participle: *ja bih čitao* (I would read)

### Non-finite Forms

- **Infinitive:** *čitati* (to read)
- **Present participle:** *čitajući* (reading)
- **Past participle:** *čitao/čitala/čitalo* (having read; also used in compound tenses)
- **Passive participle:** *čitan/čitana/čitano* (read, as in "the book was read")
- **Verbal noun:** *čitanje* (reading, the act of)

## Syntax

### Clitic Placement

Bosnian has strict rules for clitic placement. Clitics (unstressed pronouns, auxiliary verbs, the question particle *li*) follow the first stressed element in a clause (Wackernagel's position). The internal order of clitics is fixed:

1. Question particle: *li*
2. Verbal clitics: *sam, si, je, smo, ste, su* (present of *biti*); *ću, ćeš, će, ćemo, ćete, će* (future); *bih, bi, bi, bismo, biste, bi* (conditional)
3. Dative pronouns: *mi, ti, mu, joj, nam, vam, im*
4. Accusative pronouns: *me, te, ga, je/ju, nas, vas, ih*
5. Reflexive: *se*
6. Third person of *biti*: *je* (when it functions as a copula after another clitic)

Example: *Dao sam mu ga* (I gave it to him) — the clitics *sam*, *mu*, *ga* follow the first stressed word *dao*.

### Pro-drop

Bosnian is a pro-drop language. Since verb conjugation indicates person and number, subject pronouns are typically omitted unless emphasis or contrast is intended:

- *Čitam knjigu.* (I am reading a book.) — pronoun *ja* omitted
- *Ja čitam, a ti gledaš televiziju.* (I am reading, but you are watching TV.) — pronouns used for contrast

### Negation

Negation is formed by placing *ne* directly before the verb: *Ne čitam.* (I am not reading.) The verb *biti* has a special negative present form: *nisam, nisi, nije, nismo, niste, nisu*. Bosnian allows double (and multiple) negation, which is grammatically required: *Nikada niko nikome ništa nije rekao.* (Nobody ever said anything to anyone.)

### Questions

Yes/no questions are formed by placing the particle *li* after the verb (or the first stressed element): *Čitaš li?* (Are you reading?). Alternatively, the particle *da li* can be placed at the beginning: *Da li čitaš?*. In colloquial speech, rising intonation alone may signal a question.

Wh-questions use interrogative words at the beginning of the sentence: *Šta čitaš?* (What are you reading?), *Gdje živiš?* (Where do you live?). Note that Bosnian typically uses *šta* (what) and *gdje* (where), though *što* and *gde* may also be encountered.

### Relative Clauses

Relative clauses are introduced by the relative pronoun *koji/koja/koje* (which, who), which declines for gender, number, and case according to its function in the relative clause: *Knjiga koju čitam je zanimljiva.* (The book that I am reading is interesting.)
