# Central Kurdish / Sorani - Identification Characteristics

## Modified Arabic Script Characters

Sorani Kurdish uses a modified Arabic script with several additional characters not found in standard Arabic. These characters are among the most reliable indicators for identifying Sorani text:

| Character | Name | Sound | Notes |
|-----------|------|-------|-------|
| ڕ | re | /r/ (trill) | Unique to Kurdish; ر is flap |
| ڵ | lam-e velar | /ɫ/ (velarized l) | Unique to Kurdish; ل is clear l |
| ێ | ye-y barkrawa | /eː/ | Kurdish-specific vowel letter |
| ۆ | waw-e barkrawa | /o/ | Kurdish-specific vowel letter |
| ڤ | ve | /v/ | Also used in some Persian texts |
| پ | pe | /p/ | Shared with Persian, Urdu |
| چ | che | /tʃ/ | Shared with Persian, Urdu |
| ژ | zhe | /ʒ/ | Shared with Persian |
| گ | gaf | /g/ | Shared with Persian, Urdu |
| ک | kaf | /k/ | Different form from Arabic ك; the Kurdish/Persian form ک has a different shape |

The characters **ڕ** and **ڵ** are the strongest single-character indicators of Kurdish text, as they are virtually absent from Arabic, Persian, and other scripts. The vowel letters **ێ** and **ۆ** are also highly diagnostic.

## Vowel Writing

A crucial difference between Sorani Kurdish and Arabic (and to a lesser extent Persian) is that Sorani writes all vowels explicitly using dedicated letters. Arabic typically omits short vowels (a, i, u) in standard text, relying on diacritics (harakat) that are usually absent. Sorani, by contrast, represents every vowel with a letter:

- /a/ is written with ئە or ە
- /e/ is written with ە or ێ
- /i/ is written with ی
- /u/ is written with و
- /o/ is written with ۆ
- /w/ (long u) is written with وو

This means Sorani text has a visually higher density of vowel letters compared to Arabic text, which often appears as strings of consonants.

## Character Frequency Patterns

In typical Sorani text, the following characters appear with notably high frequency:

1. **ە** (he/short a or e) - extremely frequent as the most common vowel marker
2. **ی** (ye) - frequent as both consonant /j/ and vowel /i/, plus ezafe marker
3. **ن** (nun) - frequent in verb endings, plural suffix -ان, and common words
4. **و** (waw) - serves as conjunction "and" and vowel /u/
5. **ک** (kaf) - very common in the definite suffix -ەکە and other morphemes
6. **د** (dal) - frequent in the present tense preverb دە-
7. **ر** (re) - common consonant
8. **ل** (lam) - frequent in the preposition لە and other common words
9. **م** (mim) - common in pronouns and verb endings
10. **ت** (te) - frequent in verb endings and common words

The high frequency of **ە** is particularly diagnostic, as this character appears far more often in Sorani than in Arabic or Persian text.

## Common Bigrams and Trigrams

### High-frequency bigrams:
- **لە** (le) - preposition "in/at/from"
- **دە** (de) - present tense marker
- **بە** (be) - preposition "with/by"
- **کە** (ke) - complementizer
- **ەک** (ek) - part of indefinite suffix and definite -ەکە
- **ان** (an) - plural suffix
- **ەو** (ew) - part of demonstrative ئەو
- **ین** (in) - verb ending
- **یی** (ii) - adjectival/nisba ending
- **ەی** (ey) - ezafe after vowel, possessive

### High-frequency trigrams:
- **ەکە** (eke) - definite suffix
- **ەکا** (ekan) - beginning of plural definite -ەکان
- **کان** (kan) - end of plural definite suffix
- **دەک** (dek) - present tense of "to do"
- **لەس** (les) - beginning of لەسەر "on/about"
- **یەک** (yek) - "one" or part of reciprocal
- **ووت** (wut) - "said" (past stem)
- **ێکی** (eki) - indefinite + ezafe
- **نەو** (new) - various morpheme combinations
- **ستا** (sta) - part of common words like دەستا

## Morphological Patterns for Identification

Several morphological patterns are highly characteristic of Sorani:

1. **Definite suffix -ەکە** (-eke): *ماڵەکە* "the house," *کتێبەکە* "the book"
2. **Plural definite -ەکان** (-ekan): *ماڵەکان* "the houses"
3. **Present tense prefix دە-** (de-): *دەڕۆم* "I go," *دەنووسم* "I write"
4. **Ezafe suffix -ی** (-i/-y): *ماڵی من* "my house"
5. **Infinitive suffix -ن** (-n) or **-تن** (-tin): *نووسین* "to write," *ڕۆیشتن* "to go"
6. **Clitic pronouns**: -م (my/I), -ت (your/you), -ی (his/her), -مان (our/we), -تان (your pl.), -یان (their/they)
7. **Subjunctive prefix بـ-** (bi-): *بنووسم* "that I write"
8. **Negative prefix نا-/نە-**: *نادەنووسم* "I don't write"

These patterns, combined with the distinctive script characters, provide strong signals for automatic language identification.
