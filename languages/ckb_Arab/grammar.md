# Central Kurdish / Sorani Grammar

## Typological Profile

- **Word Order**: SOV (Subject-Object-Verb), with the verb typically in final position
- **Morphological Type**: Fusional with some agglutinative features
- **Grammatical Gender**: None (unlike Kurmanji, which has masculine/feminine distinction)
- **Alignment**: Split-ergative (nominative-accusative in present tenses; ergative-absolutive in past tenses)
- **Head Direction**: Head-final (postpositions, relative clauses preceding head nouns in formal style, complementizer following the clause)

## Phonology

### Vowels (~8 vowel phonemes)

Sorani has a relatively rich vowel system compared to many other Iranian languages:

| Vowel | IPA | Arabic Script |
|-------|-----|---------------|
| a | /a/ | ـە (word-final: ـە) |
| e | /ɛ/ | ـە (context-dependent) |
| i | /i/ | ـی |
| ii/î | /iː/ | ـی |
| u | /u/ | ـو |
| uu | /uː/ | ـوو |
| o | /o/ | ۆ |
| w/ü | /ʉ/ | (dialectal) |

A critical feature of Sorani orthography is that all vowels are written, unlike Arabic and to a greater extent than Persian. This means Sorani text includes explicit vowel letters, making it more readable without diacritics.

### Consonants (~30 consonant phonemes)

Sorani has approximately 30 consonant phonemes, including several not found in Arabic:

- **Stops**: p پ, b ب, t ت, d د, k ک, g گ, q ق, ʔ ئ
- **Fricatives**: f ف, v ڤ, s س, z ز, ʃ ش, ʒ ژ, x خ, ɣ غ, h ه, ħ ح
- **Affricates**: tʃ چ, dʒ ج
- **Nasals**: m م, n ن, ŋ (allophonic)
- **Liquids**: l ل, ɫ ڵ (velarized l), r ر, ɾ ڕ (tapped/trilled r)
- **Glides**: w و, j ی

The distinction between ل (clear l) and ڵ (velarized/dark l), and between ر (flap r) and ڕ (trill r) is phonemic and represented in the script.

## Noun System

### No Grammatical Gender

Unlike Kurmanji Kurdish, which distinguishes masculine and feminine nouns with corresponding agreement on adjectives, demonstratives, and the ezafe particle, Sorani has no grammatical gender whatsoever. This is one of the most significant typological differences between the two major Kurdish varieties. All nouns, whether referring to male or female entities, use the same morphological patterns:

- **Kurmanji**: *kitêba mezin* "the big book" (feminine agreement) vs. *marekî mezin* "a big snake" (masculine agreement)
- **Sorani**: *کتێبی گەورە* "the big book" / *ماری گەورە* "the big snake" (no gender distinction)

### Definiteness

Sorani does not have a definite article comparable to Arabic "al-" or English "the." Instead, definiteness is expressed through context, demonstratives, and the ezafe construction. The demonstratives serve as markers of definiteness:

- **ئەم** (em) "this" - proximal demonstrative
- **ئەو** (ew) "that" - distal demonstrative

An indefinite noun can take the suffix **-ێک** (-ek): *پیاوێک* (pyawek) "a man," *کتێبێک* (kitebek) "a book."

### Ezafe Construction

The ezafe (ئیزافە) is a linking particle that connects nouns to their modifiers (adjectives, possessors, relative clauses). In Sorani, the ezafe takes the form:

- **-î / -ی** after consonants: *مالی گەورە* (mali gewre) "big house"
- **-y / -ی** after vowels: *باوکی من* (bawki min) "my father"

The ezafe chain can be extended: *دەروازەی گەورەی شاری کەرکوک* "the big gate of the city of Kirkuk."

### Plural Formation

Plurals are formed with suffixes:

- **-ان** (-an) for definite/specific plurals: *پیاوان* (pyawan) "the men," *ژنان* (zhinan) "the women"
- **-ەکان** (-ekan) for definite plurals with specificity: *کتێبەکان* (kitêbekan) "the (specific) books"

The plural suffix interacts with the ezafe: *کتێبەکانی من* (kitêbekani min) "my books."

## Verb System

### Stem System

Sorani verbs have two basic stems:

- **Present stem**: used for present tense, subjunctive, imperative
- **Past stem**: used for past tense, perfect, pluperfect

Examples:
| Infinitive | Present Stem | Past Stem | Meaning |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|---------|
| خواردن | خو- (xo-) | خوارد (xward) | to eat |
| نووسین | نووس- (nus-) | نووسی (nusi) | to write |
| ڕۆیشتن | ڕۆ- (rro-) | ڕۆیشت (rrosht) | to go |
| هاتن | ئێ- (e-) | هات (hat) | to come |
| کردن | کە- (ke-) | کرد (kird) | to do/make |

### Present Tense

The present tense is formed with the preverb **دە-** (de-) plus the present stem plus person endings:

- من دەنووسم (min denusim) "I write"
- تۆ دەنووسیت (to denusit) "you write"
- ئەو دەنووسێت (ew denuset) "he/she writes"
- ئێمە دەنووسین (eme denusin) "we write"
- ئێوە دەنووسن (ewe denusn) "you (pl.) write"
- ئەوان دەنووسن (ewan denusn) "they write"

### Past Tense and Split Ergativity

Sorani exhibits split ergativity in the past tense of transitive verbs. In the present tense, Sorani uses a nominative-accusative alignment, where the subject (agent) is in the nominative case and the verb agrees with the subject. However, in the past tense of transitive verbs, the alignment shifts to ergative-absolutive:

- The **agent** is marked with the **oblique case**
- The **verb agrees with the patient/object** (which is in the absolutive/direct case)

**Present tense (nominative-accusative)**:
- من نامەکە دەنووسم (min namekey denusim) - "I write the letter" (verb agrees with "I")

**Past tense (ergative-absolutive)**:
- (من) نامەکەم نووسی ((min) namekeym nusi) - "I wrote the letter" (the clitic -m refers to the agent; the verb form is 3rd person, agreeing with "letter")

This ergative pattern is realized through a set of pronominal clitics that attach to the first stressed element in the clause:

| Person | Clitic |
|--------|--------|
| 1sg | -م (-m) |
| 2sg | -ت (-t) |
| 3sg | -ی (-y) |
| 1pl | -مان (-man) |
| 2pl | -تان (-tan) |
| 3pl | -یان (-yan) |

### Preverbs

Sorani uses several preverbs that modify the meaning of verbs:

- **دە-** (de-): present/habitual marker
- **دا-** (da-): directional/completive
- **هەڵ-** (hell-): upward, outward
- **تێ-** (te-): into, through
- **پێ-** (pe-): to, toward (with dative meaning)
- **لێ-** (le-): from, off

Examples: *هەڵسان* (hellsan) "to rise up," *تێگەیشتن* (tegeyshtin) "to understand (lit. to reach through)."

### Subjunctive and Imperative

The subjunctive is formed with the preverb **بـ-** (bi-) replacing **دە-**: *بنووسم* (binusim) "that I write." The imperative uses the bare present stem or **بـ-**: *بنووسە!* (binuse!) "write!"

### Negative

Negation uses **نا-** (na-) for present: *نانووسم* (nanusim) "I don't write"; and **نە-** (ne-) for past and subjunctive: *نەمنووسی* (nemnusi) "I didn't write."

## Syntax Features

### SOV Word Order

The basic word order is Subject-Object-Verb, with the verb consistently in final position:

- *من کتێبێکم کڕی* (min kitebek-im kirri) - "I bought a book" (S-O-V)

However, topicalization and focus can alter this order, and the verb-final requirement is less strict in colloquial speech.

### Complementizer "کە" (ke)

Subordinate clauses are introduced by the complementizer **کە** (ke), equivalent to English "that/which/who/when":

- *ئەو پیاوەی کە دوێنێ هات* (ew pyaway ke dwene hat) - "the man who came yesterday"
- *دەزانم کە دێت* (dezanim ke det) - "I know that he/she is coming"

### Postpositions

Sorani uses postpositions and circumpositions rather than prepositions alone. Many spatial and grammatical relations are expressed with combinations:

- **لە ... دا** (le ... da) "in": *لە ماڵدا* (le mallda) "in the house"
- **لە ... ەوە** (le ... ewe) "from": *لە شارەوە* (le sharewe) "from the city"
- **بۆ** (bo) "for, to": *بۆ من* (bo min) "for me"
- **لەگەڵ** (legell) "with": *لەگەڵ من* (legell min) "with me"

### Relative Clauses

Relative clauses are typically introduced by **کە** and can follow the head noun (the most common pattern), linked by the ezafe:

- *کتێبەکەی کە کڕیم* (kitebekey ke kirrim) - "the book that I bought"

## Clitic Placement

Pronominal clitics in Sorani follow strict placement rules, typically attaching to the first stressed element in the clause (Wackernagel position). This is particularly important in past transitive constructions where the clitic represents the ergative agent.
