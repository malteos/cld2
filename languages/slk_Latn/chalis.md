# Slovak (slk_Latn) under CHALIS conditions

CHALIS (Tichý & Libovický 2026, arXiv 2606.06088) tests Slovak in three ways that matter here: against its cousin **Czech (ces)**, **without diacritics**, and with **homoglyphs** (Latin letters swapped for Cyrillic/Greek look-alikes). The gold label is the ISO 639-3 code **slk**. Local reference material:

- `../chalis/discriminators.md`, `../chalis/discriminators/ces-slk.tsv`, `../chalis/discriminators/ces-slk_nodiac.tsv`
- `variants/no_diacritics/` and `variants/homoglyphs/` (synthetic transforms of real UD text; they model, but are not, the CHALIS data)

## 1. Cousin-language cues robust to noise

Ordered from most to least robust:

1. **Reflexive *sa*** (rank 3 in Slovak) vs. Czech **se** (rank 2). Pure ASCII, survives diacritic stripping, and occurs in almost every sentence.
2. **Copula**: *som, sme, ste* (and *su* < *sú*) vs. Czech *jsem, jsme, jste, jsou*. The Czech *js-* onset never occurs in Slovak.
3. **Relative pronoun stem** *ktor-* (*ktory, ktora, ktore, ktorych*) vs. Czech *kter-*.
4. **Past tense** *bol, bola, bolo, boli* vs. Czech *byl, byla, bylo, byli*.
5. **Conjunctions/adverbs**: *ako, ak, alebo, ked, pretoze, lebo, len, iba, tiez, este, viac, medzi, preco, kto* vs. Czech *jako, pokud, nebo, kdyz, protoze, jen/pouze, take/taky, jeste, vice, mezi, proc, kdo*.
6. **Prepositions** *pre, vo, zo, ku* vs. Czech *pro, ve, ze, ke*.
7. **Endings** *-om* (instr./loc.), *-ej* (fem. adjectives: *slovenskej*), *-ov* (gen. pl.: *rokov*), *-nie* (*riesenie*), 1sg *-m* (*chcem, mozem*), 3pl *-u/-ia* (*budu, robia*) vs. Czech *-em, -e, -u, -ni, chci/muzu, budou*.
8. **Lexicon**: Slovak *teraz, vdaka, naozaj, hovorit, povedal* vs. Czech *ted, diky, opravdu, mluvit, rekl*.

## 2. Slovak without diacritics

When *ä ô ľ ĺ ŕ ť ď ň č š ž* and the acutes are stripped, the letter-level cues vanish (*ä→a, ô→o, ľ→l, ť→t*). Example from `variants/no_diacritics/ud_01.txt`:

> *Napriek nezaujmu o zapad sa aj Zenon snazil zachovat ideu jednotnej Rimskej rise, aj ked jej polovica sa momentalne nachadzala pod kontrolou Germanov.*

Cues that survive here: *sa* (twice), *aj*, *ked*, *napriek*, *-ej* (*jednotnej Rimskej*), *-ov* (*Germanov*), *-at* infinitive is ambiguous (Cz. *-at* too).

Top Slovak markers in stripped form (from `ces-slk_nodiac.tsv`): *ako, som, bol, pre, ak, alebo, nie, ich, len, vo, ked, ktory, ktore, bola, ktora, bolo, este, zo, podla, viac, medzi, moze, tiez, pozri, pocas*. Czech counterparts: *jsem, jako, jsou, kdyz, byl, jsme, ktere, ktery, bylo, jeste, byla, pak, ktera, bych, protoze, prvni, podle, pokud, neco, ted, mezi, jste, proc, sve, jejich*.

**Ambiguity cases** (identical after stripping; do not count them): *co* (Cz. *co* / Sk. *čo*), *pri*, *pred*, *byt*, *ze*, *uz*, *az*, *velmi*, *mam*, *ma*, *nic*, *tri*, *dobre*, many infinitives in *-at/-it/-ovat* (Sk. *-ať/-iť/-ovať* = Cz. *-at/-it/-ovat* once stripped). *su* is Slovak (*sú*) but also Croatian/Bosnian *su*. *nie* is Slovak but also Polish. A short ASCII sentence such as *To je dobre.* is genuinely undecidable between Czech and Slovak; say so only if no marker is present, and otherwise follow the markers.

Two spelling traces survive stripping: the cluster *dz* where Czech has *z* (*medzi, cudzi* vs. Cz. *mezi, cizi*), and the diphthong spellings *ie/ia/iu* (*viac, miesto, riesenie, niekolko* vs. Cz. *vice, misto, reseni, nekolik*). In native Czech words *ie/ia* are uncommon (they appear mainly in loanwords such as *Asie, studia*), so a high density of *ie/ia* favours Slovak (qualitative observation, not a measured rate).

## 3. Homoglyph handling

CHALIS homoglyph text replaces some Latin letters with visually identical Cyrillic or Greek characters. In this repo's simulation (`variants/homoglyphs/`, generated by `../scripts/build_chalis_variants.py`), about half of the eligible letters are swapped: a→а U+0430, c→с U+0441, e→е U+0435, o→о U+043E, p→р U+0440, x→х U+0445, y→у U+0443, i→і U+0456, j→ј U+0458, s→ѕ U+0455, v→ν (Greek nu) U+03BD, and capitals А В С Е Н К М О Р Т Х. The real CHALIS set may use other look-alikes (e.g. Greek ο, α); this is not verified here.

Procedure:

1. **Normalize first**: map every Cyrillic/Greek look-alike back to its Latin letter, then identify the language on the normalized text.
2. A word that is mostly Latin but contains one or two Cyrillic letters (e.g. *ѕа*, *νеľmi*, *kеď*) is a **homoglyph attack, not Russian/Serbian/Bulgarian**. Genuine Cyrillic text has whole words in Cyrillic and Cyrillic-only letters (*ы, щ, ж, ш, ч, я, ю, ё, ъ, ь, ќ, ђ*).
3. Precomposed Slovak letters (*ľ, ô, ä, ť, ď, ň, č, š, ž, á, é, í, ú, ý*) are **not** replaced in the simulation, so they remain intact and keep their full diagnostic value. A text containing *ľ* or *ô* plus scattered Cyrillic *а/е/о* is Slovak.

Example from `variants/homoglyphs/ud_01.txt` (raw):

> *Naprіеk nezáuјmu o záраd sa aј Zenón snаžil zachoνať ideu јеdnоtnej Rímskеj ríšе, aj kеď jеj pоlоvісa ѕa ...*

Normalized: *Napriek nezáujmu o západ sa aj Zenón snažil zachovať ideu jednotnej Rímskej ríše, aj keď jej polovica sa ...* → **slk** (*sa, aj, keď, zachovať, -ej*).

## 4. Decision checklist

1. Strip/normalize homoglyphs (Cyrillic/Greek look-alikes → Latin).
2. Recurring *ä, ô, ľ, ĺ, ŕ*, or many word-final *-ť* → **slk**. Recurring *ě, ř, ů* → **ces**.
3. No diagnostic letters? Count markers: *sa, som, sme, su, ako, ak, ked, bol/bola/bolo, ktor-, pre, alebo, nie je, este, viac, medzi, tiez, len/iba* vs. Czech *se, jsem, jsou, jako, kdyz, byl/byla/bylo, kter-, pro, nebo, neni, jeste, vice, mezi, take, jen*.
4. Still tied? Use endings: *-om, -ej, -ov, -nie, 3pl -u* vs. *-em, -e, -u (gen.pl.), -ni, 3pl -ou*.
5. Rule out non-Czech confusables: Polish (*ł, ę, ą, się, przez*), Croatian/Slovenian (*što, koji, kao, ki, tudi*, no length marks).
6. Only if nothing distinguishes, choose by the overall marker balance; very short shared-vocabulary strings remain ambiguous.

## 5. Illustrative noisy snippets

1. *Vcera som bol v meste, ale obchod uz bol zatvoreny, takze sme isli domov.* → **slk** (*som, bol, sme, isli*; Czech would be *Včera jsem byl ve městě ... takže jsme šli domů*).
2. *Ľudіа, ktоrí ѕú tu, hονоrіа pо slονеnѕkу.* (Cyrillic а е о і ѕ у and Greek ν) → normalize → *Ľudia, ktorí sú tu, hovoria po slovensky.* → **slk** (*ľ, ktorí, sú, hovoria*).
3. *Myslim, ze to nie je pravda, pretoze tam nikto nebol.* → **slk** (*nie je, pretoze, nikto, nebol*; Czech: *není, protože, nikdo, nebyl*).
