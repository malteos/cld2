# Slovak (slk_Latn) - Grammar Sketch

## Typology

Slovak is a West Slavic, strongly **fusional** (inflecting) language. Grammatical relations are marked mainly by case and agreement endings, so word order is relatively free and driven by information structure (topic first, new information last). The neutral order is **SVO**. Slovak is a pro-drop language: subject pronouns are usually omitted because the verb ending identifies the person (*Robím* "I work", not normally *Ja robím*).

## Phonology (as reflected in spelling)

- **Stress** is fixed on the first syllable of a word.
- **Vowel length** is phonemic and marked with the acute accent: *á é í ó ú ý*.
- **Diphthongs** *ia, ie, iu* and *ô* (/uo/): *viac* "more", *miesto* "place", *cudziu*, *môj* "my", *kôň* "horse". Where Czech has *í* or *ů*, Slovak often has *ie* or *ô* (Cz. *místo* / Sk. *miesto*; Cz. *kůň* / Sk. *kôň*).
- **ä** is a separate letter, pronounced in the standard mostly like *e* (/ɛ/; an /æ/ pronunciation also exists): *mäso* "meat", *päť* "five", *pamäť* "memory".
- **Syllabic liquids** *r, l* and their long forms *ŕ, ĺ*: *krk* "neck", *vlk* "wolf", *vŕba* "willow", *vĺča* "wolf cub", *dĺžka* "length".
- **Palatal consonants** *ď ť ň ľ*. Before *e, i* the softness is usually not written (*deti* is read with soft d). *ľ* is a distinct phoneme: *ľudia* "people", *veľmi* "very".
- **Affricates** *dz, dž* are separate letters: *medzi* "between", *cudzí* "foreign", *džem*.
- **Rhythmic law** (*rytmické krátenie*): two long syllables normally may not follow each other, so a long ending is shortened after a long stem: *pekný* but *krásny*, *robím* but *chválim*. There are exceptions (e.g. *-ia* in *vidia*, and some word classes).
- Slovak has **no ř and no ě** (both Czech) and no vowel *ů* (Czech).

## Nouns

Three genders (masculine, feminine, neuter); masculine distinguishes **animate vs. inanimate**. Six productive cases: nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, locative (only after prepositions), instrumental. The vocative survives only in a few fixed forms (*pane*, *bože*, *človeče*); normally the nominative is used for address (contrast Czech, where the vocative is productive).

Characteristic endings:

| Form | Slovak | Czech (for contrast) |
|---|---|---|
| masc. instr. sg. | *s bratom* | *s bratrem* (-em) |
| masc. gen. pl. | *rokov, chlapov* | *roků, chlapů* (-ů) |
| masc. anim. nom. pl. | *chlapi, synovia, hrdinovia* (-ia/-ovia) | *synové* (-ové) |
| fem. instr. sg. | *so ženou* | *se ženou* |
| neuter verbal noun | *riešenie, vzdelanie* (-ie) | *řešení, vzdělání* (-í) |
| fem. acc. sg. (soft) | *prácu, ulicu* | *práci, ulici* |

## Adjectives and pronouns

Adjectives agree in gender, number and case: *dobrý muž, dobrá žena, dobré dieťa*. Feminine genitive/dative/locative singular ends in **-ej**: *slovenskej republiky*, *v tejto časti*, *k dobrej žene* (Czech: *české republiky*, *v této části*). Common pronouns: *ja, ty, on, ona, ono, my, vy, oni/ony*; demonstratives *ten, tá, to, tento, táto*; relative *ktorý, ktorá, ktoré* (Czech *který*); interrogatives *kto* "who", *čo* "what" (Czech *kdo, co*). The reflexive pronoun is **sa** (acc.) / **si** (dat.); Czech uses *se* / *si*.

## Verbs

Verbs inflect for person and number and distinguish **perfective vs. imperfective aspect** (*robiť / urobiť* "do", *písať / napísať* "write"). The infinitive ends in **-ť**: *byť, mať, robiť, povedať* (Czech *-t*: *být, mít, dělat*).

Present tense of *byť* "be": *som, si, je, sme, ste, sú*. Negation: *nie som, nie si, nie je, nie sme, nie ste, nie sú* (written as two words; Czech fuses: *nejsem, není, nejsou*).

Present-tense endings: 1sg is always **-m** (*robím, píšem, chcem, môžem, idem*), 3pl is **-ú/-u** or **-ia/-a** (*robia, píšu, chcú, majú, sú*). Czech often has 1sg *-u/-i* (*píšu, chci, můžu, jdu*) and 3pl *-ou/-í/-ají* (*píšou, chtějí, mají, jsou*).

Past tense: l-participle plus auxiliary *byť* in the 1st and 2nd person: *robil som* "I did", *bola si* "you (f.) were", *boli sme* "we were"; 3rd person has no auxiliary (*bol, bola, boli*). Future: *budem, budeš, bude, budeme, budete, budú* + imperfective infinitive (*budem robiť*), or the present form of a perfective verb (*urobím* "I will do"). Conditional: *by* + l-participle with the person marked by the auxiliary: **by som robil**, *by si*, *by sme*, *by ste* (Czech: *dělal bych, bys, bychom, byste*).

## Syntax

- **Clitic cluster** in second position: *sa, si, som, si, sme, ste, by, mu, ho, ju, mi*: *Včera som sa mu to snažil vysvetliť* "Yesterday I tried to explain it to him."
- Negation is prefixed to the verb: *nemám* "I don't have", *nevie* "doesn't know"; only forms of *byť* are negated with separate *nie*.
- Yes/no questions use intonation or verb-first order; particle *či* introduces embedded questions: *Neviem, či príde.* "I don't know whether he will come."
- Common conjunctions: *a, aj, ale, alebo, že, aby, keď, ak, pretože, lebo, ako*.
- Prepositions take vocalized forms before clusters: *v / vo* (*vo vode*), *s / so* (*so mnou*), *z / zo* (*zo školy*), *k / ku* (*ku koncu*). Czech uses *ve, se, ze, ke*.

## Short examples

- *Som študent a bývam v Bratislave.* "I am a student and live in Bratislava."
- *To nie je pravda, pretože sme tam boli.* "That's not true, because we were there."
- *Keby som mal čas, išiel by som s vami.* "If I had time, I would go with you."
- *Ľudia, ktorí sú tu, hovoria po slovensky.* "The people who are here speak Slovak."
