# Marathi (mar)

## Summary

Marathi (endonym: **मराठी** *marāṭhī*) is an Indo-European → Indo-Aryan → Southern-Zone language. ISO codes: `mr` (639-1), `mar` (639-2 / 639-3). It is the official language of the Indian state of Maharashtra and a co-official language of Goa. L1 speakers number about 83–85 million, making it one of the top twenty languages in the world. Principal regions are Maharashtra, Goa, northern Karnataka, and a sizeable diaspora in Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat and Tamil Nadu.

## Writing system

Marathi uses the Devanagari abugida (block U+0900–U+097F) in a slightly adapted form sometimes called **बाळबोध** *Bāḷbodh*, distinct from the traditional Modi script that was in earlier use. Marathi retains three letters Hindi generally does not employ: **ळ** (retroflex lateral ḷ), **ऱ** (eyelash-ra, flap of /ɾ/ in certain dialects) and occasionally **ज़ / ऱ्य** combinations. It also uses **ऍ** and **ऑ** for English-loan vowels. The headline bar, conjunct ligatures, anusvāra and visarga are present as in Hindi. Unlike Hindi, Marathi shows a productive schwa contrast at word-end.

## History and status

Marathi is one of the oldest Indo-Aryan literary languages, with inscriptions from the 11th century and a rich literary corpus from the Bhakti poets (Dnyaneshwar, Tukaram, Eknath, Ramdas). Modern literary Marathi developed in the 19th century alongside printing in Bombay/Pune. Marathi received the status of *Classical Language of India* in 2024.
