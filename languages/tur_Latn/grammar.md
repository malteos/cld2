# Turkish grammar

- **Word order**: Verb-final SOV but relatively free thanks to rich
  case marking. Modifiers precede their heads (adjective–noun,
  genitive–noun, relative–noun). Topic–comment ordering is common
  in discourse.
- **Morphology**: Strongly agglutinative. Words are built by
  stacking suffixes with predictable boundaries: *ev-ler-im-de-ki-ler*
  "the ones at my houses". Eight-vowel backness harmony plus
  four-vowel roundness harmony regulate suffix allomorphs.
- **Nouns**: No grammatical gender, no definite article. Six
  productive cases: nominative (∅), accusative (*-i/-ı/-u/-ü*),
  dative (*-e/-a*), locative (*-de/-da/-te/-ta*), ablative
  (*-den/-dan/-ten/-tan*), genitive (*-in/-ın/-un/-ün*). Plural
  *-ler/-lar*. Possessives *-im, -in, -i, -imiz, -iniz, -leri*
  attach before case.
- **Verbs**: Tense/aspect/mood suffixes follow the stem in a fixed
  order: negation (*-me/-ma*), ability (*-ebil*), tense/aspect
  (*-di* past, *-miş* evidential past, *-iyor* progressive,
  *-ecek/-acak* future, *-ir/-er/-ar* aorist), then personal
  ending. The copula is a clitic (*-dir/-dır/-dur/-dür*).
- **Phonology**: Eight vowels in four front/back × rounded/unrounded
  pairs (a e ı i o ö u ü). Consonant alternations include final
  devoicing (*kitap → kitabı*) and k ↔ ğ before vowel suffixes.
  Stress is usually word-final.
- **Syntax**: Pro-drop in all persons since verbs mark subject. No
  copular verb "to be" in the present (*o bir öğretmen* "he is a
  teacher"). Negation is a verbal suffix; polar questions are marked
  by the enclitic *mi/mı/mu/mü* (written as a separate word). No
  relative pronouns; participial constructions (*-en*, *-diği*)
  replace them.
