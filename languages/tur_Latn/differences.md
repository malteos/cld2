# Turkish vs confusable languages

Turkish sits in the Oghuz-Turkic close set with Azerbaijani (aze, azj),
Turkmen (tuk), Crimean Tatar (crh) and Gagauz (gag). Latin-script Uzbek
(uzb) can also look superficially similar at the alphabet level.

## tur ↔ azj / aze (Azerbaijani, Latin)

- `ə` (schwa) is the single strongest diagnostic: Azerbaijani uses
  it constantly (*mən, səhər, gələn, dəfə*); Turkish never writes
  `ə`.
- Azerbaijani keeps `q` and `x` as native phonemes (*qardaş, xoş,
  xalq*); Turkish uses `k` and `h` in the cognates (*kardeş, hoş,
  halk*).
- Copula: Azerbaijani *-dir/-dır/-dur/-dür* matches Turkish, but
  Azerbaijani also frequently shortens to bare *-di*.
- The aze macrocode material sometimes shows older or mixed
  orthography, while azj is the modern North Azerbaijani Latin
  standard — both share `ə q x` against Turkish.

## tur ↔ tuk (Turkmen)

- Turkmen has `ä ý ň ž`; Turkish has none of these.
- Turkmen regularly writes long vowels as doubled letters (*aalym,
  gaarag*); Turkish does not double vowels in native words.

## tur ↔ crh (Crimean Tatar, Latin)

- Crimean Tatar uses `ñ` for the velar nasal; Turkish has no `ñ`.
- Both share `ç ğ ı ö ş ü`, so absence/presence of `ñ` is the
  cleanest cue. Crimean Tatar also uses `q` in native words; Turkish
  does not.

## tur ↔ uzb (Uzbek, Latin)

- Uzbek uses the digraphs `oʻ` and `gʻ` with a modifier-letter
  apostrophe `ʻ` (U+02BB); Turkish has neither.
- Uzbek lacks `ı`, `ğ`, `ş`; Turkish has all three and uses them
  very frequently.
