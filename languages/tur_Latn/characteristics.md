# Turkish characteristics

## Script signals

Latin script with six diacritic letters: `ç ğ ı ö ş ü` plus the
case-contrasting pair `i / İ` and `ı / I`. The dotless `ı` and the
soft-g `ğ` are the two most diagnostic characters — they appear in
almost every Turkish sentence of any length. The apostrophe `'`
inside a running word usually marks a proper-noun + suffix boundary
(*Türkiye'nin*). Turkish has **no** `ä ə ñ ý ň ǝ`, and `q w x` do
not appear in native vocabulary.

## Top function words

*ve, bir, bu, şu, o, için, ile, da, de, ki, ama, fakat, veya, veyahut,
çok, daha, en, var, yok, değil, ben, sen, biz, siz, onlar, gibi,
kadar, sonra, önce, hem, ise, mi, ne, nasıl, niçin, niye*.

## N-gram and spelling patterns

- Plural endings `-ler`, `-lar` at word end are extremely frequent.
- Locative `-de/-da/-te/-ta` and ablative `-den/-dan/-ten/-tan`.
- Person endings `-im`, `-in`, `-iz`, `-siniz`, `-ler`.
- High-frequency suffix clusters: `-ıyor`, `-iyor`, `-eceği`,
  `-acağı`, `-lığı`, `-liği`, `-diği`, `-mış`, `-miş`, `-muş`.
- Word-initial `ş-` and `ç-` occur freely; `ğ` never begins a native
  word.
- Very long agglutinative word-forms are normal (eight to twelve
  syllables in one orthographic word is unremarkable).

## Morphology hooks

- Nominal possessive + case: *ev-im-de, ev-imiz-in*.
- Aorist `-r/-er/-ar/-ir/-ır/-ur/-ür`.
- Progressive `-iyor` is a fixed, non-harmonising morpheme — strong
  cue.
- Subordinator `-diği` / `-eceği` builds participial complements.
- Enclitic question particle *mi/mı/mu/mü* written as a separate
  orthographic word.

## Punctuation and orthography

Standard Latin punctuation. The apostrophe before suffixes on proper
nouns is distinctive. Quotations use `"…"` or French `«…»`. Decimal
separator is the comma; thousands separator is the period.
