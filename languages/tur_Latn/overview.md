# Turkish (tur)

## Summary

Turkish (endonym: *Türkçe*) is a Turkic language in the Oghuz branch
(Turkic → Common Turkic → Oghuz → Western Oghuz). ISO codes: 639-1 `tr`,
639-2 `tur`, 639-3 `tur`. It is written in the Latin script and has
roughly 80–90 million native speakers, with 10–15 million L2 users.
Türkiye (Turkey) is the principal homeland; sizeable communities live
in Cyprus, Bulgaria, Greece, Germany, the Netherlands, Austria,
Belgium, and France.

## Writing system

Modern Turkish uses a 29-letter Latin alphabet adopted in the 1928
*Harf Devrimi* (alphabet reform) that replaced the Ottoman
Perso-Arabic script. Distinctive letters are `ç ğ ı i ö ş ü` with a
case-sensitive i/ı pair: uppercase dotted `İ` and dotless `I`. The
letters `q w x` are not used in native words and appear only in
loanwords and proper names. An apostrophe `'` separates proper-noun
stems from case suffixes: *İstanbul'da*, *Ankara'nın*. Decimal
separator is the comma.

## History and status

Turkish is the official language of the Republic of Türkiye and a
co-official language of Cyprus. The 20th-century *Öz Türkçe* purism
movement replaced many Arabic and Persian loanwords with native
Turkic or newly coined roots, giving modern Turkish a more distinctly
Turkic lexicon than pre-1928 Ottoman written material.
