# Azerbaijani (macro) characteristics

## Script signals

Latin alphabet with `ə ç ğ ı i ö ş ü` plus phonemic `q` and `x`.
**`ə` (schwa, U+0259) is the single most discriminating character**
against Turkish. The letter `q` in native words (*qardaş, qız, qara,
qapı*) and word-final `x` (*çox, xoş, xalq*) also separate this
language from Turkish. Some macrocode-era material shows `ä` in
place of `ə` (1992–1993 orthography); either counts as an
Azerbaijani cue.

## Top function words

*və, bir, bu, o, üçün, ilə, də, da, ki, amma, lakin, və ya, çox,
daha, ən, var, yox, deyil, mən, sən, biz, siz, onlar, kimi, qədər,
sonra, əvvəl, həm, isə, mı, nə, necə, niyə*.

## N-gram and spelling patterns

- Locative/ablative `-də/-dən` (front) alongside `-da/-dan` (back).
- Plural `-lər/-lar`.
- Verbal `-ir/-ür/-ır/-ur` (present) and `-əcək/-acaq` (future).
- Word-final `-dir/-dır/-dur/-dür` copula.
- High-frequency schwa sequences: `ər`, `əl`, `əm`, `ən`, `əsi`,
  `ədə`.
- `q` is frequent word-initially in native stems and `x` appears
  medially/finally.

## Morphology hooks

- Dative *-a/-ə* contrast is diagnostic: the front-vowel dative `-ə`
  never occurs in Turkish.
- Copula *-dir*; predicative past *-idi* (written separately or
  attached).
- Interrogative clitic `mı/mi/mu/mü` written as a separate word.

## Punctuation and orthography

Standard Latin punctuation; quotations use `“…”` or `«…»`. Decimal
comma, thousands period. Apostrophe historically marked glottal
stop/hiatus in Arabic loans (*məs'ul*); the reformed orthography
usually omits it, but older macrocode text may still contain it.
