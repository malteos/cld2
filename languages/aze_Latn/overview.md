# Azerbaijani — macro (aze)

## Summary

Azerbaijani (endonym: *Azərbaycan dili*, *Azərbaycanca*) is a Turkic
language of the Oghuz branch (Turkic → Common Turkic → Oghuz →
Western Oghuz), closely related to Turkish. The macrolanguage code
ISO 639-3 `aze` (639-1 `az`, 639-2 `aze`) covers two varieties:
North Azerbaijani `azj` (Republic of Azerbaijan, Russia, Georgia)
and South Azerbaijani `azb` (Iranian Azerbaijan). Total speakers are
estimated at 24–35 million. In CLD2/CommonLID the `aze` macrocode
typically aggregates Latin-script North Azerbaijani material and may
include older orthographic variants.

## Writing system

This folder covers the Latin-script tradition. The modern alphabet
was re-adopted in 1991–1992 after Cyrillic (1939–1991) and pre-1929
Perso-Arabic usage. Distinctive Latin letters are
`ə ç ğ ı i ö ş ü q x`. The schwa `ə` (U+0259) is the single
strongest signal versus Turkish. The letter `Q q` and `X x` are used
with native phonemic values (/ɢ/–/k/ and /x/), unlike Turkish. The
historic digraph `ə` was briefly replaced by `ä` in 1992–1993; some
macrocode corpora therefore contain `ä` in otherwise
Azerbaijani-looking text.

## History and status

Azerbaijani is the state language of the Republic of Azerbaijan,
where the Latin script is official. Sizable communities exist in
Iran (using Perso-Arabic, covered by `azb`), Russia, Turkey, and
Georgia.
