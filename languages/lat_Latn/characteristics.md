# Latin characteristics (LID signals)

## Script signals

Plain 26-letter Roman alphabet with **no diacritics** in running text. The **absence** of language-specific characters (no `á é í ó ú ñ ç ã õ ë ü ö ß å ø æ ł ą ę ś ż ž č š ć đ ř å`) against the presence of Latin inflectional endings is itself diagnostic: Latin is one of the few widely-attested Latin-script languages with no non-ASCII letters. Editorial macrons (ā ē ī ō ū) may appear in pedagogical text but never in classical/medieval originals.

## Top function words

**et** (and), **ac / atque** (and), **-que** (enclitic "and"), **sed** (but), **aut** (or), **vel** (or), **nec / neque** (nor), **non** (not), **si** (if), **ut** (as/that/so-that), **ne** (lest), **cum** (when/with), **ubi** (where/when), **quod / quia** (because), **quoniam** (since), **autem** (however), **enim** (for), **igitur / ergo / itaque** (therefore), **quidem** (indeed), **tamen** (nevertheless), **in** (in/into), **ad** (to), **de** (from/about), **ex / e** (out of), **per** (through), **pro** (for), **sine** (without), **qui / quae / quod** (who/which), **hic / haec / hoc** (this), **ille / illa / illud** (that), **is / ea / id** (he/she/it/that), **sum / est / sunt / erat / erant / fuit** (forms of *esse* "to be").

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- Word-final **`-us`**, **`-um`**, **`-am`**, **`-ae`**, **`-is`**, **`-ibus`**, **`-ōrum`** (`-orum`), **`-ārum`** (`-arum`) — the hallmark case endings.
- Verb endings **`-t`**, **`-nt`**, **`-mus`**, **`-tis`**, **`-bat`**, **`-bant`**, **`-erit`**, **`-issent`**, **`-ētur`**, **`-ntur`**.
- Enclitic **`-que`** at word end joining coordinands.
- Frequent prefixes: **`prae-`**, **`con-`**, **`in-`**, **`ex-`**, **`dis-`**, **`sub-`**, **`trans-`**, **`ad-`**, **`re-`**.
- Absence of `w`, rarity of `k` and `y` (restricted to Greek loans), moderately frequent `qu`, `ph`, `th`, `ch` (the last three in Hellenisms).

## Morphology hooks

- Nominal endings by declension as above: `-a/-ae/-am/-ārum` (1st), `-us/-ī/-um/-ōrum` (2nd m.), `-um/-ī/-a` (2nd n.), `-is/-em/-ibus` (3rd), `-us/-ūs/-uī/-uum` (4th), `-ēs/-eī/-em` (5th).
- Verb endings: present `-ō/-s/-t/-mus/-tis/-nt`, imperfect `-bam/-bās/-bat`, future `-bō/-bis/-bit` (1st-2nd conj.) or `-am/-ēs/-et` (3rd-4th conj.), perfect `-ī/-istī/-it/-imus/-istis/-ērunt`.

## Punctuation / orthography

Medieval and Renaissance Latin often uses abbreviations (**&** or **7** for *et*, macrons over vowels for nasals, `q̃` for *quod*). Modern printed editions normalise to `i/j` and `u/v`, use standard `. , ; : ! ?` punctuation, and capitalise proper names and sentence starts.
