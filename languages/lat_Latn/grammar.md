# Latin grammar

- **Word order**: Predominantly SOV in classical prose but highly free because of rich case morphology. Verbs often appear clause-finally; modifiers may precede or follow heads; discontinuous constituents (hyperbaton) are routine in literary texts.
- **Morphology**: Strongly **fusional** (inflectional), not agglutinative. A single suffix typically encodes several features at once (e.g. *-ōrum* = genitive + plural + masculine/neuter). Productive use of prefixes (*ad-, con-, dis-, ex-, in-, prae-, re-, sub-*).
- **Nouns**: Three genders (masculine, feminine, neuter), two numbers, six cases (nominative, vocative, accusative, genitive, dative, ablative), organised into five declensions. Case endings are the key LID signal: 1st decl. fem. `-a / -ae / -am / -ārum / -īs`; 2nd decl. masc. `-us / -ī / -um / -ōrum / -īs`; 2nd decl. neut. `-um / -ī / -a / -ōrum / -īs`; 3rd decl. varied, frequent `-is / -em / -e / -um / -ibus`.
- **Verbs**: Four conjugations (`-āre, -ēre, -ĕre, -īre`). Categories: three persons × two numbers; six tenses (present, imperfect, future, perfect, pluperfect, future perfect); three moods (indicative, subjunctive, imperative); two voices (active, passive — the latter synthetic in non-perfect tenses, periphrastic in perfect). Characteristic endings: active 1sg `-ō/-m`, 2sg `-s`, 3sg `-t`, 1pl `-mus`, 2pl `-tis`, 3pl `-nt`; perfect 1sg `-ī`, 3sg `-it`, 3pl `-ērunt`.
- **Phonology (classical)**: Five vowel qualities × two lengths (a, e, i, o, u each short/long), diphthongs `ae`, `au`, `oe`, `ei`, `eu`, `ui`. Stress is predictable from syllable weight. Geminate consonants are phonemic and written.
- **Syntax notes**: **Pro-drop** (subject pronouns are usually omitted; person is carried by the verb ending). No definite or indefinite articles. Copula **esse** is normally present but may be omitted in gnomic/proverbial contexts. Enclitic **-que** "and" attaches to the second conjunct (*Senātus Populusque Rōmānus*).
