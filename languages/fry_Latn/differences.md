# Western Frisian vs confusable languages

Western Frisian sits in heavy contact with Dutch and shares many function-word skeletons with it, so Dutch is by far the most frequent confuser in CommonLID. Frisian is also the continental Germanic language most historically related to English, which can cause short-snippet confusion when Frisian text contains Anglo-Frisian cognates.

## fry ↔ nld (primary confuser)

- Circumflex vowels `â, ê, ô, û` occur in Frisian only; Dutch has none.
- Frisian `y` in pronouns *wy, hy, sy, my, dy* corresponds to Dutch `ij` in *wij, hij, zij, mijn, dij*.
- Frisian `ea`, `ie`, `oa` diphthong digraphs are absent from native Dutch spelling.
- Neuter article *it* (Frisian) vs *het* (Dutch); negation *net* vs *niet*; 2pl pronoun *jimme* vs *jullie*.
- Frisian perfect uses *hat*/*hawwe* (cf. Dutch *heeft*/*hebben*), past participles often lack the Dutch *ge-* prefix (*makke* vs Dutch *gemaakt*, *sjoen* vs Dutch *gezien*).

## fry ↔ eng (Anglo-Frisian cognates)

- Many Frisian high-frequency words resemble English: *tsiis* 'cheese', *kaai* 'key', *brea* 'bread', *tsjerke* 'church'. Diagnostic: circumflex vowels and function words *de, it, yn, en, net, hy, sy, jimme*.
- Frisian has no `-ing` progressive, no *do*-support, no `'s` possessive clitic, and always writes `y` in *my, hy, sy*.

## fry ↔ afr (Afrikaans)

- Both use circumflex on vowels, but Afrikaans lacks `ea/ie/oa` diphthongs and the pronouns *jimme, jo, hy, sy* in the Frisian distribution; Afrikaans has double negation *nie … nie* which Frisian does not.

## fry ↔ deu (German)

- No `ß`, no umlaut `ä/ö/ü`, no noun capitalisation in Frisian; circumflex vowels are diagnostic in Frisian rather than umlauts.
