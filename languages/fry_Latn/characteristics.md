# Western Frisian characteristics (LID signals)

## Script signals

Circumflex vowels `â, ê, ô, û` are the single strongest positive cue for Western Frisian in the Germanic set (*hûn, wêze, wâld, fjouwer, tûk*). Diaereses are rare and only in loans; acute `é` occurs on stressed monosyllables. Digraphs `ea`, `ie`, `oa`, `ue` as true falling diphthongs (*read, brief, boat, bluesfestival*) are distinctive. Frisian does **not** use `ß`, umlauts `ä/ö/ü`, or the Dutch digraph `ij` — instead it writes `y` or `ii` where Dutch has *ij* (*wy, myn, tiid*).

## Top function words

*de, it, in, en, fan, yn, is, op, te, dat, dy, net, wêze, mei, foar, oan, der, ek, as, mar, troch, om, oer, of, wat, wa, hy, sy, wy, ik, do, jo, jimme, se, hat, hawwe, wie, waard, wurdt, gjin, noch, al, by, nei, út, oant, dan, doe, sa*.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- Diphthong digraphs `ea` (*read*), `ie` (*brief, stien*), `oa` (*boat, hoas*), `ue` (rare, loans), `ui` (*húske, muis*), `ji-` in pronouns (*jimme, jim*).
- Word `it` as neuter definite article (cf. Dutch *het*, English *the*/*it*).
- Suffixes `-ens` (*wierheidens*), `-skip` (*freonskip*), `-heid` (*wierheid*), `-en` (infinitive/plural), diminutive `-ke`, `-tsje`, `-je`.
- `y` as vowel in *wy, hy, sy, my, dy, tiid* (where Dutch uses *ij*/*hij*/*mijn*).
- Words ending in `-je` are often verbs (*prate, spylje*) or diminutives.
- No `ß`, no `ij`, no Afrikaans *nie … nie* double negation.

## Morphology hooks

Infinitives in `-e` or `-en` (*prate, rinne, sjonge, sjogge, praten, rinnen*). Perfect with *hat*/*hawwe*/*hie* + participle; passive with *wurdt*/*waard* + participle. Second-person plural *jimme* / polite *jo* are near-unique pronoun shapes.

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

Apostrophe-*t* contractions (*it's* → *'t is*, *'t hûs* 'the house') are common. Decimal comma, period thousands, like Dutch. Quotation marks `'...'` or `"..."`.
