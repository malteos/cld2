# Shona (sna)

## Summary

Shona (endonym: **chiShona**) is a Bantu language of the Niger-Congo
family (Niger-Congo → Atlantic-Congo → Benue-Congo → Bantu → S10). ISO
codes: 639-1 `sn`, 639-2 `sna`, 639-3 `sna` (macrolanguage covering
Karanga, Zezuru, Manyika, Korekore, Ndau and others). It has
approximately 10–15 million speakers, the majority in Zimbabwe where
it is co-official with English and Ndebele. Significant Shona-speaking
communities also live in Mozambique, Zambia and Botswana.

## Writing system

Latin script, bicameral, no diacritics. 26 letters. The "Unified
Shona" orthography of 1931 (revised 1955) is standard. Characteristic
digraphs and trigraphs for prenasalised and "whistled" consonants:
`nd`, `mb`, `nh`, `nz`, `nj`, `ng`, `mh`, `nk`, `mp`, `sv`, `zv`,
`tsv`, `dzv`, `mhw`, `ngw`, `dy`, `ty`, `pf`, `bv`. The letters `c`,
`l`, `q`, `x` do not occur in native roots (`l` appears only in
loanwords; Shona uses `r` where related languages use `l`).

## History

Shona was unified in orthography by Clement Doke in 1931, drawing
mainly on the Karanga and Zezuru dialects. It has a strong literary
tradition in Zimbabwe and features in broadcasting, education and
Bible translation across southern Africa.
