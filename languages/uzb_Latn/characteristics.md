# Uzbek (Latin) characteristics

## Script signals

Latin script with the distinctive **digraphs `oʻ` and `gʻ`**
formed with the modifier letter `ʻ` (U+02BB — *not* the ASCII
apostrophe `'` U+0027, though web text often substitutes it).
Digraphs **`sh`** and **`ch`** also mark single phonemes. Uzbek
Latin has **no** `ı ğ ş ö ü ä ə ñ ý ň ž` — the absence of any
Turkic diacritics combined with presence of `oʻ`/`gʻ` is a very
strong positive identifier.

## Top function words

*va, bir, bu, shu, o, uchun, bilan, ham, lekin, ammo, yoki, yo,
juda, ko'p, koʻp, eng, bor, yoʻq, emas, men, sen, biz, siz, ular,
kabi, qadar, keyin, oldin, hali, yana, qachon, nima, qanday, nega,
kim, qayerda*.

## N-gram and spelling patterns

- Locative `-da`, ablative `-dan`, genitive `-ning`,
  accusative `-ni`, dative `-ga/-ka/-qa`.
- Plural `-lar`.
- Present/future `-adi`, perfective `-gan`, progressive
  `-yapti/-yapman`.
- Common Uzbek words with `oʻ`: *oʻzbek, soʻz, oʻqituvchi, oʻn,
  oʻylash, koʻp, koʻcha*.
- Common Uzbek words with `gʻ`: *togʻ, bogʻ, ogʻiz, yogʻ, oʻrgʻa,
  ogʻir*.
- Digraphs `sh` and `ch` occur extremely frequently where Turkish
  would have `ş` and `ç`.
- Word-final `-q` is very common (*yoʻq, xalq, yaxshiq*).

## Morphology hooks

- Fixed suffix shapes (no harmony): `-lar` plural, `-ning`
  genitive, `-ga` dative — all invariant.
- Present `-adi` is a distinct Uzbek cue versus Turkish `-iyor` or
  Azerbaijani `-ir`.
- `-moqchi` (intentional future) is unique to Uzbek.

## Punctuation and orthography

Standard Latin punctuation. Quotations `"…"` or `«…»`. Comma
decimal. The modifier letter `ʻ` is often mis-encoded as `'`
(U+0027) or `’` (U+2019) in online text; normalising these to one
character improves LID recall.
