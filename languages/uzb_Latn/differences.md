# Uzbek (Latin) vs confusable languages

Closest confusables: Turkish (tur), Azerbaijani (azj), Crimean
Tatar (crh), Turkmen (tuk), Kazakh Latin (kaz_Latn).

## uzb ↔ tur (Turkish)

- **`oʻ` and `gʻ` with modifier letter `ʻ`** are Uzbek-only;
  Turkish has neither.
- Uzbek **lacks `ı ğ ş`**; Turkish has all three and uses them
  very frequently.
- Uzbek morphology has no vowel harmony: plural `-lar` is
  invariant, not `-lar/-ler`; genitive is always `-ning`, not
  `-in/-ın/-un/-ün`.
- Digraphs `sh`/`ch` where Turkish has `ş`/`ç`.

## uzb ↔ azj (Azerbaijani)

- Uzbek lacks `ə`, which Azerbaijani uses constantly.
- Uzbek `oʻ gʻ` are unique; Azerbaijani has no modifier-letter
  digraphs.
- Azerbaijani has harmonic `-lar/-lər`; Uzbek has invariant
  `-lar`.

## uzb ↔ crh (Crimean Tatar, Latin)

- Crimean Tatar uses `ñ ğ ı ş`; Uzbek has none of these.
- Uzbek's `oʻ gʻ` digraphs do not occur in Crimean Tatar.

## uzb ↔ tuk (Turkmen)

- Turkmen has `ä ý ň ž`; Uzbek has none.
- Turkmen doubles long vowels; Uzbek does not.
- Uzbek's `oʻ gʻ sh ch` digraphs are diagnostic against Turkmen.

## uzb ↔ kaz_Latn (Kazakh Latin)

- New Kazakh Latin uses acute diacritics (`á ǵ ń ó ú ý`); Uzbek
  does not.
- Uzbek's `oʻ gʻ` are unique.
