# Uzbek — macro, Latin (uzb)

## Summary

Uzbek (endonym: *oʻzbek tili*, *oʻzbekcha*) is a Turkic language
in the Karluk branch (Turkic → Common Turkic → Karluk). The ISO
639-3 macrolanguage `uzb` (639-1 `uz`, 639-2 `uzb`) has two
component codes: Northern Uzbek `uzn` (the dominant Latin/Cyrillic
standard of Uzbekistan) and Southern Uzbek `uzs` (Arabic-script,
spoken mainly in Afghanistan — **handled elsewhere in CommonLID
under an Arabic-script folder**). Total speakers are roughly
34–44 million, of whom about 27 million are in Uzbekistan, with
sizable minorities in Afghanistan, Tajikistan, Kyrgyzstan,
Kazakhstan, Turkmenistan, and Russia.

## Writing system

This folder covers the modern post-1993 Latin script used in
Uzbekistan. The current alphabet has 29 letters plus the modifier
letter `ʻ` (U+02BB). Distinctive features are the **digraphs `oʻ`
and `gʻ`** (with the modifier-letter apostrophe, **not** a regular
`'`) and the digraphs **`sh`** and **`ch`**. Reforms in 2019 and
2021 proposed replacing `oʻ gʻ sh ch` with `ō ḡ ş ç`, but the
digraph form remains dominant in practice. Uzbek Latin has **no**
`ı ğ ş ö ü ä`.

## History and status

Uzbek has cycled Arabic → Latin (1929) → Cyrillic (1940) → Latin
(1993, revised 1995). Cyrillic is still used in parallel by older
media, so parallel `uzb_Cyrl` material exists outside this folder.
