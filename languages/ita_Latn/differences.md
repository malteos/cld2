# Italian vs. confusable languages

Closest confusables in this set: **Ligurian (lij)** and **Venetian (vec)**, plus (at a greater distance) Spanish/Catalan. Italian and its regional relatives share lexicon but differ systematically in orthography.

## Italian vs. Ligurian (lij)

- Ligurian uses the digraph/letter `ç` (e.g. *çercâ, coscinç*), `æ` (e.g. *tempesta → tempestæ*), and circumflex on long vowels (`â ê î ô û`). Standard Italian has none of these.
- Ligurian drops many final vowels and intervocalic consonants (*scria, caroggio, öxellu*); Italian preserves them.
- Article forms `o, a, i, e` (Ligurian) vs. `il, lo, la, i, gli, le` (Italian).
- Ligurian `-n-` often corresponds to Italian geminate `-nn-`; Ligurian lacks systematic geminates.

## Italian vs. Venetian (vec)

- Venetian uses the unique letter `ł` (*el, ła*) and apostrophes/grave on `è ò`. Standard Italian never uses `ł`.
- Venetian truncates final vowels (*can, ogni, paròła*) and has `x` for `z/s` phonemes (*paexe*); Italian keeps final vowels and uses `s/z`.
- Venetian subject clitics (`el, ła, i, łe`) appear between subject and verb (*el gà, i xe*); Italian is pro-drop without such clitics.
- Auxiliary *gà/xe/ga* (Venetian) vs. *ha/è/aveva* (Italian).

## Italian vs. Spanish/Catalan

- Italian has no `ñ`, `ç`, `·l`, `ny`, or tilde; Spanish has `ñ ¿ ¡`, Catalan has `·l ny ç`.
- Italian word endings stay `-zione -mente -ità`; Spanish `-ción -mente -idad`; Catalan `-ció -ment -itat`.
- Italian doubles consonants productively; Iberian Romance rarely does.
