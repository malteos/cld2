# Italian (ita)

## Summary

Italian (endonym: *italiano*) is a Romance language (Indo-European → Italic → Romance → Italo-Dalmatian). ISO codes: 639-1 `it`, 639-2 `ita`, 639-3 `ita`. It is written in the Latin script and has roughly 65 million native speakers and up to 85 million total speakers. Italian is the official language of Italy, San Marino, Vatican City, and one of the four national languages of Switzerland; it is co-official in Istria (Slovenia, Croatia) and widely spoken by diaspora communities in Argentina, Brazil, Australia, Canada, and the US.

## Writing system

Italian uses a 21-letter core Latin alphabet; the letters `j k w x y` appear almost exclusively in loanwords and proper names. Diacritics are restricted to grave `à è ì ò ù` (on final-stressed polysyllables) and acute `é` (in *perché, affinché*); circumflex is archaic. There is no `ñ`, `ç`, `œ`, `ã`, or `ł`. Casing is bicameral; Italian uses Italian-style quotation marks `« »` (or `" "`) and a standard space before em-dashes.

## Status

Standardized from Tuscan (Florentine) since the 14th century (Dante, Petrarch, Boccaccio). The Accademia della Crusca monitors usage. Dozens of regional varieties (Neapolitan, Sicilian, Venetian, Ligurian, Piedmontese) coexist but are linguistically separate Romance languages rather than dialects of standard Italian.
