# Italian grammar

- **Word order**: SVO default but flexible thanks to rich verb morphology. Topicalisation and left-dislocation are frequent. Clitic pronouns precede finite verbs and attach to infinitives/imperatives (*dimmelo, parlargli*).
- **Morphology**: Fusional; heavy agreement. Highly synthetic verbal paradigm; nominal morphology shows gender and number only.
- **Nouns**: Two genders (M/F). Plurals change the final vowel: M `-o → -i`, F `-a → -e`, both genders `-e → -i`. Definite articles `il lo la l' i gli le`; indefinite `un uno una un'`.
- **Adjectives**: Agree in gender and number; most are postnominal, a closed class precedes (*buon, bel, gran, vecchio*). Forms like `bel/bello/bei/begli/belle` show determiner-like allomorphy.
- **Verbs**: Three conjugations (`-are, -ere, -ire`). TAM includes present, imperfect, preterite (*passato remoto*, still active in the south and in writing), future, conditional, present/past subjunctive, imperative, gerund, two participles. Compound tenses use auxiliaries `avere` and `essere`; participle agrees with subject (for `essere`) or preceding object clitic.
- **Pronouns**: Pro-drop: subject pronouns are omitted except for emphasis. Clitic clusters (*glielo, se ne, me lo*) are productive. Polite `Lei` (3sg feminine) vs. `tu/voi`.
- **Phonology**: Seven-vowel system with open/close `e o`; contrastive consonant gemination (*cadde* vs. *cade*); palatals `gl` `gn`; digraphs `sc(i/e)`, `ch`, `gh`. Stress is generally penultimate but lexically variable; marked on final syllable with grave accent.
- **Syntax notes**: Pro-drop. Negation with preverbal `non`. Polar questions by intonation. Copula `essere`; existential *c'è / ci sono*.
