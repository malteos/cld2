# Southern Uzbek grammar

- **Word order**: Strict SOV; modifiers precede the noun (Adj-N, Gen-N); postpositions only. Questions are marked by clause-final particle **ـمی / می**.
- **Morphology**: Agglutinative, like all Turkic languages. Words build long suffix chains: noun + plural + possessive + case, or verb + voice + negation + tense/aspect + person/number. Vowel harmony (mostly front/back) is reflected orthographically through the choice of **ی / ې**, **و / ۉ**, **ا / ه**.
- **Nouns**: No grammatical gender. Plural **ـلر** (*-lar*). Six cases marked by harmonising suffixes: nominative (∅), genitive **ـنینگ / ـنیڭ** (*-ning*), accusative **ـنی** (*-ni*), dative **ـگه / ـقه** (*-ga/-qa*), locative **ـده / ـته** (*-da/-ta*), ablative **ـدن / ـتن** (*-dan/-tan*).
- **Possession**: Possessive endings attach to the possessed noun: 1sg **ـیم**, 2sg **ـینگ**, 3sg **ـی / ـسی**, 1pl **ـیمیز**, etc.
- **Verbs**: Rich tense/aspect/mood system formed by stacking suffixes. Common markers: present-future **ـه / ـیه**, past definite **ـدی**, perfect **ـگن**, negation **ـمه**. Personal endings follow the tense suffix.
- **Phonology**: Back/front vowel harmony is partly eroded in some dialects but still visible orthographically. Consonants include /q/ **ق** and /χ/ **خ**, plus /ŋ/ written **نگ / ڭ**.
- **Syntax**: Pro-drop; no copula in the present (*men talaba* "I am a student"); overt copula **ـدیر** / **ایر-** in writing for emphasis. Subordination uses non-finite participial/converb forms rather than relative pronouns.
