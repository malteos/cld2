# Najdi Arabic LID characteristics

## Script signals

Arabic script U+0600–U+06FF; letter inventory identical to MSA. Najdi writers
often keep **ق** in spelling even though pronounced /g/, so orthographic cues
are lexical/morphological rather than character-level. Occasional use of
**گ** to represent /g/ in borrowings or place names.

## Top function words

وش / ويش (what), وين (where), متى (when), ليش (why), كيف / كيفك (how), مين /
منو (who), ذا / ذي / ذول (this m/f/pl — or هذا/هذي/هذول), هاللي / اللي
(relative that), جذي / كذا (thus/like this), هني / هناك (here/there), الحين /
هالحين (now), عاد (now/still), بعد (also, still, yet), بس (only/but), هه (part),
يبي / أبي / تبي / يبون (want), ما (neg), مو / موب (not), أبد (at all), شوي
(a little), وايد (a lot — diagnostic), حيل (very), بـ (future prefix), راح /
رح (will), عساك (may you), طيب (ok), زين (good), ترى (by the way / note
that), يلا (come on), أنت / انتي / انتو / انتن (2sg m/f, 2pl m/f).

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- Word **وش / ويش** (what) at clause start is highly diagnostic.
- Pronoun **منو / مين هو** and relative **اللي / هاللي** used very frequently.
- Sequence **يبي / أبي / تبي** for "want" is Gulf-typical and strongly Najdi
  when combined with other markers.
- Word final **ـون** for 3pl masc verb + negation **ما...ـوا** without the
  Egyptian/Maghrebi **ـش**: **ما سافروا**, not **ماسافروش**.
- Demonstratives **ذولا / هذولا / هذيله** (pl) and numbers with final -ة
  (ثنتين, ست, ثلاث) have Bedouin shape.

## Morphology hooks

Bedouin 2pl/3pl feminine forms (انتن, هن) still productive. Tanwīn-style
*-in* linker between noun and adjective (كتابن كبير), Bedouin hallmark.
Intrusive /n/ in subordinate constructions (يقولونه inn...).

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

Standard Arabic punctuation (،؛؟). Arabic-Indic digits ٠–٩ preferred in
formal contexts. Very heavy use of social-media elongation (ههههه) and
emoji; this is common to all Gulf dialects.
