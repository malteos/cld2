# Extremaduran LID characteristics

## Script signals

Diagnostic features: word-final `-h` (represents aspirated `-s`: *loh nenoh, muchah vezih*), `-l` for neutralised `-r/-l` (*comel, salil, papel*), and raised unstressed finals `-u -i` (*muchu, genti, hombri, brasilinu*). Acute `á é í ó ú`. No `ç`, `l·l`, `ny`, `ò`, `œ`, `ã`. `ñ` is used where Spanish uses it.

## Top function words

`el la lu los las un una unuh unah de del d' a al en con pa entre sobre y e o peru porqui qui quin cuandu cómu si no sí mah menuh muchu pocu tó tos cada otru mesmu esti esa aquéh aquélla yo tú él ella nusotrus vusotrus ellus mi ti si nu vu lu la lus lah li leh eh son era eran ha han habíal habían`.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- Final `-h` (aspirated `-s`): *loh, lah, muchah, doh, treh, díah*.
- `-l` for final `-r/-l`: *comel, querel, salil, papel, azul*.
- Unstressed `-u -i` finals: *muchu, hombri, perru, genti, nuevu, noisi*.
- Preserved Latin F- either as `h` (silent) or `j` (/x/): *jigu, jumbri, jazel, jechu*.
- Spanish-like digraphs `ch ll rr`.

## Morphology hooks

Infinitives `-al -el -il` (with `-r` → `-l`). Participles `-áu -íu` (from `-ado -ido`). Diminutive `-ín -ina` (*chiquinín*). Neuter `-u/-o` attached to adjectives/pronouns. 1pl `-emus/-imus` in some varieties.

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

No inverted `¿ ¡` in OSCEC-normative texts, though they appear in castilianised writing. Uses `«»` or `""`. Acute accent is used to mark irregular stress as in Spanish.
