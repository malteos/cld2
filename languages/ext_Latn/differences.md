# Extremaduran vs. confusable languages

Closest confusables in this set: **Spanish (spa)**, **Aragonese (arg)**, **Catalan (cat)**. Because Extremaduran orthography visibly encodes dialectal phonology, its diagnostics are the *surface spellings* rather than etymology.

## Extremaduran vs. Spanish

- Final `-h` for aspirated `-s` (*loh nenoh, treh díah*); Spanish keeps `-s` in writing.
- Final `-l` for merged `-r/-l` (*comel, hazel, salil*); Spanish keeps `-r`.
- Unstressed `-u -i` for Spanish `-o -e` (*muchu, genti, nuevu, hombri*); Spanish keeps `-o -e`.
- Initial `j-`/`h-` for Latin F- (*jigu, jumbri*); Spanish has silent `h-` or nothing.
- No inverted `¿ ¡` in OSCEC texts; Spanish requires them.
- Neuter `lo X` with adjectives; Spanish's `lo` is less productive.

## Extremaduran vs. Aragonese

- Aragonese articles `o/a/os/as`; Extremaduran `el/la/lu/los/las`.
- Aragonese clitics `en/ne bi/i`; Extremaduran none.
- Aragonese preserves F- as `f-` (*fer, fillo*); Extremaduran as `j-/h-` (*jazel, jijo*).
- Aragonese keeps `-r` in infinitives (*fer, dezir*); Extremaduran merges to `-l` (*hazel, dizil*).

## Extremaduran vs. Catalan

- Clear orthographic separation: Catalan `ny l·l ç è ò à` vs. Extremaduran Spanish-like diacritics plus `-h -l`.
- Catalan articles `el/la/els/les`; Extremaduran `el/la/lu/los/las`.
- Catalan periphrastic preterite `vaig + INF` is absent in Extremaduran.

## Extremaduran vs. Portuguese/Galician

- Portuguese has nasal vowels `ã õ` and `nh lh`; Extremaduran has none of these.
- Extremaduran aspiration (`loh`, `jigu`) is acoustic/orthographic; Portuguese spells etymologically.
