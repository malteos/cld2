# Extremaduran (ext)

## Summary

Extremaduran (endonym: *estremeñu*, *castúo*) is a Romance speech variety of the Ibero-Romance continuum (Indo-European → Italic → Romance → Ibero-Romance → Astur-Leonese, with heavy Castilian influence). ISO 639-3 `ext`. Written in the Latin script. Estimated 200,000 active speakers and up to 500,000 with passive exposure, concentrated in Extremadura (Cáceres and northern Badajoz, Spain) and in adjoining areas of Salamanca. UNESCO lists it as *definitely endangered*.

## Summary (ctd.) — genealogy

Extremaduran is a northern (highland) Astur-Leonese variety overlaid with Castilian features since the 13th-century Reconquista. It shares many traits with Leonese and Asturian (diphthongs, neuter gender, participle endings) but has diverged substantially under Spanish influence. It is clearly not a dialect of Spanish, even though modern spoken Extremaduran is strongly castilianised.

## Writing system

Uses the Latin alphabet. The standard used by *Órgánu de Seguimientu i Cordinación del Estremeñu i la sua Coltura (OSCEC)* includes: acute `á é í ó ú` (rarely `ñ`), final `-h` to mark aspirated `-s` (*loh nenoh*), `-l` for neutralised `-r/-l` (*comel, salil* for *comer, salir*), and raised unstressed finals `-u -i` (*muchu, genti*). No `ç`, `l·l`, `ny`, or `ò`.

## Status

Recognised as a cultural heritage language of Extremadura but without co-official status. A small modern literature exists, and Extremaduran has a Wikipedia edition.
