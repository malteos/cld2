# Assamese (asm_Beng) - Grammar Reference

## Typological Overview

Assamese is an **SOV (Subject-Object-Verb)** language belonging to the Eastern Indo-Aryan branch. It exhibits a mix of **agglutinative and fusional** morphological features, with postpositions rather than prepositions, and modifiers generally preceding the head noun. It is notably the easternmost member of the Indo-European language family.

## Phonology

### Vowel System

Assamese has a rich vowel system with 8 oral vowels and corresponding nasalized vowels:

| Vowel | IPA | Example |
|-------|-----|---------|
| অ | /ɔ/ | কম (kom, "less") |
| আ | /a/ | আম (am, "mango") |
| ই | /i/ | ইমান (iman, "so much") |
| উ | /u/ | উৰা (ura, "to fly") |
| এ | /e/ | এক (ek, "one") |
| ঐ | /oi/ | বৈ (boi, "book") |
| ও | /o/ | ওচৰ (osor, "near") |
| ঔ | /ou/ | ঔষধ (ousodh, "medicine") |

Nasalization is phonemic and marked with চন্দ্ৰবিন্দু (ঁ): আঁহত (ãhot, "fig tree").

### Consonant System

A distinctive feature of Assamese phonology within the Indo-Aryan family is the **voiceless velar fricative /x/**, written as খ in certain positions. This sound corresponds to /kʰ/ (aspirated k) in Bengali and most other Indo-Aryan languages. This is one of the most important phonological markers for distinguishing Assamese from Bengali.

Key consonantal features:

- **Voiceless velar fricative /x/:** Assamese খাই (xai, "eating") vs. Bengali খাই (khai)
- **Voiceless glottal fricative /h/:** In positions where other Indo-Aryan languages have /s/ or /ʃ/: Assamese হাত (hat, "hand") where the initial is truly /h/
- **Reduced retroflex series:** In many dialects, the distinction between dental and retroflex stops is neutralized, particularly in intervocalic positions. Standard Assamese maintains the distinction orthographically but not always phonetically.
- **Aspirated consonants:** The aspirated/unaspirated distinction is maintained: ক /k/ vs. খ /x/ (or /kʰ/), গ /ɡ/ vs. ঘ /ɡʱ/, etc.
- **Alveolar fricatives:** Assamese uses /s/ and /z/ where Bengali uses /ʃ/ and other sibilants. The three-way sibilant distinction (শ, ষ, স) of Sanskrit has merged to /x/ (শ/ষ) and /s/ (স) in Assamese.

### Tone and Stress

Assamese is not a tonal language, but it does have phonemic vowel length distinctions in some analyses. Stress is generally on the initial syllable in native words.

## Noun System

### Definiteness and Classifiers

One of the most distinctive features of Assamese grammar is its extensive **classifier system**, which is unusual among Indo-Aryan languages and likely influenced by neighboring Tibeto-Burman languages. Classifiers (বিভক্তি/পদাশ্ৰয়ী শব্দ) are suffixed to nouns or numerals:

| Classifier | Use | Example |
|-----------|-----|---------|
| -জন (-jon) | Humans (respectful) | মানুহজন (the person) |
| -গৰাকী (-goraki) | Humans (very respectful) | মহিলাগৰাকী (the woman) |
| -টো (-to) | General inanimate, animals | কিতাপখন (the book) |
| -খন (-khon) | Flat objects, books, lands | মাটিখন (the land) |
| -ডাল (-dal) | Long thin objects | লাঠিডাল (the stick) |
| -টা (-ta) | Informal/round objects | লৰাটো (the boy) |
| -কণ (-kon) | Small objects | ছোৱালীকণ (the little girl) |
| -পাত (-pat) | Very flat objects, paper | কাগজখিলা (the sheet of paper) |

These classifiers also serve as **definiteness markers**. Unlike English articles, Assamese uses these suffixed classifiers to indicate definiteness. The bare noun without a classifier is generally indefinite.

### Case System

Modern Assamese does not use the traditional Sanskrit case inflections. Instead, it uses **postpositions** (বিভক্তি) that attach to the noun:

| Case Function | Marker | Example |
|--------------|--------|---------|
| Nominative | -এ/-ই (-e/-i) for agent | ৰামে কৰিলে (Ram did it) |
| Accusative | -ক (-k) | ৰামক দিলে (gave to Ram) |
| Instrumental | -ৰে (-re) / -এৰে (-ere) | হাতেৰে (by hand) |
| Dative | -লৈ (-loi) | ঘৰলৈ (towards home) |
| Ablative | -ৰ পৰা (-r pora) | ঘৰৰ পৰা (from home) |
| Genitive | -ৰ (-r) | ৰামৰ (Ram's) |
| Locative | -ত (-t) | ঘৰত (at home) |

### Gender

Modern Assamese has effectively **lost grammatical gender**, which is a significant typological difference from Hindi, Bengali, and most other Indo-Aryan languages. Natural gender is expressed through separate lexical items (মতা/মাতা for male, মাইকী for female) or suffixes for some animate nouns, but this does not trigger agreement on verbs or adjectives.

### Number

Plurality is marked with suffixes:
- -বোৰ (-bor): for people and general plural: মানুহবোৰ (the people)
- -বিলাক (-bilak): for objects and less respectful: কিতাপবিলাক (the books)
- -সকল (-xokol): for people (respectful): ছাত্ৰসকল (the students)

## Verb System

### Verb Structure

The Assamese verb is highly inflected and carries information about **tense, aspect, mood, person, number**, and **honorific level**. The general structure is:

**Root + (Aspect/Causative) + Tense + Person/Number/Honorific**

### Tense-Aspect System

Assamese distinguishes several tense-aspect combinations:

| Tense-Aspect | Example (কৰ- "to do") | Translation |
|-------------|----------------------|-------------|
| Simple Present | কৰোঁ / কৰে | I do / he does |
| Present Continuous | কৰি আছোঁ / কৰি আছে | I am doing / he is doing |
| Simple Past | কৰিলোঁ / কৰিলে | I did / he did |
| Past Habitual | কৰিছিলোঁ / কৰিছিল | I used to do |
| Present Perfect | কৰিছোঁ / কৰিছে | I have done / he has done |
| Past Perfect | কৰিছিলোঁ / কৰিছিল | I had done |
| Future | কৰিম / কৰিব | I will do / he will do |

### Honorific Distinctions

A crucial feature of Assamese verb conjugation is the **three-level honorific system** for second and third person:

| Level | 2nd Person | 3rd Person | Usage |
|-------|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Intimate/Inferior (তই toi) | কৰ | কৰে | Very close friends, children, inferiors |
| Familiar (তুমি tumi) | কৰা | কৰে | Friends, equals, younger people |
| Honorific (আপুনি apuni) | কৰে | কৰে | Elders, respected persons, formal |

The third person honorific often uses the pronoun তেওঁ (teõ) with distinct verb endings.

### Negative Conjugation

Assamese has a distinctive **negative conjugation** system where negation is expressed through modified verb forms rather than a simple negative particle:

- Positive: মই যাওঁ (moi jaõ, "I go")
- Negative: মই নাযাওঁ (moi najaoõ, "I don't go")

The negative prefix ন- (n-) attaches directly to the verb stem. In past tense, the negative form uses a different construction: নকৰিলে (nokorile, "did not do").

### Non-finite Forms

- **Infinitive:** কৰিবলৈ (koriboloi, "to do")
- **Gerund/Verbal Noun:** কৰা (kora, "doing")
- **Conditional Participle:** কৰিলে (korile, "if/when done")
- **Conjunctive Participle:** কৰি (kori, "having done")

## Syntax

### Basic Word Order

The canonical word order is **SOV** (Subject-Object-Verb):

মই ভাত খাওঁ (Moi bhat khaoõ) - "I rice eat" = "I eat rice"

However, word order is relatively flexible due to case marking, and pragmatic factors (topic, focus) can alter the order.

### Relative Clauses

Assamese uses **correlative-relative** clause structures, where the relative clause (with যি- yi- words) typically precedes the main clause (with সি-/তা- xi-/ta- words):

যিজন মানুহ আহিল, সেইজন মোৰ বন্ধু। (The person who came is my friend.)

### Subordination

Subordinate clauses are typically formed with:
- **যে** (je, "that"): complement clauses
- **যদি** (jodi, "if"): conditional clauses
- **কাৰণে / বাবে** (karone / babe, "because"): causal clauses
- **যেতিয়া** (jetia, "when"): temporal clauses

### Question Formation

Yes-no questions are formed by adding the particle **নে** (ne) or **নেকি** (neki) at the end of a statement, or by intonation alone:

তুমি আহিবানে? (Tumi ahibane?) - "Will you come?"

Wh-questions use interrogative words (কি ki "what", কোন kon "who", ক'ত kot "where", কেতিয়া ketia "when", কিয় kio "why") which typically appear in situ (in the position of the element being questioned).

### Postpositions

Assamese uses postpositions rather than prepositions:
- ঘৰৰ ভিতৰত (ghoror bhitorot, "inside the house")
- নদীৰ কাষত (nodir kakhot, "beside the river")
- মোৰ লগত (mor logot, "with me")

### Copular Constructions

Assamese uses different copular forms depending on context:
- **হ-** (h-): equative/identificational: তেওঁ শিক্ষক হয় (He is a teacher)
- **আছ-** (as-): existential/locative: মই ঘৰত আছোঁ (I am at home)
- **থাক-** (thak-): habitual location: তেওঁ দিল্লীত থাকে (He lives in Delhi)
