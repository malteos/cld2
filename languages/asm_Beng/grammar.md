# Assamese grammar

- **Word order**: SOV; postpositional; modifiers before head. Constituent order is fairly rigid compared with Hindi.
- **Morphology**: Moderately fusional. Like Bengali, Assamese has no grammatical gender. Nouns inflect for number (plural suffix **-বোৰ** for inanimates, **-সকল / -হঁত** for animates) and for case via enclitics **-ৰ** (genitive), **-ক** (accusative/dative), **-ত** (locative), **-এ / -এৰে** (instrumental/ergative-like).
- **Classifiers**: Rich classifier system — **জন** (human), **গৰাকী** (polite human), **টা** (general), **খন** (flat things), **ডাল** (long thin), **জোপা** (tree-like). These are bound to the numeral (e.g. *duta kitap* "two books").
- **Pronouns**: মই (I), আমি (we), তই (you-intimate), তুমি (you-familiar), তহঁত / তোমালোক (you-plural), আপুনি (you-polite), সি / তাই (he / she neutral), তেওঁ (he/she honorific), তেওঁলোক (they honorific).
- **Verbs**: Three honorific levels of 2nd person and two of 3rd; tense/aspect marked by suffix plus subject-agreement ending. Diagnostic endings include 1sg present **-ওঁ** (নাসিলোঁ "I did not go"), progressive **-ই আছোঁ / -ই আছে**, future **-ম / -বা / -ব / -বে**, perfect **-ইছে / -ইছোঁ**. Copula: **আছে** (is/exist), **নাই** (is not / does not exist), **হয়** (becomes/equational).
- **Phonology**: Sibilants collapsed to a single velar fricative /x/ (ô-xômiyā), which is typologically unusual among Indo-Aryan. Alveolar stops /t, d, tʰ, dʰ/ instead of Bengali-style dental-retroflex pair, and rhotic /ɹ/ rather than flap /ɾ/.
- **Syntax**: Pro-drop; honorific-marked verb agreement makes the subject recoverable. Negation precedes the verb (**নাই**, **নহয়**).
