# Assamese / Asamiya (asm)

## Summary

Assamese (endonym: **অসমীয়া** *ôxômiyā*) is an Indo-European → Indo-Aryan → Eastern-Zone language, the easternmost member of the Indo-Aryan family. ISO codes: `as` (639-1), `asm` (639-2 / 639-3). It is the official language of the Indian state of Assam and is also spoken in Arunachal Pradesh and parts of Nagaland, Meghalaya and Bangladesh. L1 speakers are estimated at 15 million, with several million additional L2 users in the Brahmaputra valley. It serves as the primary lingua franca of north-eastern India.

## Writing system

Assamese is written in the Eastern Nāgarī script (Unicode block U+0980–U+09FF), which it shares with Bengali. The two orthographies overlap almost entirely, but Assamese has **two extra letters**: **ৰ** (U+09F0, Assamese *ra* with hook) and **ৱ** (U+09F1, *wavy ba*, representing /w/). Where Bengali writes **র** and **ব**, Assamese writes **ৰ** and **ৱ**; this substitution is the single strongest LID diagnostic between the two. Assamese also shows distinctive pronunciation of sibilants (**স, শ, ষ** all → /x/) and a partially unique conjunct inventory. Digits and numerals are the Bangla set ০ ১ ২ ৩ ৪ ৫ ৬ ৭ ৮ ৯.

## History and status

Classical Assamese has a documented history from the 13th-century *Charyāpada* tradition and the Vaishnava movement of Sankardeva (16th c.). The Assamese printing press was pioneered at Serampore in 1813 and later at Sibsagar by American Baptist missionaries, who standardised the modern written form.
