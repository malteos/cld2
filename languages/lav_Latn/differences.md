# Latvian (macro) vs. confusable languages

As a macrolanguage, `lav` groups Standard Latvian (`lvs`) and Latgalian
(`ltg`). The closest confusables are its own sub-varieties and Lithuanian
(not in the CommonLID set).

## Latvian (lav) vs. Latgalian (ltg)

- Latgalian commonly uses letter **y** (for /ɨ/) — absent in Standard
  Latvian.
- Latgalian retains the consonant cluster **ct** where standard Latvian has
  **kt** / **t** (ltg `ačtyns` ~ lvs `aktis`).
- Latgalian preserves palatalised consonants more broadly (ltg `dz`, `dž`
  contrasts) and has distinct case endings (e.g. dat.pl. `-im` vs lvs
  `-iem`).
- Function words differ: ltg `tei, tys, jei, nav`; lvs `tā, tas, ja, nav`.
- Latgalian uses additional apostrophe/diacritic conventions (e.g. softening
  mark).

## Latvian vs. Standard Latvian (lvs)

The `lav` macro label typically corresponds closely with `lvs` since
Latgalian is classified separately; use `lvs` when the text is demonstrably
the standard written norm and reserve `lav` when variety cannot be decided.

## Latvian vs. Lithuanian (not in CommonLID)

- Lithuanian has **ą, ę, į, ų, ė** (Polish-style ogoneks and one dotted e);
  Latvian has **ā, ē, ī, ū** (macrons).
- Lithuanian retains more archaic Indo-European morphology (dual number
  relics, more complex case system).
- Function words: lit `ir, kas, bet, labai`; lav `un, kas, bet, ļoti`.

## Latvian vs. other CommonLID Baltic-region languages

- No other Baltic-family language appears in the CommonLID set, so the
  most common distraction is Lithuanian or Estonian (Uralic). Estonian
  lacks macrons and cedilla consonants; it uses **õ, ä, ö, ü** instead.
