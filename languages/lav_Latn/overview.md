# Latvian macrolanguage (lav)

## Summary

Latvian (endonym: *latviešu valoda*) is a Baltic language of the
Indo-European family (Indo-European → Balto-Slavic → Baltic → Eastern
Baltic). ISO codes: 639-1 `lv`, 639-2 `lav`, 639-3 `lav` (macrolanguage
covering **lvs** Standard Latvian and **ltg** Latgalian). Combined native
speakers total approximately 1.7–1.8 million, overwhelmingly in Latvia where
Latvian is the sole official state language; diaspora in the United States,
Germany, the United Kingdom, Ireland, Russia, Australia, Canada and Brazil.
Together with Lithuanian it forms the surviving Baltic branch, with
Latgalian historically regarded as a dialect or closely related language.

## Writing system

Latvian uses a 33-letter Latin alphabet: a ā b c č d e ē f g ģ h i ī j k ķ l
ļ m n ņ o p r s š t u ū v z ž. The **macron** `¯` marks long vowels **ā ē ī
ū**; the **caron/háček** marks the post-alveolars **č š ž**; the **cedilla**
(comma below, or "mīkstinājums") on **ģ ķ ļ ņ** marks palatalisation. The
letter **o** conventionally represents /uɔ/ in native words but /o(ː)/ in
loans (no distinct spelling). The letters **q, w, x, y** do not occur
natively. Casing is bicameral. Since Latvian is a macrolanguage, this folder
describes both the standard variety and the Latgalian subvariety jointly.

## Status

Latvian literary tradition begins with 16th-century religious translations.
Modern orthography (with macrons and cedilla diacritics) was codified around
1908. Since 1991 Latvian has been the sole official language of Latvia, and
Latgalian was granted official recognition as a historical variant of
Latvian in 2000.
