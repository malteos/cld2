# Latvian grammar

- **Word order**: SVO default; word order is relatively free owing to case
  marking, with information structure driving surface order. Fusional and
  synthetic, though less morphologically conservative than Lithuanian.
- **Morphology**: Fusional with extensive suffixation. Productive derivation
  by `-ība/-ums` (abstract), `-nieks/-niece` (agents), `-īgs` (adjectives),
  `-iba-` and `-āt/-ēt/-īt/-ot` verb classes.
- **Nouns**: Two genders (masculine, feminine). Seven cases: nominative,
  genitive, dative, accusative, instrumental (syncretic with accusative
  singular / dative plural in many paradigms), locative, vocative. Six
  declension classes (four masculine, two feminine, plus i-stems and
  consonant-stems). Typical masc.sg.nom endings `-s, -š, -is, -us`;
  fem.sg.nom `-a, -e`. No articles.
- **Verbs**: Six conjugations. Three tenses (present, past, future), three
  moods (indicative, imperative, conditional), plus a distinctive
  **oblique/relative** (evidential) mood formed with the `-ot` participle
  (`esot, runājot`). Debitive mood with `jā-` + 3rd-person stem
  (`man jāstrādā` "I must work"). Person endings: 1sg `-u`, 2sg `-i`, 3
  `-Ø/-a`, 1pl `-am/-ām`, 2pl `-at/-āt`, 3 `-Ø/-a`. Third person is shared
  across singular and plural.
- **Phonology**: Vowel length is phonemic (á vs ā, e vs ē, i vs ī, u vs ū).
  Diphthongs ai, ei, ie, au, ui, oi, uo (spelled `o` in native words).
  Three **pitch accents** (level, falling, broken) on long syllables —
  preserved in dialects, weakly realised in standard speech. Stress is
  fixed on the first syllable.
- **Syntax**: Pro-drop in the third person; first and second person subjects
  usually expressed. Copula `būt` present (`esmu, esi, ir, esam, esat, ir`)
  but often elidable in the 3rd person. Negation with prefix `ne-` on the
  verb (`nezinu, neesmu, nav` from `ne + ir`).
