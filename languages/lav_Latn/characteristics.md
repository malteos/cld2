# Latvian LID characteristics

## Script signals

Latin alphabet with a distinctive diacritic system. Highly diagnostic
letters, in order of strength:
- **Macron vowels** **ā ē ī ū** — vowel length marking is shared with
  Lithuanian (which however uses **ą ę į ų ė**) but not with most European
  languages. The combination of macron vowels **and** háček consonants is
  nearly unique to Latvian.
- **Cedilla/comma** consonants **ģ ķ ļ ņ** — a Latvian fingerprint. Note
  **ģ** with a comma above is characteristic.
- **Háček** consonants **č š ž** shared with Czech/Slovak/Slovene but paired
  with macrons is Latvian.
- Letters **q, w, x, y** do not occur (y in particular is a Latgalian
  signal — its presence suggests ltg rather than lvs).

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- **Noun nominative endings**: m.sg. `-s, -š, -is, -us`; f.sg. `-a, -e`;
  m.pl. `-i`; f.pl. `-as, -es`. Every masculine singular noun ending in
  `-s` is a Latvian flag (`vīrs, koks, kalns`).
- **Adjective agreement** with additional definite/indefinite distinction:
  `labs/labais, laba/labā`.
- **Verb 3rd-person** ends in bare stem: `iet, ir, ēd, brauc`.
- Infinitive in **-t**: `runāt, iet, lasīt, būt`.
- Common suffixes: **-ība, -ums, -šana, -nieks, -īgs, -ošs**.
- Diphthongs **ai, ei, ie, au, uo** (written `o`), **ui**.

## Morphology hooks

- Debitive `jā-` + 3sg: `jāstrādā, jālasa, jāiet`.
- Oblique/evidential with participle `-ot`: `esot, runājot, lasot`.
- Reflexive verbs in `-ties`: `mazgāties, atrasties, smieties`.

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

- Quotation marks: outer `„…"` (German-style, low-high).
- Dialogue marked with em-dash.
- Capitalisation: months, weekdays, nationalities lowercase.
