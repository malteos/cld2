# Korean (kor)

## Summary

Korean, endonym 한국어 (Hangugeo) in South Korea and 조선말 (Chosŏnmal) in North Korea, is the sole widely spoken member of the Koreanic family. Some linguists propose a distant relation to Japonic; most treat it as a language isolate or its own small family. ISO codes: 639-1 `ko`, 639-2 `kor`, 639-3 `kor`. Native speakers number roughly 80 million — South Korea (≈52M), North Korea (≈26M), plus diaspora (China's Yanbian, Japan, North America, Central Asia).

## Writing system

Korean is written in **Hangul** (한글), a featural alphabet invented under King Sejong in 1443 and promulgated in 1446. Letters (jamo) are grouped into **syllable blocks** that occupy the Unicode range U+AC00–U+D7A3 (pre-composed syllables) with component jamo in U+1100–U+11FF and U+3130–U+318F. Each syllable block encodes an onset consonant + medial vowel + optional coda. Modern Korean text is almost entirely Hangul; Han characters (漢字 / 한자) are occasionally used for disambiguation, names, or scholarly writing, typically parenthesised. Words are **separated by spaces** (unlike Chinese and Japanese). Punctuation mostly follows Western conventions: `.` `,` `?` `!` `"` `'`, though full-width variants appear.

## Status

Official language of both the Republic of Korea and the Democratic People's Republic of Korea; a co-official language in the Yanbian Korean Autonomous Prefecture of China. South and North Korean orthographies diverge in some spellings, vocabulary, and spacing conventions, but both use Hangul.
