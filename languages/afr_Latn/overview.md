# Afrikaans (afr)

## Summary

Afrikaans (endonym: *Afrikaans*) is an Indo-European language of the West Germanic branch (Indo-European → Germanic → West → Low Franconian → Dutch → Afrikaans). ISO codes: 639-1 `af`, 639-2 `afr`, 639-3 `afr`. It is written in the Latin script. Afrikaans has approximately 7.2 million native speakers and 10–15 million additional L2 speakers, mostly in South Africa and Namibia. It is an official language of South Africa and a recognised regional language of Namibia. It developed during the 17th–19th centuries from the Dutch spoken by settlers at the Cape, with significant substrate and contact input from Khoekhoe, Malay, Portuguese creoles, and later English.

## Writing system

Afrikaans uses the 26-letter Latin alphabet with occasional diacritics: circumflex `â, ê, î, ô, û` (often on loans or to disambiguate), acute `é, á, ó, ú` for stress/disambiguation, diaeresis `ë, ï` across morpheme boundaries (*geëet, reën*), and grave `è`. Crucially, it does *not* use `ß`, umlaut `ä/ö/ü`, or the Dutch digraph `ij`; where Dutch writes *ij*, Afrikaans writes `y` (*my, jy, hy, sy, byvoorbeeld*). Only proper nouns and sentence starts are capitalised. The indefinite article *'n* is always lowercase and written with a leading apostrophe even at sentence start (the following word then carries the capital).

## History

Afrikaans was first codified in the late 19th century, gained official status in 1925, and diverged grammatically from Dutch by losing verb conjugation, grammatical case, and most strong-verb ablaut.
