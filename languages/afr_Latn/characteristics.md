# Afrikaans characteristics (LID signals)

## Script signals

Afrikaans uses Latin with occasional circumflex vowels `â, ê, î, ô, û` (*wêreld, môre, sê*), a diaeresis on *ë* and *ï* to mark syllable boundaries (*geëet, reën, beïnvloed*), and the sentence-initial indefinite article *'n* written as an apostrophe plus *n*. It has **no** `ß`, **no** umlaut `ä/ö/ü`, and **no** digraph `ij`. Heavy use of `y` as a vowel (*my, jy, hy, sy, byvoorbeeld, wyd*) is a strong positive cue relative to Dutch (which uses `ij` in these slots).

## Top function words

*die, en, van, in, is, nie, het, te, op, om, met, vir, 'n, dat, word, was, sal, kan, moet, wil, hy, sy, ons, hulle, ek, jy, julle, u, word, ook, maar, as, deur, tot, na, uit, by, oor, soos, wat, wie, hier, daar, nog, al, baie, min, geen, iets, niks*.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- Double negation *nie … nie* at clause end is a near-unique signature.
- Diminutive suffixes `-tjie`, `-jie`, `-kie`, `-pie`, `-ie` (*boompie, bietjie*).
- Past-participle prefix `ge-` with no inflected ending (*gewerk, gehad, geloop, gevoel*).
- `y` in function words (*my, jy, hy, sy*) where Dutch writes `ij`/`mijn`.
- `oe` for /u/ (*boek, goed, doen*), `ui` for /œy/ (*huis, buite*), `aa/ee/oo` for long vowels (*maan, see, boom*), `eu` rare.
- Suffixes `-heid` (*vryheid*), `-ing` (*verwerking*), `-lik` (*moontlik*, vs Dutch `-lijk`), `-se` (possessive/adjectival).

## Morphology hooks

Uninflected verb forms are diagnostic: *ek werk, jy werk, hy werk, ons werk, julle werk, hulle werk*. The copula *is* is invariable for all persons and numbers; past is always *was*. Auxiliaries *het, sal, kan, moet, wil, word, is* govern bare infinitives or past participles.

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

Sentence-initial *'n* keeps its apostrophe and lowercase; the following capitalised word is a strong Afrikaans signature (e.g. *'n Huis staan daar*). Quotation marks typically `"..."` or `'...'`; decimal comma and period thousands separator, like Dutch.
