# Afrikaans vs confusable languages

Afrikaans is a daughter of Dutch and by far the closest CommonLID language to it. A secondary confuser is English because of the very large number of English loanwords in colloquial Afrikaans and the shared Latin script without diacritics in many sentences.

## afr ↔ nld (closest pair)

- Double negation *nie … nie* is obligatory in Afrikaans and absent in Dutch.
- Afrikaans verbs are uninflected (*ek is, jy is, hy is, ons is, julle is, hulle is*); Dutch keeps full paradigms (*ik ben, jij bent, hij is, wij zijn*).
- Past tense: Afrikaans almost always uses *het* + *ge-* participle; Dutch alternates synthetic preterite (*werkte, liep*) with *hebben/zijn* perfect.
- Plurals: Afrikaans *honde, huise, boeke* vs Dutch *honden, huizen, boeken* (Dutch keeps the `-n`).
- Spelling: Afrikaans *my, jy, hy, sy* vs Dutch *mijn, jij, hij, zij*; Afrikaans *moontlik* vs Dutch *mogelijk*.

## afr ↔ eng (English loanwords can fool)

- Afrikaans colloquial text may contain many English words (*is reg, oukei, troubleshoot*). Disambiguate on function words: presence of *die, 'n, nie, het, sal, kan, moet, wil, hy, sy, ons* or double *nie … nie* is decisive for Afrikaans.
- Afrikaans has `y` in pronouns and *ge-*-prefixed participles; English has neither.
- Circumflex diacritics on vowels indicate Afrikaans, not English.

## afr ↔ fry (Western Frisian)

- Both use circumflex, but Frisian adds `â, ê, û` heavily and uses diphthongs `ea/ie/oa` (*read, brief, boat*) not found in Afrikaans. Frisian 2pl pronoun *jimme* vs Afrikaans *julle*.

## afr ↔ deu (German)

- No `ß`, no umlaut `ä/ö/ü`, no noun capitalisation in Afrikaans. Analytic grammar vs German fusional case system.
