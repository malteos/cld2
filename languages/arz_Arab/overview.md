# Egyptian Arabic (arz)

## Summary

Egyptian Arabic (endonym: اللغة المصرية *al-luġa l-maṣriyya* or more colloquially
المصري / العامية المصرية) is the most widely spoken and understood Arabic
variety, thanks to Egypt's cultural dominance in cinema, television, and
music. Family: Afro-Asiatic → Semitic → Central Semitic → Arabic → North
Egyptian Arabic. ISO codes: **639-3 `arz`** (no 639-1 code; falls under the
`ara` macrolanguage). Roughly **70–100 million** native speakers, with
hundreds of millions of passive comprehenders across the Arab world.
Principal region: Egypt (especially Cairo, Delta, Alexandria).

## Writing system

Written in the **Arabic script** (U+0600–U+06FF), typically using
MSA-compatible orthography. Egyptians also write heavily in Arabic via social
media and film subtitles. Informal "Arabizi" transliteration using Latin
letters and digits (2, 3, 7) is common online but is not the `arz_Arab`
CommonLID target. No casing. Short vowels unmarked. Occasional spelling of
/g/ as **ج** (since Egyptian ج = /g/, not /dʒ/ as in MSA) and /q/ as **ء**
(glottal stop).

## Status

De facto lingua franca of the Arab film/TV industry; widely understood across
the Arab League due to the prominence of Egyptian media. Used online, in
personal communication, advertising, and some literature, but diglossia with
MSA remains: formal writing defaults to MSA.
