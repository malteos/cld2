# Indonesian grammar

- **Word order**: Basic SVO, strongly analytic. Modifiers follow the noun
  head (`rumah besar` "house big" = "big house"). Topic-comment structures
  are frequent in speech and media.
- **Morphology**: Mildly agglutinative affixation on a largely isolating
  base. Productive prefixes `meN-`, `di-`, `ber-`, `peN-`, `ter-`, `se-`
  combine with suffixes `-kan`, `-i`, `-an` and circumfixes `ke-...-an`,
  `peN-...-an`. Reduplication (full, partial, rhyming) marks plurality,
  variety, intensity, or reciprocity: `buku-buku`, `sayur-mayur`,
  `tolong-menolong`.
- **Nouns**: No grammatical gender, no articles, no obligatory number.
  Plural expressed by reduplication, quantifiers (`banyak`, `beberapa`) or
  classifiers (`seorang`, `seekor`, `sebuah`). No case marking; grammatical
  role is configurational or marked by prepositions (`di` at, `ke` to,
  `dari` from, `pada` on).
- **Verbs**: No tense or person agreement. Aspect/mood are periphrastic
  particles: `sudah` (perfective), `sedang` (progressive), `akan` (future),
  `belum` (not yet), `pernah` (ever). Voice is central: active `meN-` versus
  undergoer-focus `di-` ("passive"). The `ter-` prefix marks accidental or
  resultative state.
- **Phonology**: Six vowels `a e i o u ə` (schwa written `e`), simple
  (C)(C)V(C) syllables, predictable penultimate stress. Few native
  clusters; loan clusters are retained (`struktur`, `kritik`).
- **Syntax notes**: Pro-drop for subject and object; zero copula in the
  present (`Dia guru` "He is a teacher"); the particle `yang` is a
  relativiser/focus marker; negation by `tidak`/`tak` for verbs/adjectives
  and `bukan` for nouns.
