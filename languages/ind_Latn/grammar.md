# Indonesian Grammar

## Word Order

Indonesian follows a basic **SVO (Subject-Verb-Object)** word order, though this can be altered for emphasis or in passive constructions. Modifiers follow the head noun (head-initial), so adjectives come after nouns: "rumah besar" (house big = big house), "buku merah" (book red = red book).

## Morphology: Affixation

Indonesian is an **agglutinative** language with a rich system of affixation. Affixes are the primary mechanism for deriving new words and marking voice, causation, and nominalization. The major affixes include:

### Prefixes

- **me(N)-**: Active voice marker for transitive verbs. The nasal (N) assimilates to the following consonant: menulis (to write, from tulis), membaca (to read, from baca), mengambil (to take, from ambil), menyapu (to sweep, from sapu). The base-initial consonant may be nasalized or dropped.
- **ber-**: Intransitive/stative verb prefix indicating a state or habitual action: berbicara (to speak), berjalan (to walk), bermain (to play), bernyanyi (to sing).
- **di-**: Passive voice prefix: ditulis (was written), dibaca (was read), dimakan (was eaten). The agent may follow with "oleh" (by).
- **ter-**: Accidental or superlative prefix: terjatuh (fell accidentally), terbesar (biggest), tertulis (written/happened to be written).
- **pe(N)-**: Agentive/instrumental nominalizer: penulis (writer), pembaca (reader), penyanyi (singer), pemain (player).
- **per-**: Forms nouns related to an action or state, often combined with suffixes.
- **se-**: Means "one" or "the same": sehari (one day), seluruh (entire), seorang (one person).
- **ke-**: Ordinal numbers and some abstractions (when combined with -an): ketiga (third), ketua (chairman).

### Suffixes

- **-kan**: Causative or benefactive suffix, indicating that the action is done for someone or causes something: memberikan (to give), menuliskan (to write for someone), menjalankan (to run/operate something).
- **-i**: Locative or repetitive suffix, indicating action directed at a location or repeated action: mendatangi (to come to/visit), mengetahui (to know about), menandatangani (to sign).
- **-an**: Nominalizer forming result nouns or collectives: tulisan (writing/a written work), makanan (food), bacaan (reading material), minuman (drink/beverage).

### Circumfixes (Confixes)

- **ke-...-an**: Forms abstract nouns or states: keindahan (beauty), kesehatan (health), kemampuan (ability), kebahagiaan (happiness), kementerian (ministry).
- **pe(N)-...-an**: Forms process or place nouns: pendidikan (education), pemerintahan (government/governance), pembangunan (development), penerbangan (aviation/flight).
- **per-...-an**: Forms nouns related to systems or domains: pertanian (agriculture), perdagangan (trade), perjanjian (agreement), perjalanan (journey).

## Tense and Aspect

Indonesian has **no grammatical tense inflection**. Temporal information is conveyed through context or time adverbs:

- **sudah / telah**: completed action (sudah makan = already ate)
- **sedang / lagi**: ongoing action (sedang makan = is eating)
- **akan**: future action (akan makan = will eat)
- **belum**: not yet (belum makan = has not yet eaten)
- **pernah**: ever/have experienced (pernah makan = have eaten before)

## Nouns and Plurals

Indonesian nouns have **no grammatical gender** and **no obligatory plural marking**. Plurality is expressed through:

- **Reduplication**: rumah-rumah (houses), anak-anak (children), buku-buku (books)
- **Quantifiers**: banyak buku (many books), beberapa orang (several people)
- **Context**: when plurality is clear from context, the base form suffices

## Classifier System

Indonesian uses **classifiers** (kata penggolong) with numerals, though less strictly than languages like Chinese or Thai:

- **orang**: for people (tiga orang guru = three teachers)
- **buah**: for large objects, abstract things (dua buah rumah = two houses)
- **ekor**: for animals (lima ekor kucing = five cats)
- **batang**: for long, cylindrical objects (sebatang pensil = a pencil)
- **lembar/helai**: for flat, thin objects (selembar kertas = a sheet of paper)

## Articles and Definiteness

Indonesian has **no articles** (no equivalent of "a/the"). Definiteness is conveyed through:

- Demonstratives: **ini** (this) and **itu** (that), placed after the noun: buku ini (this book), rumah itu (that house)
- Word order: known/definite information tends to precede new/indefinite information
- Context and shared knowledge

## Passive and Active Voice (Focus System)

Indonesian has a prominent voice system:

- **Active voice**: Subject is agent, verb takes me(N)- prefix. "Saya membaca buku" (I read a book).
- **Passive voice (di-)**: Patient is subject, verb takes di- prefix. "Buku itu dibaca oleh saya" (The book was read by me).
- **Passive voice (zero prefix)**: With first/second person agents, the agent precedes the bare verb stem. "Buku itu saya baca" (The book, I read it).

This voice alternation is central to Indonesian syntax and discourse structure, used to manage information flow and topic-comment structure.

## Pronouns

Indonesian pronouns vary by formality:

| Person | Informal | Formal |
|--------|----------|--------|
| 1st sg | aku, gue/gua | saya |
| 2nd sg | kamu, lu/lo | Anda, Bapak/Ibu |
| 3rd sg | dia, ia | beliau (respectful) |
| 1st pl (excl) | kami | kami |
| 1st pl (incl) | kita | kita |
| 2nd pl | kalian | Anda sekalian |
| 3rd pl | mereka | mereka |

## Negation

- **tidak / tak**: negates verbs and adjectives (tidak suka = do not like)
- **bukan**: negates nouns and noun phrases (bukan guru = not a teacher)
- **belum**: not yet (belum datang = has not yet come)
- **jangan**: prohibitive (jangan pergi = don't go)
