# Indonesian (ind)

## Summary

Indonesian (endonym: *Bahasa Indonesia*) is an Austronesian language in the
Malayo-Polynesian → Malayic branch. ISO codes: 639-1 `id`, 639-2 `ind`,
639-3 `ind`. It is a standardised register of Malay adopted as the official
language of the Republic of Indonesia in 1945 and now serves roughly 43
million native speakers plus 150–200 million second-language users across the
archipelago, making total speakers the third- or fourth-largest in Asia.
Indonesian functions as a pan-ethnic lingua franca for education, media,
government and business.

## Writing system

Indonesian is written in the 26-letter Latin alphabet with no diacritics and
a post-1972 EYD (*Ejaan Yang Disempurnakan*) spelling reform that aligned its
orthography with Malaysian Malay: `oe → u`, `tj → c`, `dj → j`, `nj → ny`,
`sj → sy`, `ch → kh`. Upper and lower case are used conventionally. The
apostrophe and hyphen are the only common non-alphabetic marks inside words;
the hyphen specifically marks reduplication (`anak-anak` "children",
`pelan-pelan` "slowly"). Loanwords from Arabic, Dutch, Sanskrit, Portuguese
and English are nativised spelling-wise (`universitas`, `kualitas`,
`aktivitas`).

## History and status

Derived from Classical Malay (the historical trade language of the Malacca
Strait), Indonesian was proclaimed in the 1928 *Sumpah Pemuda* youth oath and
codified progressively through the Balai Pustaka publishing house and the
*Pusat Bahasa*. It shares mutual intelligibility of roughly 80 % with
Malaysian Malay but diverges in vocabulary due to Dutch versus English
influence.
