# Cebuano (ceb_Latn)

## Language Names

- **Primary name:** Cebuano
- **Alternate names:** Bisaya, Sinugbuanon, Sugbuanon, Sugbuhanon, Bisayan, Sebuano
- **Autonym:** Sinugbuanong Binisaya

## Classification

- **ISO 639-3:** ceb
- **Language family:** Austronesian > Malayo-Polynesian > Philippine > Greater Central Philippine > Central Philippine > Bisayan > Cebuan
- **Script:** Latin (Latn)
- **Writing system:** Latin alphabet, historically Badlit (Baybayin variant)

## Speakers

- **Native speakers:** approximately 20 million
- **Total speakers (L1 + L2):** approximately 27 million
- **Ethnologue status:** 3 (Wider communication)

Cebuano ranks as the second most widely spoken native language in the Philippines after Tagalog. It serves as a lingua franca across a vast area of the central and southern Philippines, extending well beyond the island of Cebu from which it takes its name. The language has one of the largest speaker populations among Austronesian languages worldwide and maintains a vigorous presence in daily life, media, education, and local governance across the Visayas and Mindanao regions.

## Geographic Distribution

Cebuano is spoken as a native language across a large and geographically diverse region of the Philippines:

- **Cebu:** The historical heartland of the language, spoken across the entire island and its surrounding smaller islands including Mactan, Bantayan, and the Camotes Islands.
- **Bohol:** The entire island of Bohol speaks Cebuano as its primary language, with local dialectal variations.
- **Eastern Negros:** The eastern portions of Negros Island, particularly Negros Oriental province, are predominantly Cebuano-speaking, in contrast to the Hiligaynon-speaking western side.
- **Western Leyte:** Parts of western Leyte province use Cebuano, transitioning to Waray in the eastern portions.
- **Mindanao:** Cebuano is the dominant language across most of Mindanao, including major cities such as Cagayan de Oro, Davao, General Santos, Butuan, and Zamboanga del Norte. This widespread presence in Mindanao resulted from waves of Visayan migration during the 19th and 20th centuries.
- **Siquijor:** The entire island province speaks Cebuano.
- **Parts of Masbate and Sorsogon:** Some communities in these southern Luzon provinces also speak Cebuano due to historical migration.

## Official Status

Cebuano holds the status of a recognized regional language within the Philippines. While Filipino (based on Tagalog) and English are the two official languages of the Philippines as specified in the 1987 Constitution, Cebuano functions as a de facto official language in many local government units, courts, and institutions throughout the Visayas and Mindanao. Under the Mother Tongue-Based Multilingual Education (MTB-MLE) policy implemented by the Department of Education beginning in 2012, Cebuano is used as the medium of instruction in early elementary education in Cebuano-speaking areas.

## Historical Development

The Cebuano language has a rich historical trajectory closely intertwined with the broader history of the Philippines. Before Spanish colonization, the language was written using Badlit, a script related to the Baybayin writing system used by Tagalog speakers. The earliest known written records in what is now the Philippines come from the Cebuano-speaking region, including inscriptions and early colonial-era documents.

The arrival of the Spanish in 1521, led by Ferdinand Magellan who landed in Cebu, marked a transformative period. Spanish missionaries produced the first grammars and dictionaries of Cebuano, and the language absorbed a substantial number of Spanish loanwords relating to religion, governance, technology, and daily life. Words such as "kwarta" (money, from Spanish "cuarta"), "lamesa" (table, from "la mesa"), and "bintana" (window, from "ventana") remain in everyday use.

During the American colonial period (1898-1946), English became the medium of instruction in schools, leading to the incorporation of English loanwords. The postwar period saw significant migration of Cebuano speakers to Mindanao under government resettlement programs, dramatically expanding the language's geographic reach.

In the modern era, Cebuano has developed a vibrant literary tradition, a presence in broadcast media and film, and an increasingly visible digital footprint. Cebuano Wikipedia (known as "Sugbuanon nga Wikipedia") was at one point one of the largest Wikipedia editions by article count, though many of its articles were generated by automated processes. The language continues to evolve, absorbing influences from English, Tagalog, and other languages while maintaining its distinctive grammatical structures and core vocabulary.
