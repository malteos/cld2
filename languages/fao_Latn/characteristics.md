# Faroese (fao_Latn) -- Language Characteristics

## Script and Special Characters

Faroese is written in the Latin script with several additional characters that serve as strong diagnostic markers for language identification.

### Extended Characters

| Character | Name | Notes |
|-----------|------|-------|
| ð (eth) | Faroese eth | Highly diagnostic; shared only with Icelandic among modern languages |
| ø | O-slash | Diagnostic in combination with ð; Icelandic uses ö instead |
| á | A-acute | Common vowel marker |
| í | I-acute | Common vowel marker |
| ó | O-acute | Common vowel marker |
| ú | U-acute | Common vowel marker |
| ý | Y-acute | Less frequent but distinctive |
| æ | Ash | Shared with Danish and Norwegian |

The co-occurrence of **ð and ø** in the same text is the single strongest diagnostic signal for Faroese. Icelandic uses ð but pairs it with ö (not ø). Danish and Norwegian use ø but lack ð entirely. No other modern language combines both characters productively.

## Character Frequency

In typical Faroese text, the most frequent characters (case-insensitive, excluding spaces) are:

**a, r, i, n, e, t, u, s, g, ð, l, o, k, m, v, á, h, f, d, ø, í, b, j, ó, ú, y, p, æ, ý**

The character **ð** typically ranks among the top 10-12 most frequent characters, which is notably high. The character **ø** appears less frequently but consistently. Acute-accented vowels (á, í, ó, ú) collectively account for a significant share of the vowel inventory.

## Common Bigrams and Trigrams

### Frequent Bigrams

ar, er, ur, an, in, un, ig, id, og, ir, is, st, al, at, av, ei, en, or, ti, ta

### Frequent Trigrams

tur, ung, ing, ari, rin, iga, and, ist, sta, tal, all, ald, dur, gan, inn, ina, lig, ara

The bigrams **id** and **ig** are particularly characteristic, as they reflect the high frequency of the eth character. The bigram **og** (also the conjunction "and") is extremely common.

## Morphological Patterns

Faroese retains a rich inflectional system inherited from Old Norse, with four cases (nominative, accusative, dative, genitive) and three genders (masculine, feminine, neuter).

### Noun Declension Markers

**Masculine nouns** (strong declension, e.g., *hestur* "horse"):
- Nominative: **-ur** (hestur)
- Accusative: **-an** (hest) or bare stem
- Dative: **-anum** (hesti) or **-i**
- Genitive: **-ans** (hests) or **-s**

The **-ur** nominative ending is extremely frequent and is one of the most visible morphological markers of Faroese text. It is shared with Icelandic but distinguishes Faroese from mainland Scandinavian languages, which have largely lost case inflection.

**Feminine nouns** (e.g., *genta* "girl"):
- Often end in **-a** in the nominative
- Show case variation: -u (accusative/dative), -u (genitive)

**Neuter nouns** (e.g., *barn* "child"):
- Nominative/accusative: **-id** (definite: barnid)
- The neuter definite article suffix **-id** is highly diagnostic

### Verb Conjugation Patterns

Faroese verbs inflect for person and number. Common endings include:
- 1st person singular: **-i** (eg komi "I come")
- 2nd person singular: **-ir** (tu komir "you come")
- 3rd person singular: **-ur** (hann komir / hannkemur "he comes")
- Plural forms often use **-a** (vit koma "we come")

The **-ur** ending appearing on both masculine nouns and certain verb forms contributes to its extremely high frequency in Faroese text.

### Definite Article Suffixes

Like other Scandinavian languages, Faroese uses suffixed definite articles:
- Masculine: **-in** (hesturin "the horse")
- Feminine: **-in** (gentan "the girl")
- Neuter: **-id** (barnid "the child")
- Plural: **-nar, -nar, -ini**

## Usage of ð (eth)

The letter ð in Faroese behaves differently from its Icelandic counterpart. In Faroese orthography, ð never appears word-initially (unlike Old English historical usage). It appears medially and finally in words, where it is often silent or realized as a glide in modern pronunciation. Despite being frequently silent, it is preserved in the conservative orthography, making it a consistent visual marker. Common words containing ð include: tað, ið, við, verður, aðrir, faðir, miðviku, heðan, niður.

The combination of high-frequency ð usage with the vowel ø creates a character co-occurrence profile that is essentially unique to Faroese among all languages using the Latin script.
