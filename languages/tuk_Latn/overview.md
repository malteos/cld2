# Turkmen (tuk)

## Summary

Turkmen (endonym: *Türkmen dili*, *Türkmençe*) is a Turkic language
in the Oghuz branch (Turkic → Common Turkic → Oghuz → Eastern
Oghuz). ISO codes: 639-1 `tk`, 639-2/`639-3` `tuk`. Native speakers
number roughly 6–8 million, mostly in Turkmenistan, with additional
communities in Iran, Afghanistan, Uzbekistan, Russia, and Turkey.
Turkmen is closely related to Azerbaijani and Turkish but is more
conservative phonologically (notably, it retains contrastive vowel
length).

## Writing system

This folder covers the Latin orthography. Turkmenistan adopted a
Latin alphabet in 1993 (refined in 1999 and 2000); it replaced the
Cyrillic script used from 1940 to 1993. The current 30-letter Latin
inventory uses `a b ç d e ä f g h i j ž k l m n ň o ö p r s ş t u ü
w y ý z`. Diagnostic characters versus Turkish are
**`ä ý ň ž`**; Turkmen also uses `ş ö ü ç` as in Turkish. Long
vowels are often represented by doubled letters in careful writing
(*aalym, gaarag*). Decimal separator is the comma.

## History and status

Turkmen is the state language of Turkmenistan. Script policy has
shifted repeatedly since the early 20th century: Arabic → Latin
(1928) → Cyrillic (1940) → Latin (1993). Some older corpora still
show Cyrillic or transitional orthographies, but the post-1999
Latin form is standard today.
