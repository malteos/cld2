# Turkmen characteristics

## Script signals

Latin alphabet with the diagnostic cluster **`ä ý ň ž`** alongside
shared Turkic letters `ç ş ö ü`. The front-vowel `ä` (U+00E4) and
the acute-`ý` (U+00FD) are specifically Turkmen among Latin Turkic
languages. `ň` (U+0148) marks /ŋ/; `ž` (U+017E) marks /ʒ/. Turkmen
has **no** `ı` (dotless i) — it uses `y` in its place — and **no**
`ğ`; Turkish has both. Doubled vowels in stems (`aa ee ii oo ää`)
indicate Turkmen vowel length and are a strong cue.

## Top function words

*we, bir, bu, şu, ol, üçin, bilen, hem, emma, ýöne, ýa, ýa-da, köp,
has, iň, bar, ýok, däl, men, sen, biz, siz, olar, ýaly, ýaly-da,
soň, öň, indi, eýýäm, ýene, haçan, näme, nähili, näçe, nirede*.

## N-gram and spelling patterns

- Genitive `-yň/-iň/-uň/-üň` word-end is common.
- Locative `-da/-de`, ablative `-dan/-den`, dative `-a/-e`.
- Plural `-lar/-ler`.
- Progressive/present `-ýar/-ýär` + person is a high-signal
  Turkmen-specific morpheme.
- Future `-jak/-jek`.
- Word-final `ň` is distinctive.
- Digraphs `ýa`, `ýe`, `ýo`, `ýu` initial (where `ý` represents
  /j/).

## Morphology hooks

- `-ýar/-ýär` present is the clearest finite-verb diagnostic.
- `-jak/-jek` future only occurs in Turkmen among Oghuz Latin
  languages.
- Vowel length written as doubled vowel (*aalym, gaarag, söýüü*)
  distinguishes Turkmen orthographically even when other letters
  look Turkic.

## Punctuation and orthography

Standard Latin punctuation. Quotations `“…”` or `«…»`. Comma
decimal separator. Some recent texts use plain ASCII `y` in place
of `ý` and plain `n` for `ň` when diacritics cannot be rendered —
those fallbacks degrade LID precision but common words (*biz,
olar, bar, ýok*) remain identifiable.
