# Vietnamese (vie)

## Summary

Vietnamese, endonym tiếng Việt, is an Austroasiatic language in the Vietic branch. ISO codes: 639-1 `vi`, 639-2 `vie`, 639-3 `vie`. Native speakers number roughly 85–95 million, overwhelmingly in Vietnam, with substantial diasporas in the United States, France, Australia, Canada, Germany and the Czech Republic. Historically written in Chữ Nôm (classical Han-derived characters) and Classical Chinese (Hán văn) until the 20th century.

## Writing system

Modern Vietnamese is written in **Quốc ngữ**, a Latin-based orthography devised by Jesuit missionaries (notably Alexandre de Rhodes, 17th c.) and standardised under French colonial administration. Key features:

- **Latin base alphabet** with additional letters: ă, â, đ, ê, ô, ơ, ư (and their capitals). The letter **đ** (d with stroke, U+0111 / U+0110) is diagnostic.
- **Tone marks** stacked as diacritics on vowels: no mark = level tone (ngang); acute ´ = sắc (high rising); grave ` = huyền (low falling); hook ả = hỏi (dipping); tilde ã = ngã (creaky rising); dot below ạ = nặng (low falling creaky).
- **Combining diacritics** stack on already-modified vowels, producing characters like ế, ớ, ễ, ộ, ợ, ử, ậ — a hallmark visual density.
- **Spaces separate syllables, not words**: most content words are disyllabic but written as two space-separated syllables (nhà cửa, học sinh).
- Western punctuation (. , ? !) and capitalisation rules follow European conventions.

## Status

Sole official language of Vietnam. One of the most-spoken Austroasiatic languages worldwide and the only one written in Latin script by default.
