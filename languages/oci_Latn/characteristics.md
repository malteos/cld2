# Occitan LID characteristics

## Script signals

Signature characters: grave `ò` (unique among major Romance — indicates open /ɔ/) and `è`, plus `ç` and acute `é`. Digraphs `nh lh` for palatal nasal/lateral (like Portuguese, unlike French `gn`, Italian `gn`, Spanish `ñ`, Catalan `ny`). No `ñ`, no `ã õ`, no `œ`, no `l·l`.

## Top function words

`lo la los las eth era un una uns unas de del dels a al als en amb per e o mas perque que qui ont quand coma si non pas mai mens fòrça pauc tot totes cada autre meteish aqueste aqueth ieu jo tu el era eth nosautres vosautres eles me te se nos vos li lor ne i es son èra èran a an aviá avián i a`.

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- `nh` (palatal nasal) and `lh` (palatal lateral): *senhor, filha, Occitània*.
- Grave `ò` and `è` used productively (Spanish/Italian use these only in limited positions).
- Ending `-cion` (not `-ció` like Catalan, not `-tion` like French, not `-ción` like Spanish): *nacion, solucion, atencion*.
- Digraph `sh` in Gascon for /ʃ/: *shens, peish*.
- Frequent `-atz`, `-etz`, `-itz` (2pl verb endings).

## Morphology hooks

Infinitives `-ar / -er / -re / -ir`; 2pl `-atz/-etz/-itz`; participle `-at / -ut / -it`. Articles `lo/la/los/las` (Languedocien) and Gascon `eth/era/eths/eras` are highly diagnostic. Gascon enunciative particle *que* preceding verbs (*que canti*).

## Punctuation / orthography quirks

Uses French-style guillemets `« »` but often with ordinary spacing (unlike French narrow NBSP). Apostrophe is used for elision (*l'òme, d'aiga, n'avèm*) — similar to Catalan. No inverted `¿ ¡`.
