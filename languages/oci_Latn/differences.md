# Occitan vs. confusable languages

Closest confusables in this set: **French (fra)**, **Catalan (cat)**, and to a lesser extent **Aragonese (arg)**.

## Occitan vs. French

- Occitan writes `-cion` (*nacion, canton*); French writes `-tion` (*nation, canton*).
- Occitan uses `ò` (unique) and has `nh lh`; French uses `gn` and never writes `ò`.
- Occitan articles `lo la los las` (Languedocien) or `eth era eths eras` (Gascon); French `le la les`.
- Occitan negation `non`/postverbal `pas`; French obligatory `ne…pas`.
- Occitan retains preterite and imperfect subjunctive in living use; French preterite is literary only.
- Occitan lacks `œ`, silent `-ent`, and nasal graphs `an en in un`; these are French-exclusive.

## Occitan vs. Catalan

- Occitan `nh lh` vs. Catalan `ny l·l` — the single most reliable split.
- Endings: Occitan `-cion -tat`; Catalan `-ció -tat`.
- Occitan Gascon `eth/era` articles are not found in Catalan.
- Occitan grave `ò` is orthographically routine; Catalan uses `ò` only on specific stressed vowels, less frequently.
- Occitan apostrophe elision is similar to Catalan's but less dense.

## Occitan vs. Aragonese

- Aragonese articles `o/a/os/as`; Occitan `lo/la/los/las`.
- Aragonese keeps Latin initial F (*fillo, fer*); Occitan keeps F too (*filh, far*), so this does not split them — use articles and `nh/lh` vs. Aragonese `ll/ny`.
- Aragonese preterite is periphrastic (*vai fer*) in some varieties; Occitan has a synthetic preterite.
- Aragonese lacks the dense use of grave `ò` and cedilla `ç`.
