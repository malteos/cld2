# Dhivehi Grammar

## Word Order

Dhivehi is a Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) language. The basic clause structure places the subject first, followed by the object (or other complements), and the verb at the end. Modifiers generally precede the elements they modify: adjectives precede nouns, adverbs precede verbs, and genitives precede their head nouns. This typological profile aligns Dhivehi with many other South Asian languages.

Example structure: Subject + Indirect Object + Direct Object + Verb

Question formation does not require a change in word order; instead, interrogative particles or intonation are used.

## Phonology

### Consonants

Dhivehi has a rich consonant inventory that includes several notable features:

**Retroflex Consonants:** Like many Indo-Aryan languages, Dhivehi distinguishes between dental and retroflex stops. The retroflex series includes /ɖ/, /ʈ/, and the retroflex nasal. These are represented by distinct characters in the Thaana script (e.g., ޑ for /ɖ/, ޓ for /ʈ/).

**Prenasalized Stops:** A distinctive feature of Dhivehi is the presence of prenasalized stops, which are consonant clusters where a nasal precedes a homorganic stop. These include /mb/, /nd/, /ɳɖ/, /ɲɟ/, and /ŋɡ/. Prenasalized stops function as single phonological units and are common in native vocabulary.

**Gemination:** Consonant gemination (doubling) is phonemically contrastive in Dhivehi. The distinction between single and geminate consonants affects meaning. Gemination is frequent and occurs in medial positions. In Thaana script, a special diacritic called sukun (ް) is used on the first consonant of a geminate pair.

**Glottal Stop:** The glottal stop /ʔ/ is phonemically significant and is represented by the letter alifu (އ) in Thaana script.

### Vowels

Dhivehi has a five-vowel system with both short and long variants: /a, e, i, o, u/ and /aː, eː, iː, oː, uː/. Vowel length is contrastive. Vowels are indicated by diacritical marks (fili) placed above or below consonant letters in the Thaana script, rather than by independent vowel letters.

### Stress and Intonation

Stress in Dhivehi typically falls on the first syllable of a word. In compound words and words with certain suffixes, stress patterns may shift. Intonation plays a role in distinguishing declarative sentences from questions.

## Noun System

### Cases

Dhivehi nouns inflect for case through suffixation. The major cases include:

- **Nominative (unmarked):** The subject of a sentence. No overt suffix in most contexts.
- **Accusative:** Marks the direct object. Typically unmarked for indefinite objects; definite objects may take specific forms.
- **Dative:** Marks the indirect object or recipient. Formed with the suffix -އަށް (-ah).
- **Genitive:** Marks possession or association. Formed with the suffix -ގެ (-ge).
- **Locative:** Indicates location. Formed with the suffix -ގައި (-gai).
- **Ablative:** Indicates source or origin. Formed with the suffix -އިން (-in).
- **Instrumental:** Indicates means or instrument. Also uses -އިން (-in) or related forms.
- **Comitative:** Indicates accompaniment. Formed with the suffix -އާ (-aa) or -އާއެކު (-aaeku).

### Number

Dhivehi distinguishes singular and plural. Plurals are formed through several mechanisms:
- Suffixation: Common plural suffixes include -ތައް (-tah) for inanimate nouns and -އިން (-in) for animate/human nouns.
- Some nouns have irregular plural forms derived from Arabic plural patterns, especially Arabic loanwords.

### Gender

Dhivehi does not have grammatical gender. There is no masculine/feminine distinction in nouns, pronouns, adjectives, or verb agreement. Natural gender for animates is expressed lexically (e.g., separate words for male and female).

### Definiteness

Dhivehi marks definiteness on nouns. Definite nouns take specific suffixes that vary depending on the final sound of the stem. For example, nouns ending in a consonant may take different definite suffixes than those ending in a vowel. The indefinite form is typically the bare stem or uses an indefinite marker.

## Verb System

### Verb Structure

Dhivehi verbs are morphologically complex and display agglutinative characteristics. The verb stem can take multiple suffixes to express tense, aspect, mood, negation, and focus.

### Tenses

Dhivehi distinguishes several tenses:

- **Present tense:** Indicates current or habitual actions. Formed with specific suffixes on the verb stem (e.g., -ން / -ey, -aam).
- **Past tense:** Indicates completed actions. Uses suffixes such as -ީ (-ee) or -ި (-i) depending on the verb class.
- **Future tense:** Indicates prospective actions. Formed with suffixes such as -ާނެ (-aane) or -ާނަން (-aanan) for first person.

### Aspect

Dhivehi verbs encode aspect through various morphological means:
- **Perfective:** Indicates completed actions, often marked by the past tense suffixes.
- **Imperfective/Progressive:** Indicates ongoing or habitual actions. Participial forms combined with auxiliary verbs express progressive aspect.
- **Habitual:** Expressed through present tense forms or specific constructions indicating repeated actions.

### Mood

- **Indicative:** The default mood for statements of fact.
- **Imperative:** Used for commands and requests. Formed by specific verb endings, with distinctions for politeness levels.
- **Conditional:** Expresses hypothetical situations. Uses specific conditional suffixes or particles.
- **Optative/Hortative:** Expresses wishes or suggestions.

### Negation

Verbal negation in Dhivehi uses the negative particle ނު- (nu-) prefixed to the verb, or the negative auxiliary ނޫން (noon). Different tenses have distinct negative forms. The negative construction interacts with tense and aspect marking.

### Focus Forms

A distinctive feature of Dhivehi is the focus verb form, where different suffixes indicate which element of the sentence is in focus. This is a typologically notable feature shared with Sinhalese and some Dravidian languages. The focus system allows the verb to mark whether the subject, object, or an adverbial element is the pragmatic focus of the sentence.

### Participial Constructions

Dhivehi makes extensive use of participial forms. Participles function as relative clauses, adverbial clauses, and complement clauses. The participial construction involves a non-finite verb form that can take tense and negation marking. Participial clauses typically precede the head noun they modify.

## Syntax

### Postpositions

As an SOV language, Dhivehi uses postpositions rather than prepositions. Case-marked nouns combine with postpositions to express spatial, temporal, and other relationships. Some postpositions require specific case forms on their complement noun.

### Relative Clauses

Relative clauses in Dhivehi are prenominal, meaning they precede the noun they modify. They are formed using participial verb forms rather than relative pronouns. The relativized element is typically gapped (left unexpressed) within the relative clause.

### Coordination and Subordination

Coordination of clauses uses conjunctions such as -އަދި (adu, "and") and -ނޫނީ (noonee, "or"). Subordination is achieved through participial forms, conditional suffixes, and complementizers. Purpose clauses, reason clauses, and temporal clauses each have specific morphological or syntactic strategies.

### Topic and Focus

Dhivehi employs topic-comment structure. Topic markers can highlight the topic of the sentence. The focus system, as mentioned above, interacts with verbal morphology to indicate information structure. These features give Dhivehi considerable flexibility in pragmatic organization of discourse despite its relatively fixed SOV word order.

### Copular Constructions

Dhivehi uses copular verbs for identificational and equative sentences. The copula can be omitted in certain present-tense constructions. Existential sentences use a distinct existential verb.

### Causative and Passive

Causative constructions are formed morphologically by adding suffixes to the verb stem. Passive constructions also involve morphological derivation, though periphrastic passive constructions using auxiliary verbs are also common.

### Serial Verb Constructions

Dhivehi employs serial verb constructions and compound verbs, where two or more verb forms combine to express a single complex event. Light verb constructions, where a content verb combines with a semantically light verb (such as "do" or "become"), are productive.
