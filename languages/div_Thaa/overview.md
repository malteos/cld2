# Dhivehi (Divehi / Maldivian)

## Basic Information

- **Language Name:** Dhivehi (also spelled Divehi; also called Maldivian)
- **ISO 639-3 Code:** div
- **ISO 639-1 Code:** dv
- **Language Family:** Indo-European > Indo-Iranian > Indo-Aryan > Southern Zone > Insular Indo-Aryan
- **Script:** Thaana (written right-to-left)
- **Native Speakers:** Approximately 340,000
- **Official Status:** Official language of the Republic of Maldives

## Geographic Distribution

Dhivehi is spoken primarily in the Republic of Maldives, an archipelago of approximately 1,190 coral islands grouped into 26 natural atolls in the Indian Ocean, southwest of Sri Lanka. Beyond the Maldives, Dhivehi is also spoken on Minicoy Island (known as Maliku in Dhivehi), which is the southernmost island of the Lakshadweep archipelago administered by India. The Minicoy dialect, known as Mahl or Mahal, exhibits some distinct features but remains mutually intelligible with standard Dhivehi. The language has a small diaspora community in Sri Lanka, India, Malaysia, and the United Kingdom.

## Classification and Genetic Relations

Dhivehi belongs to the Insular Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European language family. Its closest genetic relative is Sinhalese (Sinhala), spoken in Sri Lanka. Together, Dhivehi and Sinhalese form the Insular Indo-Aryan subgroup, which separated from the continental Indo-Aryan languages at an early stage. The two languages diverged significantly over the centuries due to geographic isolation, though they retain shared vocabulary, phonological features, and grammatical structures that are absent from other Indo-Aryan languages.

## Historical Development

The history of Dhivehi can be broadly divided into several periods:

**Elu Prakrit Origins:** Dhivehi evolved from Elu Prakrit, a form of Middle Indo-Aryan closely related to the ancestor of Sinhalese. The earliest settlers of the Maldives are believed to have come from the Indian subcontinent and Sri Lanka, bringing with them a Prakrit language that would eventually develop into Dhivehi.

**Old Dhivehi (pre-12th century):** The earliest linguistic evidence of Dhivehi comes from inscriptions on coral stone (loamafaanu) written in the Evela Akuru script, a Brahmi-derived script related to the Grantha and Sinhalese scripts. These inscriptions date from the 6th century onward and show a language already diverging from Sinhalese.

**Middle Dhivehi (12th-17th century):** Following the conversion of the Maldives to Islam in 1153 CE, the language underwent significant changes. Arabic and Persian loanwords entered the vocabulary extensively. The earlier Dhives Akuru script (a descendant of Evela Akuru) continued in use for several centuries after Islamization.

**Development of Thaana Script:** The Thaana script was developed in the 16th or 17th century during or after the period of Portuguese occupation. Uniquely among scripts, Thaana derives its consonant letters from Arabic numerals and other sources rather than from any existing alphabetic tradition. The first nine letters are based on Arabic numerals 1-9, while subsequent letters derive from local and Arabic letter forms. Vowels are represented using diacritical marks (fili) placed above or below consonant letters, similar to Arabic but with a distinct system. Thaana is written from right to left, consistent with Arabic script conventions, reflecting the Islamic cultural influence on the Maldives.

**Modern Dhivehi (18th century-present):** The modern standard language is based on the dialect of Male (Male' bas), the capital city. Dhivehi continues to incorporate loanwords from Arabic (religious and administrative terms), English (modern technology and governance), Hindi/Urdu (cultural terms), and Persian (literary vocabulary). The language is used in government, education, media, and daily life across the Maldives.

## Dialects

Dhivehi has several regional dialects, including the Male dialect (standard), Addu dialect (Addu bas), Huvadhu dialect, and Mulaku dialect. The southern dialects, particularly Addu and Fuvahmulah, differ substantially from the Male standard in phonology and vocabulary. The Mahl dialect of Minicoy is sometimes classified as a separate language due to political boundaries but remains linguistically close to Dhivehi.

## Sociolinguistic Status

As the sole official language of the Maldives, Dhivehi enjoys a strong position in government, media, and education. English serves as a second language, particularly in higher education and tourism. The Maldivian constitution mandates Dhivehi as the language of the state. The Dhivehi Language Academy (Dhivehi Bahuge Akademii) oversees language planning and standardization.
