# Mandarin grammar

- **Word order**: SVO, with strong topic-comment overlay. Modifiers always precede the head (adjectives, relative clauses, possessors). Time and place phrases typically precede the verb: 我昨天在北京吃了饭.
- **Morphology**: Strongly analytic/isolating. Words are largely monomorphemic or compound; almost no inflection. Grammatical relations are expressed with particles and word order, not affixes.
- **Nouns**: No grammatical gender. No plural inflection on most nouns (们 marks animate plurals: 他们, 学生们). Classifiers (量词) are obligatory between numerals/demonstratives and nouns: 一个人, 两本书, 这只猫.
- **Verbs**: No person/number agreement, no tense morphology. Aspect is marked by particles: 了 (perfective/change of state), 过 (experiential), 着 (durative/continuative). Future/past is expressed lexically (昨天, 明天, 将, 要) or inferred from context. Modals: 会, 能, 可以, 应该.
- **Phonology**: Four lexical tones plus a neutral tone. Syllable structure is (C)(G)V(C) with limited codas (only -n, -ng, and historically -r). Retroflex initials zh/ch/sh/r distinguish standard Mandarin from southern topolects. Erhua 儿化 is a northern feature.
- **Syntax notes**: Pro-drop is common when the referent is clear. The copula 是 links two nominals (他是老师) but is omitted before adjectival predicates, which take 很: 他很高 (not *他是高). Negation: 不 (general/present/future), 没 / 没有 (perfective and for 有 'have'). Questions are formed with the particle 吗, A-not-A reduplication (去不去), or question words in situ (你去哪儿). The 把 construction fronts affected objects; 被 marks passives.
