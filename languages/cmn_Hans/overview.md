# Mandarin Chinese (cmn)

## Summary

Mandarin Chinese, endonym 普通话 (Pǔtōnghuà) in mainland China and 國語/国语 (Guóyǔ) in Taiwan, is a Sinitic branch of the Sino-Tibetan family. ISO codes: 639-1 `zh`, 639-2 `chi`/`zho`, 639-3 `cmn` (the specific variety; `zho` is the macrolanguage). In CommonLID the row `cmn_Hans` fixes the written form to Simplified Han. Native speakers number roughly 900 million to 1.1 billion, with hundreds of millions of L2 speakers. Principal regions are mainland China, Taiwan (Traditional), Singapore and diaspora communities worldwide.

## Writing system

Written in Chinese characters (Han, 汉字). The `cmn_Hans` row uses **Simplified** forms introduced in the PRC from the 1950s onward: 国 (not 國), 个 (not 個), 万 (not 萬), 实 (not 實), 这 (not 這), 为 (not 為), 会 (not 會). There is no letter case; punctuation uses full-width forms: 。， 、 ； ： 「」 『』 （）. Romanization (Hanyu Pinyin) is used pedagogically but rarely in running text. Digits are either Arabic (1,2,3) or Chinese (一二三); large-number characters 亿/万 are common.

## Status

Official language of the PRC, Taiwan, and Singapore; a working language of the UN. `cmn` is the Putonghua/Guoyu standard form, and in running written text it is effectively indistinguishable from the `zho` macrocode entry in CommonLID.
