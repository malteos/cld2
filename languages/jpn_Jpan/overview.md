# Japanese (jpn)

## Summary

Japanese, endonym 日本語 (Nihongo), is the sole major member of the Japonic family (some classifications include Ryūkyūan languages as sisters). ISO codes: 639-1 `ja`, 639-2 `jpn`, 639-3 `jpn`. Native speakers number roughly 125 million, overwhelmingly in Japan, with sizeable heritage communities in Brazil, the USA, Peru, Hawaii and the Ryukyu Islands. The ISO 15924 script code used in CommonLID is `Jpan`, a composite covering the three co-occurring scripts.

## Writing system

Japanese uses a **tri-script** orthography that mixes in the same sentence:

- **Kanji** (漢字) — logographic characters imported from Chinese, used for most content roots.
- **Hiragana** (ひらがな, U+3040–U+309F) — a cursive syllabary used for native grammatical morphology, particles (の は を が に で と), and words without common Kanji.
- **Katakana** (カタカナ, U+30A0–U+30FF) — an angular syllabary used primarily for foreign loanwords, onomatopoeia, and emphasis.

Full-width Latin (Romaji) and Arabic digits are common. Punctuation is full-width: 。 (maru) sentence stop, 、 (ten) comma, 「 」 and 『 』 for quotation, ・ (middle dot) for separating katakana compounds. There is no inter-word spacing and no letter case. Modern Japanese uses post-1946 Tōyō/Jōyō Kanji simplifications (国 学 会 来 — largely matching PRC Simplified but not identical).

## Status

National and de facto official language of Japan; a major language of international business, popular culture, and technology documentation.
