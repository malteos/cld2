# Crimean Tatar (crh)

## Summary

Crimean Tatar (endonym: *qırımtatar tili*, *qırımca*) is a Turkic
language that sits on the Oghuz–Kipchak boundary (Turkic → Common
Turkic, with Kipchak-Nogai and Oghuz-influenced subdialects). ISO
codes: 639-2/639-3 `crh`. Native speakers number roughly 260–500
thousand, with communities in Crimea (Ukraine), Uzbekistan
(descendants of 1944 deportees), Romania, Bulgaria, Turkey, and
the wider Crimean Tatar diaspora. This folder covers the Latin
script (`crh_Latn`), which is the officially adopted modern form.

## Writing system

Crimean Tatar is historically written in Arabic, Cyrillic, and
Latin scripts. The Latin alphabet was re-adopted as official in
1997 (revised 2021). Distinctive letters are **`â ç ğ ı ñ ö ş ü q
x`**. The velar-nasal **`ñ` (U+00F1)** is the single most
diagnostic character against Turkish. `q` and `x` are used for
phonemic /q/ and /x/ as in Azerbaijani, not as loanword letters.
Crimean Tatar occasionally uses `â` (circumflex a) to mark
palatalised consonants in Arabic/Persian loans. Mixed Cyrillic
text is common outside official contexts, but CLD2 material here
is dominantly Latin.

## History and status

Crimean Tatar is recognised as an indigenous language of Ukraine
and is classified as "severely endangered" by UNESCO after the
1944 deportation and subsequent language shift.
