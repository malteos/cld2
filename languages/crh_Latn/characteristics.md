# Crimean Tatar characteristics

## Script signals

Latin script with **`ñ ç ğ ı ö ş ü q x`** and occasional `â`. The
**velar-nasal `ñ` (U+00F1)** is the single most discriminating
letter versus Turkish and versus most other Latin-Turkic
languages. Native `q` (*qırım, qız, qart*) and `x` (*xalq, xastalıq*)
are also strong cues. Crimean Tatar does **not** use `ə` (Azerbaijani)
or `ä ý ň ž` (Turkmen) or the Uzbek digraphs `oʻ gʻ`.

## Top function words

*ve, bir, bu, şu, o, için, ile, da, de, ta, te, amma, lâkin, ya,
yaki, çoq, daa, eñ, bar, yoq, degil, men, sen, biz, siz, olar, kibi,
qadar, soñ, evel, nasıl, qayda, qaçan, nege, ne, kim*.

## N-gram and spelling patterns

- Plural `-lar/-ler`.
- Locative `-da/-de/-ta/-te`, ablative `-dan/-den/-tan/-ten`.
- Genitive `-nıñ/-niñ/-nuñ/-nüñ` — the trigram `ñ` + case suffix is
  a strong fingerprint.
- Perfective participle `-gan/-gen/-qan/-ken`.
- Word-final `-ñ` is frequent (second-person possessive and
  genitive).
- Initial `q-` (*qart, qırım, qara, qız*) appears in native stems.

## Morphology hooks

- Genitive ending contains `ñ`, so `ñ` at word end is often a
  case marker.
- Perfective `-gan/-qan` contrasts with Turkish `-miş`.
- Second-person singular possessive `-ıñ/-iñ/-uñ/-üñ` likewise
  ends in `ñ`.

## Punctuation and orthography

Standard Latin punctuation. Quotations `"…"` or `«…»`. Apostrophe
may separate proper-noun stems from suffixes (Turkish-style).
Comma decimal. Some web text still uses plain `n` where `ñ` should
appear — degraded input that weakens the strongest LID signal.
