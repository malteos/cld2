# Differences (sot)

## Confusable languages

### sot vs nso (Northern Sotho / Sepedi)

The central confusable pair. Both are Sotho-Tswana languages with
disjunctive writing and overlapping vocabulary.

- **`š` is the headline cue**: **absent** in sot, present in nso
  (`šoma`, `tšhelete`). sot writes these as `sh`/`s`/`tsh` or
  applies different lexicon (`sebetsa` for "work").
- **Circumflex `ê` / `ô`** appear in sot (Lesotho tradition) but
  not in nso.
- Infinitive / locative particle: **sot `ho`**, nso **`go`**
  (`ho bua` vs `go bolela`).
- Function words: sot `hore`, `ha`, `empa`, `hobane`, `kapa`,
  `jwale`; nso `gore`, `ge`, `eupša`, `ka gobane`, `goba`,
  `bjale`.
- Causative: sot `-isa-`, nso `-iša-`.
- `g`/`h` alternation: sot `tsoha` "wake up" vs nso `tsoga`; sot
  `ha` "not/when" vs nso `ga`.

### sot vs other Bantu

- vs **tsn** (Tswana, not in this set): same disjunctive family;
  Tswana uses more `g` and `tlh`.
- vs **zul/xho**: sot has no clicks, no augments, and uses
  disjunctive spelling; Nguni is conjunctive.
- vs **swh/swa**: different prefix set (`mo-/ba-/le-/se-/bo-/ho-`
  vs Swahili `m-/wa-/ki-/vi-`), `hl`/`tlh` digraphs, and `ho`
  infinitive.
- vs **sna**: no prenasalised `nd/mb/nh/nz`; sot has `tlh`, `hl`
  instead.
- vs **lin/lug/nyn/kik**: Southern African orthography (`hl`,
  `tlh`, `kg`) is the distinguishing feature.
