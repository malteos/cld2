# Southern Sotho / Sesotho (sot)

## Summary

Southern Sotho (endonym: **Sesotho**) is a Bantu language of the
Sotho-Tswana group (Niger-Congo → Atlantic-Congo → Benue-Congo →
Southern Bantu → Sotho-Tswana). ISO codes: 639-1 `st`, 639-2 `sot`,
639-3 `sot`. It has approximately 6–7 million speakers in Lesotho
(where it is co-official with English) and in the Free State and
Gauteng provinces of South Africa; it is one of the eleven official
languages of South Africa.

## Writing system

Latin script, bicameral. Sesotho uses **circumflex vowels `ê` and
`ô`** in careful orthography (`lefatše`, `sebêtsa` in some printings;
standard SA orthography more often drops the circumflex). It does
**not** use `š`. Common digraphs: `hl`, `tlh`, `kh`, `ph`, `th`, `tsh`,
`ts`, `tj`, `tjh`, `kg`, `ng`, `ny`, `sh`. Disjunctive spelling: verb
concord and TAM auxiliaries are written as separate words
(`re ne re sebetsa`).

## History

Sesotho was reduced to writing in the 1830s by the Paris Evangelical
Missionary Society (Casalis, Arbousset) in what is now Lesotho. Two
orthographic traditions coexist: the Lesotho tradition, which keeps
the circumflex `ê` / `ô`, and the South African tradition, which
tends to omit it. Sesotho is closely related to Northern Sotho
(`nso`) and Tswana (`tsn`).
