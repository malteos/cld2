# Characteristics (sot)

## Script signals

**No `š`** — sot uses `sh` or plain `s` where nso has `š`.
Circumflex **`ê`** and **`ô`** appear in the Lesotho spelling
tradition (`lefatše` in nso vs `lefatshe` / `lefatsê` in sot;
`sebetsa`). Common digraphs/trigraphs: `hl`, `tlh`, `tsh`, `tjh`,
`tj`, `kg`, `kh`, `ph`, `th`, `sh`, `ng`, `ny`. No clicks, no
augments. Disjunctive spelling gives a short-word rhythm.

## Top function words

`hore` (that/so that), `ha` (when/if/not), `empa` (but), `kapa`
(or), `hobane` / `hobaneng` (because), `ka lebaka la` (because of),
`le` (and/with/also), `ka` (with/by), `ho` (to/of/infinitive), `ya`
(of — cl. 9), `wa` (of — cl. 1/3), `jwale` (now), `moo` (there),
`mona` (here), `teng` (there/inside), `ke` (it is / 1sg), `ha se`
(is not), `ena` / `eo` (this/that), `bao` / `bana` (those/these
people), `eng` (what), `mang` (who), `neng` (when), `kae` (where),
`kaofela` (all), `feela` (only), `hape` (again), `e`/`ee` (yes),
`che` (no).

## N-gram / spelling patterns

- **No `š`** anywhere — the key negative cue vs nso.
- Verb concord + TAM as separate words: `ke a`, `o a`, `re ne re`,
  `ba tla`, `ha ke`, `ha a`.
- Class prefixes: `mo-`, `ba-`, `me-`, `le-`, `ma-`, `se-`, `di-`,
  `bo-`, `ho-`, `n-`.
- Final `-ile`, `-a`, `-e`. Perfect `-ile` (`sebeditse` "has
  worked").
- Characteristic digraphs/trigraphs: `hl`, `tlh`, `tsh`, `tjh`,
  `kg`, `ng`, `ny`, `ph`, `th`, `kh`.
- `ho` (not `go`) for infinitive / locative particle is a strong
  sot cue.
- Circumflex `ê`/`ô` appears in Lesotho printings.

## Morphology hooks

Extensions: applicative `-el-/-etse-`, causative `-isa-` (note:
`-isa-`, not nso `-iša-`!), passive `-w-`, reciprocal `-an-`,
stative `-eh-`. Locative suffix `-ng`.

## Orthography quirks

Two spelling traditions: Lesotho (with circumflex) and South African
(without). Text may mix them. `tjh` / `tšh` equivalents further
confuse cross-border readers. No apostrophes.
