# Igbo (ibo)

## Summary

Igbo (endonym: **Asụsụ Igbo**) is a Niger-Congo language of the Volta-Niger branch (Niger-Congo → Atlantic-Congo → Volta-Niger → Igboid → Igbo). ISO codes: **ig** (639-1), **ibo** (639-2/T and 639-3). It is spoken by roughly **30–45 million** people, overwhelmingly in **southeastern Nigeria** (Abia, Anambra, Ebonyi, Enugu, Imo states and adjoining areas of Delta and Rivers), with diaspora communities in Equatorial Guinea and the Caribbean. Igbo is one of the three major languages of Nigeria alongside Hausa and Yoruba.

## Writing system

Igbo uses a Latin alphabet with **under-dot vowels** and **tone marks**. The under-dotted letters **ị** (U+1ECB), **ọ** (U+1ECD), **ụ** (U+1EE5) represent /ɪ/, /ɔ/, /ʊ/ and are contrastive with plain **i, o, u**. Consonant digraphs include **gb**, **kp**, **gh**, **kw**, **gw**, **nw**, **ny**, **ch**, **sh**. Syllabic nasal **ṅ** (with dot above, U+1E45) is used in some orthographies but is often written simply **n** or **ṅ**. Three tones (high, low, downstep) are marked with acute ◌́, grave ◌̀ and macron ◌̄ respectively, mainly in dictionaries and textbooks; running text often leaves tone unmarked.

## History and status

Modern Igbo orthography (the 1961 *Ọnwụ* alphabet) is a unified compromise between earlier Central and Union Igbo standards. Igbo is a recognised national language in Nigeria and is used in education, media, and a growing online corpus.
