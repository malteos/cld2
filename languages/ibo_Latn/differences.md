# Igbo vs. confusable languages

Igbo's main LID confusables are **Yoruba** (the other diacritic-heavy Nigerian Niger-Congo language in Latin script) and, less often, Efik/Ibibio and Igboid relatives like Ekpeye.

## Igbo (ibo) vs. Yoruba (yor)

- Different under-dot sets, **disjoint**:
  - Igbo: **ị, ọ, ụ** (underdots on i, o, u).
  - Yoruba: **ẹ, ọ, ṣ** (underdots on e, o, s).
  - Both share **ọ**, but **ị / ụ** are Igbo-only, **ẹ / ṣ** are Yoruba-only.
- Function words: Igbo **na, ka, bụ, dị, nke, ndị, ma, mana**; Yoruba **ni, ti, kì, kò, àwọn, sì, pé, bí**.
- Consonant digraph density is much higher in Igbo (*gb, kp, gh, kw, gw, nw, ny*). Yoruba uses *gb* but not *kp* (it writes *p* for the labial-velar), and does not use *gh/nw/ny* digraphs.
- Contracted **n'-** preposition (*n'ụlọ, n'ihi*) and apostrophe-vowel are Igbo-only.
- Tone marking is usually lighter in Igbo text than in Yoruba text; Yoruba running text is densely marked with acute/grave on almost every syllable.

## Igbo vs. Efik / Ibibio

- Efik/Ibibio use some under-dot vowels but also the letter **ñ** and lack **gb/kp** labial-velars. Function words differ (*ami, afo, enye, eke* Efik).

## Igbo vs. Nigerian Pidgin / English

- Both are Latin but pcm/English are ASCII with no under-dots. Any presence of **ị / ụ** rules them out.
