# Igbo - Language Identification Characteristics

## Special Characters

Igbo uses the standard Latin alphabet augmented with several distinctive characters:

- **Subdotted vowels**: ị/Ị, ọ/Ọ, ụ/Ụ - These represent -ATR vowels and are the single most diagnostic orthographic feature of Igbo. The Unicode characters are formed with combining dot below (U+0323) or precomposed forms (U+1ECB, U+1ECD, U+1EE5).
- **Subdotted nasal**: ñ (n with tilde) or ṅ/Ṅ (n with overdot) - represents the velar nasal /ŋ/. The Onwu orthography uses "ñ" but some texts use the digraph "nw" or "ng."
- **Tone marks** (when used): acute accent (a) for high tone, grave accent (a) for low tone. Most running text omits these.
- **Digraphs**: ch, gb, gh, gw, kp, kw, nw, ny, sh are standard digraphs in the Igbo alphabet.

## Character Frequency Patterns

High-frequency characters in Igbo text include: a, e, i, n, o, u, r, k, m, w, d, g, h, b, s. The subdotted vowels ọ, ụ, ị appear with moderate-to-high frequency and are strong language identifiers. The letter "n" is extremely common due to its role as a syllabic nasal, prefix, and part of many digraphs. The letters "q", "x", "c" (alone), "v", "z" are absent or extremely rare in native Igbo words.

## Tone Pattern Diagnostics

While tones are rarely written, their presence influences the morphophonological system in ways visible in text:

- Verb forms show regular suffixation patterns tied to tone: the completive suffix "-rV" (where V harmonizes), the negative "-ghi"/"-gho"
- The auxiliary "na-" plus harmonizing vowel (na-a-, na-e-) creates characteristic letter sequences
- Tonal polarity in certain constructions forces alternating prefix vowels (a-/e- vs. o-/i-)

## Morphological Features for LID

### Verbal extensions and suffixes
- **-rV** (completive/past): biara (came), gara (went), mere (did)
- **-ghi/-gho** (negative): abiaghi (did not come), emeghi (did not do)
- **-la/-le** (perfective): abiala (has come), emela (has done)
- **-kwa** (also/emphasis): biakwa (also came)
- **-re/-ra** (benefactive/past): nyere (gave), kwure (spoke)

### Noun prefixes and patterns
- **o-/a-/e-** prefixes on verbal nouns and agent nouns: onye (person), ogbu (killer), asụsụ (language)
- **m-/n-** prefixes on deverbal nouns: mmiri (water), nri (food), nkwurịta (conversation)
- **a-** prefix on abstract nouns: ahụike (health), asụsụ (language), amụma (prophecy)
- **ụ-/o-** prefixes: ụlọ (house), ọrụ (work), ọgụ (fight)

### Reduplication
Igbo uses reduplication productively:
- Full reduplication for emphasis or distribution: "nwayọọ nwayọọ" (very slowly)
- Partial reduplication in certain ideophonic constructions

### Compound words
Igbo compounds are common and often transparent:
- ụlọ akwụkwọ (school, lit. "house of book")
- ụlọ ọgwụ (hospital, lit. "house of medicine")
- onye nkuzi (teacher, lit. "person of teaching")

## Distinguishing Igbo from Similar Orthographies

The combination of subdotted vowels (ị, ọ, ụ) with the digraphs (ch, gb, gw, kp, kw, nw, ny, sh) and the high frequency of function words like "na," "nke," "ndi," "maka," "ihe" forms a robust fingerprint. No other major language shares this exact combination of features. The subdotted vowels alone eliminate most non-Igboid languages, and the specific function word inventory distinguishes Igbo from related Igboid varieties.
