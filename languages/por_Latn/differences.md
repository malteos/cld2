# Portuguese vs. confusable languages

Closest confusables in this set: **Spanish (spa)**, and to lesser degrees **Catalan (cat)** and **Extremaduran (ext)**. Galician (not in this set) is the nearest relative; all Portuguese diagnostics below also separate it from Spanish.

## Portuguese vs. Spanish

- Portuguese has `ã õ` (nasal tildes), `â ê ô`, and `ç`; Spanish uses none of these — Spanish uses `ñ ¿ ¡` which Portuguese never uses.
- Digraphs: Portuguese `nh lh` vs. Spanish `ñ ll` (*senhor/mulher* vs. *señor/mujer*).
- Endings: Portuguese `-ção -são -dade -ão` vs. Spanish `-ción -sión -dad -ón` (*coração/corazón, nação/nación, cidade/ciudad*).
- Article contractions: Portuguese routinely contracts (*do, da, no, na, pelo, ao, à*); Spanish only `del, al`.
- Auxiliaries: Portuguese compound tenses prefer `ter` (*tenho feito*); Spanish uses only `haber` (*he hecho*).
- Personal infinitive and future subjunctive exist in Portuguese but not Spanish.

## Portuguese vs. Catalan

- Catalan uses `ny ·l ç è ò à í ú`; Portuguese uses `nh lh ã õ â ê ô ç`.
- Catalan definite articles `el/la/els/les` (and `l'` before vowels); Portuguese `o/a/os/as`.
- Catalan endings `-ció -tat`; Portuguese `-ção -dade`.
- Catalan has weak object clitics `em et es ens us` vs. Portuguese `me te se nos vos`.

## Portuguese vs. Extremaduran / Galician

- Extremaduran is Ibero-Romance close to Spanish/Asturian: final `-h` aspiration (*loh*), `-l` neutralisation (*comel*), `-u/-i` in unstressed finals; absent in Portuguese.
- Galician (outside the set) shares `-ão` as `-ón` and lacks nasal diphthongs `-ães -ões`; writes `nh` as `n`; uses articles `o/a/os/as` like Portuguese.
