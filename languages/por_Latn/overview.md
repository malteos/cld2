# Portuguese (por_Latn)

## Language Name

Portuguese (*Portugues*)

## ISO 639-3 Code

`por`

## Language Family

Indo-European > Italic > Romance > Western Romance > Ibero-Romance > West Iberian > Galician-Portuguese

## Script

Latin (Latn)

## Speakers

Approximately 260 million native speakers worldwide, with over 300 million total speakers including second-language users. Portuguese is the sixth most spoken language in the world by native speakers and the most spoken language in the Southern Hemisphere.

## Geographic Distribution

Portuguese is spoken across four continents and holds official status in nine sovereign states:

- **Portugal** (~10.3 million) -- the historical homeland of the language, where European Portuguese (PE) is the standard variety.
- **Brazil** (~215 million) -- by far the largest Portuguese-speaking country, home to Brazilian Portuguese (PB), which has developed its own phonological, lexical, and syntactic features.
- **Mozambique** (~32 million population; ~14 million speakers) -- official language and the primary lingua franca in urban areas.
- **Angola** (~34 million population; ~25 million speakers) -- official language used in administration, education, and media.
- **Guinea-Bissau** (~2 million) -- official language alongside Kriol.
- **East Timor (Timor-Leste)** (~1.3 million) -- co-official language alongside Tetum.
- **Equatorial Guinea** (~1.5 million) -- adopted as a third official language in 2007.
- **Cape Verde (Cabo Verde)** (~590,000) -- official language alongside Cape Verdean Creole.
- **Sao Tome and Principe** (~220,000) -- official language.

Portuguese is also spoken by significant diaspora communities in the United States, Canada, France, Luxembourg, Switzerland, South Africa, Japan, Venezuela, and elsewhere. Macau (China) retains Portuguese as a co-official language despite a small speaker population. Goa (India) preserves a historical Portuguese-speaking community.

## Official Status

Portuguese is an official language of the **Community of Portuguese Language Countries (CPLP)**, the **European Union**, **Mercosur**, the **African Union**, the **Organization of American States**, and **ASEAN** (as an observer language through Timor-Leste). It serves as one of the working languages of several international organizations.

## Brief History

Portuguese descends from the Vulgar Latin brought to the Iberian Peninsula by Roman soldiers and settlers beginning in the 3rd century BCE. Following the fall of the Western Roman Empire, the language was influenced by Germanic (Visigothic and Suebi) and later Arabic elements during the Moorish period (711--1249 CE). The language that emerged in the northwest of the peninsula, in the region of Galicia and northern Portugal, was initially known as Galician-Portuguese (*galego-portugues*), a single linguistic system documented from the 12th century onward in a rich tradition of medieval lyric poetry (*cantigas*).

The political separation between Portugal and Galicia (which remained part of the Kingdom of Leon and later Castile) led to the gradual divergence of Portuguese and Galician from the 14th century onward. The establishment of the Portuguese kingdom, with its capital moving south to Lisbon, shifted the linguistic center of gravity and introduced southern dialectal features into the emerging standard.

The Age of Discoveries in the 15th and 16th centuries carried Portuguese across the globe. The colonization of Brazil (1500), the establishment of trading posts in Africa and Asia, and the formation of creole languages in contact zones spread Portuguese far beyond its European homeland. Brazilian Portuguese began diverging from the European standard as early as the 16th century due to contact with indigenous Tupi languages and later with African languages brought by enslaved peoples.

The 1990 Orthographic Agreement (*Acordo Ortografico*), signed in 1990 and gradually implemented from 2009 onward, aimed to unify the spelling systems of European and Brazilian Portuguese, though significant pronunciation and lexical differences persist between the two major varieties.
