# Bashkir vs confusable languages

Closest confusables (Cyrillic Turkic): Tatar (tat), Kazakh (kaz),
Russian (rus), and Crimean Tatar Cyrillic (crh_Cyrl).

## bak ↔ tat (Tatar)

- **`ҙ` and `ҫ` are Bashkir-only** — their presence rules Tatar
  out. Corresponding Tatar text uses `з` and `с`.
- **`ҡ ғ` are Bashkir-only** — Tatar uses `к г` in the same
  cognates.
- **Tatar `җ` does not appear in Bashkir** — where Tatar has `җ`,
  Bashkir typically has `й` (*tat* **җ**ир ↔ *bak* **й**ер
  "earth"; *tat* **җ**итте ↔ *bak* **й**етте).
- Word-initial `һ-` in Bashkir where Tatar has `с-` (*bak* һыу ↔
  *tat* су "water"; *bak* һин ↔ *tat* син "you").
- `ә ө ү ң` are shared and not diagnostic by themselves.

## bak ↔ kaz (Kazakh)

- Kazakh lacks `ҙ ҫ`; Bashkir has both.
- Kazakh has `ұ` and `і`, neither of which Bashkir uses.
- Kazakh uses `қ` (U+049B) for /q/, Bashkir uses `ҡ` (U+04A1) —
  different codepoints.

## bak ↔ rus (Russian)

- Any of `ә ө ү ң һ ҙ ҫ ҡ ғ` rules Russian out. Long agglutinative
  suffix chains likewise.

## bak ↔ crh_Cyrl

- Crimean Tatar Cyrillic uses digraphs (`къ гъ нъ`) rather than
  Bashkir's single-character extras. Absence of `ҙ ҫ ҡ ғ` combined
  with presence of `къ` or `нъ` points to Crimean Tatar.
