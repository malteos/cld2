# Arabic grammar (macrolanguage)

- **Word order**: Classical/MSA default is **VSO**; SVO is also common in modern
  prose and dominant in most spoken dialects. Arabic is synthetic with rich
  templatic morphology.
- **Morphology**: Non-concatenative, **root-and-pattern** (typically triliteral
  roots such as k-t-b "write" → kataba, kitāb, maktab, maktūb, kātib). Clitics
  stack: definite article **ال** al-, prepositions **ب، ل، ك**, conjunction
  **و**, and pronominal suffixes (ـه، ـها، ـهم، ـك، ـي، ـنا).
- **Nouns**: Two grammatical genders (masc/fem, fem often marked by ة *tāʾ
  marbūṭa*). Three numbers: singular, dual (ـان/ـين), plural (sound masc ـون/ـين,
  sound fem ـات, or "broken" plurals by internal vowel change). MSA keeps three
  cases (nominative -u, accusative -a, genitive -i); cases are lost in dialects.
- **Verbs**: Two core aspects — **perfective** (past) vs **imperfective**
  (non-past), plus imperative and derived stems (Forms I–X) for causative,
  reflexive, reciprocal, intensive meanings. Rich person/number/gender
  agreement via prefix-suffix combinations (yaktub-u, ta-ktub-īna, na-ktub-u).
- **Phonology**: Emphatic (pharyngealised) consonants ص ض ط ظ, pharyngeals ع ح,
  uvular ق, velar fricatives خ غ. Three short and three long vowels (a, i, u /
  ā, ī, ū) in MSA; dialects enrich the inventory with e, o.
- **Syntax notes**: **Pro-drop** (subject is optional; encoded on the verb).
  No overt present-tense copula — "*Muḥammad ṭālib*" = "Muhammad [is a] student."
  Negation with **لا** / **ما** / **لم** / **لن** / **ليس** depending on tense
  and register. Definiteness by prefixing **ال**; the ل assimilates to "sun
  letters" (الشمس → *aš-šams*).
