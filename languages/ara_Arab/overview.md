# Arabic — macrolanguage (ara)

## Summary

Arabic (endonym: العربية, *al-ʿarabiyya*) is the macrolanguage code covering the
diasystem of spoken and written Arabic varieties. Family tree:
Afro-Asiatic → Semitic → Central Semitic → Arabic. ISO codes: **639-1 `ar`**,
**639-2 `ara`**, **639-3 `ara`** (macrolanguage, with ~30 individual members
including `arb`, `arz`, `ary`, `ars`, `aeb`, `apd`). Total speakers exceed 400
million across the Arab League states (from Morocco and Mauritania in the west
to Oman and Iraq in the east) plus large diasporas in Europe, the Americas,
and Sub-Saharan Africa.

## Writing system

Written in the **Arabic script** (U+0600–U+06FF, plus Arabic Supplement and
Arabic Presentation Forms). The script is an abjad: 28 basic consonant letters,
written right-to-left, cursive, with contextual letter forms (isolated, initial,
medial, final). Short vowels are usually omitted; optional harakat (ـَ ـِ ـُ ـْ
ـّ) mark vocalisation. No casing. Punctuation uses Arabic comma ، and Arabic
question mark ؟.

## Status

In CommonLID and most LID corpora, the `ara` label is applied to text that is
broadly formal and Modern-Standard-Arabic–like in register; spontaneous
dialectal text tends to be tagged with variety-specific codes such as `arz`,
`ary`, `ars`, `aeb`, or `apd`. Treat `ara` as MSA-flavoured macrolabel in
practice.
