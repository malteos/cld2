# Serbian (српски језик / srpski jezik)

## General Information

Serbian, natively **српски језик** / **srpski jezik**, is a South Slavic language of the Indo-European family. Its ISO 639-3 code is **srp** (ISO 639-1 *sr*). It is one of the standardized varieties of the pluricentric **Serbo-Croatian** (Shtokavian) language, alongside Croatian (*hrv*), Bosnian (*bos*) and Montenegrin (*cnr*). Speaker estimates vary by source and by how diaspora is counted; figures on the order of 8-12 million native speakers are often cited (approximate).

Serbian is **digraphic**: it is written in **Serbian Cyrillic** (*ћирилица*) and in **Gaj's Latin alphabet** (*latinica*). The two alphabets map one-to-one (30 letters each), so any Serbian text can be converted mechanically between them (cf. `sr_lat2cyr` in `../scripts/translit.py`). The 2006 Constitution of Serbia names Cyrillic as the official script, but Latin is extremely common in media, advertising and especially online.

## Geographic Distribution and Official Status

Serbian is the official language of **Serbia** and one of the official languages of **Bosnia and Herzegovina** (dominant in the Republika Srpska entity). It is co-official in **Kosovo** with Albanian, and is widely spoken in **Montenegro**, where it is the most commonly declared mother tongue in recent censuses (it has a recognized status there; Montenegrin is the constitutional official language). Serbian is a recognized minority language in Croatia, Hungary, Romania, North Macedonia, Slovakia and the Czech Republic, and there are large diaspora communities in Germany, Austria, Switzerland, the USA, Canada and Australia.

## Language Family

Indo-European > Balto-Slavic > Slavic > **South Slavic > Western South Slavic** > Serbo-Croatian (Shtokavian). Standard Serbian is based on the **Neo-Shtokavian** dialects. The Torlakian dialects of south-eastern Serbia are transitional towards Macedonian and Bulgarian (some loss of cases, postposed articles) but are not the basis of the standard.

## Yat Reflexes: Ekavian and Ijekavian

The Proto-Slavic vowel *yat* (ě) has two standard reflexes in Serbian:

- **Ekavian** (*е*): used in Serbia -- *време, дете, лепо, млеко, река, где, пре, после*.
- **Ijekavian** (*ије/је*): used by Serbs in Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro and Croatia -- *вријеме, дијете, лијепо, мл(иј)еко, ријека, гдје, прије, послије*.

Both are standard. Most written Serbian from Serbia (and most news text) is ekavian; Croatian, Bosnian and Montenegrin are ijekavian. Ekavian forms are therefore a strong signal for Serbian, while ijekavian forms do not exclude it.

## Historical Development

Medieval Serbian literacy used Serbian recensions of Church Slavonic (e.g. the 12th-century *Miroslav Gospel*). In the 18th century, Russian-influenced Slavonic-Serbian (*славеносербски*) was the literary language. The decisive reform was carried out by **Vuk Stefanović Karadžić** in the first half of the 19th century: a phonemic Cyrillic alphabet ("write as you speak"), adding **ј, љ, њ, ђ, ћ, џ** and dropping redundant letters (no ъ, ь, я, ю, щ, ы). His dictionary (*Srpski rječnik*, 1818) and the 1850 Vienna Literary Agreement with Croatian writers laid the foundation for a common Shtokavian standard. During Yugoslavia, the joint Serbo-Croatian standard had eastern (ekavian, Cyrillic-and-Latin) and western variants; after 1991 Serbian, Croatian, Bosnian and Montenegrin have been codified separately.

## Writing System Summary

- Cyrillic: а б в г д **ђ** е ж з и **ј** к л **љ** м н **њ** о п р с т **ћ** у ф х ц ч **џ** ш
- Latin: a b c č ć d dž đ e f g h i j k l lj m n nj o p r s š t u v z ž
- Letters ђ/đ and ћ/ć are the most distinctive: they occur in no other Cyrillic standard and, in Latin, only in the Serbo-Croatian standards.
