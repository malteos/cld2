# Ancient / Biblical Hebrew (hbo)

## Summary

Ancient Hebrew — more precisely **Biblical Hebrew** (endonym: **עִבְרִית
מִקְרָאִית** *ʿIvrit Miqraʾit*, or simply **לְשׁוֹן הַקֹּדֶשׁ** *Leshon HaQodesh*,
"the holy tongue") — is the form of Hebrew attested in the Hebrew Bible
(Tanakh) and epigraphic sources from roughly the 10th century BCE to the
2nd century CE. Family: Afro-Asiatic → Semitic → Central Semitic → Northwest
Semitic → Canaanite → Hebrew. ISO: **639-3 `hbo`** (Ancient Hebrew;
distinct from `heb` Modern Hebrew). No native speakers today; used in
religious, liturgical, and scholarly contexts by Jewish and Christian
communities worldwide.

## Writing system

Uses the **Hebrew script** (U+0590–U+05FF) — the same 22-letter abjad as
Modern Hebrew, written right-to-left. Scholarly and liturgical editions
typically include:
- **Niqqud** (vowel points): ַ ָ ֵ ֶ ִ ֹ ֻ ְ ֲ ֱ ֳ ׁ ׂ
- **Te'amim** (cantillation / trope): ֑ ֖ ֥ ֔ ֛ ֣ ֜ ֧ ֗ ֞ ֚ ֙ and many more
- **Maqqef** ־ (hyphen joining words), **sof pasuq** ׃ (verse terminator),
  **paseq** ׀ (separator).

Pointed and cantillated text is the strongest single LID cue separating hbo
from modern heb and Yiddish.

## Status

Primary language of the Hebrew Bible (Torah, Nevi'im, Ketuvim) and of some
post-biblical and Qumranic texts. Studied in Jewish education, Christian
seminaries, Semitic philology, and now NLP for historical/religious corpora.
CommonLID `hbo_Hebr` samples are almost exclusively biblical or liturgical.
