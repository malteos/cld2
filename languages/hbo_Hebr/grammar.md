# Biblical / Ancient Hebrew grammar

- **Word order**: **VSO** dominant in prose narrative; verb-initial clauses
  are the norm. Poetry allows freer order for parallelism.
- **Morphology**: Classical root-and-pattern Semitic system. Seven binyanim
  (qal, nifal, piel, pual, hifil, hofal, hitpael) — same as Modern Hebrew but
  with archaic forms (e.g. poel, polel for geminate/hollow roots). Rich
  pronominal suffix system on verbs, nouns, and prepositions (כְּתָבוֹ "he
  wrote it", סוּסוֹ "his horse", לוֹ "to him", עִמָּהֶם "with them").
- **Nouns**: Masc/fem; sing/dual/pl (dual productive on body parts and pairs).
  Vestigial cases: **ה locative** (ירושלמה "towards Jerusalem"), construct
  state (סוּס דָּוִד "David's horse"), and the object marker **אֵת** with
  definite accusatives.
- **Verbs — critically distinctive**: Two stem "tenses": **qatal**
  (perfective/past) and **yiqtol** (imperfective/future/modal), plus
  **wayyiqtol** (וַיִּקְטֹל — waw-consecutive imperfect, narrative past: וַיֹּאמֶר,
  וַיְהִי, וַיֵּלֶךְ) and **weqatal** (וְקָטַל — waw-consecutive perfect, used for
  future/sequential). Jussive, cohortative (אֶקְטְלָה), imperative, infinitive
  absolute (קָטוֹל), infinitive construct (לִקְטֹל).
- **Phonology**: Full set of Semitic pharyngeals ח ע and emphatics ט צ ק,
  with the begadkefat stops (ב ג ד כ פ ת) contrasting stop vs fricative
  allophones signalled by **dagesh** (ּ). Tiberian vocalisation has 7 full
  vowels + three chataf (half) vowels (ֲ ֱ ֳ).
- **Syntax notes**: **Waw-consecutive** is the signature of biblical narrative.
  Heavy use of **אֲשֶׁר** as relative ("that/which") — the equivalent of modern
  **ש-**. Object marker **אֵת / אֶת-** with definite DOs. Existential **יֵשׁ /
  אֵין**. Negation: **לֹא** (indicative), **אַל** (jussive/prohibition), **בַּל**
  (poetic).
