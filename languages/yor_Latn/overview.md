# Yoruba (yor)

## Summary

Yoruba (endonym: **Èdè Yorùbá**) is a Niger-Congo language of the Volta-Niger branch (Niger-Congo → Atlantic-Congo → Volta-Niger → Yoruboid → Edekiri → Yoruba). ISO codes: **yo** (639-1), **yor** (639-2/T and 639-3). It is spoken by roughly **45–55 million** people, predominantly in **southwestern Nigeria** (Lagos, Oyo, Ogun, Osun, Ondo, Ekiti, Kwara), **Benin** (Idaasha, Ketu), and **Togo**, with further communities in Sierra Leone and among Afro-descendant populations in **Cuba** (Lucumí), **Brazil** (Nagô), and **Trinidad**, where Yoruba is a liturgical language of Santería, Candomblé, and related traditions.

## Writing system

Yoruba is written in a Latin alphabet augmented with **tone marks** and **underdots**. The vowels **e**, **o** and the consonant **s** take an **under-dot**: **ẹ** (U+1EB9), **ọ** (U+1ECD), **ṣ** (U+1E63) — these represent /ɛ/, /ɔ/, /ʃ/. All vowels carry one of three tones, written with the acute **◌́** (high), grave **◌̀** (low), or macron/absent **◌̄ / ◌** (mid): *bàtà*, *ọ̀rọ̀*, *Yorùbá*. The grapheme **gb** is a single phoneme (labial-velar). The alphabet does not use the letters **c, q, v, x, z**.

## History and status

Yoruba was one of the first West African languages with a standard orthography (Samuel Ajayi Crowther, 1840s). It is a recognised language in Nigerian education, media, and government, and has a rich literary tradition in novels, drama (Soyinka, Fagunwa), and Ifá divination verse.
