# Yoruba vs. confusable languages

Yoruba is most often confused with **Igbo** (both Nigerian Niger-Congo languages written in Latin with heavy diacritics) and, to a lesser extent, with other Volta-Niger languages such as **Fon / Gun (guw)** and **Ewe**.

## Yoruba (yor) vs. Igbo (ibo)

- Under-dot letters differ: Yoruba uses **ẹ**, **ọ**, **ṣ** (under-dot on e, o, s). Igbo uses **ị**, **ọ**, **ụ** (under-dot on i, o, u). The presence of **ẹ** or **ṣ** strongly indicates Yoruba; the presence of **ị** or **ụ** strongly indicates Igbo.
- Yoruba marks tones with acute and grave on every vowel; Igbo uses acute/grave on vowels **and** a distinct macron/no-mark for downstep, but Igbo tone marking is often lighter in running text.
- Function words: Yoruba **ni, ti, kì, kò, àwọn, pé, bí, sì**; Igbo **na, ka, bụ, dị, nke, niile, mana, ma**.
- Yoruba has the **gb** onset and labial-velar; Igbo has **gb**, **kp**, **gh**, **kw**, **gw**, **nw**, **ny** — the density of digraphs is higher in Igbo.

## Yoruba vs. Gun / Gungbe (guw) and Fon

- Gun/Fon also use underdot vowels **ɛ ɔ** and tone, but critically they use the **ɛ** (U+025B) and **ɔ** (U+0254) letters, not under-dots. If you see **ɛ ɔ**, it is Gbe, not Yoruba.
- Gun lacks Yoruba function words *ni, ti, àwọn*; instead uses **nǔ, ɖó, wɛ̀, dó, bo**.

## Yoruba vs. Hausa, pcm, or English

- Hausa has **ɓ ɗ ƙ** and no tone marks — mutually exclusive with Yoruba diacritics.
- Nigerian Pidgin and English are mostly ASCII; any diacritics rule them out.
